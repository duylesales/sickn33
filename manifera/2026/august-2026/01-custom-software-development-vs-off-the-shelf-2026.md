There is a well-documented reason technology leaders keep getting this decision wrong. A joint study by McKinsey and the University of Oxford analysing more than 5,400 large-scale IT initiatives (each with a starting budget above $15 million) found that these projects run 45% over budget and 7% over schedule on average — while delivering 56% less value than originally projected. The overrun rarely announces itself as a single bad decision. It shows up like this:

Every quarter, a CTO somewhere approves a €200,000 Salesforce implementation convinced it will solve every problem. Eighteen months later, they have spent €450,000 on customizations, hired two full-time Salesforce admins, and the sales team still keeps a shadow spreadsheet because the workflow does not match how they actually sell.

The "buy vs. build" decision is the single most consequential technology choice a growing company makes. And in 2026, the economics have shifted dramatically — in ways most analyses published before the AI-assisted development era simply do not account for.

## The Off-The-Shelf Illusion: Why Licensing Costs Are the Smallest Line Item

When a CFO evaluates an off-the-shelf SaaS product, they see a predictable monthly subscription. What they do not see is the iceberg beneath it.

**The hidden cost structure of "buying" software:**

| Cost Category | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 5 Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| License/subscription | €24,000 | €26,400 | €29,040 | €139,128 |
| Implementation partner | €80,000 | — | — | €80,000 |
| Custom integrations | €35,000 | €15,000 | €12,000 | €89,000 |
| Training & change management | €15,000 | €5,000 | €5,000 | €35,000 |
| Admin headcount (1 FTE) | €65,000 | €67,000 | €69,000 | €339,000 |
| Vendor price increases (avg 10%/yr) | — | included | included | included |
| **Total** | **€219,000** | **€113,400** | **€115,040** | **€682,128** |

Gartner's own research on enterprise SaaS spend confirms the pattern from the inside. In its "Why Are You Wasting Your SaaS Expenditure?" analysis, Gartner found that roughly 25% of enterprise SaaS spend goes to underutilized or overdeployed licenses — the direct cost of forcing a workflow into software it was never shaped for. Layer on Gartner's own 2025 vendor tracking, which found that subscription costs from several major SaaS vendors rose 10–20% at renewal — comfortably outpacing average IT budget growth — and the "predictable monthly subscription" a CFO signed off on stops looking predictable by renewal three.

The moment you need a SaaS product to behave differently from its default workflow, you are no longer "buying" software. You are paying a premium to fight against someone else's architectural decisions.

## When Custom Software Development Actually Wins

[Custom software development](https://www.manifera.com/services/custom-software-development/) is not always the right answer. But it is definitively the right answer in three specific scenarios:

### Scenario 1: Your Business Process IS Your Competitive Advantage

If the way you operate is what differentiates you from competitors, forcing that process into a generic tool is strategic suicide. A logistics company whose routing algorithm is 15% more efficient than the industry standard cannot afford to use the same TMS as every competitor.

> *"In short, software is eating the world."* — **Marc Andreessen**, "Why Software Is Eating the World," The Wall Street Journal, 2011

### Scenario 2: Integration Complexity Exceeds 3+ Systems

When your new tool needs to exchange real-time data with more than three existing systems (ERP, CRM, data warehouse, payment processor), the integration cost alone will exceed the cost of building a purpose-built solution. This is not a niche problem — Okta's 2025 "Businesses at Work" report found the average enterprise now runs 101 separate applications, up from under 90 as recently as 2023, meaning integration sprawl is the default state, not the exception. Every API connector you build against a SaaS product's API is a dependency you do not control. When they deprecate that API version — and they will — you are rebuilding on their timeline.

### Scenario 3: You Need It For 5+ Years

The breakeven point for custom vs. off-the-shelf typically hits between Year 3 and Year 4. After that, custom software becomes dramatically cheaper per year because you are not paying recurring license fees or vendor markup on every seat.

## The 2026 Factor: AI-Assisted Development Changed the Math

The economics of custom software shifted in 2024-2026 because AI-assisted development tools (GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Windsurf) reduced the cost of writing boilerplate code substantially. This is not vendor marketing — it is a measured, controlled result. A 2023 randomized controlled study run by researchers from Microsoft, GitHub, and MIT (published as "The Impact of AI on Developer Productivity: Evidence from GitHub Copilot") had developers implement an HTTP server in JavaScript with and without Copilot access. The group using Copilot completed the task 55.8% faster than the control group — a statistically significant result that has since been broadly consistent with what agencies report on well-scoped, boilerplate-heavy work such as CRUD endpoints, form validation, and test scaffolding.

The effect is uneven, though: the same research base shows the gains concentrate in repetitive, well-specified work, not in architectural design, system integration logic, or security hardening — which is exactly why experienced senior engineers remain non-negotiable even as AI tooling matures. This means a competent offshore [software development company](https://www.manifera.com/) equipped with modern AI tooling can deliver custom software faster and cheaper than at any point in the past two decades, provided the judgment-heavy 40% of the work still runs through senior hands.

## The Exit Cost Nobody Prices In: Vendor Lock-In and Data Portability

Every TCO model above assumes you keep using what you build or buy for the full term. The scenario nobody models — because it is uncomfortable to plan for — is what it costs to *leave*. Exit cost is the single most underpriced line item in any buy vs. build decision, and it behaves in opposite directions for the two paths.

**How off-the-shelf lock-in compounds.** Gartner's 2025 vendor-pricing tracking found subscription costs from several major SaaS vendors rising 10–20% at renewal, comfortably outpacing typical IT budget growth of low single digits. That alone is manageable in isolation. What makes it dangerous is what happens when you try to leave: three to five years into a SaaS relationship, your operational data, custom fields, workflow automations, and integration logic all live inside a proprietary schema you do not control. Migrating out means re-mapping every one of those objects into a new system — and vendors have little commercial incentive to make that export process painless. The result is a well-known dynamic in enterprise software procurement: the cost of switching frequently exceeds the cost of one more renewal, even at a bad price, which is precisely what sustains the double-digit annual increases Gartner is tracking.

**How custom software lock-in works differently.** With a custom-built system, "lock-in" is a function of code ownership and documentation quality, not vendor goodwill. If your contract guarantees full IP ownership and source code access from day one (not on project completion), and the system is built on mainstream, well-documented frameworks, the switching cost is bounded by the effort of onboarding a new team — not by a vendor's export limitations or API rate caps on data extraction.

**A pre-signature due-diligence checklist**, whichever path you choose:

| Question | Off-the-Shelf Red Flag | Custom Development Red Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Can we export 100% of our data in a non-proprietary format on demand? | "Enterprise plan only" or throttled export APIs | N/A — you own the database |
| Who owns the integration/workflow logic we build on top? | Often the vendor, by default ToS | Must be explicit in the contract — demand it |
| What is the contractual price-increase cap at renewal? | Frequently uncapped or capped only above 15-20% | N/A — no recurring license fee |
| How long would migration to an alternative take? | Often 6-18 months for embedded, multi-year deployments | Bounded by documentation quality and framework choice |
| Is the source code / schema accessible today, not "at offboarding"? | Rarely, by design | Should be non-negotiable from contract signature |

The practical takeaway: if you are evaluating off-the-shelf software for a process that is genuinely core to your operations, negotiate the exit terms with the same rigor you negotiate the entry price. If you are commissioning custom software, make source code access and framework choice — not just delivery timeline — the first two lines of the contract.

## FAQ

### What is the typical breakeven point between custom software and SaaS licensing? (Scenario: Mid-Market Scale-Up Evaluation)
For a mid-market company with 50-200 users, the breakeven point typically falls between Month 30 and Month 42. Before that point, the upfront investment in custom development exceeds cumulative SaaS licensing costs. After that point, the absence of per-seat licensing fees and vendor price increases makes custom software progressively cheaper each year. The exact crossover depends heavily on the number of integrations required and the complexity of customizations needed in the SaaS alternative.

### How does AI-assisted development impact the cost of building custom software in 2026? (Scenario: CTO Budget Planning)
A controlled 2023 study by researchers from Microsoft, GitHub, and MIT found developers using GitHub Copilot completed a representative coding task 55.8% faster than a control group with no AI assistance. For well-scoped, repetitive work — CRUD operations, API endpoints, test scaffolding — that translates into real hours saved: on a typical €150,000 custom project, agencies applying modern AI tooling to this category of work commonly report €35,000-€55,000 in reduced development hours. What these tools do not reduce is the cost of architectural design, system integration logic, or security hardening — which is why experienced senior engineers remain non-negotiable. The net effect is that custom development has become meaningfully more cost-competitive against off-the-shelf licensing than it was even two to three years ago.

### What happens to my custom software if the development partner goes out of business? (Scenario: Risk-Averse Enterprise CTO)
This is the single most valid concern about custom development. The mitigation is contractual and architectural. First, your contract must guarantee 100% IP ownership and full source code access from Day 1 — not upon project completion. Second, the codebase must use mainstream, well-documented frameworks (React, Node.js, Laravel, .NET) so any competent development team can maintain it. Third, demand CI/CD pipeline documentation and infrastructure-as-code so your hosting is reproducible. At Manifera, we enforce all three by default because we have seen what happens when vendors do not.

### Should a startup with limited funding choose custom development or SaaS tools? (Scenario: Pre-Seed Founder With €500K Runway)
Almost always start with SaaS. At the pre-seed and seed stage, your business model is still a hypothesis. Building custom software for a workflow you will change six times in the next 18 months is burning cash on concrete when you need to be iterating on paper. Use Notion, Airtable, Zapier, and off-the-shelf tools to validate your processes. Once you have proven product-market fit and your operational workflows have stabilized — typically after Series A — that is when custom software development becomes the smart investment.

### How does Manifera ensure quality when the development team is offshore? (Scenario: European Enterprise Compliance)
Three structural controls. First, every project has a Technical Lead based in our Amsterdam office who owns architecture decisions, conducts code reviews, and joins every client sprint review. Second, our Vietnam and Singapore teams follow ISO 27001-aligned development practices with mandatory automated testing coverage above 80%. Third, we use trunk-based development with CI/CD pipelines that block any merge failing automated security scans (SonarQube, Snyk). The client has real-time access to the repository, the Jira board, and the CI/CD dashboard — full transparency, not quarterly reports.

### What should we ask a SaaS vendor about before signing a multi-year contract, to avoid getting locked in? (Scenario: Procurement / Vendor Due Diligence)
Ask five things before signing, not after: (1) Can we export 100% of our data in a non-proprietary format on demand, without a support ticket? (2) Who legally owns the custom fields, workflows, and integration logic we build on top of your platform? (3) Is there a contractual cap on price increases at renewal — Gartner's 2025 vendor-pricing tracking found several major SaaS vendors raising subscription costs 10-20% at renewal, well above typical IT budget growth. (4) How long has migration to an alternative platform taken for comparable customers? (5) Is source-level data schema access available today, or only "at offboarding"? Gartner also estimates roughly 25% of enterprise SaaS spend goes to underutilized or overdeployed licenses — a strong signal that most companies are not asking these questions early enough.
