A Series A term sheet got re-priced 15% down and delayed six weeks — because a founding engineer never signed an IP assignment. Nothing dramatic happened. Nobody had checked. 📋

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **The IP Chain-of-Title Gap:** Every contributor who ever touched the codebase — founder, contractor, "a friend who helped for a weekend" — needs a signed IP assignment, or ownership stays ambiguous.
❌ **The Ad Hoc Contract Problem:** Informal or verbal vendor agreements from the early, chaotic months routinely need expensive reconstruction right before a raise.
❌ **The Automated Scan Surprise:** Diligence teams now run codebase scans for security and provenance issues — gaps invisible in conversation surface directly in the audit.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **IP Assignment By Default:** Every engagement includes explicit, unambiguous work-for-hire language from day one.
✅ **Client-Owned Repositories:** Code lives in your repo throughout the build, not transferred at the end.
✅ **Documented Security Practices:** Credential handling and access control built to hold up under a diligence team's scrutiny.

The cheapest time to fix a diligence gap is 12 months before your raise. The most expensive time is during it. ⏱️
