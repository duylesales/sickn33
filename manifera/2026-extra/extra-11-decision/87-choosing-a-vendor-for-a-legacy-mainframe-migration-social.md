Two million lines of COBOL. Thirty-one years old. The original architects retired. That's the starting condition for most mainframe migrations — and it's why the vendor decision carries more downside than almost any modernization project. 🖥️

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **One-strategy vendors:** Some firms pitch the same rehost/refactor/rearchitect approach to every client regardless of what a real code assessment would show.
❌ **LOC-based pricing:** Quoting by line count ignores cyclomatic complexity and copybook dependency fan-out — the actual predictors of migration risk.
❌ **Skipped parallel run:** Proposing a single cutover for a financial settlement batch job instead of weeks of parallel running to catch problems before production does.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Complexity-weighted assessment first:** We map dead code, copybook dependencies, and business-rule density before recommending rehost, replatform, refactor, or rearchitect.
✅ **Data risk we've actually handled:** EBCDIC conversion, packed decimal translation, and REDEFINES structures — the specific traps that cause silent corruption if mishandled.
✅ **Real parallel-run discipline:** For anything touching financial settlement, we run legacy and modernized systems side by side and reconcile outputs before full cutover.

Cheapest bid, fastest timeline — neither should win a mainframe migration without real scrutiny of the methodology behind it.
