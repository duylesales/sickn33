She inherited the contracts, the lease, and the bank relationships in an orderly transition. What she inherited informally: a decade-long vendor deal that's never once been written down. 📋

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Loyalty Disguised as Due Diligence:** "I've known your father for fifteen years" isn't a technical or commercial argument, but it's often treated like one.
❌ **Never Actually a Business Contract:** Informal arrangements are sometimes literally invoiced to the founder personally — meaning the software was never legally the company's to begin with.
❌ **Pricing Nobody's Ever Challenged:** Rates drift favorable-to-the-vendor for years because nobody previously had the standing to push back.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Fact-Based Vendor Audit:** Written contract, market pricing, documented quality, real ownership — evaluated on the business case, not the family history.
✅ **Renegotiate From New Standing:** Formalize verbal arrangements into contracts that protect both sides, even for vendors worth keeping.
✅ **Clean IP and Account Transfer:** Confirm licenses and source code genuinely sit with the business entity before the transition closes.

A generational handover is the cleanest opportunity you'll get to reset vendor relationships onto a professional footing. 🔄
