A $40M acquisition closes. Week two: the sole contractor who built the core platform exercises a change-of-control termination clause nobody read during diligence. ⚠️

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Unreviewed Change-of-Control Clauses:** Vendor contracts vary wildly on what happens at close — termination rights, repricing, consent requirements — and there's no safe default assumption.
❌ **Assignment vs Novation Confusion:** Buyers assume contracts just "transfer," missing consent requirements that stall or unwind vendor relationships post-close.
❌ **Undocumented Key-Person Risk:** A single small vendor with exclusive, undocumented knowledge of a business-critical system is a landmine, not a line item.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Contract-by-Contract Diligence:** Every material vendor agreement reviewed individually for change-of-control and assignment language before the price is finalized.
✅ **Concentration Risk Quantified:** Real assessment of replacement time and irrecoverable institutional knowledge if a key vendor relationship ends abruptly.
✅ **Durable Replacement Capacity:** Documented, escrow-backed development capacity ready to step in when a vendor relationship doesn't survive the transaction.

Vendor risk in M&A isn't a legal footnote — it's sitting in the data room, waiting to be read. 📄
