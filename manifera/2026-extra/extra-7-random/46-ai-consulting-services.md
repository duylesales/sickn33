Most AI consulting services produce a strategy deck describing AI trends and general opportunity areas, a document that reads well and says relatively little a CEO couldn't have found through reasonably diligent independent research — and the actual value of AI consulting should be measured by a considerably more specific, harder-to-produce deliverable than that.

**The Pain:** A CEO commissioning AI consulting services is typically looking for genuine strategic clarity on where AI creates real opportunity for the specific business, but a large share of AI consulting engagements deliver instead a well-produced but genuinely generic overview of AI capabilities and industry trends, dressed in the company's own branding and terminology but not meaningfully different from what any company in a similar industry would receive from the same provider.

**The Agitation:** A CEO who pays for AI consulting services and receives a generic strategy deck has spent real budget on something that doesn't actually advance the specific decision the engagement was meant to inform — which use cases genuinely fit this company's specific data, systems, and operational reality — leaving the harder, company-specific analysis that would actually enable a confident investment decision either undone or left for a subsequent, separately-billed engagement that a more focused initial engagement would have delivered as its core output.

## The Deliverable That Actually Signals Genuine Value

A genuinely valuable AI consulting engagement should produce a specific, company-grounded output: a prioritized, quantified list of AI use cases specifically evaluated against this company's actual data availability, actual technical constraints, and actual business value potential — not a generic list of possibilities every company in the industry could apply equally, but an analysis that required genuinely understanding this specific company to produce.

The practical test a CEO can apply to evaluate a prospective AI consulting engagement, before committing budget, is to ask what specific inputs the engagement requires from the company itself. A consulting engagement that can be delivered entirely from public information and general AI expertise, without requiring the provider to genuinely examine the company's actual data landscape, existing systems, and specific operational processes, is structurally incapable of producing company-specific recommendations, regardless of how the final deliverable is framed. A genuinely valuable engagement requires access to and analysis of the company's actual data and systems as a core part of the process, because the recommendations it produces need to reflect what's genuinely feasible given that specific reality, not what's generically possible with AI in the abstract.

The second practical test is whether the engagement's recommendations come with the value-and-difficulty quantification covered earlier in the context of generative AI prioritization broadly — genuine business value estimated in specific terms, and genuine implementation difficulty assessed against the company's actual technical environment, not a qualitative "high potential" label applied without the underlying analysis to support it. A CEO should expect and specifically request this quantification as a deliverable, not accept its absence as a normal limitation of strategic consulting.

The third practical test is whether the engagement produces a genuinely prioritized, ranked recommendation — a small number of specific use cases the company should pursue first, in a specific order, with the reasoning behind that ranking made explicit — rather than a broad menu of possibilities left for the company itself to prioritize without the specific analysis that a paid consulting engagement should have already done as its core contribution.

A CEO evaluating AI consulting services against these three tests — genuine company-specific data analysis, explicit value-and-difficulty quantification, and a specific prioritized recommendation — filters out generic strategy-deck engagements in favor of ones structured to actually inform the investment decision the engagement was commissioned to support.
