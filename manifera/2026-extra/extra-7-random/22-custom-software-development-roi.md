Most custom software development ROI calculations compare the build cost against the value of solving the immediate problem, and stop there — leaving out both the ongoing maintenance cost that continues for years and the compounding value the software might deliver well beyond the original problem it was built to solve.

**The Pain:** A CFO evaluating a custom software development business case has a number for what the build will cost and a rough sense of the value it's expected to deliver, but the calculation underlying that comparison typically captures only the most visible costs and benefits, missing categories on both sides that materially change whether the investment actually makes financial sense.

**The Agitation:** A CFO who approves a custom software investment based on an incomplete ROI calculation risks two symmetric mistakes: approving a project whose true total cost, once ongoing maintenance is counted honestly, actually exceeds its value, or rejecting a project whose apparent cost looks too high because the calculation missed compounding benefits that would have made the investment clearly worthwhile. Both mistakes are expensive, and both stem from the same root cause — an ROI calculation that's too narrow to reflect the real decision.

## The Full ROI Calculation, Cost and Benefit Sides

A genuine custom software development ROI calculation requires categories most initial business cases skip, and a CFO who insists on the complete version makes a materially better-informed decision.

On the cost side, the first commonly-missed category is ongoing maintenance and support, which for most custom software runs 15-25% of the original build cost annually, indefinitely, for as long as the software remains in use — a cost that compounds over a multi-year horizon into a total that frequently exceeds the original build cost itself. An ROI calculation that only counts the initial build cost is comparing the full value of the software against a fraction of its true total cost.

The second commonly-missed cost category is the opportunity cost of the engineering capacity consumed — not just the direct cost of the team building the software, but what that same capacity could have delivered if allocated to a different priority. This matters especially for companies building custom software with internal teams, where the "cost" often gets treated as already-sunk headcount rather than genuinely competing capacity that has other valuable uses.

On the benefit side, the first commonly-missed category is compounding value beyond the original problem — custom software built to solve one specific problem often creates a reusable capability or data asset that enables future initiatives at a fraction of what building them from scratch would cost, and an ROI calculation that only counts the value of solving the original problem misses this genuine, if harder-to-quantify, additional return.

The second commonly-missed benefit category is risk reduction — value that doesn't show up as new revenue or direct cost savings but as reduced exposure to a specific operational or compliance risk the custom software addresses. A CFO's calculation that only counts positive value creation, and ignores risk reduction as a real form of return, systematically undervalues custom software built specifically to close a risk gap rather than to generate new revenue.

A complete ROI calculation — full lifetime cost including maintenance, opportunity cost properly counted, compounding value beyond the immediate problem, and risk reduction quantified where genuinely applicable — gives a CFO a number that actually reflects the decision being made, rather than a number that flatters or unfairly penalizes the investment based on which categories happened to be counted.

## A Worked ROI Calculation, Three-Year Horizon

Take a €200,000 custom software build. A narrow ROI calculation compares that €200,000 against, say, €280,000 in year-one value from solving the immediate problem — a 40% ROI that looks solid on a single slide. The complete calculation changes the picture: total three-year cost is €200,000 build plus 20% annual maintenance (€40,000/year × 3 = €120,000) = €320,000, not €200,000. Total three-year value adds the original €280,000 to two compounding streams — a reusable data pipeline enabling a second initiative at roughly 60% of its from-scratch cost (a further €90,000 in avoided spend) and a quantified risk-reduction value from closing a specific compliance gap (conservatively priced at the cost of the incident it prevents, discounted by probability — often €30,000-€60,000 for a moderate-likelihood, moderate-severity exposure). Complete three-year value: roughly €415,000 against €320,000 total cost — a 30% three-year ROI, lower than the flattering single-year 40% but the number that actually survives board scrutiny, because every input is defensible line by line rather than a single optimistic slide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CFO building a custom software business case that only counts build cost) What cost categories does a typical custom software development ROI calculation miss?

Ongoing maintenance and support, typically 15-25% of the build cost annually, and the opportunity cost of the engineering capacity consumed.

### (Scenario: CFO trying to capture value beyond the immediate problem being solved) What benefit categories does a narrow ROI calculation typically miss?

Compounding value from reusable capabilities or data assets beyond the original problem, and risk reduction value that doesn't show up as direct revenue or cost savings.

### (Scenario: CFO wondering how much ongoing maintenance typically costs) How much does custom software maintenance typically cost annually relative to the original build?

Typically 15-25% of the original build cost annually, indefinitely, for as long as the software remains in use.

### (Scenario: CFO trying to account for risk-reduction value in an ROI calculation) How should a CFO quantify risk-reduction value in a custom software ROI calculation?

By estimating the probability and cost of the specific operational or compliance risk being addressed, and counting the reduction in that expected cost as a genuine form of return.

### (Scenario: CFO trying to avoid an inaccurate investment decision) What's the risk of using an incomplete custom software ROI calculation?

It can lead to approving an investment whose true total cost exceeds its value, or rejecting one whose real, compounding return was underestimated.

### (Scenario: CFO trying to build a board-ready ROI calculation for a specific custom software investment) What's a realistic worked example of a complete custom software development ROI calculation over three years?

For a €200,000 build, a narrow single-year view might show 40% ROI, but a complete three-year calculation — adding ~€120,000 in maintenance to cost and compounding value plus risk reduction to benefit — typically nets closer to 25-35% ROI, a lower but far more defensible number for board presentation.

### (Scenario: CFO comparing custom software development ROI against buying an off-the-shelf SaaS alternative) How should ROI calculation differ when comparing custom software development against a SaaS subscription?

Add the SaaS option's multi-year subscription cost and per-seat scaling to its side of the comparison, and add the custom option's ownership advantages — no vendor lock-in, no per-seat cost growth, and the compounding reusability of a data asset the SaaS tool won't expose via API.

### (Scenario: CFO trying to quantify risk-reduction value without an actuarial background) What's a practical way to put a number on risk-reduction value in an ROI calculation?

Estimate the cost of the specific incident the software prevents (a breach, an outage, a compliance fine) and multiply by a conservative annual probability estimate — even a rough discounted figure is more defensible to a board than omitting risk reduction from the ROI calculation entirely.

### (Scenario: CFO deciding whether an offshore delivery model changes the ROI calculation) Does building through a Vietnam-based delivery pod change the custom software development ROI calculation?

Yes, favorably — it lowers the build-cost side of the equation by 40-60% versus Western European rates without reducing the compounding-value or risk-reduction benefit categories, which raises the ROI percentage independent of the underlying business case's strength.
