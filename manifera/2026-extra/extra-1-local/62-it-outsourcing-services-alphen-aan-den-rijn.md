**The Pain:** A CTO at a greenhouse-technology company near Alphen aan den Rijn — a region built around the Dutch horticulture and floriculture cluster — needs to modernize a climate-control monitoring platform and has never bought IT outsourcing services before. Every vendor conversation so far has focused entirely on technical capability, and none has addressed how the engagement is actually structured once work begins.

**The Agitation:** A first-time buyer who skips the structural questions typically discovers the gaps only after signing — no clarity on who owns architectural decisions, no independent quality check, no documented plan for ramp-up. Recovering from a structurally unclear first engagement costs a scale-up 8-12 weeks of lost momentum and a second, more skeptical vendor search.

## The Structural Questions That Matter More Than the Tech Stack

Every vendor pitch for IT outsourcing services leads with technical capability because it's the easiest thing to demonstrate. A first-time buyer needs to get past that and ask about structure instead, because structure is what determines whether the technical capability actually reaches production.

The first question is architectural ownership: does the vendor bring its own technical leadership, or does the CTO's team have to supply all the architectural judgment while the vendor supplies hands? Both models exist and neither is wrong, but a first-time buyer needs to know which one is being sold, because the operational reality differs enormously.

The second question is independent quality verification — whether anyone separate from the people writing the code checks progress and can flag concerns honestly. Without it, a first engagement runs on trust alone, which is a risky foundation for a CTO who has no prior experience calibrating whether a status report is accurate.

The third is a documented onboarding plan. A vendor who can produce a specific ramp-up timeline with named milestones has done this before; one who answers vaguely is likely to figure it out live, on the CTO's first engagement.

### (Scenario: CTO buying IT outsourcing services for the first time) What should a first-time buyer ask before discussing the tech stack at all?
