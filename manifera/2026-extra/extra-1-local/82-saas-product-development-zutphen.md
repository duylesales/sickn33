Every off-the-shelf SaaS tool looks cheaper than SaaS product development until a CFO counts what the company loses by adapting its own process to fit software that was never built for it — a cost that never appears on the subscription invoice.

**The Pain:** A CFO at a manufacturing-technology company in Zutphen — a historic Hanseatic town in the Gelderland river region with a strong manufacturing and metalworking heritage — is weighing SaaS product development for a bespoke production-tracking platform against subscribing to an off-the-shelf manufacturing SaaS tool that almost, but not quite, fits how the company actually operates.

**The Agitation:** A CFO who picks the off-the-shelf tool purely on lower sticker price often discovers, months later, that the company has spent significant time and headcount adapting its own workflows around the tool's limitations — a hidden cost that a build-versus-buy spreadsheet comparing subscription fee against development cost never captured.

## The Time Cost a Build-Versus-Buy Spreadsheet Misses

A build-versus-buy decision for SaaS product development is usually reduced to comparing a subscription fee against a development budget, which misses the largest cost driver in either direction: how much the company's own operational time is consumed adapting to the tool, whichever one is chosen.

The first variable is workflow-fit cost. An off-the-shelf tool that covers 80% of a company's actual process well and forces workarounds for the remaining 20% doesn't just cost the subscription fee — it costs the ongoing operational friction of every workaround, multiplied across every user, every day, indefinitely.

The second is the true cost of a custom build, which a CFO needs priced honestly against realistic timelines and governance overhead, not an optimistic vendor estimate — a custom platform genuinely fitted to the company's process has real cost, but the comparison needs to be to the true off-the-shelf workaround cost, not to a naively cheap subscription number.

The third is the compounding value of a workflow-fitted tool over time — a custom platform that matches the company's actual process saves incremental time on every single use, a benefit that compounds across years in a way a build-versus-buy spreadsheet evaluated only at year one typically misses entirely.

Kovinska Proizvodnja d.o.o., a metalworking manufacturer based near Ljubljana, Slovenia, had subscribed to an off-the-shelf production-tracking SaaS tool that fit most of its process but required daily manual workarounds for a batch-tracking requirement the tool didn't natively support. Over eighteen months, the CFO calculated the cumulative time cost of those workarounds across the shop floor exceeded what a custom-fitted platform would have cost to build.
