You've narrowed it to two options — build the AI team in the Netherlands, or build it offshore in Vietnam under Amsterdam governance. What does the actual line-by-line comparison look like once you strip out the vendor sales language on both sides?

**The Pain:** A Netherlands-based CTO has a board-approved AI budget and needs to make a structural decision within the quarter: hire locally, or contract an offshore pod. Every internal stakeholder has an opinion — engineering wants local hires for "cultural fit," finance wants the offshore number, legal wants to know about IP — and the CTO needs a comparison built on real figures, not vendor pitch decks from either side.

**The Agitation:** Deciding on vibes instead of numbers is how AI budgets get burned twice — once on a slow local search that doesn't produce a hire, and again on a rushed offshore engagement chosen without proper governance because the first plan already ate the timeline. Netherlands-based CTOs who delay this decision past one quarter typically watch 15-20% of the original AI budget disappear into extended recruiting costs, contractor bridges, and opportunity cost before either path is fully committed to.

## The Line-Item Comparison CTOs Actually Need

Strip away the sales language from both sides and the comparison comes down to five variables that actually move the outcome, and a CTO should be able to score any offshore or local option against all five before committing budget.

Cost per unit of capability. A senior AI/ML engineer hired locally in the Netherlands runs well into six figures in fully-loaded annual cost — salary, employer contributions, benefits, and typical Dutch labor overhead — for one person's worth of specialization. An Amsterdam-governed Vietnam pod delivering three to four specialist roles (MLOps, retrieval architecture, evaluation engineering) typically lands at a fraction of that single local salary, which means the real comparison isn't "cheaper labor" — it's "more specialized capability per euro spent."

Time to productive delivery. A local senior AI hire, once found, typically needs four to eight weeks of onboarding before shipping independently — and that's after a search that averages three to six months in the current Dutch AI labor market. An offshore pod with a documented ramp-up plan reaches sprint-level productivity in three to four weeks from contract signature, because the pod arrives with established internal process rather than needing to build it from scratch inside your organization.

Continuity risk. A single local hire is a single point of failure — if they leave, which happens more often than most CTOs want to admit in a market this competitive, the initiative stalls until a replacement is found and re-onboarded. A pod structure spreads that risk across multiple engineers with overlapping knowledge, and Manifera's contract structure specifically protects against the pod being reassigned mid-engagement.

Governance and compliance overhead. A local hire embeds governance responsibility inside the same person doing the technical work, which sounds efficient but means no independent check on model evaluation standards or data handling. An Amsterdam-governed offshore structure separates these functions by design — a second set of eyes that a solo local hire structurally can't provide.

Scalability. Scaling a local team means repeating the six-month search for every additional specialist. Scaling a pod means adding engineers to an already-established process, typically in weeks rather than another multi-month search cycle.

## Technical Deep-Dive: What the Three-Specialist AI Pod Actually Covers

The MLOps, retrieval architecture, and evaluation engineering split isn't arbitrary — it maps to the three failure points that sink most in-house AI initiatives. MLOps ownership means someone is responsible for model versioning, deployment pipelines, and inference cost monitoring from day one, rather than a data scientist bolting deployment onto a role they weren't hired for; without it, a working prototype routinely stalls for two to three months trying to reach production. Retrieval architecture covers the vector database, chunking strategy, and embedding pipeline design specific to a RAG-based system — the difference between a demo that works on ten test documents and a system that holds up against fifty thousand. Evaluation engineering is the discipline most local hiring skips entirely: a dedicated function building labeled test sets, tracking accuracy and hallucination rates release over release, and gating deployment against a defined threshold rather than shipping on developer confidence alone.

A single local AI/ML generalist hire typically covers one, maybe two, of these three competently. The Fenwick pilot's 91% accuracy result came specifically from having evaluation engineering as a dedicated function from week one, not bolted on after the fact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CTO preparing a board recommendation this quarter) We need to decide this quarter. What's the fastest way to get a real comparison, not a sales pitch?

Run a contained pilot on both paths in parallel if timing allows, as Fenwick Cloud Systems did, or request a fully-loaded cost breakdown from Manifera against your specific local salary benchmarks to compare directly.

### (Scenario: CTO worried about losing internal engineering buy-in) How do we get internal engineering buy-in for an offshore decision over a local hire?

Frame it as capability, not cost-cutting: a pod delivers specialist coverage across disciplines a single local hire can't match, which is usually a more persuasive internal argument than a pure cost comparison.

### (Scenario: CTO wanting to know actual onboarding speed) How long before an offshore AI pod is actually shipping production work?

With a documented ramp-up plan, most pods reach full sprint productivity within three to four weeks of contract signature, compared to a three-to-six month search-plus-onboarding cycle for a local hire.

### (Scenario: CTO concerned about scaling later) If the initiative grows, how easily can we scale the team compared to hiring more locally?

Significantly more easily. Adding specialists to an established pod typically takes weeks, versus repeating a multi-month recruiting cycle for each additional local hire.

### (Scenario: CTO wanting independent verification before committing) Can we validate quality before committing to the full engagement?

Yes — a scoped six-to-eight week pilot on a contained use case, like Fenwick's document-classification proof of concept, is the standard way to verify delivery quality before expanding the engagement.

### (Scenario: CTO concerned about GDPR when sending customer data to an offshore AI pod) Is it safe to route customer data to an offshore AI pod for model evaluation or fine-tuning given GDPR?

Yes, provided a Data Processing Agreement is signed with an Amsterdam-domiciled entity that scopes and logs data access, and evaluation datasets are anonymized or synthetic wherever the use case allows it — the accountable party for GDPR purposes remains the EU entity, not the offshore execution team.

### (Scenario: CTO wanting specifics on the evaluation function) What does a dedicated evaluation engineering function actually track sprint over sprint?

A labeled test set scored for accuracy and hallucination rate on every model or prompt change, with results logged release over release and a defined threshold, commonly 90%+ accuracy on the target task, gating whether a change ships to production.

### (Scenario: CTO comparing MLOps role cost specifically) What's the fully-loaded cost difference specifically for an MLOps specialist role between a Netherlands hire and a Vietnam pod seat?

A Netherlands-based MLOps specialist typically runs well into six figures fully loaded annually; an equivalent seat within an Amsterdam-governed Vietnam pod typically costs 55-70% less for comparable seniority, reflecting genuine labor-market differences rather than a capability gap.

### (Scenario: CTO wondering about inference cost management) Does the offshore pod also manage LLM API and inference cost optimization, or only build the pipeline?

A properly scoped MLOps function within the pod owns ongoing inference cost monitoring and optimization, prompt or response caching strategy, and cost-per-query tracking as a standing responsibility, not just initial pipeline construction — a gap many in-house teams discover only after the first surprising API bill.
