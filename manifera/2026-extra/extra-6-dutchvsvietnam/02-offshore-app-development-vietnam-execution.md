Every offshore vendor's website shows the same photo of an open-plan office in Ho Chi Minh City with engineers at standing desks — so how is a VP of Engineering in the Netherlands supposed to tell which of those offices actually ships reliable software and which one is a coworking space rented for the sales call?

**The Pain:** A VP of Engineering at a Netherlands-based scale-up has been asked to evaluate three Vietnam-based offshore app development vendors for a customer-facing mobile and web build. All three decks look nearly identical: "senior engineers," "agile process," "24/7 coverage." None of them explain, in a way that survives scrutiny, what actually happens when a sprint slips or a senior engineer leaves mid-project.

**The Agitation:** Picking a vendor on deck quality alone is how VPs of Engineering end up explaining a six-week slip to their own leadership team with no good answer for why. A poorly-vetted Vietnam engagement typically shows its cracks between month two and month four — after the honeymoon sprint velocity fades and real complexity hits — by which point re-scoping or replacing the vendor costs an additional €40,000–€90,000 in lost time and rework, plus the credibility cost of having recommended the vendor in the first place.

## The Five Signals That Actually Separate Reliable Execution From a Good Sales Deck

Vietnam's software development sector has matured considerably over the past decade, and Ho Chi Minh City in particular now has a genuine depth of senior engineering talent serving European and North American clients across fintech, logistics, and B2B SaaS. But maturity at the market level doesn't guarantee reliability at the vendor level, and a VP of Engineering evaluating offshore app development needs to look past the deck at five specific structural signals.

The first is engineer seniority verification, not self-reported titles. "Senior engineer" means something different at every vendor. Ask for actual years of production experience on the specific stack your app needs, request to interview the named engineers who will be assigned — not a generic "similar profile" — and confirm the tech lead has shipped comparable apps to European markets before, ideally with GDPR-relevant data handling experience if your app touches EU user data.

The second is process maturity that survives a bad sprint, not just a good one. Any vendor looks competent during a smooth onboarding sprint. What matters is what happens when a requirement changes mid-sprint, a third-party API breaks, or a senior engineer needs to be swapped out. Ask specifically how the vendor handles scope change requests, what their code review and CI/CD discipline looks like on a day-to-day basis, and whether QA is embedded in the sprint or bolted on at the end — a distinction that shows up directly in defect rates during UAT.

The third is documented ramp-up velocity with evidence, not a promise. A credible Vietnam-based pod should be able to show — not just claim — a track record of reaching full sprint contribution within three to four weeks: codebase access and walkthrough in week one, first shipped pull request by week two, full ownership by week four. Vendors who can't produce past examples of this timeline are asking you to be their first test case.

The fourth is communication infrastructure across the timezone gap. Vietnam sits six hours ahead of CET (five during Dutch summer time), which leaves a workable overlap window in the Vietnam late afternoon and Dutch late morning. A reliable pod treats this window as sacred for live standups and architecture discussions, and backs it with async-friendly documentation — written handoffs, recorded demos, clear ticket context — so a Netherlands-based VP of Engineering never waits a full day cycle for an answer to a blocking question.

The fifth, and the one most sales decks obscure entirely, is what sits above the execution layer. Pure execution-only vendors put the entire burden of architecture decisions, IP protection, and quality accountability on the buyer's own engineering leadership — which defeats much of the point of outsourcing in the first place. The vendors worth evaluating seriously are the ones with a genuine European-based governance layer sitting above the Vietnam execution team, reviewing architecture and owning risk, so a VP of Engineering isn't personally the last line of defense against a bad technical decision made eight time zones away.

## The Economics

The cost of choosing on deck quality alone rarely shows up in month one — it shows up in month three, when sprint velocity that looked fine in the sales pitch starts slipping and nobody can explain why.
