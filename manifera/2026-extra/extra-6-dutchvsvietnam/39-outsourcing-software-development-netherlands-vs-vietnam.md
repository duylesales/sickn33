Which specific role on a CTO's org chart is hardest to fill in the Netherlands right now — and has anyone actually checked whether a Vietnam software development company can fill it faster, better, or both?

**The Pain:** A CTO comparing Netherlands-based hiring against a Vietnam-based software development company keeps getting compared at the wrong level of granularity — "Dutch developers" versus "Vietnamese developers," as if every engineering role has the same scarcity, cost, and skill-availability profile. It doesn't, and a decision made at that level of abstraction misses where the real comparison actually matters.

**The Agitation:** Generalizing the comparison leads to a common, expensive mistake: assuming the Netherlands-vs-Vietnam decision applies uniformly across the whole team, when in reality some roles are genuinely worth the local premium and others aren't remotely close. A CTO who outsources a role that needed daily on-site product context, or who insists on local hiring for a role the Vietnam market fills exceptionally well, is leaving money and speed on the table in both directions — often €50,000-€100,000 a year in avoidable inefficiency once the wrong roles are assigned to the wrong structure.

## Comparing by Role, Not by Nationality

The role-by-role comparison tells a more useful story than a blanket geography comparison, and a CTO evaluating outsourcing software development companies should run this breakdown before deciding anything at the org-chart level.

Backend and platform engineering is where the cost-versus-availability gap is largest in Vietnam's favor. Mainstream backend stacks — Node.js, Java/Spring, .NET, Python/Django — have deep talent pools in Ho Chi Minh City, with strong CS fundamentals from a university system producing tens of thousands of graduates annually. A senior backend engineer in the Netherlands costs €95,000-€115,000 fully loaded; a comparable-seniority engineer through a governed Vietnam pod runs materially less, without a meaningful skill-depth gap on mainstream stacks — this is the role category where outsourcing software development to Vietnam has the clearest, least-debatable case.

Frontend and mobile development follows a similar pattern, with one caveat: design-system fluency and close product-iteration cycles benefit from tight feedback loops, which a well-structured overlap window handles adequately but not identically to co-location. For most product companies, this is still a strong candidate for offshore execution, provided product ownership and design direction stay close to the founder or product lead.

DevOps and platform infrastructure is a role category worth flagging specifically, because it's frequently under-resourced in both structures. Netherlands-based DevOps talent is scarce and expensive — often commanding a premium above general backend rates due to genuine scarcity — while a mature Vietnam-based delivery organization with ISO 27001-aligned practices and real production infrastructure experience (Kubernetes, cloud-native CI/CD, observability tooling) closes this gap effectively, provided the vendor can show actual production infrastructure ownership, not just application-layer development experience.

Specialized and niche-stack roles — embedded systems, certain data-engineering specializations, deeply regulated-industry compliance engineering — are where local Dutch hiring often still wins, not because Vietnam lacks the talent entirely, but because the pool is thinner and the due-diligence cost of verifying deep specialization from a distance is higher. This is the category a CTO should be most willing to pay the local premium for, rather than the category most outsourcing conversations default to first.

Product and engineering leadership — architects, staff engineers who set technical direction, roles requiring constant proximity to the founder or executive team — is the category that should almost always stay local or, at minimum, stay with people who have deep, sustained context with the company, regardless of geography. The comparison here isn't really cost at all; it's whether the role requires the kind of tacit, accumulated organizational knowledge that doesn't transfer cleanly across a contract boundary.

## The Economics

A flat geography policy — everything local, or everything offshore — routinely wastes €50,000-€100,000 a year in avoidable inefficiency, either through paying local premiums for roles that don't need them or through outsourcing roles that genuinely required local proximity and having to unwind that mistake later. A role-by-role mapping, done honestly against actual scarcity and context requirements, typically identifies 50-70% of a mid-sized engineering org as strong candidates for governed Vietnam execution, with the remainder justified for local retention.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CTO deciding which roles to move offshore first) Which engineering roles have the clearest case for moving to a Vietnam pod?

Backend and platform engineering on mainstream stacks — Node.js, Java/Spring, .NET, Python — has the clearest case, given deep talent availability and minimal skill-depth gap versus a materially lower cost base.

### (Scenario: CTO with unfilled DevOps vacancies) We can't fill DevOps roles locally — is Vietnam a realistic alternative for infrastructure work?

Yes, provided the vendor can demonstrate actual production infrastructure ownership — Kubernetes, cloud-native CI/CD, observability — rather than only application-layer development experience. This is a role category where the cost and availability gap both favor Vietnam.

### (Scenario: CTO unsure whether to keep specialized roles local) Should highly specialized or niche-stack roles stay local in the Netherlands?

Generally yes, or at minimum verified with extra due diligence — the talent pool for niche specializations is thinner everywhere, and the cost of verifying deep specialization remotely is higher, making the local premium more defensible for these specific roles.

### (Scenario: CTO worried about outsourcing leadership roles) Should architecture or technical leadership roles ever be outsourced?

These roles depend heavily on accumulated organizational context and should generally stay local or with long-tenured team members regardless of geography — the comparison here isn't primarily about cost.

### (Scenario: CTO wanting a practical starting point) What's a practical first step to apply a role-by-role comparison to our own team?

Map every role on your current org chart against two questions: does it require daily proximity to founders or domain experts, and is it a mainstream or niche skill set. The answers sort roles into local-premium-justified versus strong-offshore-candidate categories quickly.
