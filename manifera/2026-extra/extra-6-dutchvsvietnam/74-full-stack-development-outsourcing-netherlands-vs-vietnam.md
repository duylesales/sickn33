A Netherlands-based development agency just quoted €850 a day for a senior full stack engineer. A properly governed Vietnam pod quotes less than half that for comparable seniority. What is the CTO actually paying for in the gap, and is it real?

**The Pain:** A CTO comparing full stack development outsourcing quotes has two proposals on the desk: a Dutch agency at a familiar, defensible day rate, and a Vietnam-executed pod at roughly 45-55% of that number. The board wants the cost case explained in the next meeting, and "the Dutch one feels safer" isn't an answer that survives scrutiny — but neither is picking the cheaper number without understanding what specifically the price gap buys or doesn't buy.

**The Agitation:** CTOs who default to the higher-priced local option without interrogating what it actually includes routinely overpay by €120,000-€200,000 a year on a mid-sized full stack engagement for a governance and proximity premium that a properly structured Amsterdam-governed offshore model delivers anyway — and CTOs who default to the cheaper offshore option without verifying governance routinely lose that entire savings back in rework, missed handoffs, and a board that stops trusting the outsourcing line item after the first visible failure.

## The Four-Line Comparison That Actually Matters

Reduce the Netherlands-vs-Vietnam decision to day rate alone and you'll make the wrong call regardless of which way you lean, because day rate answers the wrong question. The comparison that actually predicts engagement success runs across four lines, and a CTO should be able to fill in every cell before signing either contract.

The first line is true hourly cost, not headline day rate. A Dutch agency's €850/day figure typically reflects a blended rate across a team where genuinely senior engineers are a minority; a Vietnam pod's €350-€420/day figure, from a properly governed vendor, reflects a team where the seniority mix is explicit and verifiable. The comparison that matters isn't the headline number — it's cost per verified-senior-hour, and that comparison narrows the apparent gap less than CTOs expect but still favors offshore by a wide margin when governance is genuine on both sides.

The second line is proximity value, and this is where CTOs most overpay for something they don't actually need. A Dutch agency sitting in the same city offers same-timezone meetings and the psychological comfort of a handshake — genuinely valuable for certain engagement types, like a highly exploratory early-stage product where requirements are still being discovered in real time through in-person whiteboarding. For a scoped, architecturally-defined full stack build, that proximity value is close to zero: a 4-6 hour overlap window with Vietnam, structured properly, delivers effectively the same collaboration quality at a fraction of the cost, because the actual bottleneck in most engagements isn't physical distance, it's whether requirements are clear enough to hand off asynchronously.

The third line is governance, and this is the one variable that genuinely should cost something regardless of which country executes. A Dutch agency's governance is implicit in its location — the assumption being that local presence equals oversight, which is often not actually true; plenty of Dutch agencies run thin, understaffed senior review the same way an ungoverned offshore vendor does. A properly structured Amsterdam-governed Vietnam pod makes this governance explicit and contractual rather than assumed, which is a genuinely comparable or better guarantee than "they're in the same city" provides on its own.

The fourth line is scaling elasticity. A Dutch agency, operating in the same tight local labor market the client itself is competing in, faces real capacity constraints scaling a team up mid-engagement — they're recruiting from the same pool. A Vietnam-based delivery model, backstopped by a much larger regional engineering talent base, can scale a pod from four to eight engineers within weeks without the vendor itself hitting a hiring wall, which matters enormously the moment a roadmap accelerates.

## Tech Stack Coverage: What "Full Stack" Should Actually Mean in the Contract

"Full stack" is a vague enough term that two proposals claiming to cover it can differ by an entire layer of infrastructure ownership, and CTOs rarely catch the gap until a deployment issue surfaces mid-project. Before signing either quote, pin down four things explicitly: frontend framework and version commitments (React 18+ versus a legacy jQuery-era codebase inherited from a prior vendor carries a materially different hourly rate, even at the same headline day rate); backend and database ownership, including whether the vendor is responsible for schema migrations and query performance tuning or only feature-level CRUD code; DevOps and infrastructure scope, specifically whether CI/CD pipeline maintenance, container orchestration, and cloud cost optimization sit inside the day rate or bill separately as a specialized add-on most Dutch agencies quote at a 20-30% premium; and a minimum automated test coverage target, typically 70-80% for business-critical paths, written into the definition of "done" rather than left to the delivery team's discretion. A properly governed Vietnam pod should name all four in the statement of work with the same specificity a Dutch agency's proposal claims but frequently doesn't itemize either.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CTO justifying a Vietnam pod choice to a skeptical board) How do we prove governance is comparable when the team isn't physically local?

Insist on contractual governance terms: named architecture reviewers, a defined audit cadence, and binding escalation authority written into the engagement, not assumed from proximity. Manifera provides all three as standard contract terms, which a board can verify directly.

### (Scenario: CTO comparing headline day rates) Is comparing day rates directly a fair way to evaluate the two options?

Not on its own. Compare cost per verified-senior-hour instead, since a blended local day rate can hide a junior-heavy team just as easily as an offshore rate can, and the seniority mix is what actually predicts delivery quality.

### (Scenario: CTO worried about losing collaboration quality without physical proximity) Does a 4-6 hour timezone overlap actually work for a full stack build?

Yes for scoped, architecturally-defined engagements, which cover most full stack outsourcing scenarios. It works less well for highly exploratory early-stage product discovery still being defined through in-person collaboration, which is a narrower use case.

### (Scenario: CTO anticipating a roadmap acceleration) What happens if we need to scale the team up mid-engagement?

A Vietnam-based pod, backed by a larger regional talent base, can typically scale from a few engineers to double that within weeks. A same-market local agency competing in the same tight labor pool the client is in usually faces materially slower scaling.

### (Scenario: CTO deciding how to structure the comparison for the board) What should we actually ask both vendors before deciding?

Ask both for a named team with verifiable seniority, a documented governance and escalation structure, and a stated scaling timeline if requirements grow. Comparing those three answers side by side is more useful to a board than comparing day rates alone.

### (Scenario: CTO reviewing a vague full-stack SOW) What should "full stack" actually mean in an outsourcing statement of work?

Name four things explicitly: frontend framework and version, backend and database ownership (including schema migrations and query tuning), DevOps and infrastructure scope, and a minimum test coverage target. A vague "full stack" line item without these specifics is where scope disputes originate mid-project.

### (Scenario: CTO worried about DevOps being billed separately) Is DevOps and CI/CD pipeline maintenance included in a standard full stack outsourcing day rate?

Not always — confirm it explicitly. Many Dutch agencies quote DevOps and infrastructure work as a specialized add-on at a 20-30% premium over the base day rate, which changes the real comparison if one vendor's quote already includes it and the other doesn't.

### (Scenario: CTO setting quality bar in contract) What test coverage target should be written into the contract's definition of done?

70-80% automated test coverage on business-critical paths is a reasonable floor, written explicitly into the definition of "done" rather than left to the delivery team's discretion, so it's enforceable rather than aspirational.

### (Scenario: CTO inheriting a legacy codebase) Does a legacy frontend framework change the outsourcing rate compared to a modern stack?

Yes — a legacy jQuery-era or unsupported-framework codebase typically commands a higher effective hourly rate than a modern React or equivalent stack, even at the same headline day rate, because unfamiliar legacy patterns slow delivery regardless of engineer seniority.
