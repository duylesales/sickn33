If three vendors pitch you three different "outsourcing solutions" and none of them names which of the three actual models they're selling, how would you even compare the proposals side by side?

**The Pain:** A Netherlands-based CTO has been asked to scale engineering capacity without a matching headcount budget, and the natural first instinct is to open a local vacancy. But local hiring in the current Dutch tech market means competing against Randstad scale-ups for the same thin pool of mid-level and senior engineers, and the CTO hasn't yet mapped out what the offshore alternative actually looks like structurally, beyond the word "outsourcing" itself.

**The Agitation:** CTOs who evaluate offshore options without first understanding the three underlying models end up comparing proposals that aren't actually comparable — a staff-augmentation quote against a dedicated-pod quote look similar on a one-page summary but carry completely different management overhead and risk profiles. Choosing the wrong model for the situation typically costs a mid-market company €40,000-€90,000 in wasted management time, rework, and a delayed restart with a different structure once the mismatch becomes obvious, usually two to three months in.

## The Three Models, and Why the Difference Actually Matters

Every offshore software engagement on the market fits into one of three structural models, and a CTO's first job — before evaluating a single vendor — is understanding which one actually fits the problem being solved, because vendors rarely volunteer this distinction unprompted.

Staff augmentation places individual engineers into your existing team and process. You get flexibility and typically the lowest headline rate, but you inherit the full management burden: sprint planning, code review standards, onboarding each individual, and absorbing attrition risk one hire at a time. This model fits well when you have strong internal technical leadership already and simply need more hands executing against your own process — a genuine capacity extension, not a capability gap.

Project-based outsourcing fixes scope, timeline, and price upfront for a defined deliverable. It looks safe on paper because the cost is known before work starts, but the model breaks down the moment requirements shift — and in real software development, requirements almost always shift once users see a working version. Change orders then rebuild the cost uncertainty the fixed price was supposed to eliminate, often at a premium, since the vendor now has leverage mid-contract.

Dedicated pods are a self-contained team — engineers, a tech lead, and typically embedded QA — that owns a product area end-to-end, arriving with its own established delivery process rather than plugging into yours. This model costs more per hour than raw staff augmentation but dramatically less in management overhead, because the pod's tech lead absorbs the coordination burden a CTO would otherwise carry personally across a roster of individual contractors.

The model choice should follow from the actual problem, not from whichever a vendor's sales team defaults to pitching. A capacity gap on a stable, well-understood codebase often fits staff augmentation well. A one-time, clearly-scoped deliverable — a migration, an integration — can fit project-based work if the scope genuinely won't move. An ongoing product area needing sustained ownership, technical judgment, and institutional continuity needs a dedicated pod, and trying to force that need into a staff-augmentation contract is where most CTOs discover the mismatch the hard way, usually around month three when the coordination overhead becomes visible on their own calendar.

## The Economics

The wrong outsourcing model doesn't fail on the invoice — it fails in hidden management time. A CTO personally coordinating six individual contractors under a staff-augmentation contract for a sustained product area is easily spending 15-20 hours a week on coordination that a dedicated pod's tech lead would otherwise absorb, worth roughly €4,000-€6,000 a month in executive time the board never sees itemized. Add the two-to-three month delay typically required to recognize the mismatch and restructure the engagement, and the "cheaper" model ends up costing more per shipped feature than the properly structured alternative would have from day one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CTO comparing vendor proposals that look similar) How do I tell which outsourcing model a vendor is actually proposing?

Ask directly whether engineers report into your process (staff augmentation), deliver a fixed defined scope (project-based), or arrive as a self-contained team with its own tech lead and process (dedicated pod). Vendors rarely volunteer this distinction unless asked.

### (Scenario: CTO with a stable, well-understood codebase) When does staff augmentation actually make sense over a dedicated pod?

When you already have strong internal technical leadership and simply need more hands executing against an established process on a codebase your team already understands well.

### (Scenario: CTO with a one-time defined deliverable) Is project-based outsourcing ever the right choice?

Yes, for genuinely fixed-scope work like a one-time migration or integration where requirements are unlikely to shift. It becomes risky the moment scope is uncertain, since change orders rebuild the cost the fixed price was meant to avoid.

### (Scenario: CTO realizing a model mismatch mid-engagement) We're three months into a staff-augmentation contract and it feels wrong for an ongoing product area. Can we restructure?

Yes, and it's a common correction. Manifera regularly converts staff-augmentation engagements into dedicated pods once the ongoing-ownership need becomes clear, without discarding the work already completed.

### (Scenario: CTO wanting to test a model before full commitment) Can we pilot a dedicated pod before committing to a long-term engagement?

Yes, a four-to-six week scoped pilot is the standard way to validate ramp-up speed and delivery quality before extending into a longer engagement.
