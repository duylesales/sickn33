A CFO notices a familiar Dutch software vendor's team roster has quietly shifted over eighteen months — more Vietnamese surnames on the sprint reports, the same Amsterdam account manager, the same invoice format. Is this a bait-and-switch, or a structural shift worth understanding on its own terms?

**The Pain:** A CFO managing a long-standing relationship with a Netherlands software company is noticing a pattern across the vendor market: firms that used to deliver purely with Dutch-based staff are increasingly routing actual execution through Vietnam-based teams, while keeping Amsterdam as the client-facing and governance layer. Nobody explains this shift proactively, and a CFO who discovers it after the fact reasonably wonders what else has changed without disclosure.

**The Agitation:** A CFO who doesn't understand why this shift is happening, and evaluates it purely as a red flag, risks two costly mistakes: staying with a vendor for the wrong reasons out of misplaced loyalty to a "Dutch delivery" model that no longer actually exists as advertised, or overreacting and switching vendors entirely without recognizing that a well-executed version of this same shift is exactly what a properly governed offshore engagement should look like — losing the relationship continuity that took years to build over a structural change that, done right, isn't actually a problem.

## Why the Shift Is Happening, and What Determines Whether It's Handled Well

The shift toward Netherlands-headquartered software companies routing execution through Vietnam isn't a secret industry trend — it's a rational response to a labor market that's been structurally tight for senior engineering talent across the Netherlands and wider EU for years, combined with margin pressure that's made pure Dutch-delivery pricing increasingly uncompetitive against hybrid and pure offshore alternatives. Understanding why it's happening helps a CFO evaluate whether a specific vendor's version of it is trustworthy.

The first driver is talent market reality. The Netherlands has produced fewer senior software engineers than its scale-up and enterprise software sector demands for the better part of a decade, and Dutch software companies compete for the same shrinking senior talent pool their own clients compete for. Routing execution to Vietnam, where the graduate and mid-career engineering talent base is considerably deeper, is often simply how a Netherlands software company keeps its delivery capacity from becoming the bottleneck on its own growth.

The second driver is client cost pressure. As offshore and hybrid vendors have matured and become a credible, lower-risk alternative to pure local delivery, purely Dutch-delivered pricing has become harder to defend against a CFO doing a genuine market comparison. Netherlands software companies that don't adapt their delivery model lose deals on price to hybrid competitors who can offer comparable governance at a lower total cost — which pushes even traditionally local-only firms toward a hybrid structure simply to remain competitive.

The third driver, less discussed but real, is that a well-run hybrid structure is genuinely a better delivery model in several respects, not just a cheaper one — a Vietnam-based team backed by a large regional talent market offers better scaling elasticity than a Netherlands-only bench competing in the same tight local labor market as its own clients.

What determines whether a specific vendor's shift toward this model is trustworthy comes down to three things a CFO should ask directly rather than infer from a changing sprint roster. First: was the shift disclosed proactively, with a clear explanation of what governance remains Netherlands-based and what has moved? A vendor that lets a CFO discover the shift by noticing names on a sprint report has already failed a transparency test that matters regardless of whether the underlying delivery quality is fine. Second: did contract terms — pricing, IP assignment, quality guarantees — get renegotiated to reflect the new cost structure, or is the CFO still paying Netherlands-delivery pricing for a partially offshore-delivered engagement? Third: is there still a genuine, functioning Amsterdam governance layer, or has "Amsterdam" become just the invoicing address while governance quietly thinned out along with the delivery shift?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CFO noticing an undisclosed shift in vendor delivery) Why is our long-standing Netherlands vendor increasingly delivering through Vietnam-based engineers?

Most commonly, structurally tight Dutch senior engineering talent supply and increasing client cost pressure from hybrid competitors are pushing traditionally local-only vendors toward offshore-delivered execution to remain competitive and to fill capacity.

### (Scenario: CFO deciding whether to react negatively to the shift) Is a vendor shifting delivery to Vietnam automatically a red flag?

Not automatically. The red flag is non-disclosure and pricing that doesn't reflect the actual delivery structure, not the shift itself. A well-governed, transparently structured hybrid model can be a genuine improvement.

### (Scenario: CFO wanting to check if pricing still reflects reality) How do we know if we're still paying legacy Netherlands-delivery rates for what's now partially offshore-delivered work?

Ask your vendor directly for a breakdown of what portion of the team is Netherlands-based versus Vietnam-based, and compare current pricing against market rates for a properly structured hybrid engagement at that composition.

### (Scenario: CFO worried governance has quietly thinned) How do we verify governance hasn't eroded along with the delivery shift?

Request a documented governance cadence — architecture review frequency, audit history, escalation record — the same way you'd evaluate any offshore vendor's governance function, regardless of the vendor's Dutch heritage or brand history.

### (Scenario: CFO considering switching to a vendor built as hybrid from the start) Is it better to work with a vendor built as a hybrid model from day one rather than one that drifted into it?

Often yes, because a vendor structured as hybrid from the outset typically has pricing, governance, and disclosure built around that model natively, rather than carrying legacy pricing and communication habits from a purely local delivery era.
