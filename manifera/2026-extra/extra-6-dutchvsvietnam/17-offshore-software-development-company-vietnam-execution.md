Ho Chi Minh City has hundreds of software development companies claiming senior engineering talent and mature process — so what actually separates the handful that are genuinely reliable from the majority that only look that way in a sales deck?

**The Pain:** A VP of Engineering at a Netherlands or EU-based company is running a vendor evaluation across several Vietnam-based software development companies, and every proposal reads nearly identically: senior engineers, agile process, competitive rates. Nothing in the RFP responses actually differentiates the companies that will deliver from the ones that will produce a plausible-looking mess that surfaces as technical debt eighteen months later.

**The Agitation:** Selecting a Vietnam-based company on proposal quality alone, without structural verification, routinely produces a costly mismatch. Industry data on offshore engagement failure consistently points to process and communication gaps, not raw technical skill, as the dominant failure cause — and a mismatched engagement typically costs a VP 25-40% of the original contract value in rework once the gap surfaces, usually well after the vendor relationship has already consumed a budget cycle.

## Five Structural Signals That Separate Reliable Vietnam Companies From the Rest

Vietnam's software development sector has genuinely deep talent — Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi produce a large volume of strong computer-science graduates annually, and the country's engineering sector has matured substantially over the past decade. The differentiation a VP of Engineering needs isn't "does Vietnam have good engineers" — it does — but which specific companies have built the structure to deploy that talent reliably for an international, EU-based client.

**Signal one: verifiable seniority, not claimed seniority.** Any proposal will describe its team as senior. A reliable company can name the actual tech lead who will be assigned, describe their specific prior project experience, and make them available for a technical interview before signing — not a generalist sales engineer standing in for the real delivery team.

**Signal two: a documented, auditable development process.** Ask specifically how code review works — who reviews, at what cadence, against what standard — and ask to see an example of the artifact (a completed PR review, a sprint retrospective document). Companies with a real process can produce this immediately; companies without one improvise an answer that sounds plausible but has no artifact behind it.

**Signal three: client concentration and specialization.** A company that has delivered repeatedly for European clients, ideally in a comparable industry vertical, understands the specific expectations that differ from domestic Vietnamese or broader APAC client relationships — communication cadence, documentation formality, regulatory context like GDPR. A company with no track record serving EU clients specifically is a higher-variance bet, regardless of technical skill.

**Signal four: English-language technical communication quality**, assessed directly rather than assumed. This isn't about accent or fluency in casual conversation — it's about whether the tech lead can write a clear architecture decision document or explain a technical tradeoff in writing without ambiguity. A VP should request a written technical sample as part of the evaluation, not rely on the verbal impression from a single sales call.

**Signal five: what sits above the execution team.** This is the signal most RFP processes skip entirely, and it's the one with the most long-run consequence. A Vietnam-based company that is purely an execution shop, with no independent governance or quality-accountability layer, places the entire burden of catching problems on the client's own oversight. A structure where an accountable governance function — ideally under a jurisdiction the client's own legal and compliance teams recognize — sits above the execution team changes the risk profile substantially, because quality assessment isn't self-reported by the same party being paid for the work.

A VP running a rigorous evaluation across these five signals will find the pool of genuinely reliable Vietnam-based companies is considerably smaller than the pool of companies that respond competently to an RFP. That's the gap the evaluation process needs to close.
