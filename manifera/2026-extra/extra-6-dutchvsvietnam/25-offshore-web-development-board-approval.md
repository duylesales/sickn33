The budget line reads "offshore web development, Vietnam, €140,000" — and the first question from the board isn't going to be about the number, it's going to be "who's accountable if this goes wrong," and a CFO without a rehearsed answer loses the room before the number even gets discussed.

**The Pain:** A CFO at a European company has built a solid financial case for an offshore web development engagement in Vietnam — the savings math works, the vendor looks credible — but the board approval meeting isn't a financial review, it's a risk interrogation, and the questions that actually derail approval are rarely about cost at all.

**The Agitation:** A CFO who walks into board approval prepared only with cost savings and gets blindsided by a governance, compliance, or accountability question often sees the decision deferred to "further review" — which in practice means another quarter of delay, competitors moving faster, and the CFO's credibility on the next budget ask taking a hit before it's even proposed.

## What the Board Actually Wants to See Before Sign-Off

Boards approving an offshore engagement for the first time are pattern-matching against a specific fear: an outsourcing decision that looked good on a slide and turned into a governance failure eighteen months later, a story most board members have heard secondhand if not lived through directly. A CFO who anticipates the actual questions, rather than just defending the savings number, gets through approval faster and with fewer follow-up rounds.

**Who is legally accountable if delivery fails or data is mishandled?** This is almost always the first substantive question, and "the Vietnam vendor" is not an acceptable answer to a European board. The answer needs to name a specific EU-based accountable entity — in Manifera's structure, the Amsterdam-based governance layer — that holds contractual responsibility under Dutch or EU law, not an offshore entity in a jurisdiction the board has no practical enforcement path against.

**What happens to our IP?** Boards, particularly at venture-backed companies anticipating future funding rounds, will probe IP assignment specifically because a gap here becomes a due-diligence blocker at the worst possible moment — mid-raise. The answer needs to cover individual engineer NDAs (not just vendor-entity-level), source-controlled repositories owned by the client from day one, and an IP assignment clause that survives contract termination and is enforceable under EU-familiar law.

**How is this different from the failed offshore story someone on this board has heard before?** Nearly every board has a secondhand horror story — the vendor that rotated staff constantly, the project that needed a full rebuild eighteen months in. The CFO's answer needs to name the specific structural difference: a governance layer separated from execution, engineer continuity contracted for project duration, and documented process standards, not just "we chose a better vendor this time."

**What's the actual risk-adjusted cost comparison, not just the headline savings?** A sophisticated board will ask the CFO to defend the savings number against the realistic risk of rework, delay, or governance failure — not just quote the day-rate differential. The strongest answer includes the cost of the governance layer explicitly (typically 8-15% over pure execution pricing) as the line item that prices out the very risks the board is asking about.

**Can we start small and validate before full commitment?** Boards are far more comfortable approving a bounded pilot than an open-ended commitment, especially for a first offshore engagement. A CFO who proposes a scoped four-to-six-week pilot with clear success criteria, rather than asking for a full annual budget upfront, converts a harder yes/no decision into an easier phased one.

A CFO who walks in with prepared, specific answers to these five questions — not generic reassurances — typically gets through board approval in a single session instead of being sent back for "more information," which itself has a real cost in delayed project start.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CFO anticipating the board's first and hardest question) What's the single most important question to be ready for in board approval?

Who is legally accountable if something goes wrong. A European board needs a named, EU-based accountable entity — not just the offshore execution vendor — and this is almost always the first substantive question asked.

### (Scenario: CFO worried about IP questions derailing a future funding round) How specific does the IP answer need to be for a board that's anticipating a future raise?

Very specific: individual engineer NDAs, client-owned source repositories from day one, and an IP assignment clause enforceable under EU law that survives contract termination. A vague "IP is covered" answer will not satisfy a board anticipating due diligence.

### (Scenario: CFO trying to address the board's secondhand horror story) How do we respond when a board member cites a colleague's bad offshore experience?

Name the specific structural difference in your proposal — a separated governance and execution layer, contracted engineer continuity, documented process standards — rather than offering general reassurance that "this vendor is different."

### (Scenario: CFO deciding how to frame the savings number) Should we lead the board presentation with the cost savings or the risk structure?

Present both, but frame the savings as risk-adjusted from the start, explicitly including the governance premium as the line item that prices out legal, compliance, and IP risk, rather than presenting a headline number the board will later discount.

### (Scenario: CFO deciding whether to ask for the full budget or a smaller pilot first) Is it better to ask for full budget approval or propose a pilot first?
