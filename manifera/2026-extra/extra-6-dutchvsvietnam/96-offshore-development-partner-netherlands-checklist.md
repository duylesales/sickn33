## The Four Questions a Standard RFP Never Asks

Most due-diligence checklists a CTO inherits from procurement were built for enterprise vendor selection and optimize for the wrong signals when applied to offshore development partner. Years in business and client logos predict institutional stability, not delivery quality on your specific engagement — and a CTO evaluating a first offshore engagement needs criteria that actually differentiate.

The first real question is who owns architectural decisions once the contract is signed. A staff-augmentation vendor rents individual engineers into your existing process, meaning you retain full architectural ownership and management overhead. A dedicated pod arrives with its own technical leadership and shared accountability for outcomes. Neither structure is inherently wrong, but a CTO needs to know which one is actually being sold, because vendor pitch decks routinely blur the distinction, and the operational reality — who you call when something goes wrong — differs enormously between them.

The second question is governance separation: does anyone independent of the delivery team have visibility into what's actually happening in the sprint? A vendor where the same people delivering the code are also the only source of status reporting has a structural conflict of interest baked in — bad news has no independent channel to surface through. The offshore development partner worth shortlisting can name a specific governance layer, separate from day-to-day delivery, that reviews progress and can flag problems the delivery team itself might prefer not to highlight.

The third question is IP protection specificity. "We sign an NDA" is not an answer — a CTO needs to know whether IP assignment happens at the individual engineer level or only the vendor-entity level, whether source repositories are client-owned from day one, and whether the IP clause survives contract termination. This question matters disproportionately for a Netherlands-based company because it resurfaces, often painfully, during due diligence for a future funding round or acquisition, at precisely the moment a CTO has the least room to fix a gap discovered late.

The fourth question is documented ramp-up velocity. Ask for a specific, named onboarding timeline with milestones, not a vague estimate. A vendor that can produce a 30/60/90 day plan with concrete deliverables at each stage has clearly done this before and knows what productive ramp-up actually requires; a vendor that answers with "it varies" is signaling they figure it out live, on your engagement's clock.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CTO building a first-time offshore vendor evaluation checklist) What questions actually differentiate offshore development partner vendors beyond logos and years in business?

Ask who owns architectural decisions post-signature, whether an independent governance layer verifies delivery status, how specific the IP assignment structure is, and whether they can produce a documented onboarding timeline with milestones.

### (Scenario: CTO worried about IP exposure surfacing during a future funding round) Why does IP assignment specificity matter more for Netherlands companies planning to raise funding?

Vague, vendor-entity-only IP clauses are exactly the kind of gap that surfaces during investor due diligence, often with no time to renegotiate cleanly. Individual engineer NDAs and client-owned repositories from day one avoid that exposure entirely.

### (Scenario: CTO evaluating whether a vendor's status reports can be trusted) How do we know a vendor's progress reports aren't just what the delivery team wants us to hear?

Ask whether anyone independent of day-to-day delivery reviews progress and has authority to flag problems. A vendor where the same people delivering the work are the only source of status has a built-in conflict of interest.

### (Scenario: CTO comparing staff augmentation against a dedicated pod structure) What's the practical difference between staff augmentation and a dedicated offshore pod for due-diligence purposes?

Staff augmentation leaves architectural ownership and management overhead with you; a dedicated pod arrives with its own technical leadership and shared accountability. Know which one you're evaluating, since vendor pitches often blur the line.

### (Scenario: CTO trying to verify a vendor's onboarding claims before signing) How do we verify a vendor's ramp-up claims before committing budget?

Ask for a specific 30/60/90 day plan with named deliverables at each stage, not a general estimate. A vendor who can produce one has done this before; one who answers "it varies" likely hasn't planned it.
