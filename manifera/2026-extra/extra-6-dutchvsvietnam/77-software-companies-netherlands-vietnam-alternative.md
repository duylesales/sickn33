A VP of Engineering assumes a Netherlands-delivered engagement is inherently lower-risk than a Vietnam-delivered one. Then they ask their current Dutch vendor to name the actual seniority breakdown of the assigned team, and the answer is noticeably vaguer than expected.

**The Pain:** A VP of Engineering evaluating software companies in the Netherlands against a Vietnam-delivered alternative is working from an unexamined assumption: that "delivered in the Netherlands" and "lower risk" are the same thing. That assumption drives a lot of vendor selection without ever being tested directly against what actually varies between the two models and what, on closer inspection, doesn't vary nearly as much as assumed.

**The Agitation:** A VP of Engineering who selects purely on delivery geography, without testing the assumption, risks two opposite failure modes: overpaying 60-90% more for a Netherlands-delivered engagement that turns out to have the same team continuity and quality gaps as a poorly-governed offshore one, or underpaying for a Vietnam-delivered engagement that lacks the governance layer that would have made the cost difference safe. Either mistake typically costs a mid-sized engagement €50,000-€120,000 in the first year, either as an avoidable premium or as unmanaged delivery risk.

## Separating What Actually Differs From What's Assumed to Differ

There are exactly three things that genuinely differ between a purely Netherlands-delivered engagement and a properly governed Vietnam-delivered one, and a much longer list of things VPs of Engineering assume differ but, on close inspection, don't reliably differ at all — because both models can be done well or badly, and the geography alone doesn't determine which.

What genuinely differs, first: cost base. Vietnam-based senior engineering talent, at comparable verified seniority, runs at roughly 40-55% of Netherlands-based rates, a structural function of regional cost-of-living and wage economics rather than a quality discount. This is real and doesn't require any asterisk.

Second, what genuinely differs: timezone overlap structure. Netherlands-delivered teams share full working-hour overlap by default; Vietnam-delivered teams require a deliberately structured 4-6 hour daily overlap window with async handoff for the remainder. This is a real operational difference that needs deliberate process design — not a blocker, but not nothing either, and a VP of Engineering should expect the vendor to have a specific, tested cadence for it rather than a vague assurance.

Third, what genuinely differs: talent pool depth for specific niche skills. For a small number of highly specialized, low-supply skill sets — certain legacy enterprise systems, some deep specialization areas — the Netherlands' talent pool, while smaller overall, may have marginally deeper representation in a specific niche than a general Vietnam talent search would surface quickly. This is a narrow, skill-specific consideration, not a general rule.

Now what's commonly assumed to differ but doesn't reliably: team continuity. Dutch software companies rotate engineers off projects for new business pitches or competing priorities at rates that are, anecdotally and in vendor comparison surveys, not meaningfully lower than poorly-managed offshore arrangements — continuity is a function of contract structure and internal incentives, not geography. Code quality standards: a Netherlands-delivered team with weak code review discipline produces worse code than a well-governed Vietnam pod with strong review discipline, and vice versa — quality is a function of process rigor, not location. Communication clarity: a Dutch team with poor documentation habits creates more friction than a Vietnam pod with excellent async documentation practices, even accounting for the language and timezone difference. And architectural accountability: unless a Netherlands-delivered vendor has an explicit, named architecture governance function — which many don't, defaulting to "the senior engineer figures it out" — it offers no structural advantage over a Vietnam pod governed by a dedicated Amsterdam function that does exactly that as its defined job.

The practical implication for a VP of Engineering: evaluate the three things that genuinely differ (cost, overlap structure, niche skill depth) on their actual merits for your specific engagement, and evaluate everything else — continuity, quality, communication, governance — as vendor-specific questions to interrogate directly, regardless of which geography is proposed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: VP of Engineering questioning an unexamined assumption) Is a Netherlands-delivered engagement automatically lower-risk than a Vietnam-delivered one?

Not automatically. Team continuity, code quality, and architectural governance are functions of vendor process and contract structure, not geography. Some Netherlands-only vendors lack an explicit governance function that a well-structured Vietnam engagement provides by design.

### (Scenario: VP of Engineering trying to identify genuine differences) What actually genuinely differs between the two delivery models?

Three things: cost base (Vietnam runs 40-55% lower at comparable seniority), timezone overlap structure (requiring deliberate process design for Vietnam-delivered work), and marginal talent pool depth for a small number of highly niche skill sets.

### (Scenario: VP of Engineering evaluating a Netherlands-only vendor) How do we check whether our current Netherlands vendor actually has a real governance function?

Ask them to name the specific person or function accountable for architecture sign-off, independent of the delivery team itself. If the answer is "the senior engineer handles it," that's not a separated governance function, regardless of delivery location.

### (Scenario: VP of Engineering concerned about timezone overlap) How much does the timezone gap with Vietnam actually affect delivery quality?

With a deliberately structured 4-6 hour daily overlap window and documented async handoff practices, it's a manageable process design question rather than a quality blocker, though it does require the vendor to have a tested cadence rather than an improvised one.

### (Scenario: VP of Engineering deciding how to run the comparison) What's the right way to compare a Netherlands vendor against a Vietnam-delivered alternative?

Evaluate cost, overlap structure, and niche skill fit on their actual merits for your engagement, and interrogate continuity, quality process, and governance as vendor-specific questions for both options rather than assuming either geography answers them automatically.
