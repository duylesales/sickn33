The enterprise procurement checklist had a single question — "can we get our data out in a standard format if we ever leave" — and the honest answer was no, which quietly ended a six-figure deal three weeks before the sales team even found out that was the reason.

**The Pain:** A CFO's platform was built without a genuine, complete data export capability, because in the early days no customer asked for one and building it never competed successfully against features that drove immediate new revenue. Existing customers who want their own data can get a partial CSV export of a few core tables, but there's no comprehensive, structured export covering the full dataset, and no documented import path for a competitor's format either — a gap that shows up as vague customer frustration for small accounts but as a hard procurement blocker for larger enterprise buyers whose legal and IT teams explicitly require data portability guarantees before signing.

**The Agitation:** Enterprise buyers increasingly treat data portability as a non-negotiable procurement requirement, not a nice-to-have, because their own risk teams have learned the hard way what vendor lock-in costs when a relationship sours or a vendor's roadmap diverges from their needs. A platform that can't demonstrate a clean export path doesn't just lose deals overtly — it loses them silently, often without direct feedback, because a procurement team that flags data portability as a blocker rarely explains the rejection in detail back to the sales rep who never even knew it was being evaluated as a criterion.

## The Data Portability Mandate

The first mandate is building a genuinely comprehensive, structured data export covering the complete customer dataset, not just a handful of core tables — in a well-documented, standard format that a customer's own technical team or a competitor's import tooling could realistically consume without a custom translation project of its own.

The second mandate is treating export capability as a commercial asset in sales conversations, not just a churn-mitigation feature — a documented, demonstrable export path answers the procurement question before it becomes a blocker, and being able to say "here's exactly what a full export looks like" during a sales cycle removes a specific, common objection from larger buyers before it ever costs a deal.

The third mandate is API-based programmatic export in addition to a manual download option, since enterprise buyers with their own data infrastructure typically want an automatable path for ongoing data synchronization or backup, not just a one-time manual dump they'd need for an actual departure scenario.

The fourth mandate is transparent, proactive communication about data ownership and portability in the product's own terms and documentation, since enterprise legal and procurement teams specifically look for this information during vendor evaluation, and a platform that addresses it clearly and confidently signals maturity that a platform staying silent on the topic does not.
