A merchandising director or e-commerce lead at a retail catalog operation evaluating AI-generated product descriptions — automated copy for large SKU catalogs, dynamic description variants for different channels, AI-assisted drafting of care instructions and material composition claims — often approaches the technology with assumptions shaped by AI's visible progress in general marketing copywriting, assumptions that don't fully account for the specific accuracy and liability considerations product descriptions actually carry. Several of these assumptions deserve direct correction before they shape a catalog production pipeline decision.

## Myth 1: "AI-Generated Product Copy Can Simply Replace Human Merchandising Copywriters at Similar Quality"

Generative AI tools have genuinely improved at producing product copy that reads fluently and converts reasonably well in isolated samples, and it's reasonable to extrapolate from strong individual outputs toward an assumption that AI generation can substitute for human merchandising copywriters broadly across a catalog. What this underweights is the difference between fluent, generically appealing copy and product descriptions that hold up under the specific scrutiny a merchandising team actually cares about: brand voice consistency across thousands of listings, the kind of category-specific persuasive framing an experienced merchandiser develops through direct conversion-rate feedback, and the intentional editorial judgment about which product attributes actually matter to a specific customer segment — judgment current generative approaches, however impressive at surface-level fluency, don't reliably substitute for at the level of conversion performance real merchandising copy actually requires.

## Myth 2: "AI Content Generation Cuts Catalog-Production Cost Roughly Proportionally to SKU Volume"

A merchandising team reasonably expects that if AI generation can produce descriptions for a large catalog quickly, the cost savings should scale proportionally with SKU count. What this underweights is that AI-generated product descriptions, particularly for anything beyond simple templated variants, typically require a genuine human accuracy-checking pass against actual product specifications to reach publishable quality — verifying material composition claims, dimensions, and care instructions against the actual manufacturer data, and correcting the fluent-sounding factual errors generative models are known to produce when specific product attributes aren't explicitly provided. This verification cost doesn't scale down proportionally with catalog volume the way raw generation cost might, meaning the actual cost savings from AI-assisted catalog production are often considerably more modest than a naive volume-based cost projection would suggest, particularly for product categories where attribute accuracy matters most to both customer trust and return rates.

## Myth 3: "Legal and Liability Considerations Around AI-Generated Product Claims Are Settled and Low-Risk"

The legal landscape around AI-generated claims on material composition, care instructions, and other regulated product attributes remains genuinely unsettled in multiple respects relevant to retail catalog operations, since an inaccurate AI-generated claim about fiber content or care handling can create genuine consumer-protection liability exposure regardless of whether a human or a generative model originally drafted the text, and several consumer-protection regulators have specifically signaled increased scrutiny of automated content pipelines that generate regulated product claims without adequate human verification. A merchandising team treating this as a solved, low-risk legal question, rather than an actively evolving area requiring ongoing accuracy verification specific to regulated product attributes, risks building a catalog production pipeline that creates real consumer-protection and liability exposure the team may not fully appreciate until a specific claim is challenged.

## Why These Myths Deserve Direct Correction Before Production Decisions

These assumptions aren't unreasonable — AI generation's genuine, visible progress in general marketing copywriting naturally creates optimism about its broader applicability, and it's a reasonable instinct to explore the cost and speed advantages a mature technology appears to offer for a large catalog. What makes product descriptions specifically different from some other AI-generation use cases is the combination of genuine brand-voice and conversion-performance stakes a merchandising team cares about directly, a real, non-proportional accuracy-verification cost that limits how directly catalog volume translates into production savings, and a genuinely unsettled liability landscape specifically relevant to regulated product attribute claims like material composition and care instructions.

## What This Means for Scoping an AI-Assisted Catalog Pipeline Correctly

- **Position AI generation as an acceleration tool within a human-reviewed pipeline, not a wholesale replacement for merchandising copywriting**, particularly for product categories where brand voice and conversion performance matter most.
- **Budget realistic attribute-verification cost alongside generation cost**, rather than projecting cost savings that scale proportionally with raw SKU volume without accounting for the genuine human accuracy pass most generated descriptions actually require.
- **Maintain active legal and compliance review specific to regulated product claims**, treating material composition and care-instruction accuracy as an ongoing risk-management responsibility rather than a settled question resolved once at pipeline launch.
- **Reserve AI generation for description categories where its actual strengths align well with the use case**, like generating structured attribute-based variants within a human-verified framework, rather than applying it uniformly across categories with genuinely different accuracy and liability requirements.

## Why Return Rates Add a Real Commercial Dimension Beyond Production Cost

A specific, additional consideration worth naming directly: beyond the production cost and legal considerations already discussed, an inaccurate AI-generated product description — an overstated fit description, an imprecise material composition claim — has become a genuinely measurable driver of return rates specifically, since a customer's purchase decision and eventual satisfaction depend directly on how accurately a description represents the actual product they receive. A merchandising team evaluating an AI-assisted description strategy benefits from weighing this commercial dimension explicitly, not purely as a production cost or legal question, since a technically fast and legally reviewed content pipeline can still drive real commercial cost through elevated return rates if description accuracy genuinely degrades relative to a human-verified baseline.

This is a specific reason treating accuracy verification as inseparable from the generation pipeline itself, rather than as an optional quality pass that can be trimmed under cost pressure, tends to be commercially safer than optimizing purely for generation speed and volume, since both return-rate cost and liability exposure scale directly with how much genuine verification a catalog pipeline actually builds in.
