An Oslo manufacturer's contract promised a "fully functional inventory system." No further detail. At final milestone: vendor said done, client said several workflows didn't actually work. Nine weeks of dispute followed. 📄

Nobel laureate Oliver Hart's incomplete contract theory explains why: every contract leaves gaps, and whoever holds the "residual right" to interpret them wins the argument — usually whoever has more leverage, not whoever's right.

The fix isn't a longer contract. It's a specific, testable definition of done, agreed before work starts.
