A Iași logistics company watched estimated hours run over, month after month, under a pure hourly contract — assuming it was scope complexity, maybe dishonesty. It was neither. It was the contract itself. ⚖️

Mechanism design theory (Hurwicz, Maskin, Myerson — 2007 Nobel Prize) studies exactly this: how to structure rules so self-interested behavior naturally produces the outcome you want, without relying on goodwill to bridge the gap. A pure hourly contract literally pays a vendor more the slower they work. Fixed-price flips it — pays for cutting corners on quality nobody can verify until it's too late.

Neither is "bad faith." Both are structurally misaligned, even with an honest vendor.

The test: imagine your vendor behaving in perfect accordance with the contract's literal incentives. Does that behavior match what you actually want?
