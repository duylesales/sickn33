A Patras insurer's routine security audit ahead of a GDPR review found 43 active accounts and integrations with more access to customer data than their current function required — including 2 former contractors' credentials, over a year after their engagements ended. 🔐

Saltzer & Schroeder's 1975 principle of least privilege: every account should operate with the minimum privilege necessary, no more. Excess access provides zero benefit under normal operation — but if that account is ever compromised, damage is bounded by what it COULD access, not what it actually needed.

Granting access is quick. Revoking it requires someone to notice and follow through. That asymmetry is why access footprints only ever grow by default.

The principle predates cloud computing entirely — still the backbone of zero trust architecture decades later.
