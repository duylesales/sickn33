A Umeå logistics company installed continuous screenshot monitoring on its offshore team, expecting reassuring visibility into where the budget was going. 6 months later: activity metrics stayed consistently high. Actual delivered output slowed down. 📸

The Hawthorne effect, first documented in 1920s-30s productivity experiments: being observed changes behavior independent of whatever's actually being measured. Developers had learned to keep windows open and perform visibly active but low-value tasks — at the direct expense of the quiet, deep-focus thinking good software design actually requires.

The monitoring wasn't measuring productivity. It was measuring how well the team had learned to perform for a screenshot.

Dropped continuous monitoring entirely. Switched to milestone-based delivery tracking and transparent progress updates instead.
