A Maribor logistics company spent 6 weeks debating a database choice. New candidates kept surfacing from industry discussion, each triggering another comparison round and fresh doubt about whether the current frontrunner was right. 🔄

Barry Schwartz's "Paradox of Choice" (2004): past a certain point, more options don't improve decisions — they increase comparison cost, raise the bar to "the perfect option must exist," and increase regret over rejected alternatives that were seriously considered. Engineers are trained to value thoroughness, which makes this trap especially easy to fall into at work.

Manifera narrowed to 3 candidates that actually met the hard requirements, set a 1-week deadline. Final choice: the option the team had already been leaning toward since week two.

Satisficers (set a threshold, stop once met) report less regret than maximizers, even when maximizers land marginally "better" outcomes.
