A Sofia SaaS company budgeted flat software costs for a 3-year roadmap, extrapolating from falling cloud storage prices. By year 2: costs had risen 12% in real terms. Nothing was wrong with the vendor. 📈

Baumol's cost disease (1966, originally about orchestras): a string quartet takes exactly as many musician-hours today as in 1826. Services requiring fixed skilled human time don't get cheaper the way automatable manufacturing does — their relative cost RISES as wages elsewhere in the economy climb.

AI tooling speeds up the mechanical parts of coding. The judgment-heavy core — architecture, ambiguous requirements, trade-offs — is still the human bottleneck setting the real price floor.
