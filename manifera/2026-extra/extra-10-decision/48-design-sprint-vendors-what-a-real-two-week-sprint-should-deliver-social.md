Your team just spent two weeks in a design sprint and came out with a nice prototype, one round of user reactions, and no evidence the second week changed anything. That's calendar padding, not validation. 🗓️🔁

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Week Two as Documentation Time:** If a vendor's second week is "refining deliverables" instead of a second recruited testing session, you're paying two-week pricing for one-week validation.
❌ **Recycled Test Participants:** Re-testing a revised prototype with the same people who saw the original biases the results — they already know what changed and react to the change, not the product.
❌ **Facilitator Swaps Mid-Sprint:** Handing week two to a junior team member to save cost breaks the causal thread between what testing revealed and what actually gets revised.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **A Real Second Testing Loop:** Week one tests the original prototype; week one's specific friction points drive a concrete revision, tested again with a fresh, matched cohort in week two.
✅ **Two Cohorts, Properly Screened:** Both testing groups recruited and confirmed before the sprint starts, matched to your actual target customer profile.
✅ **Comparative Synthesis:** Round one and round two findings shown side by side — the evidence that the extra week produced learning, not just polish.

The second week only earns its cost if it tells you whether the fix actually worked, not just what was wrong. 🎯
