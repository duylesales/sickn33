The vendor's technical lead pushes back on your data-layer approach in sprint one. Someone reads it as disrespect. Someone else reads your dismissal as proof this client doesn't want real input. Neither read is right. ⚖️🔧

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Jumping Tiers Too Fast:** Pulling in the CTO or account manager over a disagreement the engineers haven't even fully discussed teaches both teams that conflict itself is dangerous.
❌ **Technical Disagreement Mistaken for a Trust Problem:** One week-one architecture debate gets read as evidence about the whole relationship's reliability, when it's just a technical question with a discoverable answer.
❌ **Polite Deference, Then Default Overrule:** Acknowledging vendor pushback and overruling it anyway teaches an engaged team that raising concerns is pointless — by month four, they've stopped speaking up.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Diagnose Before Escalating:** A five-minute "walk me through what you understood" conversation resolves most apparent disagreements as miscommunication, not genuine conflict.
✅ **Escalation Tiers Actually Used:** Same-day engineer resolution, 24-hour lead-to-lead — the ladder defined at kickoff, followed instead of skipped.
✅ **Documented Resolution:** Every first-sprint disagreement resolved and recorded, building a track record that concerns get addressed, not suppressed.

Disagreement in week one isn't the red flag — an engagement with zero early friction usually is. 🎯
