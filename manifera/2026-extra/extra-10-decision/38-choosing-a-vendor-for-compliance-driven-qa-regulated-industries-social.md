An auditor asks your QA vendor for signed test evidence from eight months ago, tied to the exact requirement it verifies. Can they produce it in an afternoon? 📋🔍

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Traceability Gaps:** Vendors who backfill traceability matrices before an audit instead of building them in from day one produce evidence with broken links — tests with no requirement, requirements with no test.
❌ **Segregation of Duties on Paper Only:** If one engineer can write, test, and approve the same feature with no independent review, the evidence won't survive a serious GxP or IEC 62304 audit, no matter how thorough the testing was.
❌ **Spreadsheet Audit Trails:** A test log without tamper-evidence and enforced attribution can't satisfy 21 CFR Part 11 electronic signature requirements — auditors want to see corrected results retained, not silently overwritten.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Traceability Built Into the Tooling:** Every requirement mapped to test evidence from day one, with stale tests flagged automatically after a requirement change.
✅ **Enforced Role Separation:** Access controls that structurally prevent testers from merging code or developers from approving their own results.
✅ **Risk-Based Test Depth:** Verification rigor scoped to IEC 62304 Class A/B/C classification, not uniform coverage applied blindly across every component.
