A vendor pitches an "outcome-based" contract as risk transfer: you only pay when it delivers results. Sounds like the CFO's dream — until you look at how the outcome actually gets measured. 📊⚖️

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **The Attribution Trap:** A vendor can build a flawless recommendation engine and still miss a conversion target because your own team changed pricing the same quarter — and the contract never addressed attribution.
❌ **Metrics That Sound Simple, Aren't:** "Improve page load time" turns into a fight over median vs. 95th percentile, which pages, and whether an infrastructure change counts for or against the vendor.
❌ **The Hidden Premium:** Outcome-based structures typically carry a 15-30% pricing premium — and the probability-weighted math often still favors the boring project-based contract.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Precise Scope, Predictable Cost:** Fixed-price and milestone billing for well-specified work, with cost overrun risk sitting where it belongs.
✅ **Measurement Defined Before Day One:** Exact metric, methodology, baseline, and attribution window agreed in writing — not left as a dispute waiting to happen.
✅ **Blended Structures Where It Fits:** Project-based pricing for foundational work, outcome-based only where attribution is genuinely clean.

Neither model reduces risk categorically — the CFO's job is checking which conditions your engagement actually meets. 🎯
