The renewal notice lands 30 days before the anniversary date, rate up 6% "per standard annual escalation," and it's the easiest thing in the world to sign it and move on. That instinct costs money almost every time. 📅💰

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Renewals Treated as a Formality:** Year one is the point of maximum leverage a CFO ever has after initial selection — and it's usually the least used.
❌ **"Standard Increase" Isn't a Justification:** A vendor-set escalation with no external reference point is a number, not a cost driver.
❌ **Everything Gets Relitigated, or Nothing Does:** Renegotiating every clause creates friction; accepting every clause wastes real leverage — neither is the right default.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Twelve Months of Real Data as Leverage:** Sprint reliability, defect rates, response times — documented performance, not general impression, drives the renewal conversation.
✅ **Renegotiate What Deserves It:** Rate, misaligned payment structure, and underperformed SLAs get real attention; what's working stays untouched.
✅ **CPI-Linked, Capped Escalation:** Rate increases tied to a transparent external index with a reasonable ceiling — not an open-ended "standard" number.

The renewal notice isn't a bill to sign — it's the best negotiating moment you'll get after the original vendor selection. 🎯
