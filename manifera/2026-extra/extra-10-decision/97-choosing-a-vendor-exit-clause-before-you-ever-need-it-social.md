Nobody signs a vendor contract planning to fire the vendor. That's exactly why the exit clause gets rushed through — and exactly why you'll wish you'd spent the extra hour on it, at the one moment you had full negotiating leverage. 📝⚠️

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Notice Periods Left Vague:** Too short and you can't line up a replacement; too long and you're trapped in a deteriorating relationship — most contracts never land the right number.
❌ **Transition Assistance Left to Goodwill:** No written commitment to knowledge-transfer hours or documentation handover means relying entirely on an outgoing vendor whose incentive to help already ended.
❌ **"IP Assignment" Doesn't Always Mean Everything:** Infrastructure-as-code and internal tooling sometimes get treated as the vendor's own reusable IP unless the contract says otherwise.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **30-60 Day Notice as Standard:** Long enough to transition properly, short enough you're never locked into a bad fit.
✅ **Defined, Paid Transition Support:** Structured documentation handover — architecture notes, known issues, in-flight status — not just a repo link.
✅ **Unconditional IP From Day One:** Full source, commit history, and infrastructure access confirmed at signing, not clarified under pressure later.

The exit clause is insurance you negotiate when you don't need it, hoping you never do. That's the whole point of insurance. 🛡️
