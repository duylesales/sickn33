Your third successful project with the same vendor just wrapped. Should you keep paying monthly, or keep procuring one project at a time? 💰🔄

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **The Silent Underutilization Leak:** Retainers quietly become a fixed cost nobody revisits — if utilization drops below 75-80% for two months and no one's tracking it, you're paying for idle hours.
❌ **The Invisible Re-Procurement Tax:** Every new project cycle costs two to three weeks of reduced velocity as a team rebuilds context — across a year of episodic projects, that's 10-15% added to total spend with no line item to show for it.
❌ **False Discipline:** Project-by-project "feels" more conservative but often costs more overall once ramp-up and rate renegotiation are counted honestly.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Right-Sized Retainer Floors:** Capacity sized to your worst-case utilization, not your best-case wishlist.
✅ **Capacity Flex Clauses:** Scale retained hours 20-30% on 30 days' notice — no full renegotiation for normal demand swings.
✅ **Hybrid Structuring:** A conservative retainer floor plus project-based statements of work for defined initiatives — the model most mature vendor relationships converge on.

The right structure isn't about commitment anxiety — it's about which financial risk you're willing to hold. 🎯
