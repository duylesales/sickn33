Five users, a highlight reel, and a slide that says "users loved it." That's the deliverable from a lot of user research engagements — and it's nearly worthless for a roadmap decision. 🎥📋

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Recruitment Nobody Screens:** A vendor relying entirely on a panel with no behavioral screening layer can't tell you their no-show or disqualification rate — and "professional participants" have learned to say what researchers want to hear.
❌ **Curation Dressed Up as Synthesis:** Watching sessions and pulling the most quotable clips into a highlight reel isn't synthesis — it's confirmation bias, favoring what the team already believed going in.
❌ **False Precision:** "73% of users preferred option B" from five interviews is a statistically meaningless claim dressed up to sound authoritative — a tell that reporting rigor is thin.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Method Fits the Question:** Contextual inquiry for generative questions, moderated task testing for usability, unmoderated studies at real scale (40+) for preference validation — not one default method for everything.
✅ **Full-Dataset Synthesis:** Affinity mapping and systematic tagging across every session, with findings linked back to citable evidence, not memory and highlight clips.
✅ **Confidence-Rated Readouts:** Strong signal versus weak signal named explicitly, including an honest "what we're still not sure about" section.

Research theater produces confidence without insight — which is worse than no research at all, because it gets treated as validated when it isn't. 🎯
