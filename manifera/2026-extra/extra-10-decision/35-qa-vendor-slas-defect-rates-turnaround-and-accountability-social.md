"High-priority defects addressed promptly." Three sprints later a customer-facing bug sat untouched for 11 days. There's no clause to point to — "promptly" was never defined. ⏱️🐛

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **SLAs Written in Adjectives:** "Prompt," "thorough," and "high quality" fail by design — they can never technically be breached, so they never force process improvement.
❌ **Ambiguous Severity Tiers:** Without objective, application-specific criteria for Critical vs. High, disputes come down to opinion instead of a shared standard.
❌ **The Weekly Summary Email:** Visibility that arrives after the window to act has already passed makes an SLA unenforceable in practice, even if it's enforceable on paper.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Numeric Commitments Only:** Defect escape rate targets, severity-tiered response times, regression turnaround windows — all measurable.
✅ **2-Hour Critical Acknowledgment:** Fix or workaround proposed within 24 hours, written into the contract explicitly.
✅ **Real-Time Shared Reporting:** Live visibility into test execution and defects, not a summary that lands too late to act on.

A penalty clause nobody ever enforces because it's too painful to invoke is the same as having no penalty clause at all. 🎯
