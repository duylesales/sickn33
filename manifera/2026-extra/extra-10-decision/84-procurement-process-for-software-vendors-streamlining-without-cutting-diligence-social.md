Engineering wants a vendor onboarded in six weeks. Your standard procurement process takes fourteen. Which parts are real risk controls — and which are just steps that happen to run one after another? ⏱️✅

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **RFP Bloat:** An 80-question RFP copied from an unrelated purchase category doesn't produce better selection — it produces slower vendor responses and reviewers who skim instead of evaluate.
❌ **Sequential Gates With No Real Dependency:** Legal review waiting on reference checks to finish, when neither actually depends on the other, quietly doubles your timeline.
❌ **The One-Call Reference Check:** A single generic call with a vendor-picked reference who has every incentive to say positive things extracts almost no real signal.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Parallel-Tracked Diligence:** Technical evaluation, references, legal, and security run concurrently — typically cutting a 12-14 week process to 7-9 weeks.
✅ **A Tighter, Sharper RFP:** Questions that actually differentiate finalists on capability, continuity, and contract flexibility — not boilerplate that tests patience.
✅ **Pre-Socialized Approvals:** Five-minute heads-up conversations with each approver before formal submission, so objections surface when they're cheap to fix.

Streamlining procurement is about sequencing and scope discipline — not doing less checking. 🎯
