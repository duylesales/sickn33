A vendor's website shows an ISO 27001 badge and a SOC 2 logo. Neither tells you what's actually in scope — or whether it covers the environment your code lives in. 🛡️📜

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Scope Blind Spots:** ISO 27001 certification can cover corporate IT while excluding the actual development environment touching your data — always check the Statement of Applicability, not just the certificate.
❌ **Type I vs. Type II Confusion:** SOC 2 Type I only attests controls were well-designed at one point in time; Type II proves they operated effectively over 6-12 months. Vendors sometimes blur the two.
❌ **The Logo-Checking Trap:** A "SOC 2 certified" claim with no report from a named CPA firm on request is unverified, not evidence.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Full Report Access Under NDA:** Actual SOC 2 Type II reports and ISO 27001 scope statements, not just badges.
✅ **Currency You Can Verify:** Certifications kept current with annual surveillance audits and renewal cycles.
✅ **Certification as a Floor, Not the Whole Answer:** Domain-specific and contractual diligence layered on top of baseline security certification.

A badge on a website is marketing. The scope statement and the actual report are evidence. 🎯
