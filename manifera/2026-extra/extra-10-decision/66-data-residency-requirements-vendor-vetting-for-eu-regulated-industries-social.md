Ask a vendor where your data lives and most say "the cloud" like that settles it. It doesn't. 🌍🔒

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Residency ≠ Sovereignty:** An EU cloud region satisfies data residency but not sovereignty — the US CLOUD Act still asserts jurisdiction over data controlled by a US-headquartered provider, wherever the servers sit.
❌ **The Subprocessor Blind Spot:** Primary infrastructure in Frankfurt means nothing if error logging, email delivery, or support tooling routes personal data through a US-based subprocessor nobody disclosed.
❌ **SCCs Alone Aren't Enough Post-Schrems II:** Standard Contractual Clauses without a documented Transfer Impact Assessment and supplementary technical measures don't actually solve the transfer problem.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Full Subprocessor Transparency:** A current, complete subprocessor list cross-referenced against your residency requirement, not a verbal assurance.
✅ **EU-Based Governance:** Amsterdam-anchored delivery with data location commitments documented in the DPA, including backup and disaster recovery regions.
✅ **Advance Notice on Any Change:** Written approval required before any new subprocessor enters the data path.

"The cloud" isn't an answer — the subprocessor list is. 🎯
