Your company just started acquisition diligence, and one checklist line sounds almost paranoid — until you try to answer it: can you prove who actually wrote the codebase you're about to own? 🔍💻

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Generic Commit Accounts:** "devteam@vendor.com" attribution makes it impossible to verify real team composition or hold anyone accountable.
❌ **Rewritten Git History:** A suspiciously clean, squashed history can erase the exact evidence an audit is trying to establish.
❌ **Undisclosed Subcontractors:** Code from people you never vetted, with no flow-down attribution requirements to catch it.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Individually Attributed Commits:** Named engineers, not shared accounts, as standard practice across every project.
✅ **Automated License Scanning:** FOSSA/Snyk-style dependency and provenance checks built into the CI pipeline, available on request.
✅ **Contractual Audit Rights:** Review commit history and run independent scans during or after the engagement — no resistance, no renegotiation.

Roughly 1 in 5 vendor codebases we've reviewed showed shared-account patterns that made authorship unverifiable. Don't find that out during diligence. 📋
