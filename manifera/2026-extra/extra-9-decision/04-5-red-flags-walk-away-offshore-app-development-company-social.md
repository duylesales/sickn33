"Their price is the lowest I've found — that means I'm getting a great deal." That belief is exactly how non-technical founders get burned by rates that looked perfectly reasonable. 🚩📱

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **The Suspiciously Low Quote:** An unusually low price usually means underpricing to recoup via change requests later, a junior bench, or turnover high enough that you're funding their re-training.
❌ **The Vendor Who Agrees to Everything:** A team that pushes back on nothing hasn't thought through the complexity — or is avoiding an uncomfortable conversation you'll be having anyway, three months in.
❌ **Sales-Team Vouching:** The people pitching you are rarely the people who'll write your code, and their enthusiasm about "our senior team" tells you nothing about who's actually staffed.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Three Quotes, Same Scope:** Benchmark before judging any single number as reasonable or alarming.
✅ **Interview the Actual Engineers:** Twenty minutes with the assigned team reveals more than any sales deck across the whole evaluation.
✅ **Weekly Live Demos, Not Email Updates:** Working software you can see, with the people who built it, not a status paragraph.

Walking away from a vendor over these signals isn't failing at vetting — it's succeeding at it. ✅
