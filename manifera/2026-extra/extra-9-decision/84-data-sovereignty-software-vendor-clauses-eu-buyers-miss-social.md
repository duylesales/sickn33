If your vendor says "we're GDPR compliant," does that actually tell you where your data physically lives? For most CTOs finalizing a contract, the honest answer is no. 🌍🔒

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **"Compliant" ≠ "EU-Only":** GDPR permits transfers outside the EU under Standard Contractual Clauses — many vendors claim "SCCs are in place" without producing the signed module or transfer risk assessment behind it.
❌ **Backups Break the Promise:** A production database can sit entirely in an EU region while an automated backup job replicates to a default global bucket nobody explicitly overrode.
❌ **Snapshot, Not Guarantee:** A vendor's data residency at signing can quietly change through an acquisition or new subprocessor, with no proactive notification required unless your contract demands it.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Architecture, Not Just Contract Language:** Infrastructure diagrams and documented, auditable migration to GDPR-compliant EU regions like AWS EU or Azure West Europe.
✅ **Location Is Separate From Team Geography:** Offshore engineers can work entirely inside EU-hosted infrastructure with role-based access and full audit logging — governance determines sovereignty, not headcount location.
✅ **Forward-Looking Notification Clauses:** Named vendor-side accountability for data location and subprocessors maintained over the life of the contract, not just verified once at signing.

A vendor's compliance claim at signing is a snapshot — build your contract for what happens after. 📋
