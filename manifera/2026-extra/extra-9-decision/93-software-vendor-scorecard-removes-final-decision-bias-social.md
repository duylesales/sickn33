"Trust your gut" is close to the worst advice a non-technical founder can follow when picking a dev partner. Gut feel rewards the best salesperson, not the best engineering team. 🎲❌

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Charisma vs. Delivery:** Founders rarely get burned by bad code — they get burned by the most charismatic pitch in the room, discovered four months and one spent deposit later.
❌ **Impressions, Not Numbers:** Writing "seemed good" next to a vendor's answer fades and gets unconsciously rewritten in favor of whoever you liked personally by the end.
❌ **Deciding in the Room:** Making the call while the most recent pitch is still fresh is a well-documented recency-bias trap.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Identical Questions, Every Vendor:** The same 8-12 written questions sent to every finalist, so answers are directly comparable instead of buried in three different PDFs.
✅ **Numeric Scoring With Notes:** A 1-5 score plus a one-sentence justification for cost transparency, process specificity, portfolio relevance, and communication clarity.
✅ **Proof of Longevity:** 160+ delivered projects and 120+ clients across more than a decade — a meaningfully different signal than a slick two-year-old studio's portfolio.

Total the weighted scores before the final call, sleep on it, then decide. 😴✅
