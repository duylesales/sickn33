Gross salary on one side, vendor rate on the other — the spreadsheet every CFO starts with. It's not wrong. It's incomplete in a way that always favors the in-house column. 📉🧮

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **The Ramp-Time Blind Spot:** A new hire takes 8-16 weeks to reach full productivity — roughly a month of fully-loaded cost that never gets its own line item.
❌ **Attrition Erases the Investment:** At a typical 14-18 month tenure, most companies haven't recouped a hire's recruiting and ramp cost before they leave and the cycle restarts.
❌ **Unallocated Management Overhead:** Every in-house engineer consumes a slice of a manager's time that scales with headcount but never gets charged back to the hiring decision.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Complete TCO Model:** Salary, 20-30% employer contributions, 18-25% recruiting fees, benefits, ramp cost, and an attrition-adjusted reserve — pushing real cost to 1.4x-1.8x gross salary.
✅ **Fair Comparison, Both Directions:** Outsourced rates already fold in the vendor's recruiting and management overhead, so it's compared against your fully-loaded number, not your headline one.
✅ **Board-Ready Numbers:** A model built from 160+ delivered projects, defensible enough to survive a pointed follow-up question on any line item.

The goal isn't proving outsourcing always wins — it's a comparison honest enough that the board sees the real economics. ⚖️
