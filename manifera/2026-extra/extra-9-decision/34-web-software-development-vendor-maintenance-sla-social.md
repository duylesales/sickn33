An SLA promised "priority response within 4 hours." What it didn't say: the clock only started once the vendor's own team triaged the ticket — which they were allowed 24 hours to do. That gap cost a client eleven hours of downtime. ⏱️

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Response Time ≠ Resolution Time:** A 1-hour response commitment with no resolution guarantee means a fast "we're looking into it" email, then days of waiting — without the vendor technically breaching anything.
❌ **Vendor-Only Severity Classification:** Letting the vendor decide what counts as "critical" creates an obvious conflict of interest during the exact moment you need honesty most.
❌ **Commitments With No Teeth:** An SLA with response targets but no service credit or fee reduction for missing them is a marketing statement, not a guarantee.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Jointly Defined Severity Matrix:** Concrete, contract-level criteria for "critical" — not vendor discretion applied after the fact.
✅ **CET-Overlapping, English-Fluent Support:** A critical ticket raised during EU business hours reaches someone who acts immediately.
✅ **Retainer Hours That Flex:** Scale maintenance capacity up or down within 2-4 weeks as your actual needs shift, not a fixed commitment sized for a worst case that never comes.

Read the SLA before you sign, not during the eleven-hour outage it's supposed to prevent. 📄
