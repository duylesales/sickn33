The question that got a CFO to finally sign a vendor contract wasn't about price. It was: "What happens to our codebase the day their bank account hits zero?" 💸📉

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Lighter Review Than a Furniture Supplier:** A vendor holding your entire codebase and roadmap often gets less financial scrutiny than a hardware supplier — despite carrying far more risk.
❌ **No Escrow, No Recovery:** Without source code escrow, a sudden vendor closure can leave you with a production system you can't legally or technically maintain.
❌ **Boilerplate Transition Clauses:** Vague "we'll assist with transition" language falls apart exactly when you need it — no named deliverables, no committed hours, no real handoff.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Standard Escrow Terms:** Verified source code and documentation held by a third party, available on request for every engagement.
✅ **Named Transition Deliverables:** Architecture docs, deployment runbooks, and committed key-personnel hours specified in writing, not implied.
✅ **Diversified, Profitable Client Base:** Continuity built on process and cross-trained teams, not dependence on one or two irreplaceable people.

Price the counterparty risk. Contract around it. Then sign. 📝
