Manifera's founder wrote every statement of work as a document engineers would actually use to run the project — not a legal artifact procurement files away and forgets. That philosophy is the lens worth applying to the SOW on your desk right now. 📑⚙️

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Inclusions Without Exclusions:** "A customer-facing portal with reporting" sounds complete until you ask whether reporting includes exportable PDFs — every ambiguous noun is a future change-order conversation.
❌ **"A Dedicated Team Will Be Assigned":** Without named roles and explicit allocation percentages, that sentence is functionally unenforceable if staffing turns out thinner than promised.
❌ **"Feature Complete" as a Milestone Gate:** Vague acceptance criteria let a vendor under schedule pressure declare a milestone done on a technicality.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **A Scope Boundary Table:** In scope / out of scope / to be scoped later, walked through feature by feature with your own product team present.
✅ **A Staffing Table With Allocation %:** Named roles, exclusive to your project, with a documented replacement timeline if someone leaves.
✅ **Definition of Done Per Milestone:** Test coverage thresholds and performance benchmarks tied to payment — not client "satisfaction."

Read your SOW with the same scrutiny you'd apply to a system design document. 🔎
