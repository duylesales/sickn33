A beautifully designed product that can't survive a traffic spike. A technically flawless backend nobody wants to use. Two failure modes, same root cause: picking a vendor who specializes hard in one direction. 🎨⚙️

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Design-Led, Backend-Light:** Creative-first agencies often lightly staff backend engineering — architecture debt accumulates where clients can't see it during demos, until a real traffic spike hits.
❌ **Engineering-Led, UX-Last:** Engineering-first teams sometimes treat interface design as a cosmetic layer bolted on late, shipping something technically sound that users find confusing or uninspired.
❌ **Sequential Handoffs:** IDC's research found products where design and engineering work in separate, sequential phases ship with far more expensive rework cycles than integrated teams.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **One Accountable Team:** Frontend, backend, mobile, DevOps, and QA staffed together from the same discovery phase, not coordinated across separate departments or vendors.
✅ **Shared Sprint Cadence:** Design and engineering decisions reviewed in the same sprint reviews, so one discipline's success can't mask the other's neglect.
✅ **A Real Evaluation Test:** Ask for one example where a technical constraint changed a design decision, and one where a UX concern changed a technical one — a healthy team answers both without hesitation.

The products that win rarely succeed on architecture or interface alone. 🏆
