A routine Tuesday afternoon release took a client's order-tracking module offline for 55 minutes — and cost them €38,000. Their SLA covered general uptime. It said nothing about deployment. There was no clause to invoke. 📉⏱️

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **Bundled Uptime Math:** Vendors absorb a bad release into a monthly uptime average so it never triggers a penalty, even after a customer-facing outage.
❌ **Flat Service Credits:** A generic 10% credit pays the same whether an incident lasted ten minutes or three hours — poorly aligned with real exposure.
❌ **No Real Cost Anchor:** Most SLAs are negotiated without ever calculating what an hour of downtime actually costs the business (often €11K-€15K for a mid-sized SaaS platform).

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **Deployment as Its Own Clause:** Defined release windows, 48-hour notice requirements, and rollback commitments — separate from general uptime language.
✅ **Tiered, Cost-Referenced Remedies:** Credits scale with outage duration and are benchmarked against your actual downtime-cost figure, not an arbitrary percentage.
✅ **Third-Party Monitoring:** Downtime measured independently, never self-reported by the vendor managing the release.

Deployment risk doesn't disappear when it's left out of the contract — it just becomes uninsured. 🛡️
