## The Architectural Mandate: What Actually Drives Cost

Custom software cost isn't one number — it's the sum of five cost drivers, and a CFO who understands each one can evaluate any vendor quote by decomposing it into these categories, regardless of how the vendor chose to bundle their pricing.

The first driver is discovery and architecture. A properly scoped project starts with two to four weeks of requirements gathering, technical architecture design, and a detailed sprint-level estimate — work that should be priced and delivered before the main build contract is signed, precisely so the CFO isn't approving a number based on a guess. Vendors who skip this and jump straight to a fixed-price quote are pricing risk into a number they haven't actually calculated yet, which is exactly the kind of quote that grows in month four.

The second driver is core development labor, broken down by role: backend engineers, frontend engineers, and a tech lead who owns architecture decisions. This is the largest line item, typically 55-65% of total project cost, and it's the one most vendors quote clearly — it's the other four drivers that get buried.

The third driver is quality assurance and testing, which should be a dedicated role and dedicated hours, not an assumption that developers test their own code adequately. Projects that skip dedicated QA save 10-15% on the initial invoice and routinely spend double that later fixing production defects that proper testing would have caught before launch.

The fourth driver is DevOps and infrastructure: CI/CD pipeline setup, cloud environment configuration on AWS or Azure, and the ongoing infrastructure cost of running the application, which is separate from the development cost and often omitted from an initial quote entirely.

The fifth driver, the one CFOs underweight most, is project management and governance overhead — the cost of someone actually owning delivery risk, tracking sprint velocity against the original estimate, and flagging scope drift before it becomes a change order. As management theorist Peter Drucker put it, "what gets measured gets managed" — a project without a governance layer tracking velocity against budget isn't being managed, it's being hoped for.

A CFO evaluating any vendor quote should ask for these five categories broken out explicitly. A vendor who can't or won't decompose their number this way is either bundling risk into a padded estimate, or hasn't actually built the estimate with this level of rigor in the first place — both are reasons for caution.

## By the Numbers: Custom Software Cost Benchmarks

- Discovery and architecture typically runs **4-8% of total project budget** when scoped as a standalone phase rather than folded invisibly into development.
- Projects that skip a dedicated QA role save on the initial quote but see defect-related rework costs run **1.5-2x higher** post-launch than projects with QA built in from sprint one.
- Infrastructure and DevOps setup, when planned upfront rather than retrofitted, typically adds **8-12% to total build cost** but reduces post-launch operational incidents significantly.
- Scope changes requested after architecture sign-off, industry experience consistently shows, cost roughly **3-5x more** to implement than the same change requested during discovery.
- Fixed-price contracts without a documented change-request process see average final costs run **25-40% over** the original quoted number by project completion.

## Implementation Checklist: Vetting a Custom Software Quote Before You Sign

Before a CFO in Aalsmeer signs off on a custom software pricing proposal, run the quote through this checklist rather than trusting the headline number alone:

- **Rate card by role, not a blended average.** Demand separate day rates for backend, frontend, QA, and tech lead — a blended rate hides whether you're paying senior prices for junior staffing.
- **Named team roster in the SOW appendix.** Insist on named engineers with seniority levels and years of experience attached, with a minimum senior-to-junior ratio (at least one senior per three engineers) written into the contract, not promised verbally.
- **Payment tied to sprint-verified deliverables**, not calendar dates — e.g., 15% at architecture sign-off, then equal installments per two-sprint cycle, so invoices never outrun verified progress.
- **IP transfer clause in writing.** Full source code and IP ownership should transfer to the client on final payment; some offshore vendors default to shared or licensed rights unless this is stated explicitly.
- **Post-launch warranty period defined**, typically 30-90 days of defect fixes at no additional cost, separate from ongoing maintenance retainer pricing.
- **Exit and code-escrow terms** specifying how the client retrieves the full codebase, documentation, and infrastructure-as-code if the engagement ends early.

A quote missing three or more of these items isn't a lean quote — it's an incomplete one, and the gaps are exactly where month-four invoice surprises originate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: CFO comparing three differently structured vendor quotes) How do I compare vendor quotes that are structured completely differently?

Ask each vendor to break their number into the five core cost drivers — discovery, development labor, QA, DevOps/infrastructure, and governance overhead. Once quotes are decomposed into the same categories, the real comparison becomes visible, including what each vendor may have quietly excluded.

### (Scenario: CFO worried about scope creep inflating a fixed-price contract) How do we protect a fixed-price budget from scope creep?

Insist on a documented change-request process with cost impact quoted before any change is approved, and require sprint-level velocity-to-budget tracking rather than milestone-only reporting, so drift is visible within two weeks instead of at the end of the project.

### (Scenario: CFO deciding whether to skip QA to reduce the initial quote) Is it safe to reduce cost by cutting the dedicated QA role?

It reduces the initial invoice but transfers that cost to post-launch defect remediation, which industry experience shows typically runs 1.5-2x higher than the QA cost it replaced, on top of the reputational cost of shipping bugs to production.

### (Scenario: CFO building a board-ready business case) What should I actually present to the board as the total cost figure?

Present a range built from the five cost drivers plus a documented contingency for scope changes, rather than a single fixed number — a range with a clear methodology is more defensible in a board meeting than a precise number with no visible basis.

### (Scenario: CFO evaluating whether offshore pricing sacrifices quality) Does a lower day rate from an offshore pod mean lower engineering quality?

Not when the rate difference reflects genuine cost-of-living and market economics rather than reduced seniority — Manifera's Vietnam-based engineers work at the same seniority bands as Amsterdam-based teams, with the cost difference coming from location economics, not experience level.

### (Scenario: CFO negotiating payment milestones for a custom software contract) How should payment milestones be structured for a custom software build?

Tie invoices to sprint-verified deliverables rather than calendar dates — for example, 15% at architecture sign-off, then equal installments per two-sprint cycle — so payment never precedes verified, working progress.

### (Scenario: CFO handling VAT/BTW treatment on an offshore development invoice) Does working with a Vietnam-based pod complicate BTW/VAT on the invoice?

No — Manifera invoices through its Amsterdam entity under standard Dutch B2B VAT rules, so the invoice looks and reconciles exactly like any other Dutch vendor's, with no cross-border VAT reclaim for the CFO to model.

### (Scenario: CFO concerned about IP ownership after project completion) Who owns the source code and IP once the custom software project is delivered?

Full IP transfer to the client on final payment should be an explicit contract clause, not an assumption — require it in writing before signing, since not every offshore vendor defaults to full ownership transfer.

### (Scenario: CFO evaluating vendor team composition before approving a quote) How do I verify a vendor isn't quoting senior rates but staffing mostly junior developers?

Request named team members with seniority levels and years of experience listed in the SOW appendix, and require a minimum senior-to-junior staffing ratio written into the contract rather than promised verbally.
