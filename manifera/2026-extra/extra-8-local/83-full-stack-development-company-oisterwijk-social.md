"Full-stack" is one of the most misused terms in software procurement — and most CTOs hiring on the word alone don't find out what it's hiding until a cross-cutting bug takes three weeks to fix instead of three days. 🧩💻

**The Pain Points:**
❌ **The Label Describes the Sales Page, Not the Org Chart:** Individual developers narrowly specialized, frontend/backend handoffs still slow and lossy, just billed under one line item.
❌ **Conway's Law Predicts the Bug, Not the Vendor:** A rigid frontend team and rigid backend team coordinating only through a spec document reliably produces API contracts that match the spec but don't fit how the UI actually needs the data.
❌ **No One Owns the Whole Contract:** When frontend and backend report through separate leads, a mismatch becomes "whose bug is this" instead of getting fixed in the next hour.

**The Manifera Solution:**
✅ **One Cross-Functional Pod, Shared Backlog:** Four to six engineers spanning frontend, backend, and QA with daily direct communication, eliminating the handoff Conway's Law predicts.
✅ **Amsterdam Defines Contract Ownership Upfront:** Dutch-based leads specify pod composition and end-to-end API-contract accountability before a project starts.
✅ **Vietnam Owns Deployment Too:** The same pod that builds a feature ships and monitors it in production — catching integration issues that only surface under real load.

A Danish MRO provider's split-team vendor added six weeks of rework fixing data shapes that didn't fit the UI. One integrated pod cut post-launch cross-cutting defects to a third of the prior rate. ✈️
