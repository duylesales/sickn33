An employer spends four months recruiting a specialist, makes a competitive offer, and loses the candidate because they could not find somewhere to live within commuting distance. Another hires successfully, and the person leaves after eleven months because their partner never found work and the family was living an hour and a half from the office. Both scenarios are common in the Netherlands right now, and both are treated as bad luck rather than as a solvable part of the hiring process.

## Why Housing Has Become a Hiring Problem

The Dutch housing shortage is structural rather than cyclical. Supply has not kept pace with household formation, rental competition in and around the major cities is intense, and buying requires financial history and usually a permanent contract that a new arrival does not yet have.

For employers, three consequences follow directly.

**Candidates decline offers.** An otherwise attractive role becomes impossible when the realistic commute is ninety minutes each way.

**Start dates slip.** Someone who cannot find housing cannot register with a municipality, which delays everything else including in some cases their formal status.

**New hires leave.** A temporary arrangement that was acceptable for three months becomes intolerable after nine, and the person takes a job nearer to wherever they ended up living.

Employers frequently classify these as personal circumstances. Competitors who treat them as part of the offer win the candidates.

## What Employers Can Actually Do

The interventions vary in cost, and the cheapest are often the most effective.

**Temporary accommodation for the first months.** A serviced apartment or corporate housing for eight to twelve weeks removes the impossible sequencing problem — needing an address to register, needing registration to arrange finances, needing finances to secure a rental. This is the single highest-return intervention available.

**A relocation allowance.** A one-off contribution towards moving, agency fees and deposits. Rules exist about what can be reimbursed tax-efficiently, which is worth checking with your payroll adviser.

**A housing search partner.** Local agencies who know the market and can act quickly, which matters enormously in a market where good rentals are gone within days.

**The registration checklist.** Municipal registration, citizen service number, bank account, health insurance, energy contract, internet. Written down in order, with links and typical waiting times, this costs nothing to produce and saves a new hire weeks of confusion.

**A named person to ask.** Not a portal. A colleague in HR who has done this before and will answer a question the same day.

**Guarantor arrangements.** Some employers act as guarantor or provide an employment statement in a format landlords accept, which meaningfully improves a candidate's chances.

**Commuting support.** Where housing nearby is impossible, a rail season ticket, a mobility budget or flexibility on office days can make a longer-distance arrangement genuinely workable.

## The Partner Question Determines Retention

International hiring literature underweights this consistently: whether a family stays is determined more often by the accompanying partner's situation than by the employee's job satisfaction.

A partner who cannot find work, cannot use their qualifications, or cannot build a social network becomes unhappy, and within a year the family returns home or moves to a country where they can both work.

What employers can do is modest but effective. Provide information about the partner's right to work, which under most arrangements is more permissive than people assume. Offer introductions to local employers or networks, and language course funding for the partner as well as the employee. Where an expat centre or international community organisation exists in your region, connect them. And simply asking, during onboarding, what the partner's plans are signals that you consider the household rather than the individual.

The cost of this is a few hours and a language course. The cost of losing a specialist hire after eleven months is an agency fee, a vacancy and a project delay.

## A Concrete Scenario: The Regional Employer That Won

A manufacturing company in a regional town competes for a machine learning engineer against two Randstad employers offering more money.

They win, on the basis of a package that costs them relatively little: three months of furnished accommodation, a relocation allowance, an introduction to a local rental agency, a named HR contact, and an honest conversation about what housing in the region actually costs compared with Amsterdam.

The candidate calculates that the same salary buys a house with a garden and a fifteen-minute cycle to work rather than a small apartment and a forty-minute commute. Two years later they are still there, and they have referred a former colleague.

This is the structural advantage regional Dutch employers have and rarely use. They cannot out-pay the Randstad, but they can offer a standard of living that the Randstad cannot — provided someone does the arithmetic for the candidate rather than expecting them to discover it.

## What Not to Do

**Do not promise help you have not arranged.** "We'll help you find something" without a concrete arrangement produces a resentful hire in month two.

**Do not assume a young candidate needs less.** A single person in a competitive rental market may have a harder time than a family with a relocation budget.

**Do not ignore domestic relocation.** A Dutch candidate moving from Groningen to Eindhoven faces the same market, and employers frequently offer relocation support to international hires while offering nothing to domestic ones doing exactly the same move.

**Do not use housing as a retention lock.** Arrangements that penalise leaving harshly are resented and reflect badly; a reasonable pro-rated repayment clause for relocation costs within a short period is normal, an aggressive one is not.

**Do not skip the honest commute conversation.** A candidate who takes the job believing the commute is thirty minutes and discovers it is seventy will leave, and will tell people why.

## Making the Case Internally

Finance directors ask, reasonably, what this costs. The comparison to present is straightforward.

Set the cost of a relocation package — temporary accommodation, an allowance, some HR time — against the cost of the alternative: an agency fee on a replacement hire, months of vacancy on a role you already struggled to fill, the project delay that follows, and the management time of running the search again.

For scarce technical roles the arithmetic is rarely close. A vacancy that stays open for six months costs an organisation far more, in delayed work and diverted management attention, than a relocation package costs.

There is also a reputational argument that matters in a small market. Candidates talk to each other, and an employer known for handling arrivals well receives applications from people who heard about it from a colleague. That effect is slow, cumulative and free.

## A Relocation Package That Fits a Mid-Sized Employer

Employers without a global mobility function assume relocation support means an expensive corporate programme. It does not. A workable package for a Dutch SME or mid-sized organisation has five components and can be assembled in a week.

**Temporary accommodation for eight to twelve weeks.** Either a corporate lease you maintain permanently for arrivals, or a booking made per hire. If you hire two or three people a year, a standing arrangement with one apartment is usually cheaper than ad hoc bookings and removes all the decision-making.

**A fixed relocation allowance.** One number, paid on arrival, with a simple pro-rated repayment clause if the person leaves within a defined short period. Simplicity beats a receipts-based scheme that generates administration for everyone.

**An introduction to a rental agency** that knows your region and will take your call. This relationship costs nothing and is worth more than money to a candidate competing against twenty other applicants for a flat.

**A written arrival guide.** Registration, citizen service number, bank, health insurance, energy, internet, schools, doctor registration — in order, with typical timelines. Written once, used for every hire, updated when something changes.

**A named person.** Whoever in your organisation has done this before, with explicit time allocated and permission to spend it.

That package costs a fraction of an agency fee and is frequently the reason a candidate chooses you over a better-paid alternative.

## What Changes When Housing Is the Constraint Rather Than the Salary

There is a broader strategic point here that regional and mid-sized employers should absorb.

For a decade, the recruitment conversation in Dutch technology has been about salary. Increasingly the binding constraint for candidates is not what they earn but what they can do with it: whether they can live within a reasonable distance of work, whether their family has space, whether the commute leaves them time.

That shift favours employers outside the most expensive areas, provided they make the comparison visible. A candidate weighing a Randstad offer against a regional one rarely calculates the difference in housing costs, commuting time and quality of life unless someone does it for them.

Doing that calculation in the recruitment conversation is legitimate and effective. Show the median house price in your area against the one they are considering. Show the commute. Show what a four-day week looks like when the office is fifteen minutes away rather than an hour.

Employers who make this argument well are winning candidates they would have lost five years ago, and the trend is running in their favour rather than against it.

## Key Takeaways

- Housing is one of the most common practical reasons Dutch offers are declined or new hires leave within a year.
- Temporary accommodation for the first months is the highest-return intervention, because it breaks the registration sequencing problem.
- A written registration checklist and a named contact cost nothing and prevent weeks of confusion.
- Partner employment and social integration determine retention for international hires more than job satisfaction does.
- Regional employers can win against higher Randstad salaries by doing the housing arithmetic for the candidate.
