Rijswijk is one of those Dutch municipalities that people pass through without registering. It is wedged between The Hague and Delft, it has a tram line running through it, and to most job seekers it reads as an office suburb. That impression hides something specific: the search and examination work for a large share of Europe's patents happens here, alongside applied research facilities and a business park that is among the largest in the region. For anyone who works with text, search, classification or applied research data, Rijswijk is a more interesting address than its reputation suggests.

## Why Rijswijk Is a Document City

Some cities run on factories, some on ports. Rijswijk runs on documents — highly technical ones. A patent application is a structured legal-technical text, filed in one of several languages, describing an invention precisely enough that an examiner can determine whether it is genuinely new. Processing millions of such documents, in multiple languages, across every field of technology, is one of the largest and least publicised information-retrieval problems in Europe.

That shapes the local labour market in a way few candidates anticipate. The dominant data skills here are not computer vision or reinforcement learning. They are information retrieval, natural language processing, multilingual search, classification at scale, and the evaluation methodology that tells you whether a retrieval system actually improved.

## The Named Organisations Behind Rijswijk's Data Market

**European Patent Office.** The EPO is the patent-granting authority under the **European Patent Convention**, and its site in Rijswijk is one of its principal locations, alongside Munich, Berlin, Vienna and Brussels. Thousands of patent examiners — nearly all with a technical or scientific degree — search prior art and examine applications there. Around them sits a substantial IT and data organisation: search infrastructure, classification systems such as the **Cooperative Patent Classification (CPC)**, the public **Espacenet** database, machine translation between patent languages, and increasingly AI-assisted tooling to support examiners. The EPO also publishes large open patent datasets that researchers and companies use worldwide.

**Applied research.** **TNO**, the Dutch organisation for applied scientific research, has facilities in Rijswijk alongside its locations in The Hague, Delft and elsewhere. Applied research work spans defence and security, materials, and information technology, and it produces roles for people who can combine domain science with modelling and data analysis.

**The Plaspoelpolder business park.** One of the largest business parks in the Haaglanden region, Plaspoelpolder hosts a wide mix of companies — IT services, logistics, professional services and smaller technology firms — plus offices of national and international organisations. It is unglamorous and it is where a significant part of the local employment sits.

**The wider corridor.** Rijswijk is minutes from **Delft**, home to **Delft University of Technology**, and from **The Hague**, home to ministries, international organisations and the security cluster known as **The Hague Security Delta**. In commuting terms these are effectively the same market: most people working in Rijswijk could equally take a role in either neighbour.

## What Kind of AI and Data Work Actually Happens Here

**Search and information retrieval.** Prior-art search is a recall problem before it is a precision problem: missing a relevant document is far more serious than returning an extra one. That inverts many assumptions from consumer search. Query formulation, ranking, semantic search over technical language, and rigorous evaluation against expert judgements are the core skills.

**Multilingual NLP.** Patent documents arrive in multiple languages, and examiners need to read prior art regardless of its original language. Machine translation tuned for technical and legal register, terminology consistency and cross-lingual retrieval are practical daily problems rather than research curiosities.

**Classification at scale.** Assigning documents to a classification scheme with hundreds of thousands of categories — hierarchical, sparse in places, evolving over time — is a genuinely hard machine learning problem that almost nobody encounters in a typical commercial job.

**Applied research data.** In the research institutes, the work runs from sensor and materials data to modelling and simulation, often under confidentiality constraints that shape how data can be stored, shared and published.

## The Evaluation Problem That Defines This Work

In a consumer product you can run an A/B test and read the conversion numbers next week. In patent search you cannot: the outcome that matters — whether a granted patent survives future challenges — is unknown for years. So evaluation has to be built differently. Expert-annotated benchmarks, relevance judgements from examiners, carefully constructed test collections and statistical care about small sample sizes all matter more than raw model size.

Candidates who have only worked in environments with fast feedback loops often underestimate this. The technically interesting problem in Rijswijk is frequently not "can we train a better ranker" but "how do we know, defensibly, that this ranker is better for the people who must rely on it".

## Regulation That Shapes the Work

**GDPR.** Patent documents are largely public, but the surrounding systems process personal data of applicants, representatives and staff. Research institutes handle personal data in studies. The usual obligations — lawful basis, minimisation, retention — apply.

**Confidentiality before publication.** A patent application is confidential until publication, typically eighteen months after filing. That creates strict internal access controls and shapes what data may be used to train or test systems.

**EU AI Act.** Systems that support decisions with legal effect attract attention around transparency, human oversight and documentation. In examination work, the consistent institutional position across patent offices is that AI supports examiners rather than replaces their judgement, and tooling is designed accordingly.

**Security and export control.** In defence-related applied research, confidentiality, screening and export-control rules can restrict who may work on a project and where data may be processed. Candidates should expect screening requirements for some roles.

## A Concrete Scenario: The Semantic Search That Made Examiners Slower

A team introduces a semantic search model alongside the existing keyword-based system. Offline metrics improve: relevant documents rank higher on the benchmark set.

In the pilot, examiners report the opposite experience. The new system surfaces documents that look plausible but are conceptually unrelated, and because the ranking no longer maps to an explicable query, examiners spend longer checking results they cannot quickly dismiss. Worse, they lose confidence in the completeness of the search — and completeness is the whole point.

The fix is not a bigger model. It is explainability at the result level, a hybrid of keyword and semantic retrieval, and an interface that shows why a document was retrieved. This pattern — a model that is better on paper and worse in the workflow — is the most common failure mode in expert-user search, and it is exactly the kind of problem a candidate should ask about in an interview.

## A Common Misconception About Institutional Search Work

Engineers sometimes assume that patent and government information systems are legacy environments where nothing modern happens. In practice, the data volumes, the multilingual requirements and the evaluation rigour are beyond what most commercial teams handle, and the tooling has moved with the field. What differs is the decision horizon: changes are validated slowly because the cost of degrading an expert workflow is high. The technical depth is real; the tempo is deliberate.

## Skills and Tools That Stand Out

- **Information retrieval fundamentals.** Ranking, recall-oriented evaluation, query expansion, hybrid keyword-plus-embedding search.
- **NLP for technical and legal text.** Terminology handling, multilingual models, domain adaptation, translation quality assessment.
- **Large-scale classification.** Hierarchical and extreme multi-label settings, class imbalance, taxonomy drift.
- **Evaluation design.** Building test collections, working with expert annotators, reasoning about inter-annotator agreement.
- **Languages.** English is the working language for much of this market; German and French are genuinely useful in patent work, and Dutch matters for national public-sector roles.

## Living and Commuting in Rijswijk

Rijswijk is one of the best-connected places in the country for its size: tram and rail links to The Hague, Delft and Rotterdam, and intercity connections onward. Housing costs sit below The Hague's centre and well below Amsterdam's, and a large share of employees commute in from Delft, Zoetermeer, Rotterdam or the coastal towns rather than living in the municipality itself.

The trade-off is character: this is an office and business-park environment, not a historic city centre. Many people solve that by living in Delft or The Hague and commuting the short distance in — which is exactly why the whole corridor should be searched as a single market.

## Career Growth in Search and Applied Research

Specialists in retrieval and multilingual NLP are scarce, and the experience transfers unusually well: legal tech, scientific publishing, e-discovery, enterprise search and any organisation trying to make sense of its own document archive all need the same skills. Within institutions, growth typically runs either towards technical leadership of a search or data platform, or towards the governance side — model documentation, quality assurance and the oversight functions that regulation is steadily making more important.

## Key Takeaways

- Rijswijk hosts a major European Patent Office site, applied research facilities including TNO, and the large Plaspoelpolder business park.
- The dominant data disciplines are information retrieval, multilingual NLP and large-scale classification — not typical commercial ML.
- Recall-oriented evaluation and expert workflows matter more than raw model performance.
- Confidentiality rules, GDPR, the AI Act and security screening shape what can be built and by whom.
- Search Rijswijk, The Hague and Delft as one market; consider patent examiner roles as a serious technical career option.
