Drive north along the North Holland coast, past Bergen and Schoorl, and the road runs between dunes and polder until a fenced research site appears on the landward side. Petten is a village of a few hundred people with a research campus attached, and the work done there touches two things most Europeans rely on without thinking: the electricity system of the future, and the medical isotopes used in hospital scans and cancer treatment today.

## Why a Village in the Dunes Became a Research Site

The location was chosen in the 1950s for a research reactor — isolated, coastal, with cooling water and distance from population centres. Around that nucleus, decades of research capability accumulated: nuclear technology and materials, and later a large energy research programme covering solar, wind, energy systems and industrial heat.

The result today is a campus where two quite different communities sit side by side. One works on radiation, materials and isotope production under an exceptionally strict safety regime. The other works on the energy transition — photovoltaics, wind, storage, hydrogen and system modelling. Both are deeply quantitative, and neither advertises for "AI engineers".

## The Named Organisations Behind Petten's Data Market

**TNO.** The Dutch organisation for applied scientific research has a major energy research presence in Petten, inherited from the former energy research centre that merged into TNO. Work spans solar cell and module technology, wind energy, energy systems modelling, industrial heat and the built environment. Laboratories here develop and test photovoltaic technologies, and system studies model how the Dutch energy system can be operated with very high shares of renewables.

**NRG and the High Flux Reactor.** The nuclear research organisation operates the **High Flux Reactor** in Petten, a research reactor used for irradiation experiments, materials research and the production of medical isotopes. A substantial share of Europe's supply of certain diagnostic and therapeutic isotopes depends on a small number of reactors, and Petten is one of them. That gives operational reliability a significance well beyond the site itself: a shutdown is felt in hospitals.

**The PALLAS project.** A new reactor is being realised in Petten to secure future isotope production and irradiation capacity. Large nuclear construction projects generate their own data work — licensing documentation, safety analysis, simulation, quality assurance and construction data management.

**The campus.** The site is developed as an **Energy and Health Campus**, intended to attract companies and research groups around those two themes. The surrounding region — **Alkmaar**, Schagen, Den Helder and Hollands Kroon — supplies the workforce and adds energy infrastructure of its own, including grid, gas and offshore connections.

## What Kind of AI and Data Work Actually Happens Here

**Energy system modelling.** Simulating an electricity and heat system with high renewable shares means optimisation over time, space and uncertainty: weather-dependent generation, storage, demand flexibility, network constraints and market behaviour. These are large mathematical programmes rather than neural networks, though learned components increasingly handle forecasting inside them.

**Renewable generation forecasting.** Solar and wind output forecasting at multiple horizons, using numerical weather prediction, satellite and sensor data. Errors here have direct financial and grid consequences.

**Materials and device research.** Solar cell development produces high-dimensional experimental data: process parameters, measurements, degradation tests. Design of experiments, Bayesian optimisation and machine learning for materials discovery are natural fits and are used in practice.

**Reliability and degradation analysis.** How does a module, a blade or a component behave after ten years in the field? Accelerated testing, statistical lifetime modelling and outdoor monitoring data all feed into warranties and investment decisions.

**Nuclear and radiation modelling.** Neutron transport simulation, thermal-hydraulics, activation calculations and safety analysis. This is high-performance scientific computing with regulatory verification requirements attached.

**Operational data in isotope production.** Scheduling irradiation campaigns, tracking batches through a supply chain where the product decays measurably by the hour, and maintaining quality documentation.

## The Constraint That Makes Isotope Work Unusual

Most supply chains assume a product sits still when nobody moves it. Medical isotopes do not. Their activity decays continuously, some with half-lives measured in days or hours, which means the value of a batch falls as it travels. Production scheduling, transport planning and hospital demand must be coordinated against a physical clock that cannot be paused.

For someone with an operations research or forecasting background, this is a genuinely distinctive planning problem: time-dependent value, hard safety constraints, irregular demand from hospitals, and no possibility of building a buffer stock. It is also work where a planning improvement translates into patients scanned or treated on time.

## Regulation That Shapes the Work

**Nuclear safety and radiation protection.** The site operates under licences supervised by the **Authority for Nuclear Safety and Radiation Protection (ANVS)**. Safety cases rest on verified and validated calculation codes; you cannot simply substitute a machine learning model for an approved method. Anyone working near this domain learns the distinction between a research model and a licensed calculation quickly.

**Quality systems for medical products.** Isotopes destined for medical use fall under pharmaceutical quality regimes, with documentation, traceability and batch release requirements.

**Grid codes and energy market rules.** Energy system studies must respect the real rules of the electricity market and network operation, which are detailed, national and changing.

**Export control and security.** Nuclear technology carries non-proliferation and security obligations that affect who may access which data, and screening requirements for some positions.

**GDPR and the AI Act.** Personal data plays a smaller role than in most sectors, but energy system research using household consumption data raises real privacy questions, and AI Act obligations apply where systems touch critical infrastructure or people.

## A Concrete Scenario: The Forecast That Was Accurate and Useless

A team builds a day-ahead solar forecast for a portfolio of installations. Mean absolute error improves by a solid margin over the existing method, and everyone is pleased.

The energy traders using it report no benefit. The reason is that their costs are driven by a small number of hours with large imbalance prices, typically when a front moves through faster or slower than predicted. Average error is the wrong target: what matters is the error distribution in those specific hours, and whether the forecast communicates its own uncertainty so a trader can hedge.

Version two produces probabilistic forecasts, is evaluated with scoring rules that reward honest uncertainty, and is benchmarked on financial outcome rather than MAE. This mismatch — optimising an average when the value lies in the tail — is the single most common error in energy forecasting, and a candidate who raises it in an interview marks themselves as experienced.

## A Common Misconception About Research Campuses

Candidates often assume applied research means slow work with little practical effect. In Petten the opposite is closer to the truth: the solar work ends up in manufacturing lines, the system studies inform national energy policy, and a reactor outage shows up in hospital schedules. What is genuinely different from a startup is the verification burden. Before a method is used for anything consequential, it must be validated, documented and often independently reviewed. That is not bureaucracy for its own sake; it is what allows the results to be relied upon.

## Skills and Tools That Stand Out

- **Numerical modelling and simulation.** Finite element or finite volume methods, Monte Carlo simulation, and the discipline of verification and validation.
- **Optimisation.** Linear and mixed-integer programming for energy system and scheduling problems.
- **Probabilistic forecasting.** Ensemble methods, calibration, proper scoring rules.
- **Design of experiments and Bayesian optimisation.** Especially valuable in materials and device development, where each experiment is expensive.
- **Scientific computing.** Python plus a compiled language, HPC familiarity and reproducible workflows.
- **Dutch.** Research groups are international and English-speaking; operations, safety and contractor work run largely in Dutch.

## Living and Commuting in Noord-Holland Noord

The region offers coast, dunes, small towns and housing costs far below Amsterdam, roughly an hour away. Alkmaar is the practical urban base, with rail connections south; Schagen and Heerhugowaard are closer to the site. A car is the normal choice for commuting to Petten.

The trade-off is a thin specialist market. If a role at the campus ends, comparable local employers are few, and most people either move within the region into energy, industry and healthcare, or accept a longer commute towards Amsterdam.

## Career Growth From Energy and Nuclear Research

This is one of the strongest domain-credential markets in the country. Energy system modelling experience transfers to grid operators, utilities, consultancies and regulators — all of which are hiring as the transition accelerates. Nuclear and radiation modelling is a small world with high barriers to entry and correspondingly durable careers, including international options at research reactors and safety authorities. Materials data experience transfers to semiconductor, chemical and manufacturing research.

## Two Different Working Cultures on One Site

Candidates should understand that the campus contains two distinct professional worlds, and that choosing between them matters more than choosing between employers elsewhere.

The energy research side works like applied research generally does: project-based, internationally collaborative, publishing where possible, with a mix of public funding and industry partnerships. Timelines are measured in project years, teams are mixed-nationality, and the balance between scientific curiosity and industrial usefulness is a constant negotiation.

The nuclear side works under a licensed safety regime. Procedures are formal, access is controlled, documentation is exhaustive, and changes to anything that touches the safety case require review before implementation. That environment suits people who find rigour reassuring and frustrates those who prefer to iterate quickly. It also builds a professional profile — working inside a licensed, audited technical environment — that is directly transferable to aviation, pharmaceutical manufacturing, rail safety and other high-consequence sectors.

Neither culture is better. But arriving at one while expecting the other is the most common source of disappointment among new staff, and asking about it explicitly in an interview is entirely legitimate.

## Where the Energy Transition Creates the Most Analytical Demand

Three streams of work are expanding fastest and are worth targeting specifically.

**System integration studies.** As electrification increases, the questions shift from individual technologies to how they fit together: how much storage is needed, where flexibility comes from, what happens to heat demand, how hydrogen interacts with electricity. These are large optimisation and scenario-modelling exercises commissioned by government, grid operators and industry.

**Field performance analysis.** Solar and wind installations across the country generate performance data. Understanding degradation, soiling, shading and inverter behaviour at portfolio scale turns warranty and investment questions into statistics problems.

**Industrial heat and process electrification.** Heavy industry accounts for a large share of Dutch energy use, and converting process heat away from natural gas requires modelling of individual plants, their constraints and their economics.

All three need people who can combine engineering understanding with data analysis, and all three are commissioned repeatedly rather than once, which makes them a stable base for a career.

- Petten hosts applied energy research and nuclear research, including the High Flux Reactor and the PALLAS project, on a campus focused on energy and health.
- The data work is simulation, optimisation, forecasting, materials analytics and safety analysis rather than typical commercial ML.
- Medical isotope production is a rare planning problem where the product decays while in transit.
- Nuclear licensing, pharmaceutical quality rules and grid regulation set the verification standards.
- Almost everyone commutes by car from Alkmaar, Schagen or Den Helder; plan the region, not the village.
