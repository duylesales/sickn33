Every organisation that wants to use AI faces the same question sooner or later: do we hire people who already have the skills, or do we develop the people we already have? Hiring brings expertise quickly but is expensive and competitive. Upskilling builds on employees who know the business, the data and the customers, but takes time and does not always succeed. In the Netherlands, where the labour market for AI and data professionals remains tight and the EU AI Act requires organisations to ensure AI literacy among staff, the choice is rarely either-or. The most effective organisations combine both — deliberately.

## Why the Question Has Become Urgent

**Talent scarcity.** Experienced data scientists, ML engineers and data engineers remain in high demand, and smaller employers and organisations outside major cities often struggle to compete on salary and visibility.

**Domain knowledge matters more than expected.** Many AI projects fail not because of weak models but because teams misunderstand the business process, data or users. Existing employees bring precisely that knowledge.

**AI tools have lowered some technical barriers.** Low-code tools, AI coding assistants and managed platforms allow more employees to work with data, although they do not replace deep expertise.

**AI literacy is now a legal obligation.** Since February 2025, Article 4 of the EU AI Act requires providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy among staff dealing with AI systems on their behalf.

## Three Levels of AI Capability in an Organisation

| Level | Who | What they need | Best approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI literacy | All staff using AI tools or affected by AI decisions | Understanding of what AI can and cannot do, risks, company policies | Broad training for everyone |
| AI-enabled roles | Analysts, domain experts, product owners, managers | Ability to work with data, specify AI use cases, evaluate outputs | Upskilling of selected employees |
| AI specialists | Data scientists, ML engineers, data engineers | Deep technical expertise to build and maintain systems | Hiring, or long-term development of strong candidates |

The decision between upskilling and hiring depends heavily on which level you need.

## Roles That Are Well Suited to Upskilling

**Data analysts moving towards data science.** Analysts already understand data, business questions and stakeholders. With training in statistics, programming and model evaluation, many can take on predictive work.

**BI developers moving towards analytics engineering.** Experience with data models and reporting provides a solid foundation for modern data engineering practices.

**Domain experts moving towards AI product roles.** Planners, engineers, underwriters or clinicians can become valuable AI product owners or domain leads, translating business needs into AI requirements.

**Software engineers moving towards ML engineering.** Strong engineering skills transfer well; the gap is mainly in machine learning concepts, data handling and model evaluation.

**IT and security staff moving towards AI governance.** Knowledge of risk management and controls translates to AI governance and compliance.

## Roles That Usually Require Hiring

**Senior ML engineers and data scientists** who must design complex systems, set technical standards or lead teams usually require years of experience that cannot be built quickly through training.

**Specialists in narrow fields,** such as computer vision for medical imaging or optimisation for energy markets, often need to be hired or brought in through partners.

**The first technical lead** in an organisation starting with AI benefits from external experience to avoid foundational mistakes.

## A Decision Framework

| Question | Favours upskilling | Favours hiring |
|---|---|---|
| How quickly do we need results? | Within one to two years | Within months |
| How important is domain knowledge? | Critical to success | Less important or easily learned |
| Do we have employees with aptitude and motivation? | Yes | No suitable candidates |
| Is there senior expertise to mentor learners? | Yes, internally or externally | No mentoring available |
| How specialised is the required skill? | Broadly applicable | Highly specialised |
| How competitive is the external market? | Very competitive or location disadvantaged | Candidates available |

In many cases, the best answer is to hire one experienced specialist who mentors and multiplies the capability of upskilled internal employees.

## Costs and Time: A Realistic View

Upskilling is not free. Beyond course fees, the largest costs are employees' time away from regular work, mentoring time from experienced colleagues and the productivity dip during learning. A meaningful transition from analyst to data scientist typically takes many months of structured learning combined with practical projects.

Hiring also has costs: recruitment fees or advertising, time spent interviewing, onboarding, salary premiums in a competitive market and the risk that a new hire leaves quickly or does not understand the business.

A realistic comparison includes these hidden costs on both sides, not just salary versus course fees.

## Funding and Support in the Netherlands

**SLIM scheme.** The SLIM subsidy supports SMEs and certain other employers in strengthening learning and development in their organisation.

**Sector training funds.** Many sectors have training and development funds (O&O-fondsen) that may subsidise training for employees in that sector.

**Collective agreements.** Many collective agreements include individual development budgets or training rights.

**Lifelong learning policy.** The Dutch government promotes lifelong learning under Leven Lang Ontwikkelen; national schemes change over time, so check current options.

**Free introductory resources.** Free online courses in Dutch, such as the **Nationale AI-cursus**, can support broad AI literacy programmes.

**Universities of applied sciences.** Many offer part-time programmes, short courses and custom training for companies.

## Training Costs and Retention

Employers sometimes worry that trained employees will leave. Dutch law affects how organisations can manage this risk.

Since the **Transparent and Predictable Working Conditions Act** took effect in 2022, training that employers are required to provide by law or collective agreement to perform the job must be free of charge for employees, and repayment clauses cannot apply to such training. For other training, such as a substantial data science programme that is not required for the current role, a training cost repayment clause (studiekostenbeding) can be agreed, typically decreasing over time. Such clauses must be clearly agreed in advance.

In practice, the most effective retention measure is not a repayment clause but a clear career path: employees who complete training and then receive a role, responsibilities and pay that reflect their new skills are far more likely to stay.

## Making Upskilling Succeed

**Select for aptitude and motivation.** Not every employee wants or is suited to a technical transition. Use voluntary programmes and assess candidates on analytical thinking and curiosity.

**Combine learning with real projects.** Courses alone rarely create capability. Assign learners to real use cases with mentoring.

**Provide protected time.** Learning on top of a full workload usually fails. Agree on a percentage of working time dedicated to development.

**Arrange mentoring.** An experienced data professional — internal, hired or external — reviews work and helps avoid common mistakes.

**Define the destination.** Clarify which role the employee grows into, and how pay and responsibilities will change.

**Involve the works council** where training programmes, selection criteria or changes to job classifications affect employees collectively.

## Designing a Learning Path: From Analyst to Data Scientist

A structured learning path increases the chance that upskilling produces real capability. An example for a data analyst moving towards data science:

| Phase | Focus | Practical component |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | Python or R, version control, statistics refresher | Reproduce an existing report in code |
| Modelling basics | Regression, classification, validation, baselines | Build a simple predictive model on company data with a mentor |
| Evaluation and experimentation | Metrics, data leakage, experiment design, causal thinking | Evaluate a pilot or experiment in the business |
| Working with production | Collaboration with data and ML engineers, monitoring basics | Contribute to deploying a model |
| Responsible AI | Bias, privacy, documentation, AI Act awareness | Document a model and its limitations |

Each phase combines learning with application. The duration depends on starting level and available time, but plans should assume months rather than weeks.

## Measuring Whether Upskilling Works

Organisations often measure upskilling by the number of courses completed or certificates obtained. These measures say little about capability. More useful indicators include:

- **Projects delivered** by upskilled employees, and whether results were used.
- **Level of independence,** for example whether employees can scope and complete an analysis without constant support.
- **Quality reviews** by experienced data professionals.
- **Retention and progression** of participants after the programme.
- **Business outcomes** linked to the use cases they worked on.

Evaluating the programme after a year — and adjusting selection, mentoring or time allocation — prevents repeating an approach that does not work.

## A Concrete Scenario: One Hire, Four Upskilled Employees

A regional insurer wants to use machine learning in claims processing but struggles to hire data scientists. Instead of trying to hire a full team, it recruits one experienced lead data scientist and selects four internal employees: two analysts, a claims specialist and a software developer.

Over a year, the four combine part-time training at a university of applied sciences with projects supervised by the lead. The analysts build predictive models, the claims specialist becomes the product owner and the developer handles deployment. The insurer delivers two use cases and builds a team that understands claims processes deeply. Two of the upskilled employees are formally promoted into data science roles. The insurer later hires additional specialists — who join a team that already knows the business.

## A Common Misconception

A frequent misconception is that upskilling is always cheaper than hiring. Without mentoring, protected time and real projects, upskilling programmes produce certificates rather than capability, and the cost of failed projects can exceed recruitment costs. The opposite misconception — that only external hires can do AI work — overlooks the value of domain knowledge and the difficulty of integrating new hires into complex organisations.

## Communicating an Upskilling Programme to the Organisation

How an upskilling programme is introduced affects both participation and the reactions of employees who are not selected. Unclear communication can create anxiety — employees may interpret AI training as a sign that their jobs will disappear — or resentment if selection appears arbitrary.

**Explain the purpose.** Make clear why the organisation invests in AI capability and how it connects to strategy, for example improving planning quality or reducing administrative burden, rather than only cutting costs.

**Be transparent about selection.** Publish criteria for participation in intensive programmes, such as analytical aptitude, motivation and relevance of the role, and offer broader AI literacy training to everyone.

**Address job security concerns honestly.** If AI will change roles, say so, and explain how the organisation will support employees through those changes.

**Involve managers.** Managers must free up time for participants and recognise their development in evaluations; without their support, programmes stall.

**Share progress.** Presenting results from participants' projects shows the value of the programme and encourages others to engage.

Where a programme affects many employees or changes job structures, the works council can play a constructive role in shaping communication and selection criteria.

## Key Takeaways

- Distinguish three levels: AI literacy for all, AI-enabled roles for selected employees and AI specialists for complex technical work.
- Analysts, BI developers, domain experts, software engineers and IT risk staff are well suited to upskilling; senior and highly specialised roles usually require hiring.
- The most effective model often combines one experienced hire with several upskilled employees.
- Include hidden costs of both approaches; use SLIM, sector funds, collective agreement budgets and educational partners.
- Mandatory training must be free, repayment clauses have limits, and clear career paths are the strongest retention measure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: employer facing talent shortage) Should we train existing staff or hire data scientists?
Often both — train employees with domain knowledge and aptitude, and hire experienced specialists for complex work and mentoring.

### (Scenario: employer unsure which employees to train) Which employees are best suited to become data professionals?
Data analysts, BI developers, software engineers and motivated domain experts with analytical aptitude are often good candidates.

### (Scenario: employer concerned about retention) Can we require employees to repay training costs if they leave?
Not for training required by law or collective agreement; for other training, a clearly agreed repayment clause is possible.

### (Scenario: employer thinking about AI literacy) Does the AI Act require us to train employees?
Organisations using AI systems must take measures to ensure sufficient AI literacy among staff dealing with those systems.

### (Scenario: SME with limited budget) Are there subsidies for upskilling employees in AI?
The SLIM scheme, sector training funds and collective agreement budgets may help; check current conditions.
