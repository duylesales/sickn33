Halfway between the ports of Rotterdam and Antwerp, where the Hollands Diep widens towards the sea, lies an industrial area that most travellers only notice from the A16 bridge. Moerdijk is home to one of the largest industrial sites in the south of the Netherlands: a chemical complex, a waste-to-energy plant, a seaport and a logistics park where goods are transferred between ship, rail and road. For data professionals, the area offers work on some of the most safety-critical operations in the country — work where the quality of data and models is measured not only in efficiency, but in the prevention of serious incidents.

## Why Moerdijk Became an Industrial Port

The location at the junction of waterways, railways and motorways made Moerdijk a natural site for heavy industry and logistics. In the second half of the twentieth century, a large industrial area and port were developed along the Hollands Diep. Chemical production, energy generation, waste processing and bulk handling found space there that was no longer available closer to cities.

Today, the **Port of Moerdijk** is one of the Netherlands' seaports, handling containers, bulk and general cargo, and serving as a hub for inland shipping and rail connections into the European hinterland. Its position between the two largest ports in Europe gives it a distinctive role as an overflow and connecting hub.

## The Named Organisations Behind Moerdijk's Industrial Market

**Shell Moerdijk.** Shell operates a large chemical complex in Moerdijk that produces base chemicals used in plastics and other products. Large-scale chemical plants involve continuous processes, extensive instrumentation and strict safety management.

**Attero.** The waste management company operates a waste-to-energy plant in Moerdijk, processing residual waste and generating electricity and heat. Waste incineration involves highly variable input streams and strict emissions monitoring.

**Port of Moerdijk.** The port authority manages port infrastructure and promotes the port's development, including logistics, sustainability and energy transition initiatives.

**Moerdijk Logistics Park.** A large business park focused on logistics, where warehouses and distribution centres benefit from access to water, rail and road.

**Chemical, logistics and service companies.** The industrial area hosts numerous smaller chemical producers, tank storage operators, maintenance contractors, engineering firms and logistics service providers.

**Dutch Safety Board.** The independent national safety board investigates serious incidents, including major industrial accidents. Its reports on incidents in the chemical industry have influenced how companies and regulators approach process safety.

## Why Process Safety Defines the Work

Moerdijk's industrial area has experienced serious incidents in the past, including the large fire at the Chemie-Pack chemical storage company in 2011, which led to extensive investigations and changes in supervision and safety culture. Such events shape how companies in the area think about data: alarms, sensor readings, maintenance records and near-miss reports are safety information, not just operational data.

For data professionals, this means two things. First, there is genuine demand for analytics that improves safety: detecting abnormal process conditions earlier, identifying equipment at risk of failure, analysing near-miss reports for patterns and supporting inspections. Second, any model that influences operations must be introduced with great care, validated thoroughly and integrated into safety management systems rather than bypassing them.

## Alarm Floods: A Classic Industrial Data Problem

During a process upset in a chemical plant, control systems can generate hundreds of alarms in minutes. Operators must identify the root cause quickly while the alarm screen overflows. Investigations of industrial accidents worldwide have repeatedly identified alarm floods as a contributing factor.

Data analysis of alarm histories helps identify nuisance alarms, chattering alarms that repeatedly switch on and off, and sequences where one event triggers many consequential alarms. Rationalising alarms and presenting operators with the most relevant information is a well-established discipline, guided by industry standards for alarm management. Machine learning can support root cause identification and grouping of related alarms, but the final design must be agreed with operators and process safety experts.

## Regulation That Shapes the Work

**Seveso III Directive 2012/18/EU and the Major Accidents Risks Decree 2015 (Brzo 2015).** Sites that store or use large quantities of hazardous substances must prevent major accidents and limit their consequences. In the Netherlands, these requirements are implemented through Brzo, and Brzo companies are inspected jointly by several authorities. Safety management systems, risk analyses and emergency plans are mandatory.

**Environment and Planning Act (Omgevingswet).** In force since 2024, the act integrates environmental, planning and building rules, including permits for industrial activities and emissions.

**EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS).** Large industrial installations must monitor, report and verify their emissions and surrender allowances, making accurate emissions data financially significant.

**NIS2 Directive (EU) 2022/2555.** Chemical production, energy, waste management and transport fall within the scope of stricter cybersecurity requirements for qualifying organisations, which increases attention to operational technology security.

**REACH and chemical regulations.** Chemical substances are subject to registration, evaluation and restriction under EU chemicals legislation, creating documentation and data requirements.

**EU Artificial Intelligence Act.** AI systems used as safety components in critical infrastructure or in machinery can be high-risk, which is relevant to AI influencing process control or safety-related decisions.

## A Concrete Scenario: Detecting a Developing Leak

A plant has thousands of sensors measuring pressures, temperatures, flows and gas concentrations. A small leak in a heat exchanger may first appear as a subtle change in the relationship between several measurements rather than a single alarm. A multivariate monitoring model trained on normal operation detects a gradual deviation days before any conventional alarm would trigger.

The alert is only the start. The process engineer must decide whether the deviation is real or caused by a sensor issue, a change in feedstock or a planned operational change. The maintenance team must plan an inspection without disrupting production unnecessarily. The team learns that a successful monitoring system must provide not only an alert but also context: which variables deviate, since when, and how similar past situations developed. Over time, validated alerts and their outcomes become training data that improves the system.

## A Common Misconception About Industrial AI

Data scientists new to heavy industry sometimes expect to deploy models directly into process control. In practice, most AI in chemical plants starts as decision support for engineers and operators, and only carefully validated applications move towards closed-loop control. The reason is not conservatism for its own sake: the consequences of a wrong automated action in a plant handling hazardous substances can be severe. Engineers who respect this — and who design models that fit within safety management — earn trust and see their work adopted.

## Questions Worth Asking Before Joining

- **How are data-driven insights integrated into the site's safety management system?** This reveals whether analytics are taken seriously and handled responsibly.
- **What is the process for moving from advisory models to automated control?** Clear stages and approvals indicate maturity.
- **How is operational technology data made available for analysis?** Secure data access is often the first bottleneck.
- **How much time will I spend on site versus in an office or at home?** Industrial roles often require regular presence.
- **How does the energy transition affect the site's plans?** Many chemical sites are investing in electrification, recycling or carbon capture, creating new projects.

## Commuting and Living Near Moerdijk

The industrial area is located near the A16 and A17 motorways and is close to Breda, Dordrecht, Roosendaal and Etten-Leur. Railway stations such as Lage Zwaluwe are nearby, but most employees commute by car, as industrial sites are spread out and shift schedules do not always match public transport.
