Ask someone in the Randstad where Dutch official statistics are produced, and most will say The Hague. Ask where the pensions of millions of Dutch public-sector and education workers are administered, and few will know the answer. Both answers include Heerlen — a city in the south of Limburg that has quietly become one of the most data-intensive employment locations in the country, without ever marketing itself as an AI hub.

## Why Heerlen Is a Data City Before It Is a Tech City

Heerlen's economy was shaped by coal mining until the mines closed in the 1960s and 1970s. The national government responded partly by relocating public institutions to the region, and that decision still defines the local labour market. The result is unusual: instead of a cluster of startups, Heerlen has a small number of very large organisations whose core business is processing enormous, highly structured datasets under strict legal and methodological rules.

That profile matters for anyone working in machine learning or data science. The work here is less about shipping a consumer feature and more about producing numbers that governments, pension participants and supervisors rely on. Accuracy, auditability and reproducibility are not nice-to-haves; they are the product.

## The Named Organisations Behind Heerlen's Data Market

**Statistics Netherlands (CBS).** CBS has two main offices: The Hague and Heerlen. The Heerlen office is a large workplace for statisticians, methodologists, data engineers and IT specialists. CBS produces official statistics on the economy, population, labour market and society, and it has invested in data science and new data sources — for example web data, sensor data and administrative registers — through its Center for Big Data Statistics.

**APG.** APG is one of the largest pension administrators in the Netherlands and has major offices in Heerlen and Amsterdam. It carries out pension administration, asset management and communication for pension funds, including ABP, the pension fund for government and education employees. Pension administration at this scale is a data problem: contribution records, participant histories, actuarial calculations and — increasingly — personalised communication.

**Brightlands Smart Services Campus.** One of the four Brightlands campuses in Limburg, this campus in Heerlen focuses on data science, digital services and smart services for sectors such as government, finance and health. It hosts companies, research groups and education programmes around data, and it is the most visible place in the city where AI-related work is explicitly labelled as such.

**Knowledge institutions.** The **Open Universiteit**, the Dutch open university for distance learning, has its headquarters in Heerlen. **Zuyd University of Applied Sciences** has a campus in Heerlen, and **Maastricht University** is active on the Brightlands Smart Services Campus through data science research and education. Together they supply researchers, lecturers and graduates to the local market.

## What Kind of AI and Data Work Actually Happens Here

The work in Heerlen clusters around three themes.

**Official statistics and methodology.** Producing statistics from administrative data raises classic machine learning questions: record linkage across registers, classification of free-text answers into official coding schemes, imputation of missing values, and estimating quantities from new, non-probability data sources such as web-scraped prices or vacancy texts. The challenge is not just predictive accuracy but whether a method is statistically defensible and stable enough to publish as an official figure.

**Pension administration and asset management.** A pension administrator processes the working lives of millions of people. Machine learning applications include data quality detection in contribution records, forecasting of participant behaviour, document processing and customer contact analytics. Asset management adds a quantitative finance dimension: portfolio analytics, risk models and ESG data processing.

**Public and smart services.** Companies and research groups on the campus work on digital services for governments and organisations — from data platforms to privacy-preserving analytics.

## The Pension System Transition as a Data Project

The **Future Pensions Act (Wet toekomst pensioenen)** entered into force in 2023 and changes the Dutch pension system from defined-benefit-like promises to a system with more individual pension capital. Pension funds have a transition period to move to the new system, with a legal deadline in 2028.

For data professionals, this transition is one of the largest data migration and validation projects in the Dutch financial sector. Historical accrued entitlements must be converted, participant data must be complete and correct, and communication must explain individual consequences clearly. Supervisors such as **De Nederlandsche Bank (DNB)** and the Netherlands Authority for the Financial Markets (AFM) pay close attention to data quality in this process. Large administrators in Heerlen are in the middle of this work, which drives demand for data engineers, data quality specialists and analysts who understand both technology and pension logic.

## Regulation That Shapes the Work

**GDPR.** Almost everything processed in Heerlen's large institutions is personal data: income, employment history, household composition, pension entitlements. Data minimisation, purpose limitation and pseudonymisation are daily practice, not abstract principles.

**Statistics Netherlands Act.** CBS operates under its own law, which gives it access to administrative data for statistical purposes and imposes strict confidentiality: no published statistic may allow individuals or companies to be identified. Statistical disclosure control — making sure outputs cannot be traced back to individuals — is a specialism in its own right.

**Financial supervision.** Pension administrators are supervised by DNB and the AFM, which have expectations on data quality, outsourcing, IT risk and model governance. The EU Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) also applies to parts of the financial sector, including institutions for occupational retirement provision, with a proportional approach.

**EU AI Act.** Some applications in finance, such as certain credit and insurance assessments, fall in higher-risk categories. Public institutions using algorithms also face expectations around transparency, for example through the Dutch government's Algorithm Register.

## A Concrete Scenario: Classifying Occupations From Free Text

A statistics team wants to classify job descriptions from online vacancies into an official occupational classification. A transformer-based text classifier reaches high accuracy on a labelled test set. But the methodologists ask different questions: does accuracy differ between rare and common occupations? What happens when the vocabulary of vacancy texts shifts over two years? Can the classification error be quantified and corrected for in published figures, so that a trend in the statistic is not in fact a trend in the model's mistakes?

This is typical of Heerlen's data work. The model is only half the job; the other half is proving that its output is fit to become an official number.

## A Common Misconception About Institutional Data Work

Candidates from product companies sometimes assume that work at a statistics office or pension administrator is slow and technically outdated. The pace of decision-making can indeed be slower than at a startup, and legacy systems exist. But the technical depth is often greater than expected: methodology departments deal with bias, uncertainty and causal questions at a level many commercial teams never reach, and large data platforms and cloud migrations are common. The difference is less about technology and more about accountability — results are scrutinised by supervisors, parliament and the public.

## Skills and Tools That Stand Out

- **Statistics and methodology.** Sampling, estimation, uncertainty quantification and model evaluation beyond a single accuracy score.
- **Data quality engineering.** Validation rules, anomaly detection in administrative data, lineage and reconciliation.
- **Record linkage and entity resolution.** Matching records across registers without a perfect shared identifier.
- **Privacy-preserving methods.** Pseudonymisation, statistical disclosure control and awareness of differential privacy concepts.
- **Programming and platforms.** Python or R for analysis, SQL for large relational data, and familiarity with data platforms and cloud environments.
- **Domain translation.** The ability to explain methodological choices to policy advisers, actuaries or pension fund boards.
- **Dutch language.** Many roles at public institutions and pension administrators require good Dutch, because documentation, stakeholders and participants are Dutch-speaking.

## Why Heerlen Is Worth Considering as a Place to Live and Work

Housing in Parkstad Limburg is generally more affordable than in the Randstad, and commuting within the region is short. For candidates who grew up in Limburg and left for Amsterdam or Utrecht because they assumed there was no serious data work at home, Heerlen is exactly the kind of place OnlyAIJobs was built to make visible: large, stable employers doing technically demanding data work, a short distance from where people already live.

The trade-off is that the market is concentrated. A few large employers dominate, so career moves within the city are limited compared with Amsterdam. Many professionals therefore look at Heerlen, Maastricht, Sittard-Geleen and Aachen together as one regional market.

## Career Growth in Institutional Data Work

A data professional who starts at a statistics office or pension administrator often grows along one of two paths. The first is technical depth: becoming a methodologist or lead data scientist who designs how new data sources and models enter production statistics or core administration. The second is governance: moving into data management, model risk or data protection roles, which are in growing demand as regulation increases. Both paths build a type of experience — accountable, audited, large-scale data work — that is valued by banks, insurers and government bodies elsewhere in the country.
