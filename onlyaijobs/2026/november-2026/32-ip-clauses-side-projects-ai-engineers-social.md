💡 You maintain an open-source library, train small models at the weekend or build a tool that might become a product. Who owns that work?

📜 Under the Dutch Copyright Act, the employer is considered the maker of works created by an employee in the performance of their duties. Work created independently, outside your job and without employer resources, generally stays yours. The Patents Act gives employers rights to inventions when the role involves making them, sometimes with fair remuneration.

⚠️ Contracts often go further, claiming everything created during employment. That is where negotiation matters — and where listing prior projects in an annex pays off.

✅ Keep boundaries clean: your own equipment, your own time, never employer data or confidential methods. When changing jobs, take your skills, not code or datasets.
