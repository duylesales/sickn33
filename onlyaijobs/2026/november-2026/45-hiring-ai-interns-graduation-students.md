For many Dutch companies, the first serious experiment with machine learning does not start with a hire or a consultancy project. It starts with a student. A master's student in data science spends six months analysing production data. A student from a university of applied sciences builds a dashboard and a first forecast. An MBO student helps clean and structure data. Internships and graduation projects give employers a low-risk way to explore AI use cases, and they give students real-world experience. They are also one of the most effective recruitment channels: interns who have a good experience often return as employees. But a successful AI internship requires more preparation than many employers expect.

## Types of Internships and Projects

| Type | Education level | Typical duration | What students can realistically deliver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation or work placement internship | MBO, HBO early years | Weeks to a few months | Data collection, cleaning, basic analysis and reporting |
| Work placement as part of MBO programme | MBO | Months, often part of the curriculum | Practical support in data management or IT tasks |
| Graduation internship (afstudeerstage) | HBO | Around five months | Applied project with a concrete product, such as a model or dashboard |
| Master's thesis project in a company | WO | Several months | Research-oriented project with methodological depth |
| Student team projects | HBO and WO | Weeks to a semester | Exploratory analyses and prototypes |
| PhD collaboration or industrial PhD | WO | Years | Long-term research linked to company challenges |

For AI use cases, graduation internships and master's thesis projects are often the most valuable, because they combine enough duration with academic supervision.

## Why Internships Work Well for AI Exploration

- **Low cost relative to value,** allowing companies to test feasibility before larger investments.
- **Academic supervision,** which adds methodological quality.
- **Fresh knowledge** of current tools and techniques.
- **A natural recruitment pipeline,** as companies can assess students over months.
- **Employer branding** among students and universities.

For companies outside the Randstad, internships are particularly effective in attracting talent from nearby universities and universities of applied sciences before graduates move away.

## The Internship Agreement

In the Netherlands, internships are usually formalised in an internship agreement (stageovereenkomst), typically involving three parties: the student, the educational institution and the host organisation. The agreement specifies learning objectives, duration, working hours, supervision, allowance, confidentiality and intellectual property.

For MBO work placements that are part of a vocational programme, the host organisation generally must be an **accredited work placement company (erkend leerbedrijf)**, accredited through **SBB**. This ensures that the company can provide appropriate guidance.

A genuine internship focuses on learning. If an intern performs regular productive work without a learning component, the relationship may be considered an employment contract, with consequences such as minimum wage obligations.

## Internship Allowance

Dutch law does not generally require an internship allowance for genuine learning internships, but paying one is common and increasingly expected, especially for graduation internships in technical fields. There has been political and societal discussion about introducing stronger rules on fair internship allowances. A competitive allowance makes a clear difference in attracting strong students, particularly in AI and data, where students often have several options.

## Supervision: The Most Underestimated Factor

The most common reason AI internships fail is insufficient supervision. A student cannot be expected to find data, understand business processes, define a use case and apply advanced methods without guidance.

**Assign a company supervisor** who understands the business problem and has time for regular meetings, ideally weekly.

**Arrange technical support** where possible. If the company has no data professionals, involve an external mentor, rely more heavily on the academic supervisor or choose a less technical project.

**Align expectations with the educational institution.** Universities have requirements for graduation projects, such as research questions and methodological depth. A project that only produces a dashboard may not meet a master's thesis standard.

## Designing a Good AI Internship Project

- **Concrete business question:** for example, "Can we predict which orders will be delayed?" rather than "Explore AI opportunities."
- **Available data:** confirm before the internship starts that data exists and can be accessed.
- **Clear scope:** achievable within the internship period, with room for learning.
- **Defined deliverables:** a report, model, dashboard or recommendation.
- **Stakeholders:** people who will use the results and can provide domain knowledge.

## Data Access and Privacy

Interns often need access to company data, which may include personal data of customers or employees. Under the **GDPR**, access should be limited to what is necessary for the project. Use pseudonymised or anonymised data where possible, provide secure systems rather than allowing data on personal laptops and include confidentiality obligations in the internship agreement.

Theses are often published by universities. If the project involves confidential information, agree in advance on confidentiality arrangements, such as reviewing the thesis for sensitive information, anonymising company details or requesting a temporary publication embargo, within the institution's rules.

## Intellectual Property

Unlike employees, interns are generally not employees, so the rule in the **Dutch Copyright Act** that employers own works created by employees in the performance of their duties does not automatically apply. Copyright in code, models and reports may remain with the student unless rights are transferred in writing. Educational institutions often have their own rules and standard agreements. Agree in the internship agreement on ownership and use of results, while respecting the student's right to use the work for graduation.

## Working Hours and Wellbeing

The **Working Hours Act** also applies to interns, including limits on working hours and rest periods. Students are completing academic requirements, and graduation projects can be stressful. Clear agreements about working days, availability and deadlines help both parties.

## International Students

Many AI and data students at Dutch universities are international. Non-EU students enrolled at a Dutch educational institution can generally do an internship that is part of their study programme with an internship agreement. Rules differ for students from foreign institutions, who may need a residence permit for traineeships. Check current requirements with the **Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND)** and the educational institution. After graduation, international students may qualify for the orientation year for highly educated persons, which makes hiring former interns easier.

## AI Literacy and Responsible Use

Since February 2025, the **EU AI Act** requires organisations using AI systems to ensure sufficient AI literacy among staff and others dealing with AI systems on their behalf. When interns build or use AI tools, include them in basic guidance on responsible use, data protection and the company's policies — which also helps students learn professional practices.

## What Students Look For When Choosing an Internship

Strong AI and data students often choose between several internship offers. Understanding what they value helps employers compete — including smaller companies and organisations outside major cities.

**A meaningful problem.** Students want projects with real data and a clear purpose, not tasks invented to keep an intern busy.

**Supervision and learning.** Access to an experienced supervisor, regular feedback and the opportunity to learn professional practices matter as much as the topic.

**Feasibility for graduation.** Students need a project that meets their programme's academic requirements. Companies that discuss this openly with the student and academic supervisor are more attractive.

**Fair allowance and practical arrangements.** An allowance, reimbursement of travel costs and flexibility about working from home or on campus make a real difference.

**Prospects.** The possibility of a job after graduation is an important factor, particularly for international students who want to stay in the Netherlands.

**Location and accessibility.** Many students prefer internships reachable from their university city. Companies within reasonable travel distance of universities or universities of applied sciences have a clear advantage.

## Hybrid Internships and Remote Supervision

Many students expect some flexibility to work remotely, particularly during the writing phase of a thesis. Hybrid internships can work well, but they require structure. Agree on days when the student is present, especially at the start of the internship, when understanding the organisation and data is most important. Schedule fixed meetings with the supervisor. Provide secure remote access to data rather than allowing copies on personal devices. And include the student in team meetings and informal moments, so they experience the organisation beyond their project.

## Common Pitfalls in AI Internship Projects

- **Data access is arranged only after the internship starts,** wasting weeks.
- **The project is too broad,** such as "use AI to improve operations".
- **The company expects a production-ready system** rather than a validated prototype and recommendations.
- **No one uses the results,** because stakeholders were not involved.
- **Knowledge disappears after the internship,** because code and documentation are not handed over properly.

## Converting Interns Into Employees

Internships are a strong recruitment channel, but conversion does not happen automatically.

- **Discuss career intentions early,** without pressure, so both sides know whether employment is a possibility.
- **Give interns meaningful work and visibility,** including presentations to management.
- **Offer a clear next step** before graduation, such as a traineeship or junior role.
- **Stay in contact** if immediate hiring is not possible.

Students often decide on their first job months before graduating. Companies that wait until the thesis defence may lose candidates to employers who made offers earlier.

## A Concrete Scenario: From Graduation Project to First Data Role

A regional food producer has no data team but suspects its production planning could be improved. It offers a graduation internship to a student from a nearby university of applied sciences, with a clear question: can historical order and production data predict weekly demand per product group?

The company assigns the planning manager as supervisor, arranges access to anonymised order data and meets the student weekly. The student builds a forecasting model and a simple dashboard and presents results to management. The forecast is not perfect but clearly improves on current methods for several product groups. Before the student graduates, the company offers a junior data analyst position. A year later, the student leads the company's first data initiatives.

## A Common Misconception

A frequent misconception is that interns are a cheap way to get AI projects done. In reality, successful AI internships require significant supervision, preparation and access to data, and their primary value lies in exploration and recruitment rather than delivering production systems. Another misconception is that only large companies can attract strong interns. Clear projects, good supervision and a fair allowance allow SMEs to attract excellent students, especially in regions near universities.

## Key Takeaways

- Internships and graduation projects are effective ways to explore AI use cases and build a talent pipeline.
- Formalise internships in an agreement with the student and educational institution; MBO placements require accreditation through SBB.
- Allowances are common and important for attracting strong AI and data students.
- Supervision, a concrete question, available data and clear scope determine success.
- Arrange data protection, confidentiality and intellectual property in advance, check rules for international students and make job offers before graduation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: company new to internships) Do we need an internship agreement?
Yes — internships are usually formalised in an agreement between the student, the educational institution and the host organisation.

### (Scenario: employer considering unpaid internships) Are we required to pay interns?
Dutch law does not generally require an allowance for genuine learning internships, but paying one is common and helps attract strong students.

### (Scenario: company concerned about ownership) Who owns the code and models an intern creates?
Because interns are generally not employees, rights may remain with the student unless transferred in writing; agree on ownership in the internship agreement.

### (Scenario: SME without data staff) Can we offer an AI internship without a data team?
Yes, with a clear business question, available data and strong supervision, possibly supported by an external mentor and the academic supervisor.

### (Scenario: employer wanting to hire an intern) When should we offer a job to a good intern?
Before graduation — many students decide on their first job months before completing their studies.
