Bergen op Zoom is best known in the Netherlands for its historic centre, its fortifications and one of the country's most famous carnival celebrations. Less well known is that the town hosts one of the largest engineering plastics production sites in Europe. The materials made there end up in cars, electronics, medical devices and construction products worldwide. As the plastics industry faces pressure to reduce emissions and move towards circular materials, the data work at sites like this one is changing fast. For data professionals interested in materials science, chemistry and industrial operations, Bergen op Zoom offers specialised opportunities that rarely appear in AI job searches.

## Why Bergen op Zoom Became a Plastics Location

In the early 1970s, General Electric established a plastics production site in Bergen op Zoom, attracted by the location between Rotterdam and Antwerp, access to water and space for industry. The site grew into a major centre for engineering thermoplastics — high-performance plastics used where strength, heat resistance or transparency matter. In 2007, GE sold its plastics business to **SABIC**, which continues to operate the site.

The industrial area around the **Theodorushaven** port area also hosts other companies in logistics, food processing and manufacturing. Together with nearby Roosendaal and the wider West Brabant region, Bergen op Zoom is part of an industrial corridor that connects the ports of Rotterdam and Antwerp.

## The Named Organisations Behind Bergen op Zoom's Industrial Market

**SABIC.** The Bergen op Zoom site produces engineering thermoplastics and specialty materials and has been associated with research, application development and technical support for customers. Production of polymers involves continuous and batch chemical processes, compounding, quality control and extensive laboratory work — all generating data that can be used to improve processes and develop materials.

**Theodorushaven port and industrial area.** The port area hosts logistics companies, processors and manufacturers, with connections to inland shipping routes towards Rotterdam and Antwerp.

**Food processing and manufacturing companies.** West Brabant and neighbouring Zeeland host a significant food processing sector, including vegetable and potato processing, and other manufacturers, which add operational data roles to the regional market.

**Education.** **Curio**, a regional vocational education institution, has locations in Bergen op Zoom and trains process operators and technicians. For higher education, **Avans University of Applied Sciences** and **Breda University of Applied Sciences** in Breda are within reach, as are universities in Rotterdam, Antwerp and Eindhoven.

## Materials Informatics: Using Data to Design Materials

Developing a new plastic grade traditionally involves many rounds of formulation, compounding and testing. Engineers adjust polymer types, additives, fillers and processing conditions to meet requirements such as impact strength, flame retardancy, heat resistance or appearance. Each experiment takes time and resources.

Materials informatics applies data science to this process. Historical formulation and test data can be used to build models that predict properties of new formulations. Bayesian optimisation and active learning help decide which experiments to run next, reducing the number of trials needed. For data scientists, this is an attractive field: it combines chemistry and physics with modern machine learning, and successful models directly shorten development cycles.

The main challenge is data. Historical laboratory data is often stored in different formats, with incomplete metadata about processing conditions or test methods. Much of the early work in materials informatics projects consists of structuring and harmonising data so that it can be used reliably.

## The Circular Plastics Challenge

The plastics industry is under strong pressure to reduce its environmental impact. Policies in the EU aim to increase recycling, introduce recycled content requirements and reduce plastic waste. Producers are investing in mechanically and chemically recycled feedstocks, bio-based raw materials and products designed for recyclability.

Recycled feedstocks introduce variability. A virgin raw material has consistent properties; recycled material depends on the waste stream it came from. Process control and quality assurance must adapt to this variability while still meeting demanding customer specifications, particularly for engineering plastics used in safety-relevant applications. Data-driven approaches — characterising incoming material, adjusting process settings and predicting final product properties — are key to making circular materials work at industrial scale.

## Regulation That Shapes the Work

**REACH Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006.** Chemical substances must be registered, evaluated and, where necessary, restricted. REACH creates documentation and data requirements for substances used in plastics, including additives.

**Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (EU) 2025/40.** The new EU regulation introduces requirements for recyclability, recycled content and reduction of packaging waste, phased in over the coming years. Although engineering plastics are used in many applications beyond packaging, the regulation reflects the broader policy direction towards circular plastics.

**Major Accidents Risks Decree 2015 (Brzo 2015).** Sites handling hazardous substances above certain thresholds must implement safety management systems and are subject to joint inspections.

**EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS).** Chemical installations must monitor, report and verify emissions, making energy and emissions data financially important.

**Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD).** Large companies must report on climate impact and resource use, increasing demand for reliable environmental data.

**EU Artificial Intelligence Act.** Most materials development and process optimisation applications are not high-risk, but AI systems used as safety components in industrial operations require careful assessment.

## A Concrete Scenario: Predicting Off-Spec Material Before It Is Made

A production line occasionally produces batches that do not meet customer specifications for a key mechanical property. The property is only measured in the laboratory hours after production, so off-spec material is discovered late and must be reprocessed or sold at a lower value.

A data scientist builds a soft sensor that predicts the property in real time from process variables such as temperatures, pressures, feed rates and raw material batch information. The model identifies that deviations are often linked to specific raw material lots combined with certain temperature profiles. Operators receive an early warning and can adjust settings before large quantities of off-spec material are produced. The model is maintained carefully, because changes in raw materials — including the introduction of recycled feedstock — can shift the relationships it has learned. The project demonstrates how process data and laboratory data together create value that neither provides alone.

## A Common Misconception About Chemical Industry Data Work

Some data professionals expect chemical companies to have large, clean datasets ready for machine learning. In reality, data is often scattered across process historians, laboratory systems, maintenance systems and spreadsheets, collected over decades with changing conventions. The first year of a data role is frequently spent building reliable data connections and understanding what the data means. Those who are patient and curious about chemistry and processes find that the payoff is significant: once the foundation is in place, the opportunities for optimisation are substantial.

## Questions Worth Asking Before Joining

- **Is the role focused on production, research and development, or customer applications?** Each has different data and stakeholders.
- **How accessible are process and laboratory data today?** Data integration may be the first major task.
- **How is the site involved in circular or low-carbon materials development?** Transition projects often create new data work.
- **How are models deployed and maintained in operations?** Maintenance practices indicate the maturity of the data organisation.
- **How much collaboration is there with global R&D and data teams?** Many sites belong to international groups with central expertise.
