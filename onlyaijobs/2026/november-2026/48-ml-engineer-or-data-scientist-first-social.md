💡 One company hires a data scientist and, six months later, has promising models in notebooks that never reach production. Another hires a strong ML engineer who builds a platform for use cases nobody validated. Both hired talented people for the wrong bottleneck.

🧭 Match the role to the obstacle. Scattered, unreliable data points to a data or analytics engineer. No concrete problems points to a data scientist or analyst with business skills. Models that never ship point to an ML engineer. Building features on foundation models points to an AI engineer.

📜 Regulation shifts the balance too: high-risk AI under the AI Act requires documentation, logging, robustness and monitoring — engineering and governance capacity, not only modelling.

✅ Avoid unicorn vacancies. Expecting one person to cover statistics, deep learning, data engineering, deployment and stakeholder management produces fragile systems.
