In most European countries, part-time work is an exception. In the Netherlands, it is ordinary. European statistics consistently show that the Netherlands has one of the highest shares of part-time employment in the EU, and Statistics Netherlands data show that part-time work is common among both women and, increasingly, men. Four-day working weeks are normal in many sectors. Yet vacancies for data scientists and ML engineers often state "40 hours" or "36–40 hours" without discussion, and many candidates are unsure whether asking for 32 hours will harm their chances. The reality is more nuanced — and more favourable — than many assume.

## The Dutch Legal Framework for Working Hours

**Flexible Working Act (Wet flexibel werken).** Employees of employers with at least ten employees can request changes to their working hours, working times or workplace after a minimum period of employment. For requests to reduce or increase working hours, the employer may refuse only on the basis of substantial business or service interests. For requests about workplace, such as working from home, the employer must consider the request but has more freedom to refuse, provided it explains its reasons.

**Equal Treatment (Working Hours) Act (Wet onderscheid arbeidsduur).** Employers may not make a distinction between employees on the basis of differences in working hours unless objectively justified. Part-time employees are entitled to proportionally equal pay, benefits and treatment.

**Work and Care Act (Wet arbeid en zorg).** Regulates leave for care responsibilities, including maternity, birth and parental leave. Since August 2022, part of parental leave is partially paid, which has encouraged more parents to take leave in the first year of a child's life.

**Working Hours Act (Arbeidstijdenwet).** Sets limits on working hours and rest periods, protecting employees in both full-time and part-time arrangements.

**Collective labour agreements.** Many collective agreements define standard working weeks, such as 36 or 38 hours, and include arrangements for part-time work, seniority schemes and leave.

**Work Where You Want bill.** A proposal to strengthen employees' right to work from home, known as the "Work Where You Want" bill, was rejected by the Senate in 2023. Rights regarding workplace therefore remain governed mainly by the Flexible Working Act and individual agreements.

## How Common Is Part-Time Work in AI and Data Roles?

Part-time work in technical roles is less common than in the Dutch labour market as a whole, partly because technology companies were historically influenced by international full-time norms and partly because of perceptions about collaboration. However, part-time arrangements in AI and data roles are increasingly common for several reasons:

- Competition for talent has made employers more flexible.
- Many experienced professionals combine work with care responsibilities.
- Public sector, healthcare, education and research employers have long-standing part-time cultures.
- Four-day working weeks, sometimes as 4×9 hours, are popular among engineers.
- Some professionals combine part-time employment with freelance work, study or side projects.

Differences between employers are large. Government organisations, universities and hospitals typically accept 32-hour contracts readily. Scale-ups and international technology companies are more varied. Consultancies may accept part-time work but must align it with client expectations.

## How Part-Time Work Functions in AI Teams

| Aspect | What works well | What needs attention |
|---|---|---|
| Deep work | Modelling, analysis and engineering tasks can be planned in focused blocks | Context switching across fewer days can reduce focus time |
| Team rituals | Stand-ups and planning can be scheduled on shared working days | Missing key meetings can reduce influence on decisions |
| On-call and production support | Rotations can be adjusted proportionally | Critical systems require clear coverage agreements |
| Stakeholder availability | Clear communication of working days sets expectations | Stakeholders may expect immediate responses |
| Project deadlines | Realistic planning based on actual capacity | Pressure to compress full-time work into fewer days |
| Career progression | Performance judged on results rather than hours | Risk of being overlooked for visible projects |

The last point is important. Part-time employees may be protected legally against unequal treatment, but informal bias can still occur. Being deliberate about visibility, communicating results and discussing career goals explicitly helps prevent part-time status from slowing progression.

## 32 Hours Versus 4×9 Hours

Two common four-day arrangements work differently.

**A 32-hour contract** means working four days of eight hours with a proportionally lower salary, fewer holiday hours in absolute terms and proportionally lower pension accrual. The advantage is a genuine reduction in working time.

**A 4×9 arrangement** means working 36 hours over four days of nine hours, keeping a 36-hour salary where the collective agreement or employer defines 36 hours as full-time. The advantage is a free day without reducing pay; the disadvantage is longer working days, which not everyone finds sustainable, especially in cognitively demanding work.

Some organisations also experiment with 32-hour weeks at full pay as a benefit. These arrangements remain relatively rare and usually depend on productivity expectations.

## Financial Consequences to Consider

Reducing working hours affects more than monthly salary. Pension accrual decreases proportionally, which can have a significant long-term effect. Holiday allowance, year-end bonuses and some benefits are calculated proportionally. Social security benefits in case of unemployment or disability are based on earned income. On the other hand, lower gross income can lead to changes in tax brackets and income-dependent allowances, so the net effect may be smaller than the gross reduction. Before changing hours, calculate the net and long-term effects carefully.

## How to Negotiate Part-Time Hours for an AI Role

**During the application process.** If part-time work is essential, mention it when there is genuine mutual interest — for example, after a first positive conversation — rather than in the first sentence of an application. Explain how you organise your work and show examples of delivering results.

**Frame it around outcomes.** Emphasise what you will deliver, how you will coordinate with the team and how you handle availability for urgent issues.

**Propose a concrete arrangement.** Specify which days you work, how you handle on-call responsibilities and how stakeholders can reach you.

**Consider a trial period.** For internal requests, suggest evaluating the arrangement after a few months.

**Use the law as background, not as a threat.** The Flexible Working Act protects employees after a period of employment, but a collaborative conversation usually leads to better outcomes than formal claims.

## A Concrete Scenario: A Machine Learning Engineer Moving to 32 Hours

A machine learning engineer at an insurer becomes a parent and requests to reduce working hours from 40 to 32. The manager worries about coverage for production models and participation in sprint planning.

Together they agree on a working pattern: the engineer works Monday to Thursday, sprint planning is scheduled on Mondays, and the on-call rotation is adjusted proportionally, with the engineer not on call on Fridays. Stakeholders are informed through calendars and team channels. After three months, the team finds that output has remained strong, partly because the engineer protects focus time more deliberately. The main adjustment needed is documentation: because the engineer is absent one day per week, handovers improve — which benefits the whole team.

## How Teams Make Part-Time Work Succeed

Teams that work well with part-time colleagues tend to share several practices. They schedule important meetings on days when everyone is present. They document decisions in writing rather than relying on hallway conversations. They plan sprints based on actual capacity rather than nominal headcount. They make ownership explicit, so it is clear who handles questions about a model or pipeline on a given day. And they avoid treating the day off as an informal working day, which would undermine the purpose of the arrangement.

These practices are not only useful for part-time employees. They also help teams with colleagues in different locations, with hybrid schedules or with frequent leave. In many cases, adapting to part-time colleagues makes the entire team more resilient.

## Part-Time Work and Career Progression

A frequent concern is whether working fewer hours slows career progression. Legally, employers may not treat part-time employees less favourably without objective justification. In practice, progression depends on visibility, responsibility and the organisation's culture. Some organisations promote part-time professionals to senior and lead roles without hesitation; others implicitly expect leaders to work full-time.

Professionals can reduce the risk by discussing career goals explicitly with their manager, taking ownership of visible projects and ensuring that their results are communicated clearly. Asking during the application process whether team leads or senior specialists work part-time gives useful insight into the organisation's culture.

For people combining work with study or care responsibilities, part-time work can also enable development that would otherwise be impossible — such as a part-time master's programme or a certification track — which can accelerate progression in the longer term.

## A Common Misconception About Part-Time Tech Work

The most common misconception is that part-time work is incompatible with serious technical roles. In practice, many experienced data scientists and engineers in the Netherlands work 32 or 36 hours and deliver excellent results. A second misconception is that asking about part-time options always harms an application. Employers who reject part-time work outright may simply not be a good fit, and many employers — particularly in the public sector, healthcare and research — consider flexible hours an important part of their employer offer.

## What Employers Should State in Vacancies

Candidates would benefit from clearer vacancies. Employers can state whether part-time work is possible, which minimum hours they require, how on-call duties are organised and whether 4×9 arrangements are available. As the EU Pay Transparency Directive leads to more information about pay ranges in vacancies, clearly stating working hours becomes equally important for fair comparison of offers.

## Questions to Ask Employers

- **Is part-time work possible for this role, and what is the minimum number of hours?**
- **How many people in the team work part-time?** Existing examples indicate a supportive culture.
- **How are on-call duties and deadlines divided among part-time and full-time employees?**
- **Are 4×9 arrangements available under the collective agreement or company policy?**
- **How is performance evaluated?** Results-based evaluation supports fair treatment of part-time employees.

## Key Takeaways

- Part-time work is common in the Netherlands, and four-day weeks are increasingly accepted in AI and data roles.
- The Flexible Working Act, the Equal Treatment (Working Hours) Act, the Work and Care Act, the Working Hours Act and collective agreements shape rights around working hours.
- Part-time AI work functions well with clear working days, adjusted on-call rotations, realistic planning and good documentation.
- 32-hour and 4×9 arrangements differ in pay, pension accrual and daily workload; calculate net and long-term effects.
- Public sector, healthcare and research employers are typically most flexible, while practices at scale-ups and consultancies vary.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: engineer considering a four-day week) Can I work 32 hours as a data scientist in the Netherlands?
Often yes — part-time arrangements are increasingly common, especially in public sector, healthcare and research, though practices vary by employer.

### (Scenario: employee wanting to reduce hours) Can my employer refuse a request to work fewer hours?
Under the Flexible Working Act, employers with at least ten employees may refuse a request to change working hours only on substantial business or service grounds.

### (Scenario: candidate worried about unequal treatment) Are part-time employees entitled to the same benefits?
Yes, proportionally — the Equal Treatment (Working Hours) Act prohibits unjustified distinctions based on working hours.

### (Scenario: candidate comparing arrangements) What is the difference between 32 hours and 4×9?
32 hours reduces working time and pay proportionally; 4×9 keeps 36 hours of pay over four longer days.
