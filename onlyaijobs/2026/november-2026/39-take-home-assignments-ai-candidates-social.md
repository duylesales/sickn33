💡 Take-home assignments can be the fairest way to assess practical skills — or an unpaid weekend project that tests free time rather than ability.

📋 Reasonable: clear scope and criteria, a few hours of work, public or synthetic data, a stated policy on AI coding assistants, and a follow-up conversation about your submission. Warning signs: multi-day scope, the company's real business problem, rights transfers before you start, and no feedback afterwards.

📌 What stands out is rarely complexity. Restate the question, check data quality and leakage, build a baseline, validate honestly, keep the code readable and write a one-paragraph summary a non-technical stakeholder could follow.

📜 You generally keep copyright in your submission — you are not an employee — and candidate data falls under GDPR retention rules.

✅ Declining or proposing an alternative is professional, not disqualifying.
