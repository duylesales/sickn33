For years, Dutch vacancies for data scientists and ML engineers often said "competitive salary" and left the rest to negotiation. That practice is coming to an end. The **EU Pay Transparency Directive (EU) 2023/970** requires employers to give candidates information about initial pay or its range before the job interview, prohibits asking candidates about their pay history and requires pay structures based on objective, gender-neutral criteria. Member states had to transpose the directive by June 2026, and the Netherlands has been preparing implementing legislation; employers should check the current Dutch timeline. For AI roles — where market premiums, hybrid titles and negotiation have historically created large pay differences — the directive forces decisions many employers have postponed.

This article provides general information, not legal advice.

## What the Directive Requires in Recruitment

**Pay information before the interview.** Candidates must receive information about the initial pay or its range, based on objective, gender-neutral criteria, for the position concerned. This can be included in the vacancy or provided before the interview.

**Relevant collective agreement.** Where applicable, employers must indicate the relevant provisions of the collective agreement applied in relation to the position.

**No pay history questions.** Employers may not ask candidates about their pay history in current or previous employment.

**Gender-neutral vacancies and titles.** Job vacancy notices and job titles must be gender-neutral, and recruitment processes must be non-discriminatory.

## What It Requires Beyond Recruitment

**Transparency on pay setting and progression.** Employers must make easily accessible to employees the criteria used to determine pay, pay levels and pay progression. Member states may exempt employers with fewer than 50 employees from the obligation regarding pay progression criteria.

**Right to information.** Employees have the right to request information on their individual pay level and the average pay levels, broken down by sex, for categories of workers doing the same work or work of equal value.

**Gender pay gap reporting.** Employers with at least 100 employees will have to report on the gender pay gap, with frequency depending on size: annually for employers with 250 or more employees, and every three years for smaller groups within scope, phased in over several years.

**Joint pay assessment.** If reporting shows a gender pay gap of at least 5% in any category of workers that is not justified by objective, gender-neutral criteria and not remedied within six months, employers must conduct a joint pay assessment with workers' representatives.

**Enforcement.** The directive strengthens remedies, including compensation for workers, and shifts the burden of proof to employers in certain cases.

In the Netherlands, equal pay for men and women is already required under **Article 7:646 of the Dutch Civil Code** and the **Equal Treatment of Men and Women Act**, and the **Netherlands Institute for Human Rights** can assess complaints.

## Why Salary Setting for AI Roles Is Particularly Difficult

**Market premiums.** Demand for experienced ML engineers and data scientists has pushed salaries above those of comparable internal roles. Employers have often solved this through individual negotiation, creating differences that are hard to justify objectively.

**Hybrid and inconsistent titles.** "Data scientist" can mean an analyst role in one team and an ML engineering role in another. Without clear job definitions, comparing pay across roles is difficult.

**Rapidly changing roles.** New roles such as ML platform engineer, AI product manager or AI governance specialist do not fit easily into existing job classification systems.

**Negotiation differences.** Candidates who negotiate more assertively may receive higher starting salaries for the same work — a pattern that can contribute to unexplained pay gaps.

## Step 1: Define Roles and Levels Clearly

Before publishing ranges, define what each role involves and how levels differ. For AI and data roles, a practical framework describes levels by scope, autonomy, complexity and impact rather than by years of experience alone.

| Level | Typical scope | Example expectations |
|---|---|---|
| Junior | Well-defined tasks with guidance | Builds and evaluates models on clear problems with support |
| Medior | Independent delivery of projects | Owns models end-to-end within a team |
| Senior | Complex projects and technical leadership | Designs solutions, mentors others, influences priorities |
| Lead or principal | Strategic scope across teams | Sets technical direction and standards |

Use the same level framework across data science, ML engineering, data engineering and analytics roles so that work of equal value can be compared.

## Step 2: Use Objective, Gender-Neutral Job Evaluation

The directive requires pay structures that allow assessment of whether workers are in a comparable situation regarding work of equal value, based on objective criteria such as skills, effort, responsibility and working conditions.

Many Dutch organisations already use job evaluation methods, such as **ORBA** or the **Hay method**, or classification systems in collective agreements. Review whether these methods adequately capture AI and data roles, for example the complexity of technical judgement, responsibility for model risk and impact on decisions. Where classification systems are outdated, updating them is better than creating ad hoc exceptions for AI roles.

## Step 3: Set Ranges That Are Honest and Usable

**Base ranges on the job, not the candidate.** The range should reflect the value of the position and the organisation's pay structure.

**Choose a realistic width.** A range that is too wide provides little information and may be seen as avoiding transparency; a range that is too narrow leaves no room for justified differences in experience.

**Define criteria for positioning within the range.** Document how factors such as relevant experience, specific expertise and performance affect placement, and apply them consistently.

**Account for market premiums transparently.** If AI roles require a market premium, apply it as a documented, objective criterion across comparable roles rather than through individual exceptions.

**Include total compensation clearly.** State whether the range is gross annual or monthly, whether holiday allowance is included and which other elements apply, such as bonuses, pension contributions or a thirteenth month.

## Step 4: Rethink Negotiation

Because candidates will know the range in advance and employers cannot ask about pay history, negotiation shifts. Instead of anchoring on a candidate's previous salary, employers must justify positioning within the range using the documented criteria. This can reduce negotiation time and help avoid unexplained differences — but only if hiring managers are trained to apply criteria consistently and document their decisions.

## Step 5: Involve the Works Council and Communicate Internally

Changes to pay structures and job evaluation systems can require involvement of the works council under the **Works Councils Act**, depending on the nature of the changes. Publishing ranges externally also affects current employees, who will compare their pay with published ranges. Prepare for questions by reviewing internal pay equity before publishing vacancies, and communicate clearly how existing employees' pay relates to the new structure.

## How to Present the Range in the Vacancy Text

A salary range is most useful when candidates can interpret it without guessing. A clear presentation includes:

- **The range itself,** with a clear unit, such as gross per month based on full-time hours, or gross per year.
- **What is included,** such as whether holiday allowance, a thirteenth month or fixed allowances are part of the amount.
- **The basis for positioning,** for example "placement within the range depends on relevant experience and demonstrated expertise in production machine learning".
- **Working hours,** because a range for 40 hours differs from a range for 36 hours.
- **Other key elements,** such as pension contributions, bonus schemes, mobility budget or training budget.
- **The applicable collective agreement,** if relevant, including the salary scale.

For example: "Salary: €X–€Y gross per month based on 38 hours, excluding 8% holiday allowance. Placement in the range depends on relevant experience in forecasting and model deployment. Pension according to the collective agreement, training budget and hybrid working."

## Bonuses, Equity and Other Variable Pay

Pay under the directive includes not only base salary but also other remuneration, whether in cash or in kind, received directly or indirectly by workers. That means bonuses, allowances, equity and benefits must also be based on objective, gender-neutral criteria. Startups and scale-ups that offer share options to AI engineers should document how grants are determined. Employers should also examine whether variable pay schemes create unexplained differences between groups, for example because assessments of performance are applied inconsistently.

## Preparing for Employee Information Requests

Once employees can request information on average pay levels by sex for workers doing the same work or work of equal value, employers need reliable data about job categories and pay. For AI and data teams, where titles and responsibilities have often evolved informally, this requires clean job classification. Preparing now — defining categories, cleaning HR data and checking whether differences are explainable — reduces the risk of unpleasant surprises when the first requests arrive.

## A Concrete Scenario: The Range That Revealed a Problem

A mid-sized insurer prepares to publish a salary range for a senior data scientist vacancy. When HR compares the proposed range with current senior data scientists' salaries, it discovers that two employees hired during a tight market earn well above the range, while a colleague with similar responsibilities and experience earns below it. The difference cannot be explained by objective criteria.

The insurer reviews the role definitions, adjusts the range and creates a plan to correct the underpaid employee's salary over time, while documenting criteria for the higher salaries. The vacancy is published with a clear range and positioning criteria. Candidates respond positively, and the insurer avoids a future discrimination claim or reporting problem.

## Common Mistakes Employers Make

- **Publishing extremely wide ranges** that give little real information.
- **Excluding AI roles from job evaluation** because they are considered special.
- **Asking indirectly about pay history**, for example through "current package" questions in application forms.
- **Allowing managers to negotiate individually** without documented criteria.
- **Ignoring existing pay differences** before publishing ranges.
- **Using gendered or exclusionary language** in vacancy texts.

## What Candidates Will Notice

Transparent salary ranges influence who applies. Experienced AI professionals often receive many recruiter messages, and a vacancy with a clear, competitive range stands out. Candidates who might not have negotiated assertively — including many underrepresented groups in technology — are more likely to apply when pay is clear. For smaller employers competing with large technology companies, transparency about the total package, including development opportunities, flexibility and meaningful work, can offset lower base salaries.

## Key Takeaways

- The EU Pay Transparency Directive requires pay information before interviews, bans pay history questions and requires objective, gender-neutral pay structures; check the Dutch implementation timeline.
- Employers must also provide transparency on pay criteria and progression, respond to information requests and, from 100 employees, report on gender pay gaps.
- AI roles are challenging because of market premiums, inconsistent titles and negotiation-driven differences.
- Define roles and levels clearly, use objective job evaluation, set usable ranges and document positioning criteria.
- Review internal pay equity and involve the works council before publishing ranges externally.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: employer preparing a vacancy) Do I have to publish a salary range in AI vacancies?
Under the directive, candidates must receive information about initial pay or its range before the interview; including it in the vacancy is the simplest way to comply.

### (Scenario: recruiter used to asking about current salary) Can I still ask candidates about their current salary?
No — the directive prohibits asking candidates about their pay history in current or previous employment.

### (Scenario: employer with market premiums for AI roles) Can AI roles be paid more than other roles?
Yes, if differences are based on objective, gender-neutral criteria applied consistently, such as skills, responsibility and documented market factors.

### (Scenario: small employer) Does the directive apply to small companies?
The recruitment transparency obligations apply to employers generally; some obligations, such as gender pay gap reporting, depend on company size.

### (Scenario: employer discovering pay differences) What should I do if current employees earn outside the new range?
Review whether differences are objectively justified, document criteria and plan corrections where differences cannot be explained.
