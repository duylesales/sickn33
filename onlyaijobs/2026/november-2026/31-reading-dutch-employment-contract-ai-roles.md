After weeks of interviews, a technical assessment and a salary negotiation, the offer arrives — and with it, a contract of several pages, sometimes in Dutch, sometimes in English, often referring to a collective agreement or staff handbook you have not read. Many AI and data professionals sign quickly, relieved that the process is over. That is understandable, but it can be costly. A few clauses in a Dutch employment contract can significantly affect your freedom to change jobs, work on side projects, publish open-source code or leave without repaying training costs. This guide explains the clauses that matter most, so you know what to check and what to ask before signing.

This article provides general information, not legal advice. For your specific situation, consult an employment lawyer, your union or the free legal information service **Juridisch Loket**.

## Contract Type: Fixed-Term or Permanent

Dutch employment contracts are either for a fixed term (bepaalde tijd) or for an indefinite period (onbepaalde tijd).

**The chain rule.** Under Dutch law, an employer can offer a maximum of three consecutive fixed-term contracts within a maximum period of three years. If this limit is exceeded, the contract automatically becomes permanent. Contracts are considered consecutive if the interval between them is six months or less. Collective agreements can deviate from these rules in some situations.

**Why it matters.** A fixed-term contract ends automatically at the end of its term, unless extended. For professionals relocating to the Netherlands, contract type can also affect mortgage applications and housing.

**What to check.** Ask whether the employer intends to offer a permanent contract after the first term and under what conditions.

## Probation Period (Proeftijd)

A probation period allows both parties to end the contract immediately without notice. Dutch law limits its length:

| Contract type | Maximum probation period |
|---|---|
| Fixed-term contract of six months or less | No probation period allowed |
| Fixed-term contract longer than six months but shorter than two years | One month |
| Fixed-term contract of two years or longer, or permanent contract | Two months |

A probation period must be agreed in writing and must be the same for both parties. A longer probation period than allowed by law is invalid. Collective agreements can sometimes provide for different arrangements within legal limits.

## Notice Periods

If no notice period is specified, the statutory notice period for an employee is one month. For employers, the statutory notice period depends on the duration of employment and increases with years of service. Contracts can agree on different notice periods within legal limits, provided that the employer's notice period is at least twice the employee's if the employee's period is extended.

For AI professionals considering future moves, a long notice period can make it harder to accept a new offer quickly. Check what is agreed and whether the new employer is willing to wait.

## Non-Compete Clauses (Concurrentiebeding)

A non-compete clause restricts working for competitors or starting a competing business for a certain period after the contract ends. Under Dutch law:

- It must be agreed in writing with an adult employee.
- In a **fixed-term contract**, it is only valid if the employer explains in writing that the clause is necessary due to substantial business or service interests.
- In a **permanent contract**, the clause does not need such justification at the time of signing, but a court can still set it aside or limit it if the employee is unreasonably disadvantaged compared with the employer's interest.
- The clause may lose its effect if the employee's role changes substantially, making it considerably more burdensome.

**Why it matters for AI professionals.** Broad clauses that prohibit working for any company in "data" or "AI" for a year can seriously limit your options. Clauses in consultancy contracts sometimes also restrict working in specific sectors.

**What to check.** Scope (which companies or activities), geographic area, duration and whether there is compensation. Ask whether the clause can be narrowed to direct competitors.

## Client Non-Solicitation Clauses (Relatiebeding)

A non-solicitation clause does not prohibit working in the same field but prohibits working for or approaching the employer's clients or relations. The same formal requirements as for non-compete clauses generally apply. For consultants and seconded professionals, this clause is particularly relevant: it may prevent accepting a direct job offer from a client where you have worked.

## Intellectual Property Clauses

In AI roles, intellectual property clauses deserve close attention.

**Copyright.** Under the Dutch Copyright Act, the employer is considered the maker of works created by an employee in the performance of their duties, including software, unless otherwise agreed.

**Patents.** Under the Dutch Patents Act 1995, an employer is entitled to an invention made by an employee if the nature of the employee's position requires using special knowledge to make such inventions. The employee may be entitled to fair remuneration in some cases.

**Broad contractual clauses.** Many contracts go further, claiming rights to anything the employee creates during employment, even outside working hours. Such clauses may be broader than necessary.

**What to check.** Whether the clause covers work outside working hours and unrelated to the employer's business, and whether existing projects you bring into the job are excluded. If you maintain open-source projects or personal tools, list them explicitly as prior work.

## Side Activities (Nevenwerkzaamheden)

Since the implementation of the EU directive on transparent and predictable working conditions in 2022, Dutch law limits clauses that prohibit employees from working for others outside working hours. An employer may only prohibit or restrict side activities if there is an objective justification, such as health and safety, protection of confidential business information, preventing conflicts of interest or compliance with other legal rules.

For AI professionals who teach, freelance, consult or build startups alongside their job, this is important. A general ban on side work is not automatically valid, but restrictions related to conflicts of interest or confidential information may be.

## Training Costs (Studiekostenbeding)

Employers sometimes require repayment of training costs if an employee leaves within a certain period. Since August 2022, training that an employer is legally or contractually obliged to provide — for example, training required under a collective agreement or law to perform the job — must be free of charge, count as working time where possible and may not be subject to a repayment clause. For other training, repayment clauses remain possible if clearly agreed.

**What to check.** Which training is covered, how long the repayment obligation lasts and whether the amount decreases over time. Expensive certifications or conference programmes may come with repayment conditions.

## Confidentiality Clauses

Confidentiality clauses protect the employer's business information. In AI roles, they may cover models, training data, customer data and research results. Reasonable confidentiality obligations are standard, but check whether they prevent you from describing your work in general terms on your CV or in future interviews. Trade secrets are also protected under Dutch law implementing the EU Trade Secrets Directive.

## Salary, Holiday Allowance and Leave

**Holiday allowance.** Employees are generally entitled to holiday allowance of at least 8% of gross annual salary, often paid in May or June. Some offers quote salaries including holiday allowance; check whether the stated amount is inclusive or exclusive.

**Statutory leave.** Employees are entitled to at least four times their weekly working hours in paid holiday per year. Many employers and collective agreements provide more.

**Salary continuation during illness.** Employers generally continue to pay at least 70% of salary for up to 104 weeks of illness, and many collective agreements provide higher percentages.

**Collective agreements.** If a collective agreement applies, it may determine salary scales, working hours, leave and other conditions. Ask for the relevant agreement or staff handbook before signing.

## Pension

Most Dutch employers offer a pension scheme, and in many sectors participation is mandatory. The **Future Pensions Act** is changing the pension system, with funds moving to new arrangements during a transition period. When comparing offers, look at the employer's contribution, your own contribution and the type of scheme. A higher salary with a weaker pension can be less valuable than it appears.

## Special Points for International Professionals

**30% ruling.** Eligible employees recruited from abroad may benefit from a tax facility that makes part of their salary tax-free. The rules and percentages have changed in recent years; check the current conditions with the Dutch Tax Administration and ask whether the employer will apply for it.

**Residence permits.** For highly skilled migrants, the contract must meet salary thresholds and the employer must be a recognised sponsor. Contract changes can affect residence status.

**Contract language.** Contracts may be in English, but collective agreements and staff handbooks are often in Dutch. Ask for translations of key documents if needed.

## Collective Agreements and Staff Handbooks: The Documents Behind the Contract

A Dutch employment contract is often only a few pages long because many conditions are regulated elsewhere. Two types of documents deserve attention.

**Collective labour agreements (cao).** If your employer is bound by a sector or company collective agreement, it may determine salary scales, annual salary increases, working hours, overtime compensation, leave, pension, training rights and illness arrangements. Some sector agreements are declared generally binding by the government, meaning they apply to all employers in the sector. Your contract should state whether a collective agreement applies; if it does, read the relevant chapters before signing.

**Staff handbooks and policies.** Many employers have a staff handbook or set of policies covering topics such as remote work, expenses, mobility budgets, IT use, confidentiality and use of AI tools. Contracts often declare these policies applicable. Because employers can sometimes change policies more easily than contracts, it is worth checking which conditions are in the contract itself and which are in policies.

For AI professionals, policies on the use of external AI tools, open-source contributions and publishing research are increasingly common. Ask for these documents early: they can reveal important restrictions — or useful benefits — that the contract does not mention.

## A Concrete Scenario: The Clause That Blocked a Move

A data scientist working for a consultancy receives an offer from a client where they have been seconded for two years. The client values their domain knowledge and wants to hire them directly. The consultancy points to a client non-solicitation clause prohibiting direct employment with clients for twelve months after leaving.

The data scientist had signed without noticing the clause. After consulting an employment lawyer, they learn that the clause is formally valid, but its scope and the circumstances may be negotiable. Eventually, the client, the consultancy and the employee agree on a buy-out arrangement. The move happens, but later and at a cost. Reading the contract carefully at the start would have allowed the data scientist to negotiate a narrower clause.

## A Common Misconception

Many professionals assume that standard contract clauses are non-negotiable, especially at large organisations. In practice, clauses such as non-compete scope, IP clauses covering personal projects, training repayment conditions and start dates are often negotiable, particularly for experienced candidates. The key is asking before signing, when your negotiating position is strongest.

## A Checklist Before Signing

- **Contract type and duration:** fixed-term or permanent, and what happens after the first term?
- **Probation period:** within legal limits?
- **Notice period:** acceptable for future moves?
- **Non-compete and non-solicitation:** scope, duration, justification and compensation?
- **Intellectual property:** limited to work related to your job, with prior projects excluded?
- **Side activities:** any restrictions, and are they justified?
- **Training repayment:** which training, how long, how much?
- **Salary details:** gross annual salary, holiday allowance included or not, bonus conditions?
- **Pension and benefits:** employer contribution, scheme type, collective agreement?
- **International aspects:** 30% ruling, residence permit conditions, language of documents?

## Key Takeaways

- Dutch law sets clear rules on fixed-term contracts, probation periods and notice periods; check that contract terms comply.
- Non-compete and client non-solicitation clauses can significantly limit future moves; narrow them before signing where possible.
- Intellectual property clauses should not cover unrelated personal projects; list prior work explicitly.
- Side activity restrictions and training repayment clauses are legally limited, but still need careful reading.
- Holiday allowance, pension, collective agreements and international tax arrangements affect the real value of an offer.

## Where to Start

Before signing your next contract, go through the checklist above, ask questions about unclear clauses and seek advice when a clause could limit your career.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: candidate receiving a first Dutch contract) What is the maximum probation period in a Dutch employment contract?
One month for fixed-term contracts longer than six months but shorter than two years, and two months for contracts of two years or longer or permanent contracts; none for contracts of six months or less.

### (Scenario: candidate with a fixed-term contract) Is a non-compete clause in a fixed-term contract valid?
Only if the employer explains in writing that it is necessary due to substantial business or service interests.

### (Scenario: engineer with open-source projects) Can my employer claim ownership of my side projects?
Copyright in work created in performing your duties generally belongs to the employer; broad clauses covering unrelated projects should be checked and prior work listed explicitly.

### (Scenario: candidate who freelances on the side) Can my employer ban side jobs?
Only with objective justification, such as preventing conflicts of interest or protecting confidential information.

### (Scenario: candidate offered expensive training) Do I have to repay training costs if I leave?
Not for training the employer is legally or contractually required to provide; for other training, only if a clear repayment clause was agreed.
