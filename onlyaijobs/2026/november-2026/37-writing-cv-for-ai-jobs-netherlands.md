A hiring manager for a junior data scientist position may receive dozens or even hundreds of applications. Many CVs list the same courses, the same libraries and the same capstone projects. Most are read for less than a minute in the first round — and some are screened by software before a person sees them at all. In that context, the CV is not a complete record of everything you have done. It is a short argument for why you deserve a conversation. For AI and data roles in the Netherlands, a few conventions and choices make that argument much stronger.

## Dutch CV Conventions in Brief

**Length.** One to two pages is standard. Recent graduates usually fit on one page; experienced professionals may use two.

**Order.** Reverse chronological order — most recent experience and education first — is the norm.

**Photo.** Photos are common on Dutch CVs, but not required. Many international companies prefer CVs without photos to reduce bias. If in doubt, leave it out.

**Personal details.** Name, email, phone number, city of residence and links to your professional profile and portfolio are sufficient. Date of birth, marital status, nationality and full address are not necessary. Never include your citizen service number (BSN).

**Language.** Write the CV in the language of the vacancy. For English-language vacancies, an English CV is expected; for Dutch vacancies, a Dutch CV is usually preferred.

**Format.** A clean, simple layout works best. Complex multi-column designs with graphics can cause problems for screening software.

The **Europass CV** format from the EU is widely known, but many Dutch employers find it long and generic. A concise, tailored CV usually performs better for AI and data roles.

## The Structure That Works for AI and Data Roles

1. **Header:** name, contact details, city, links to professional profile, code repository or portfolio.
2. **Profile summary:** two or three sentences describing your focus and strongest assets.
3. **Experience:** jobs, internships, research assistant roles and relevant part-time work.
4. **Projects:** two to four strong projects with concrete results.
5. **Education:** degrees, thesis topic and relevant courses.
6. **Skills:** grouped technical skills and languages.
7. **Optional:** publications, awards, volunteering, community activities.

For recent graduates without much work experience, place projects above experience or combine them in a section called "Experience and Projects".

## Writing Experience and Projects That Stand Out

The most common weakness in data CVs is describing tasks instead of results. Compare:

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Worked on machine learning models | Built a gradient boosting model to predict delivery delays, reducing manual planning checks in a pilot region |
| Responsible for dashboards | Redesigned sales dashboards used weekly by regional managers, replacing three inconsistent reports |
| Used Python, SQL and scikit-learn | Cleaned and joined five data sources in Python and SQL, discovering a data quality issue that changed forecast inputs |
| Thesis on NLP | Master's thesis: evaluated Dutch-language models for classifying customer complaints, building a new labelled test set |

A useful structure for each bullet is: action, method, context and result. Where results cannot be quantified — which is common — describe the decision or process your work influenced.

**Projects.** For each project, include one line on the question, one on your approach and tools, and one on the result or insight. Link to a repository with a clear README. Hiring managers frequently click on one project; make sure it is your best one.

## Education in the Dutch Context

**Dutch higher education terms.** The Netherlands distinguishes research universities (WO), universities of applied sciences (HBO) and vocational education (MBO). Employers understand these terms, but international candidates should describe degrees clearly, for example "MSc Data Science, research university".

**Thesis.** For recent graduates, the thesis is often the strongest evidence of independent work. Mention the topic, method and result.

**Grades.** Dutch grades use a 1–10 scale, where grades above 8 are considered very good. Mention an average only if it is strong; some employers ask for a grade list separately.

**Foreign diplomas.** International candidates can obtain an evaluation of their diploma's Dutch equivalent through **Nuffic**'s International Credential Evaluation (IDW). This is not always necessary for private employers but can help for public sector roles or when a degree is less familiar.

## Skills and Languages

**Group technical skills.** For example: programming (Python, SQL, R), machine learning (scikit-learn, PyTorch), data platforms (a cloud platform, Spark, dbt), tools (Git, Docker). Avoid long lists of every library you have used; focus on those relevant to the role.

**Avoid skill ratings.** Graphic bars or star ratings for skills are hard to interpret and rarely convince hiring managers.

**Language levels.** Use the **Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR)**, from A1 to C2, to describe language proficiency — for example, "Dutch: B1, English: C1". This is widely understood by Dutch employers.

## Applicant Tracking Systems and AI Screening

Many medium-sized and large employers use applicant tracking systems to manage applications, and some use automated tools to rank or filter candidates.

**Make your CV machine-readable.** Use standard headings, avoid text in images and use a simple layout. Export to PDF from a text-based document.

**Mirror relevant terminology.** If the vacancy asks for "time series forecasting" and you have done it, use those words rather than only a synonym. Do not stuff keywords you cannot back up.

**Know your rights.** Under the **EU AI Act**, AI systems intended for recruitment or selection — for example to filter applications or evaluate candidates — are classified as high-risk, with requirements for risk management, human oversight and transparency. The GDPR also gives you rights regarding automated decision-making and access to your data.

## Privacy and Equal Treatment

**Data minimisation.** Under the GDPR, employers should process only data necessary for recruitment. You do not need to include sensitive personal data such as health information, religion or political opinions.

**Equal treatment.** The **General Equal Treatment Act** prohibits discrimination in recruitment on grounds such as religion, belief, political opinion, race, sex, nationality, sexual orientation or civil status. Age discrimination is prohibited under separate legislation. Leaving out a photo or date of birth can help reduce the risk of bias.

**NVP Recruitment Code.** Many employers follow the NVP Recruitment Code, which includes principles on transparency, careful handling of candidate data and retention periods.

## Using AI Tools to Write Your CV

Generative AI tools can help structure a CV, improve wording or translate content. Use them carefully. Recruiters increasingly recognise generic AI-generated phrases such as "passionate data enthusiast with a proven track record". Every claim must be accurate and something you can explain in an interview. A useful approach is to write the content yourself, use AI for editing suggestions and then make sure the final version sounds like you.

## Tailoring for Different Types of Roles

| Role type | What to emphasise |
|---|---|
| Data analyst | SQL, business questions, dashboards that changed decisions, stakeholder communication |
| Data scientist | Statistical modelling, evaluation, experiments, projects with clear questions and results |
| Machine learning engineer | Production code, deployment, MLOps practices, software engineering |
| Data engineer | Pipelines, data platforms, data quality, scalability |
| Research-oriented roles | Thesis, publications, methods, research software |
| Consultancy roles | Variety of projects, communication, certifications if relevant |

Keep a master CV with all your experience and create a tailored version for each application, selecting the most relevant projects and skills.

## Make Your Profile and Repository Match Your CV

Hiring managers rarely look at a CV in isolation. Many check your professional networking profile and, for technical roles, your code repository. Inconsistencies — different job titles, dates or project descriptions — create doubt. Make sure dates and titles match, and that the projects highlighted on your CV are easy to find in your repository.

A repository does not need dozens of projects. It is better to have two or three well-organised projects with clear READMEs, sensible folder structures and instructions to reproduce results than many unfinished experiments. Pin your best projects so they appear first. Remove or archive old coursework that does not reflect your current level.

## CV Tips for International Candidates

International candidates face additional questions from Dutch employers, and a CV can answer them proactively.

**Right to work.** If you already have the right to work in the Netherlands — for example as an EU citizen, through a residence permit or an orientation year permit — mention it briefly in your profile or header. If you require sponsorship, you do not need to state it on the CV, but be ready to discuss it.

**Location.** State where you currently live and, if relevant, when you plan to move to the Netherlands.

**Education and employers.** Briefly explain unfamiliar universities or companies, for example "leading technical university in Portugal" or "logistics software company with 200 employees".

**Dutch language.** Mention your level honestly using CEFR, including if you are currently learning Dutch. Many employers appreciate the effort even when Dutch is not required.

## A Concrete Scenario: From 40 Applications to Five Interviews

A recent graduate sends the same two-page CV to forty vacancies for junior data science roles and receives two interview invitations. The CV lists ten courses, twenty libraries and three projects described in one line each, with a colourful design and skill bars.

After feedback from a career adviser, the graduate rewrites the CV: one page, a clear profile summary, the thesis described with method and result, two strong projects with links and concrete outcomes, grouped skills without ratings and Dutch language level stated using CEFR. For each application, the graduate selects the most relevant project and adjusts terminology to match the vacancy. From the next fifteen applications, the graduate receives five interview invitations. Nothing about the graduate's actual skills changed — only how clearly they were presented.

## A Common Misconception About Data CVs

The most common misconception is that listing more tools and technologies makes a CV stronger. In practice, long lists make it harder to see what you actually do well, and hiring managers often test whatever you list. A focused CV with fewer, well-evidenced skills is more convincing. A second misconception is that projects must be large or novel. A small project with a clear question, honest evaluation and good documentation is often more persuasive than an ambitious project with unclear results.

## A Final Checklist

- **One or two pages, reverse chronological, simple layout.**
- **Profile summary tailored to the role.**
- **Results-oriented bullets for experience and projects.**
- **Links to a repository or portfolio with at least one excellent project.**
- **Thesis described clearly for recent graduates.**
- **Grouped skills without ratings; languages described with CEFR levels.**
- **No unnecessary personal data; photo optional.**
- **Terminology aligned with the vacancy where accurate.**
- **Every statement is true and explainable in an interview.**

## Key Takeaways

- Dutch CVs for AI and data roles are concise, reverse chronological and tailored, with photos optional and minimal personal data.
- Describe results and context rather than tasks, and link to well-documented projects.
- Explain education in Dutch terms where helpful, use CEFR language levels and consider Nuffic evaluation for foreign diplomas when needed.
- Make your CV readable for applicant tracking systems; recruitment AI is high-risk under the AI Act and GDPR rights apply.
- Use AI tools carefully for editing, and keep every claim accurate and personal.
