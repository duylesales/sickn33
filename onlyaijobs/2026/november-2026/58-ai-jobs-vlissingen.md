Standing on the boulevard in Vlissingen, you can watch container ships, tankers and car carriers pass close to the shore on their way to and from Antwerp. The Western Scheldt is one of the busiest and most demanding shipping routes in Europe, with strong currents, shifting sandbanks and a narrow navigation channel. Behind the boulevard, a shipyard builds complex naval vessels, and maritime education institutions train the next generation of seafarers and engineers. For data professionals with an interest in the sea, Vlissingen offers a combination of engineering, navigation and port data that is unique in the Netherlands.

## Why Vlissingen Is a Maritime Town Through and Through

Vlissingen has been a maritime town for centuries — the birthplace of the famous seventeenth-century admiral Michiel de Ruyter and a historic naval and trading port. Shipbuilding has been part of the town's economy for well over a century. Its location at the mouth of the Western Scheldt means that almost every ship heading for Antwerp or Ghent passes by, and pilots, vessel traffic services and port services have long been based in the area.

The port areas of Vlissingen and Terneuzen merged with the Port of Ghent to form **North Sea Port**, a cross-border port authority. That makes Vlissingen part of a larger international port complex while maintaining its own maritime identity.

## The Named Organisations Behind Vlissingen's Maritime Market

**Damen Naval.** The naval shipbuilding division of the Damen Shipyards Group builds complex naval vessels in Vlissingen, including frigates and support ships for the Royal Netherlands Navy and foreign navies. Naval shipbuilding involves systems engineering, integration of combat and platform systems, and extensive testing — work that increasingly involves digital engineering, data and simulation.

**HZ University of Applied Sciences.** With locations in Vlissingen and Middelburg, HZ offers programmes in engineering, logistics, water management and maritime-related fields, and conducts applied research in areas such as coastal systems and sustainable technology.

**Maritime Institute De Ruyter.** Located in Vlissingen, this institute trains officers and crew for merchant shipping, offshore and inland shipping.

**North Sea Port.** The cross-border port authority manages the port areas in Vlissingen, Terneuzen and Ghent, including industrial areas and terminals in Vlissingen-Oost.

**Vessel traffic management and pilotage.** Shipping on the Scheldt is managed through close cooperation between the Netherlands and Flanders, including the **Scheldt Radar Chain**, a system of radar stations that supports vessel traffic services along the river. The **Dutch Pilotage Service (Loodswezen)** provides pilots who guide ships through the demanding waters, in cooperation with Flemish pilotage.

**Delta Climate Center.** Based in Vlissingen, this initiative brings together knowledge institutions to research climate adaptation and the energy transition in delta regions such as Zeeland.

## Naval Shipbuilding as a Data-Intensive Engineering Discipline

A modern frigate is one of the most complex objects humans build. It combines propulsion, power generation, sensors, weapons, communications, accommodation and damage control systems in a hull that must perform in extreme conditions for decades. Naval shipbuilders manage enormous amounts of engineering data: requirements, design models, supplier specifications, test results and configuration information.

Data and AI support this work in several ways. Model-based systems engineering links requirements to designs and tests. Simulation evaluates performance before construction. Production planning coordinates thousands of work packages and suppliers. And throughout a ship's life, data from onboard systems supports maintenance and upgrades. The Netherlands and Belgium have cooperated on new anti-submarine warfare frigates and mine countermeasure capabilities, which gives the Dutch naval shipbuilding sector a long pipeline of complex projects.

## Shipping on the Scheldt: Traffic Management in Demanding Waters

The Western Scheldt combines heavy traffic, large ships, strong tidal currents and a channel whose depth changes as sand moves. Deep-draught vessels bound for Antwerp may only be able to pass within certain tidal windows. Vessel traffic services monitor ships, pilots board and disembark, and planning must account for tides, weather and other traffic.

Data plays a growing role. AIS messages from ships, radar data, tidal predictions and hydrographic surveys provide a detailed picture of traffic and conditions. Predictive models can estimate arrival times, identify potential encounter situations and optimise pilot scheduling. Because decisions affect the safety of large ships and the efficiency of one of Europe's largest port systems, operational users need reliable, explainable tools.

## Regulation and Standards That Shape the Work

**SOLAS Convention.** The International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea sets minimum safety standards for merchant ships, including navigation equipment and vessel traffic services.

**IMO Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships (MASS) Code.** The International Maritime Organization has been developing a code for autonomous ships, reflecting growing interest in remote and autonomous operations. Its development shapes how autonomy may be introduced in commercial shipping.

**Naval rules and standards.** Naval vessels follow defence requirements and often classification society naval rules, with additional security requirements.

**EU Artificial Intelligence Act.** AI systems used exclusively for military or defence purposes are excluded from the act, but civilian maritime applications — for example in traffic management of critical infrastructure — can fall within its scope and may be high-risk when used as safety components.

**Security and export control.** Defence shipbuilding involves security screening, classified information and export control rules that affect who can access certain data.

**NIS2 Directive.** Ports, vessel traffic services and shipping companies can fall within stricter cybersecurity obligations.

## A Concrete Scenario: Predicting When a Deep-Draught Ship Can Pass

A large container ship is heading for Antwerp. Its draught means it can only safely navigate certain parts of the Western Scheldt during specific tidal windows. Planners must decide when the ship should start its passage, when the pilot must board and how the passage fits with other traffic.

A data team builds a model that combines tidal predictions, actual water levels, weather forecasts, the ship's speed profile and historical passage data to predict the best departure window and expected arrival times at critical points. The model improves planning, but the team learns that experienced pilots and traffic controllers use information the model does not capture — local knowledge about how ships of a certain type behave in particular conditions. The final tool is designed to support their decisions rather than replace them, and their feedback is used to improve the model over time.

## A Common Misconception About Maritime Careers

Many data professionals assume maritime work means working at sea. In reality, much of the maritime sector's data work happens on land: in shipyards, engineering offices, traffic centres, port authorities and research institutes. At the same time, understanding how ships and crews operate is essential. Professionals who take time to learn maritime practice — visiting a ship, joining a traffic centre shift, speaking with pilots — produce far more useful work than those who treat maritime data as generic time series.

## Questions Worth Asking Before Joining

- **Is the role related to defence projects, and what security screening is required?** This affects start dates and data access.
- **How does the organisation bring data insights to operational users such as pilots, controllers or crews?** User involvement determines adoption.
- **What engineering tools and data platforms are used?** Shipbuilding and maritime organisations often have specialised systems.
- **Is the work part of a long-term programme or a short project?** Naval programmes can last many years.
- **How international is the collaboration?** Cross-border cooperation with Flanders and international naval partners is common.

## Commuting and Living in Vlissingen

Vlissingen is the terminus of the Zeeland railway line, with trains to Middelburg, Goes, Roosendaal and beyond. A foot and bicycle ferry connects Vlissingen with Breskens in Zeeuws-Vlaanderen. Middelburg is only a few minutes away by train, and many professionals live in Middelburg, Vlissingen or surrounding villages. Housing is considerably more affordable than in the Randstad, and the coastal environment attracts people who value space and sea. The trade-off is distance: travel times to Rotterdam or the Randstad are longer, so most people who work in Vlissingen also live in Zeeland.

## Career Growth in Maritime Data and Engineering

Experience in naval shipbuilding, vessel traffic or port operations is highly specialised and valued throughout the maritime sector, from Rotterdam to international shipyards and port authorities. Systems engineering and simulation skills also transfer to aerospace, defence and complex infrastructure. With European defence investment increasing and ports under pressure to become more efficient and sustainable, demand for data professionals who understand maritime operations is likely to grow.
