A hiring process for a senior data scientist can take six weeks, involve five interviewers, a technical assessment and a final conversation with leadership. The team chooses its preferred candidate, sends the offer — and receives a polite "no". The vacancy reopens, the runner-up has accepted another job and weeks of effort are lost. In the Dutch AI and data market, where experienced candidates often run several processes simultaneously, declined offers are common and costly. The good news is that most reasons for declining are predictable, and many can be addressed long before the offer is sent.

## The Most Common Reasons Candidates Decline

| Reason | What candidates experience | What employers can change |
|---|---|---|
| Pay below expectations | Offer lower than hoped or than other offers | Publish ranges early, align expectations before the final round |
| Slow process | Another employer made an offer first | Shorten the process, set decision timelines |
| Better offer or counteroffer | Current employer or competitor offers more | Understand motivations beyond pay, move quickly |
| Flexibility | Rigid office requirements or unclear hybrid policy | Clarify working arrangements upfront |
| Data and technical maturity | Interviews reveal poor data, unclear use cases or outdated tools | Be transparent, show a credible roadmap |
| Manager and team impression | Disorganised interviews or unengaged interviewers | Prepare interviewers, show the team |
| Contract terms | Non-compete, IP clauses or fixed-term contracts | Review terms, avoid unnecessary restrictions |
| Commute and location | Long daily travel becomes clear during the process | Be explicit about location, offer flexibility or local hiring |
| Relocation complexity | Housing, permits or family considerations | Offer practical relocation support |
| Role mismatch | The job differs from the vacancy description | Write accurate vacancies and consistent interviews |

## Pay: Surprises Are the Real Problem

Pay is often cited as the main reason for declined offers, but the underlying problem is usually surprise. Candidates who learn the salary level only at the offer stage may have invested weeks in a process that could never meet their expectations.

The **EU Pay Transparency Directive** requires employers to provide information about initial pay or its range before the interview and prohibits asking candidates about pay history. Beyond compliance, early transparency filters candidates whose expectations are far outside the range and allows discussion about total compensation before the offer.

Total compensation matters in the Netherlands. Holiday allowance, a thirteenth month, pension contributions, bonuses, training budgets and mobility arrangements can differ significantly between employers. Pension schemes are changing under the **Future Pensions Act**, and many candidates compare employer contributions. Presenting the full package clearly helps candidates compare offers accurately.

## Process Speed

Experienced AI candidates frequently receive offers within a few weeks. A process that takes longer — because interviews are spread out, decision-makers are unavailable or feedback takes days — increases the risk that another employer moves first.

Practical measures include scheduling interview rounds within a short period, reserving decision-makers' time in advance, combining technical and team interviews where possible and giving candidates a clear timeline. After the final interview, making an offer within days rather than weeks signals commitment.

## Counteroffers

When experienced professionals resign, their current employer may make a counteroffer: higher pay, a promotion or a new project. Some candidates accept. Employers cannot prevent counteroffers, but they can reduce their effect.

**Understand motivations early.** Ask candidates why they are considering a move. If the reasons are about development, team, technology or impact, a salary counteroffer is less likely to change their mind.

**Discuss the possibility openly.** Asking "how would you respond if your employer made a counteroffer?" helps candidates reflect and gives you insight.

**Stay in contact after acceptance.** Between signing and starting, a welcome message, introduction to the team or invitation to an event strengthens commitment.

## Flexibility and Working Arrangements

Hybrid working has become an expectation for many data professionals. Offers are frequently declined when office requirements are stricter than candidates expected or when the policy was unclear during interviews.

In the Netherlands, the **Flexible Working Act** allows employees to request changes to working hours and location, but employers have more room to refuse requests about workplace than about hours, and a proposal to strengthen the right to work from home was rejected by the Senate. That makes clear agreements at the hiring stage important. State the hybrid policy in the vacancy, explain which days or activities require presence and be consistent throughout the process.

Part-time arrangements also matter. Many experienced professionals in the Netherlands work 32 or 36 hours, and employers who are flexible about hours widen their pool.

## Data Maturity and Technical Credibility

AI candidates evaluate employers as much as employers evaluate candidates. During interviews, they ask about data quality, infrastructure, tooling, deployment practices and how decisions are made. Offers are declined when candidates conclude that the organisation lacks data foundations, has unrealistic expectations or will not provide the tools needed to succeed.

Transparency works better than overselling. Candidates respond positively to employers who acknowledge challenges but show a credible plan: "Our data is fragmented today; your first project is building a reliable foundation, and leadership has committed budget to the platform." What candidates reject is discovering the gap after they have joined — or sensing during interviews that it is being hidden.

## Contract Terms That Deter Candidates

**Non-compete clauses.** Broad non-compete clauses can make candidates hesitate, especially experienced professionals in consulting or specialised domains. Under **Article 7:653 of the Dutch Civil Code**, non-compete clauses in fixed-term contracts require written justification based on substantial business interests. Limit clauses to what is genuinely necessary.

**Intellectual property clauses.** Clauses claiming rights to all work, including unrelated side projects or open-source contributions, deter engineers who maintain personal projects.

**Fixed-term contracts.** Candidates leaving permanent positions may decline offers that start with a fixed-term contract, particularly when housing or mortgages are involved.

**Training repayment clauses.** Unclear or extensive repayment obligations can reduce the attractiveness of training benefits.

Share the contract or key terms early enough for candidates to review them before the final decision.

## Commute and Location

Commute time is one of the most underestimated reasons for declining offers. A role that looked attractive during video interviews can become less appealing when a candidate realises the daily journey takes well over an hour each way. Long commutes also affect retention after hiring.

Employers can address this by stating the exact work location in vacancies, being clear about expected office days and considering candidates who live within a reasonable distance. Reaching local candidates — who are more likely to accept and stay — is often more effective than trying to convince candidates from far away.

## International Candidates and Relocation

International candidates face additional considerations: residence permits through the **Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND)**, the **30% ruling** and its changing conditions, relocation costs, schooling and, above all, finding housing in a tight Dutch housing market. Offers are declined when candidates cannot secure housing in time or when relocation support is limited.

Practical support — temporary housing, relocation budgets, help with registration and connecting new hires with expat centres — can make the difference between acceptance and decline.

## The Period Between Acceptance and Start Date

An accepted offer is not yet a new colleague. In the Netherlands, employees commonly have a notice period of at least one month, and senior professionals may have longer notice periods agreed in their contracts. During this time, candidates may receive counteroffers, new approaches from recruiters or doubts about their decision. Occasionally, a candidate withdraws after accepting.

Employers can reduce this risk by staying in contact without being intrusive:

- **Send a welcome message** from the hiring manager and future teammates.
- **Share practical information** about the first days, equipment and onboarding plan.
- **Invite the new hire to a team event** or informal lunch, if appropriate.
- **Arrange equipment and access in advance,** so the first day feels organised.
- **Assign a buddy** who can answer informal questions before and after the start.
- **Confirm contract details promptly,** avoiding delays in sending the signed contract.

For international hires, this period also involves permits, relocation and housing. Regular updates about progress with immigration procedures and practical support make the transition less uncertain.

## Using Offer Declines as Data

Organisations that track why offers are declined can identify patterns. After a decline, ask the candidate briefly and respectfully for the main reason. Record reasons consistently and review them periodically. If most declines relate to pay, adjust ranges; if they relate to process speed, streamline interviews; if they relate to flexibility, revisit the hybrid policy.

## A Concrete Scenario: Three Declines, One Pattern

A financial services company loses three consecutive senior ML engineering candidates at the offer stage. Each gives a slightly different reason: another offer, family considerations, a counteroffer. When the hiring manager asks each candidate for more detail, a pattern emerges. The process took eight weeks, the salary range was shared only at the offer stage and the hybrid policy of four office days was mentioned in the final interview.

The company shortens its process to four weeks, publishes the salary range in the vacancy, clarifies a two-day office policy upfront and introduces a meeting with a future teammate before the final round. The next two offers are accepted.

## A Common Misconception

Many employers believe that declined offers are mainly about salary and therefore outside their control. In practice, salary is often intertwined with process speed, flexibility, contract terms and the impression the organisation makes. Another misconception is that making an offer quickly signals desperation. For experienced AI candidates, quick and decisive processes signal respect and organisational maturity.

## An Offer Acceptance Checklist

- **Salary range shared early** and total compensation explained clearly.
- **Process completed within a few weeks,** with timelines communicated.
- **Motivations beyond pay discussed,** including potential counteroffers.
- **Hybrid and part-time policies stated upfront.**
- **Honest discussion of data maturity and roadmap.**
- **Prepared, engaged interviewers and a chance to meet the team.**
- **Contract terms reviewed** for unnecessary restrictions.
- **Exact location and commute expectations clear.**
- **Relocation and housing support** for international candidates.
- **Offer declines recorded and analysed.**

## Key Takeaways

- Declined offers are costly and often predictable; common causes include pay surprises, slow processes, counteroffers, flexibility, data maturity, contract terms, commute and relocation.
- The Pay Transparency Directive makes early pay information a legal requirement and a practical tool to prevent surprises.
- Fast, well-organised processes and early discussions about motivation reduce the impact of competing offers and counteroffers.
- Clear hybrid policies, reasonable contract terms and honest communication about data maturity build trust.
- Local hiring and practical relocation support improve acceptance; track decline reasons to find patterns.
