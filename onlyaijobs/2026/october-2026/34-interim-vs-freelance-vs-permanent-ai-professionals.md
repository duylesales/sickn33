Freelance versus permanent contract is the comparison most AI professionals make, but there is a third route that is often skipped: working interim through a secondment agency (in Dutch, *detachering*). This route has its own risk profile that doesn't coincide with the other two.

## What Makes Interim Through an Agency Different

**The agency carries part of the acquisition risk.** Unlike freelancing, you don't arrange new assignments yourself — the agency does, in exchange for part of your rate. That is a real trade-off between rate and security, not free security.

**Often an employment contract with the agency itself.** This can mean continued pay between assignments and other employee benefits, depending on the agency and the specific agreements — a detail that differs per agency and must be checked explicitly.

**A less direct relationship with the client.** You work for the client, but your formal employer is the agency — which changes who is responsible for certain things, such as continued pay during illness.

## Why This Is a Real Third Option, Not a Compromise

Interim work through an agency isn't "freelance-light" or "permanent-light" — it is a model in its own right, with its own advantages and disadvantages, which suits some professionals better than either extreme.

## A Deeper Look at What a Secondment Agency Actually Does for Its Margin

The percentage an agency keeps from your rate feels to many professionals like pure profit for the agency — but in return, the agency takes over tasks that would otherwise cost you time and risk: finding the next assignment, negotiating with clients, and in some arrangements bridging periods without an assignment. Whether that trade is worth it depends entirely on how much you would want or be able to do those tasks yourself as a freelancer.

## The Named Legal Framework for Agency Work

Agency work in the Netherlands has its own rules, and knowing the names helps you ask the right questions.

**Placement of Personnel by Intermediaries Act (Waadi).** This act governs the provision of workers by intermediaries. Companies that provide workers must be registered as such in the **Netherlands Chamber of Commerce (KVK)** register, and agency workers are generally entitled to the same pay as comparable employees at the client — the equal pay principle.

**Collective labour agreements for agency work.** Employment through temporary employment and secondment agencies is often governed by sector agreements, such as the collective labour agreement for temporary agency workers associated with the **ABU** (Association of Temporary Employment Agencies) or the agreement of the **NBBU**. These agreements contain phase systems that determine how many fixed-term contracts are possible and when agency workers build more rights. Some secondment agencies employ professionals on regular permanent contracts instead, outside these phase systems.

**Stichting Normering Arbeid (SNA).** The SNA quality mark indicates that a labour-providing company has been inspected for compliance with obligations around tax, social security and wages — a useful signal when choosing an agency.

**Balanced Labour Market Act (WAB).** Since 2020, this act has introduced stricter rules for payrolling, requiring payroll employees to receive terms of employment comparable to those of the client's own employees. Some constructions marketed as interim work are in fact payrolling, which affects your rights.

**Deregulation Assessment of Employment Relationships Act (Wet DBA).** For professionals considering freelancing directly, the assessment of whether a relationship is in fact employment — with enforcement resumed by the Tax Administration from 2025 — is a key difference compared with agency employment, where you are already an employee.

## Why This Choice Often Changes During a Career

Many AI professionals don't move through these three routes in a fixed order, but switch between them depending on life stage and market conditions — someone who has just started a family more often chooses the security of interim or permanent work, while someone with a larger financial buffer and a need for control more often chooses freelancing. None of these choices is permanent.

## How Secondment Agencies in the AI Market Actually Work

Agencies in the AI and data market vary widely in specialisation. At one end are generalist IT secondment agencies that treat data roles as one of many technical categories; at the other are specialised agencies focused exclusively on data science, machine learning and related disciplines. The difference matters: a specialised agency usually understands better what you do, can match you more precisely to suitable assignments and has a network of clients actually relevant to your profile.

Business models differ too. Some agencies work with a fixed margin on your rate, others with a percentage, and transparency about this varies considerably. Asking how the margin is built up is a perfectly legitimate question that serious agencies answer without objection — and their willingness to answer clearly is itself informative.

What a good agency adds is access to assignments not publicly advertised, knowledge of which clients are pleasant to work for, and a buffer between you and the commercial negotiation. What a weaker agency does is place you in the first available assignment regardless of fit. The difference isn't always visible before the collaboration starts, which makes references from other professionals working through the same agency especially valuable.

## What Actually Matters in the Contract With an Agency

**What happens between assignments.** Some arrangements provide continued pay for a period without an assignment, others don't. This single point makes a bigger difference to your actual income security than a few euros difference in hourly rate.

**Non-compete and non-solicitation clauses.** Some agencies use clauses that restrict you from working directly for a client where the agency placed you, for a period after the assignment ends. Whether that is reasonable depends on the terms, but you should know about it in advance rather than discovering it when a client offers you a permanent contract.

**Training and development.** Some agencies invest substantially in their professionals' development, with training budgets and internal knowledge sharing; others don't. In a fast-changing field, this is a real part of the value proposition beyond the rate.

**Control over which assignments you accept.** An arrangement in which you can decline assignments without consequences differs fundamentally from one in which you can't, and that difference determines how much control you really have over the direction of your career.

## Which Route Suits Which Career Stage

Early in a career, while you are still building skills and don't yet have a network, a permanent job usually offers the best conditions: guidance, exposure to colleagues you learn from, and room to make mistakes without directly damaging a client relationship.

Mid-career, with an established reputation and network, the independent routes become more practical and financially attractive. This is also the stage in which the flexibility of independence is often worth most, for example for combining work with care responsibilities.

Later in a career, the trade-offs shift again. Pension accrual, disability risks and the value of stability weigh more heavily, while building new client relationships becomes less attractive. The answer that was right at thirty doesn't automatically stay right at forty-five.

## How to Compare the Three Routes Fairly

For the independent routes, calculate what remains after deducting non-billable time — acquisition, administration, development and the periods without assignments you realistically expect. For many freelancers, the share of billable time is considerably lower than a full working week assumes.

Then value the benefits of employment explicitly: employer pension contributions, continued pay during illness, training budget and the value of dismissal protection. These are hard to quantify exactly, but they aren't zero.

Finally, weigh the non-financial factors — team structure, control over project choice, administrative burden, social context — as heavily as the financial ones. For most people, they shape day-to-day work experience more strongly than the difference in net income.

## How the Dutch AI Market Colours These Three Routes

A considerable share of applied data work in the Netherlands happens within medium-sized organisations hiring their first or second data specialist. This type of organisation usually looks for someone who stays and builds the function, not someone who completes a defined assignment and leaves — which makes the permanent route relatively more attractive than in sectors where project work is the norm.

The highly specialised niches — governance, evaluation, specialised modelling questions — fit defined assignments well, because organisations need this expertise periodically without being able to justify a full-time role. For professionals in these niches, the independent route is considerably more feasible.

Regionally, the Randstad's high density of potential clients makes independent work easier than in regions with fewer reachable clients. For professionals outside the Randstad who want to work independently, remote work is often not a preference but a condition.

## A Final Consideration: What You Choose Says Something About What You Want to Learn

A permanent job develops organisational skills — navigating internal relationships, building influence without formal authority. Independence develops entrepreneurial skills — positioning your expertise, negotiating terms, judging which assignments are worthwhile. The interim route develops a third set: becoming productive quickly in changing organisational contexts, a specific kind of adaptability. Judging the choice purely on income and security misses this dimension, while these skills determine which options you have in ten years.

## Questions to Ask an Agency Before Signing

1. **Will I be employed by the agency, and on what type of contract?** Permanent, fixed-term, or under a phase system in a sector agreement?
2. **What happens between assignments?** Is pay continued, for how long, and at what level?
3. **How is your margin calculated?** Fixed amount or percentage, and is it transparent?
4. **Can I decline an assignment?** What happens if an assignment doesn't fit my profile?
5. **Is there a non-solicitation clause, and for how long?** What happens if a client wants to hire me directly?
6. **What training budget and development support do you offer?**
7. **Can I speak with professionals you currently place?** Their experience is the most reliable reference.

## Key Takeaways

- Interim work through an agency is a distinct third route with its own risk profile, not a compromise.
- The Waadi, sector agreements associated with ABU and NBBU, the SNA quality mark and the WAB payrolling rules define agency work.
- Check pay between assignments, non-solicitation clauses, training budget and control over assignments before signing.
- Compare routes on billable time, the value of employee benefits and non-financial factors, not only on rate.
- The right route changes with career stage, specialisation and region — revisit the choice periodically.

## Where to Start

With a concrete offer, ask explicitly about the form of contract with the agency itself (employment or something else) and about what happens between assignments, before comparing it with freelancing or a permanent contract elsewhere.

### (Scenario: professional unsure about pay between assignments) Do I get paid between assignments in interim work?
That differs per agency and per contract — ask explicitly with a concrete offer, instead of assuming it's always the same.

### (Scenario: professional unsure who is responsible during illness) Who is responsible for continued pay during illness in an interim arrangement?
Usually the agency, as the formal employer — but confirm this explicitly in the contract before accepting an assignment.

Don't compare on rate alone, but on who carries the acquisition risk, which provisions you have between assignments, and how much direct control you want over your client relationships.
