Helmond has developed — partly thanks to its test facilities for automotive and smart mobility technology — into a place where perception systems, traffic modelling and vehicle data are tested and applied. The work is close to robotics in substance, but rarely presented under that name.

## What Kind of Work Actually Happens Here

Perception models for vehicles, traffic system optimisation and sensor fusion are the core of this work — technically demanding, with real physical consequences when a prediction is wrong, comparable to robotics work elsewhere in the country but focused specifically on mobility and traffic.

## Why This Work Is Less Visible

Job titles in this sector are often automotive-specific — "Vehicle Technology Systems Engineer" — rather than AI vocabulary, even though the work involves serious application of perception and modelling techniques.

## The Named Organisations Behind Helmond's Smart Mobility Cluster

**Automotive Campus Helmond.** The campus brings together automotive companies, test facilities, research organisations and education in one location. It is the physical centre of Helmond's mobility ecosystem and hosts companies working on vehicle technology, electrification and connected driving.

**TNO.** The Netherlands Organisation for Applied Scientific Research has long been active in automotive research in Helmond, working on topics such as connected and automated driving, vehicle safety and truck platooning.

**Siemens Digital Industries Software.** Helmond is home to activities that originated in TASS International, a company specialising in simulation and testing for automated driving that was acquired by Siemens. Simulation software such as scenario-based testing for advanced driver assistance systems is exactly where perception, sensor models and data meet.

**Knowledge institutions in the region.** **Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e)** has research groups in mechanical engineering, control systems and automotive technology, while **Fontys University of Applied Sciences** trains automotive and mechatronics engineers. Both supply talent directly to Helmond employers.

**Brainport Development.** The regional development organisation supports the high-tech ecosystem of which Helmond's mobility cluster is part, including smart mobility programmes.

## The Entity Landscape by Type

**Test facilities and living labs for vehicle technology**, often in cooperation with knowledge institutions from the wider Eindhoven region, form the core of where perception and traffic systems are actually tested under controlled conditions. The region has used public roads, including stretches around the A270 between Eindhoven and Helmond, for experiments with connected and automated driving.

**Automotive suppliers** that have located around these test facilities provide the sensors, systems and software components that make the underlying work possible.

**Municipal and regional mobility projects** add a public component, focused on traffic flow optimisation and smart city applications at street level.

## The Technical Stack of Automated Driving

| Layer | What it does | Typical techniques |
|---|---|---|
| Perception | Detects and classifies vehicles, pedestrians, lanes and signs | Convolutional and transformer-based vision models, lidar point cloud processing |
| Sensor fusion | Combines camera, radar and lidar into one consistent picture | Kalman filters, Bayesian tracking, learned fusion |
| Localisation | Determines the vehicle's exact position | SLAM, map matching, GNSS correction |
| Prediction | Estimates what other road users will do | Trajectory prediction, sequence models |
| Planning and control | Decides and executes manoeuvres | Model predictive control, optimisation |
| Simulation and validation | Tests systems across millions of scenarios | Scenario generation, synthetic data, sensor simulation |
| V2X and traffic systems | Connects vehicles with infrastructure | Traffic flow models, communication protocols |

Most roles focus on one or two of these layers. Simulation and validation are particularly relevant in Helmond, given the region's history in scenario-based testing.

## Regulation and Safety Standards

Automated driving is one of the most heavily regulated applications of AI.

- **EU General Safety Regulation (EU) 2019/2144.** Makes a range of advanced driver assistance systems mandatory in new vehicles and provides the framework for type approval of automated driving systems.
- **UN Regulation No. 155.** Requires manufacturers to have a certified cyber security management system for vehicle type approval.
- **UN Regulation No. 157.** Covers Automated Lane Keeping Systems, one of the first international regulations for conditional automated driving.
- **ISO 26262 and ISO 21448 (SOTIF).** Functional safety and safety of the intended functionality — the latter addressing hazards from performance limitations of perception systems, which is exactly where machine learning comes in.
- **RDW (Netherlands Vehicle Authority).** Responsible for vehicle approval in the Netherlands, including permission to test automated vehicles on public roads.
- **EU Artificial Intelligence Act.** AI systems that are safety components of vehicles are linked to existing sectoral product legislation.

For an ML engineer, SOTIF is the key concept: it asks how a system behaves under conditions its models were never properly trained for.

## A Concrete Scenario of Perception Work in a Test Environment

A team is working on a perception system for an automated vehicle being tested at one of the test locations in the Helmond region. The model performs excellently on the training data, but during a test drive the system turns out to struggle with a specific light angle at sunset that rarely occurred in the training data. Discoveries like this — which only surface in a real test environment, never in a notebook — are the core of the work here: not training the model as such, but iteratively exposing and closing the gap between controlled training conditions and the endless variation of the real world.

## A Common Misconception About How "Finished" These Systems Ever Are

Candidates coming from consumer AI sometimes expect a perception system to be "done" at some point, similar to how a product feature is launched and then only needs maintenance. In practice, perception systems for vehicles and robotics stay in continuous development, because every new test environment, season and combination of conditions exposes new edge cases — a fundamentally different project life cycle from a typical software feature.

## Why Helmond Is a Good Place to Develop These Skills

The presence of specialised test facilities in the region means engineers here have faster and more frequent access to real, controlled test conditions than on a comparable project without such a facility nearby — a practical advantage that speeds up learning compared with regions where this kind of testing is less accessible.

## What Career Growth Looks Like in This Niche

An engineer who starts with perception work for vehicle applications in Helmond gradually builds expertise that extends to broader smart mobility questions — from individual vehicle perception to traffic system optimisation at street level, and sometimes to advising on how cities prepare for automated mobility. This trajectory, from highly technical to more system- and policy-oriented, is characteristic of this niche.

## Why This Work Is Hard to Automate or Outsource

Unlike some AI applications that can be relatively easily outsourced to external suppliers, perception work for vehicle applications often requires close, ongoing collaboration with the specific test facility and vehicle platforms involved — a degree of context dependence that makes this work less susceptible to outsourcing than more generic data science tasks, which contributes to more stable local demand.

## Skills and Tools That Stand Out

Automotive employers in and around Helmond look for engineers who combine machine learning with a solid understanding of real-time, safety-relevant systems.

- **Programming.** Python for model development and data pipelines, and C++ for performance-critical, embedded or real-time code.
- **Robotics middleware.** Experience with ROS or ROS 2 is common in prototyping and research vehicles.
- **Sensor data.** Working with camera calibration, lidar point clouds, radar data and time synchronisation between sensors.
- **Simulation.** Familiarity with scenario-based testing, including standardised scenario formats such as ASAM OpenSCENARIO, and with generating synthetic data.
- **Data management.** Automated driving produces enormous volumes of recorded drive data; selecting relevant scenarios, labelling efficiently and managing datasets are specialist skills in their own right.
- **Safety mindset.** Understanding hazard analysis, requirements traceability and why a performance limitation of a perception model is a safety question, not only a quality question.

## How to Present Experience From Other Domains

Many strong candidates for Helmond's mobility roles don't come from the automotive industry. Engineers from robotics, drones, industrial vision, agricultural automation or even medical imaging bring directly relevant skills. The key is to translate that experience into the automotive context:

- Describe how you dealt with **edge cases and distribution shift** — conditions your model hadn't seen during training.
- Explain how you **validated a system beyond test-set accuracy**, for example through field tests, hardware-in-the-loop setups or simulation.
- Show that you worked within **real-time or resource constraints**, such as latency budgets or limited compute on embedded hardware.
- Mention any experience with **formal processes**, such as quality management, medical device regulation or functional safety in industrial automation; these translate well to automotive safety standards.

Hiring managers in this sector often say that domain knowledge about vehicles can be learned within months, while a disciplined approach to validation and safety takes years to develop. Candidates who can demonstrate that discipline from another field are therefore genuinely competitive, even without an automotive background.
