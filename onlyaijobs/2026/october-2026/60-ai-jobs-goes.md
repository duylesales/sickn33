Here, the data demand comes from crop production, fruit growing, shellfish farming, food logistics and regional distribution — a different specialisation within the same province.

## Zuid-Beveland's Named Agricultural Landscape

Goes is the main town of **Zuid-Beveland**, a former island in Zeeland with fertile clay soils. The region's agriculture is diverse:

**Arable farming.** Potatoes, onions, sugar beet and grains are grown on large, open fields. Zeeland's clay soils and mild maritime climate make it a productive arable region, and crops such as seed potatoes and onions are exported widely.

**Fruit growing.** Zuid-Beveland, particularly around Kapelle and nearby villages, is known for apple and pear orchards. Fruit growing involves long-term investment in trees, weather-dependent harvests and cold storage — all data-intensive planning questions.

**Shellfish farming in Yerseke.** A short distance from Goes, **Yerseke** is the centre of Dutch mussel and oyster farming, with a mussel auction and a concentration of shellfish companies. **Wageningen Marine Research** has a location in Yerseke, conducting research on shellfish culture, ecosystems and the Eastern Scheldt and Wadden Sea.

**Farmers' organisation.** **ZLTO**, the agricultural and horticultural organisation for the southern Netherlands, represents farmers in Zeeland, Brabant and parts of Gelderland, and supports innovation and knowledge exchange.

**Water management.** The **Scheldestromen Water Board** manages water safety, water levels and water quality in Zeeland. For agriculture, freshwater availability and salinisation of groundwater are major concerns in a province surrounded by salt water.

**Education and healthcare.** **HZ University of Applied Sciences** offers programmes in areas including water management, delta technology and logistics, and **Scalda** provides vocational education, including agricultural and technical programmes. **Admiraal De Ruyter Hospital**, with locations in Goes and Vlissingen, adds healthcare data work.

## The Entity Landscape of Goes's Agricultural Economy

**Regional agricultural producers and cooperatives** generate yield-prediction and quality-monitoring demand similar in technical character to what's found in Wageningen and Ede, but concentrated within Zeeland's own agricultural base.

**Food-distribution and logistics operators** serving this output connect Goes's production side to national and international distribution networks, including along the A58 corridor towards Brabant and Belgium.

**Agri-tech suppliers and advisers**, serving local producers with sensor, drone and monitoring technology, form a less visible employer layer.

**Shellfish and seafood companies** around Yerseke add aquaculture and food safety data work.

## Regulation and Policy That Drive Data Demand

- **EU Nitrates Directive.** Limits the use of nitrogen from manure to protect water quality. The Netherlands' derogation allowing higher manure application on grassland is being phased out, which puts pressure on farmers to fertilise more precisely — a direct driver of precision agriculture data.
- **EU Water Framework Directive.** Requires good ecological and chemical status of water bodies, which shapes rules on fertiliser and crop protection use near water.
- **EU Common Agricultural Policy (CAP).** Links part of farm support to environmental and climate measures, which require documentation and monitoring.
- **Food safety for shellfish.** Shellfish production areas are monitored for water quality and toxins, with supervision by the Netherlands Food and Consumer Product Safety Authority.
- **Water management and salinisation.** Freshwater availability policy and measures against salinisation affect which crops can be grown where — increasingly supported by spatial data and models.

## A Concrete Scenario: Forecasting an Onion Harvest

An agricultural trading company in the region buys onions from many growers and needs to plan storage, sorting capacity and export contracts months before harvest. A data scientist combines satellite imagery of fields, weather data, planting dates and historical yields per grower to forecast harvest volumes and timing. As the season progresses, updated imagery and weather data refine the forecast. The company uses the forecast to book transport and storage capacity earlier and to agree export volumes with more confidence. The technical work is familiar spatial and time series modelling; the value comes from linking it to concrete commercial and logistics decisions.

## Salinisation and Climate Adaptation: A Long-Term Data Challenge

Zeeland is surrounded by salt water, and much of its farmland lies close to or below sea level. Saline groundwater can rise into the root zone, especially during dry summers, damaging crops such as potatoes and fruit trees. Climate change, with longer dry periods and rising sea levels, is expected to increase this pressure.

That creates data questions with long time horizons: where salinisation risk is highest, which crops remain viable in which locations, how freshwater can be stored and distributed more efficiently, and how drainage and irrigation should be adapted. Answering them requires combining groundwater measurements, soil data, weather and climate scenarios, and crop response models. Water boards, provinces, research institutes and agricultural advisers all work on these questions, often together. For data scientists interested in climate adaptation, Zeeland offers some of the most concrete, locally urgent applications in the Netherlands.

## Shellfish Farming as a Data Domain

Mussel and oyster farming in the Eastern Scheldt and Wadden Sea depends on natural conditions that growers can only partly influence. Mussel seed is collected, relocated to culture plots and harvested after growing, and yields depend on food availability in the water, water temperature, predators and storms. Growers and researchers use data on chlorophyll, water temperature, currents and historical harvests to decide where and when to place and harvest mussels. Food safety adds another layer: shellfish areas are monitored for toxins produced by certain algae, and harvesting can be temporarily closed when levels rise.

For data work, this means environmental time series, spatial models of water bodies and early warning models for algae blooms — a small but distinctive niche in which Yerseke's concentration of companies and research makes the Goes area the natural centre.

## Practical Points for Working in the Region

- **Seasonality.** Harvest periods for crops, fruit and shellfish bring peaks in workload for data roles close to operations.
- **Field work.** Many roles combine office work with visits to farms, orchards, storage facilities or shellfish plots.
- **Language.** Agricultural and cooperative work is mostly in Dutch; research roles are more often English-speaking.
- **Housing and living.** Housing costs in Zeeland are generally lower than in the Randstad, and the region attracts people who value space, water and a quieter environment.

## Accessibility and Search Radius

Goes lies on the railway line between Roosendaal and Vlissingen, with Middelburg roughly a quarter of an hour away and Roosendaal around half an hour. The A58 motorway connects Goes with Brabant, and the Zeeland Bridge and Western Scheldt Tunnel link it with the rest of the province. A realistic search radius includes Kapelle, Yerseke, Middelburg, Vlissingen, Bergen op Zoom and — via the tunnel — Terneuzen. Many agricultural roles involve field visits, so a car is often useful.
