Many municipalities run digital skills programs aimed at residents — basic data literacy, introductory technical courses, retraining support. These programs teach real capability, but they rarely connect graduates to concrete evidence that local jobs using those skills actually exist nearby.

## The Gap This Creates

A resident who completes a digital skills course has a new capability but often no clear next step — they're pointed toward a general job board that shows a city name, not a specific local employer with a specific distance from home. The skill and the opportunity exist; the connection between them is the missing piece.

## How This Pairs With Existing Programs

**A concrete next step at program completion.** Pointing graduates toward a visible, address-level list of local AI/ML/Data and related vacancies gives the program's outcome somewhere specific to go, rather than a vague "now go find a job."

**A feedback loop for program design.** Seeing which local employers list which kinds of roles can inform what a next cohort of the program should actually teach, grounding curriculum decisions in visible local demand rather than a generic national curriculum.

**A low-cost addition, not a new initiative.** This doesn't require building new infrastructure — it's a pointer added to an existing program's closing materials.

## What This Is Not

This is not a claim that a digital skills program alone creates jobs, or that every graduate will find a matching local role. It addresses a narrower, specific gap: connecting a program's outcome to visible, checkable local opportunity.

## Where to Start

If your municipality runs a digital skills program, consider adding a pointer to visible, address-level local AI/ML/Data vacancies as part of the program's closing materials.

## A Deeper Look at the "Skills Without a Destination" Problem

Municipal digital skills programs are often measured on completion rates and satisfaction scores — reasonable metrics for the training itself, but silent on what happens after a resident finishes the program. This creates a structural blind spot: a program can be "successful" by its own metrics while still failing to connect graduates to real outcomes, simply because nobody measured that connection in the first place.

## Comparing Program Outcomes With and Without a Concrete Next Step

| | Program without a clear next step | Program with a visible local job resource added |
|---|---|---|
| Graduate's immediate next action | Unclear, often defaults to a generic job board | Concrete: check specific local vacancies |
| Program's measurable impact | Limited to completion/satisfaction | Potentially extends to actual job-search engagement |
| Cost to add | — | Minimal — a link and brief explanation |

## Why This Small Addition Can Improve How the Program Itself Is Perceived

A digital skills program that visibly connects its graduates to concrete local opportunities is easier to justify to funders and stakeholders than one that ends with a certificate and a vague hope. Adding this connection isn't just good for graduates — it strengthens the case for the program's own continued funding and expansion, by making its real-world impact more visible and traceable.
