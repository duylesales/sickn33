A referral bonus alone — money for every hire an employee brings in — motivates employees to pass on as many names as possible, not necessarily the best ones. For specialised AI roles, where the quality of the match weighs more heavily than the number of candidates, that is the wrong incentive.

## What a More Effective Referral Programme Does Differently

**Sharing specific, current vacancies instead of a generic call.** An employee who knows which specific problem a role solves can think more precisely about who in their network actually fits — a generic "do you know anyone?" rarely produces the same result.

**Recognition for the introduction, not only payment upon hiring.** A referral that doesn't lead to a hire but did produce a good conversation still deserves appreciation — that keeps employees motivated to keep thinking along.

**Transparency about a referral's status.** Employees who put forward a name and then hear nothing stop thinking along. A short update, even after a rejection, keeps the programme alive.

## Why This Matters More for AI Roles

Specialised AI professionals often sit in small, specific networks — an employee who understands an AI vacancy well can draw on that network far more precisely than a generic recruitment channel ever can.

## A Deeper Look at Why Specific Vacancy Texts Work Better Than a General Call

An employee receiving a general call ("know anyone for our open vacancies?") has to do the thinking themselves to decide which vacancy fits whom in their network — a threshold most people don't cross, simply for lack of time. An employee receiving a specific, well-written vacancy text can forward it directly to the one person who immediately comes to mind — a much smaller cognitive step that considerably increases the chance of action.

## Comparison Table: Effective vs. Ineffective Referral Programmes

| Feature | Effective programme | Ineffective programme |
|---|---|---|
| Communication to employees | Specific vacancy texts | General, recurring calls |
| Recognition | Also for introductions without a hire | Only upon successful hire |
| Feedback | Fast and transparent | Slow or absent |
| Frequency of reminders | Linked to concrete vacancies | Generic, disconnected from current needs |

## Why Referrals Work Differently for AI Roles

First, a considerable share of suitable candidates isn't actively looking. Professionals with specialised skills in in-demand niches are usually satisfied in their current role and don't browse job sites. They are reachable through someone they know and trust — which is exactly what a referral is.

Second, the relevant communities are small. In specialised niches, people know each other or know someone who knows the other. An employee with a few years' experience in such a niche has a network that touches a considerable share of the national candidate pool for that specialism.

Third, fit for these roles is hard to judge from a CV. A colleague who has actually seen someone's work provides an assessment no selection process can replicate.

## The Legal and Practical Frame in the Netherlands

A referral programme touches several Dutch rules that are easy to overlook:

- **Tax treatment of referral bonuses.** A referral bonus paid to an employee is, in principle, part of their wages for tax purposes. Under the **work-related costs scheme** (*werkkostenregeling*), employers can designate certain allowances as part of a limited tax-free "free space." Check with your payroll administrator or the guidance of the **Dutch Tax Administration** how your bonus is best treated before announcing an amount.
- **Candidate data.** When an employee submits a contact's details, that is processing of personal data under the **GDPR**. The referred person should know their details are being shared and agree to it. The simplest approach is to let the employee send the vacancy to their contact, who then applies themselves or explicitly agrees to be contacted. The **Dutch Data Protection Authority**'s guidance on applicant data — including deleting it within four weeks after a procedure unless the candidate consents to longer retention — applies to referred candidates as well.
- **Equal treatment.** Referred candidates must go through the same selection process as other candidates. Under the **Equal Treatment Act**, selection may not discriminate, and the **Netherlands Institute for Human Rights** assesses complaints. A referral may open a door, but it must not bypass the assessment.
- **Fair process.** The **NVP Recruitment Code** recommends a transparent, equal procedure for all applicants, which also protects employees who refer someone from awkward situations.

## Enabling Employees to Refer Well

The most common obstacle is that employees don't know exactly what's being sought. A specific description — which problem, which skills, what kind of person would thrive here — activates memory in a way a general call doesn't.

The second obstacle is discomfort about approaching someone. A short, ready-made message they can adapt and forward removes most of that discomfort and noticeably increases the number of actual referrals.

The third obstacle is uncertainty about what happens next. Employees who put forward someone from their network stake their own reputation and want to know that person will be treated decently. Promising that every referred candidate gets a response within a set period — and keeping that promise — is the most important commitment a referral programme can make.

## Referrals and Team Diversity

A justified objection to referral programmes is that they can reproduce a team's existing composition, because people usually know people similar to themselves. An organisation recruiting mainly through referrals risks an increasingly homogeneous team, with the known downsides for decision-making and blind spots.

The practical response isn't abolishing referrals but treating them as one channel alongside others — open job boards, specialist platforms and targeted outreach. A second response is explicitly asking for referrals outside employees' immediate circle. Organisations that have signed the **Diversity Charter**, coordinated in the Netherlands by the Social and Economic Council's *Diversiteit in Bedrijf* programme, often combine referral programmes with such explicit diversity goals.

## What a Referral Programme Costs and Delivers

The direct costs are any bonuses and the time employees spend thinking and reaching out — usually modest compared with external recruitment channels, especially for specialised roles where agencies charge substantial fees.

The indirect benefits are often forgotten. Referred candidates usually have a more realistic picture of the organisation before they start, because they have spoken with someone who actually works there. That translates into fewer surprises after joining and a lower chance of early departure. A well-functioning referral programme is also a signal about the organisation itself: employees only refer people to an employer they are satisfied with.

## How to Set Up a Referral Programme in Practice

Start by sharing one concrete vacancy text with the team, supplemented by two or three sentences explaining what kind of person would fit — not in terms of skills but of working style and interests. Add a short, ready-made message employees can forward, for example on **LinkedIn** or by email, without having to write anything themselves.

Agree explicitly what happens to a referred candidate: within what period they get a response, and whether the employee who made the introduction gets feedback. Finally, decide how you express appreciation. A financial bonus upon a successful hire is common, but recognition for introductions that don't lead to a hire is at least as important for keeping the programme alive — and costs considerably less.

## What to Avoid

- **Treating the programme as a permanent, general call** instead of linking it to concrete vacancies.
- **Rewarding only success,** which steers employees towards volume rather than fit.
- **Slow or absent feedback,** which employees experience as a loss of face towards the person they approached.
- **Applying pressure,** which leads employees to refer people they wouldn't actually recommend.

## How Referrals Fit Within a Broader Channel Mix

No single recruitment channel reaches the full candidate pool. Referrals reach people who aren't actively searching but are reachable through trusted contacts. They don't reach anyone outside your current employees' networks — a limitation that weighs more heavily the more homogeneous your team is. Treat referrals as one component alongside a specialist job board such as **OnlyAIJobs**, targeted direct outreach and visibility in professional communities.

## What the Programme Tells You About Your Own Organisation

Employees' willingness to refer people from their network is one of the most honest signals about how they actually rate their employer. A programme that yields little despite good communication and reasonable rewards tells you something employee satisfaction surveys often miss. The productive question then isn't how to promote the programme better, but why employees are hesitant.

## A Ready-Made Message Employees Can Forward

Giving employees a message they can adapt removes the biggest practical barrier. An example:

> "Hi [name], we're looking for a machine learning engineer at [company] to help bring our demand forecasting models into production. The team is small, the data is real and a bit messy, and you'd own the deployment side. I thought of you because of the pipeline work you did on [project]. No pressure at all — here's the vacancy: [link]. Happy to tell you more about what it's like to work here."

The message is personal, specific about the problem, honest about the context and explicitly low-pressure — and it lets the contact decide whether to share their details, which keeps the process GDPR-friendly.

## A Simple Referral Tracking Sheet

Even a basic tracking sheet makes feedback and evaluation possible:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Vacancy | Links the referral to a concrete role |
| Referring employee | Enables feedback and recognition |
| Date referred | Monitors response time |
| Candidate consent confirmed | Documents that sharing details was agreed |
| Stage reached | Shows where referrals typically stop |
| Feedback sent to employee | Ensures the loop is closed |
| Outcome and recognition | Records hire, bonus or other appreciation |

Delete candidate details in line with retention guidance once the procedure ends, keeping only anonymised counts for evaluating the programme.

## Key Takeaways

- A bonus alone rewards volume; specific vacancies, recognition for introductions and fast feedback reward good matches.
- Referrals are especially valuable for AI roles because suitable candidates are often passive and communities are small.
- Check the tax treatment of bonuses under the work-related costs scheme, get consent before sharing candidate data under the GDPR, and apply the same selection process to everyone.
- Counter homogeneity by combining referrals with other channels and explicit diversity goals, for example under the Diversity Charter.
- Low referral activity is diagnostic information about how employees view the organisation.
