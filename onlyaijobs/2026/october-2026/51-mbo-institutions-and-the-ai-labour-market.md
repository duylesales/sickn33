Conversations about talent and AI employment almost automatically turn to universities and research master's programmes. That framing misses a real part of the labour market: applied data and AI-related roles that connect well to a technical programme in Dutch secondary vocational education (MBO), as discussed elsewhere on this site.

## Why This Is Relevant for MBO Institutions

Students in technical MBO programmes rarely receive concrete advice about data and AI-related career paths, simply because the prevailing image of "AI work" is academic. That's a missed opportunity to point students towards real, accessible roles.

## How This Connects to MBO Institutions' Existing Work

**A complement to existing internship placement.** MBO institutions often already have relationships with regional employers for internships — encouraging those employers to also make data-related vacancies visible is a small addition to existing work.

**Concrete material for career orientation.** Instead of pointing students only to traditional technical occupations, a reference to accessible data analyst roles broadens the picture of available career paths.

**A signal for curriculum development.** Visibility of which data-related skills regional employers actually ask for can inform the sharpening of a technical curriculum.

## What This Is Not

This isn't a plea to restructure MBO programmes around AI, nor a claim that every technical MBO student should aim for a data career. It's a concrete, supplementary signal for career orientation and employer engagement.

## The Named Structures That Already Connect MBO and Employers

The Dutch vocational system has institutions and instruments that make this kind of partnership easier than it may seem.

**SBB.** The **Foundation for Cooperation on Vocational Education, Training and the Labour Market (SBB)** brings together education and industry. It accredits companies as recognised work placement providers and publishes labour market information about vocational occupations and internship opportunities. For a college exploring data-related placements, SBB's data on recognised providers and occupational demand is a natural starting point.

**MBO Raad.** The **Netherlands Association of VET Colleges** represents MBO institutions and coordinates sector-wide initiatives, including on digitalisation in education.

**Regional Investment Fund MBO (RIF).** Through this fund, the government co-finances public-private partnerships between MBO institutions and regional employers that aim to align education with regional labour market needs. Several partnerships have focused on ICT, smart industry and digital skills — exactly the domains where applied data roles are emerging.

**Centres for Innovative Craftsmanship (CIV).** These public-private centres, often linked to the RIF, combine education, innovation and practice in a specific sector, for example smart manufacturing or logistics.

**Practorates.** MBO institutions increasingly have practorates (*practoraten*), in which a practor conducts practice-oriented research with companies and teachers. A practorate on digitalisation or smart industry can connect student projects to real data questions at regional companies.

**BBL pathway.** In the work-based BBL pathway, students work at an employer most of the week and attend school one day a week. Companies that already use data in their operations can offer BBL places in which students gradually take on data-related tasks.

**Associate degree.** For MBO level 4 graduates who want to go further, two-year Associate degree programmes at universities of applied sciences offer a step towards data and IT roles without committing to a full bachelor's immediately.

## Why This Is Becoming Relevant Now

As more traditional sectors — logistics, manufacturing, agriculture — gradually introduce data-driven processes, a growing layer of applied data analyst roles emerges that fits the education level of a technical MBO programme better than a university master's. This shift happens gradually and unnoticed inside individual companies, which means nobody "announces" the trend — it's up to education institutions to recognise the pattern and anticipate it.

Two policy developments reinforce this. First, digital literacy has become a more explicit part of MBO education, including within citizenship education requirements. Second, the **EU Artificial Intelligence Act** requires organisations that deploy AI systems to ensure sufficient AI literacy among staff working with them — including operational staff, many of whom have an MBO background. Employers will increasingly look for MBO graduates who can work sensibly with data and AI tools, even in roles that aren't labelled as data roles.

## Comparison Table: Where MBO Fit Is Strongest

| Sector | Fit with a technical MBO background | Type of role |
|---|---|---|
| Logistics and distribution | High | Supply chain data analyst |
| Manufacturing and quality control | High | Quality analyst with a data component |
| Agriculture and horticulture | Moderate to high | Precision agriculture data analyst |
| Healthcare operations | Moderate | Capacity planning or reporting specialist |
| Financial services | Lower | Often higher theoretical requirements |

## Three Concrete Forms a Partnership Can Take

**A reference in career orientation.** Add a short section to career orientation materials about data-related roles in regional sectors, with a link to a vacancy platform where students can see actual roles near their home.

**Employer conversations during internship placement.** When internship coordinators speak with companies, they can ask whether data-related tasks or vacancies exist, and encourage employers to make them visible — for example through a free first listing on a specialist platform.

**Curriculum signals.** Periodically collect which skills appear in regional data-related vacancies — SQL, spreadsheets, reporting tools, basic statistics — and discuss with teaching teams whether elective modules or certificates could address them.

## Why Early Signalling Is Worth More Than Extensive Research

An MBO institution doesn't have to wait for extensive labour market research to start signalling this opportunity to students — a small experiment, such as adding a paragraph to existing career orientation, can begin before a fully substantiated policy plan exists. The cost of starting early is low; the cost of waiting is a missed opportunity for a cohort of students who have meanwhile graduated.

## A Concrete Scenario

A regional training centre with strong logistics and ICT programmes notices that several internship companies have started using warehouse management dashboards and forecasting tools. The internship coordinator adds one question to regular employer conversations: "Are there data-related tasks a student could take on, or data roles you're recruiting for?" Within a year, a handful of companies offer BBL places with a reporting component, and two graduates move into junior data analyst roles at their internship employers. None of this required a new programme — only attention to a shift that was already happening.

## What Institutions Should Watch Out For

- **Don't oversell.** Present data roles as one realistic option among others, not as a guaranteed path.
- **Be honest about additional learning.** Most accessible data roles still require skills beyond the standard curriculum, such as SQL or reporting tools.
- **Protect student data.** When sharing information with employers or platforms, don't share student personal data without a legal basis; referring students to public vacancy information is usually enough.
- **Keep teachers involved.** Teachers are often the most trusted source of career advice; brief them on what data-related roles actually involve.

## Examples of Data Tasks Suitable for Internships and BBL Placements

Employers often don't realise which of their tasks are suitable for MBO students. A concrete list helps internship coordinators start the conversation:

| Sector | Example task | Skills practised |
|---|---|---|
| Logistics | Weekly analysis of picking errors or delivery delays in a warehouse management system | Data extraction, spreadsheets, basic analysis |
| Manufacturing | Collecting and visualising measurements from a production line for the quality department | Data collection, charts, process understanding |
| Healthcare operations | Updating dashboards on bed occupancy or appointment no-shows | Reporting tools, data accuracy |
| Agriculture and horticulture | Recording and comparing sensor readings from greenhouses or fields | Sensor data, spreadsheets, domain knowledge |
| IT and service companies | Cleaning and checking customer or asset data before migration | Data quality, structured working |
| Facilities and energy | Analysing energy consumption per building or installation | Data analysis, reporting |

None of these tasks requires advanced modelling, but each builds exactly the practical data skills that lead to junior data roles.

## Briefing Internship Coordinators and Teachers

A short briefing of about an hour prepares staff to recognise and discuss data-related opportunities:

1. **What applied data roles look like** in regional sectors, with two or three real vacancy examples.
2. **Which skills employers ask for,** such as SQL, spreadsheets, reporting tools and basic statistics.
3. **How to ask employers about data tasks** without overselling students' abilities.
4. **Where students can find vacancies** by distance from home and category.
5. **How to handle student data** when discussing placements with employers.

Coordinators who understand what "data work" means in practice are far more convincing than a general statement that "AI is important."

## An Annual Rhythm for the Partnership

| Moment | Action |
|---|---|
| Start of the school year | Career orientation session including data-related roles in the region |
| Before internship placement | Employer conversations include a question about data tasks and vacancies |
| Mid-year | Short check with employers hosting students in data-related placements |
| End of the school year | Graduates receive a link to local data vacancies and follow-up training options |
| After graduation | Alumni survey includes a question on roles with a data component |

## Measuring Whether It Works

Simple indicators help an institution judge the effect without a large research project:

- Number of internship or BBL places with a data-related component.
- Number of graduates starting in roles with a data component, from alumni or exit surveys.
- Employer feedback on students' data skills during internships.
- Student awareness of data-related career paths, measured with one question in an existing survey.

## Why This Matters for Regional Employers Too

For regional employers, MBO graduates with practical data skills are often the most realistic way to fill operational data roles. Many SMEs outside the Randstad struggle to attract university graduates, who frequently move to larger cities after graduation. MBO graduates more often stay in their region, know local companies from internships and bring hands-on process knowledge from day one. When MBO institutions actively connect students to data-related placements and vacancies, employers gain access to a talent pool that is both regionally rooted and increasingly digitally skilled. That shared interest makes the partnership easier to sustain: employers have a reason to offer placements, institutions have a reason to keep signalling opportunities, and students see a concrete path from their programme into growing roles in their own region.

## Key Takeaways

- Applied data roles in logistics, manufacturing, agriculture and healthcare operations connect well to technical MBO programmes.
- SBB, MBO Raad, the Regional Investment Fund MBO, Centres for Innovative Craftsmanship, practorates, BBL and Associate degrees already connect MBO with employers.
- Digital literacy requirements and the AI Act's AI literacy obligation make data and AI skills increasingly relevant for MBO graduates.
- Start small: career orientation references, one extra question in employer conversations and periodic curriculum signals.
- Be honest about additional learning, protect student data and measure a few simple indicators.
