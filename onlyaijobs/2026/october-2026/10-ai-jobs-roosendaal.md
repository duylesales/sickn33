Roosendaal lies close to the Belgian border and has developed, partly thanks to that location, into a concentration point for distribution centres — a sector that is becoming increasingly data-driven, even though the city is never mentioned in conversations about "Dutch AI cities."

## What Kind of Data Work Actually Happens Here

Inventory forecasting, route planning and cross-border logistics optimisation are the core of this work — technically comparable to what you see in Tilburg or Rotterdam, but specifically focused on the complexity of cross-border distribution.

## Why This Work Stays Underexposed

Distribution centres rarely compete for visibility on general job platforms, and job titles such as "Supply Chain Planner" rarely reveal the underlying modelling component.

## Why Roosendaal's Location Matters

Roosendaal sits in West Brabant, in a corridor between three major logistics nodes: the **Port of Rotterdam** to the north, the **Port of Antwerp-Bruges** to the south, and the **Port of Moerdijk** close by. The A58 and A17 motorways connect the city to both the Randstad and Belgium, and Roosendaal is a railway junction with a direct connection towards Antwerp. For distribution companies serving the Benelux and wider European markets, this means short lead times to two of Europe's largest seaports and to a dense consumer market on both sides of the border.

## The Named Places and Organisations Behind the Logistics Concentration

**Borchwerf business parks.** The Borchwerf I and II business parks, developed jointly by Roosendaal and neighbouring Halderberge, are among the largest logistics locations in the region, with direct access to motorways and rail.

**Port of Moerdijk.** A major inland and seaport and industrial area in West Brabant, with container, bulk and chemical activities, and a hub for barge and rail connections.

**REWIN West-Brabant.** The regional development agency for West Brabant supports business development and innovation, including in logistics and the biobased economy.

**Logistics Community Brabant.** A cooperation of education, business and government focused on logistics innovation and talent development in Brabant.

**Breda University of Applied Sciences (BUas).** Offers logistics management programmes and applied research in logistics and mobility, making it one of the main talent sources for the region.

**Avans University of Applied Sciences.** With campuses in Breda and elsewhere in Brabant, Avans trains professionals in areas including business, technology and informatics.

## The Entity Landscape by Type

**Large distribution centres of international retail and logistics companies** have located here specifically because of the proximity to the Belgian border, creating a concentration of inventory and route-planning work larger than the city's reputation would suggest.

**Regional transport companies** handling cross-border freight add a second layer with their own specific needs around planning and compliance.

**Suppliers to these distribution centres**, often smaller local firms, form a third, less visible layer that rarely appears on any job platform.

## Where Data and Machine Learning Are Applied

| Application | Typical data | Techniques |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory forecasting | Sales, orders, promotions, seasonality per market | Hierarchical time series, gradient boosting |
| Route planning | Orders, delivery windows, vehicle capacity, tolls | Vehicle routing, metaheuristics |

| Port and hinterland planning | Container arrivals, barge and rail schedules | Forecasting, synchromodal optimisation |
| Returns processing | Return reasons, product data | Classification, forecasting |

**Synchromodal logistics** deserves a mention: choosing flexibly between truck, barge and rail for each shipment based on real-time conditions. With two seaports and barge and rail connections within reach, West Brabant is a natural setting for this kind of optimisation.

## Regulation and Cross-Border Rules That Shape the Models

- **EU driving and rest time rules.** Hard constraints on how long truck drivers may drive and when they must rest — every route planning model must respect them.
- **Belgian kilometre charge for trucks.** Belgium charges trucks per kilometre driven on its roads, which affects route choice for cross-border transport. The Netherlands has announced its own truck charge, adding a further cost factor.
- **Electronic consignment note (e-CMR).** Digital transport documents replace paper consignment notes, generating structured data that can feed planning and tracking.
- **VAT and customs.** Intra-EU trade between the Netherlands and Belgium involves VAT rules for cross-border supplies, and goods from outside the EU arriving through Antwerp or Rotterdam involve customs procedures.
- **Different public holidays and delivery expectations.** Dutch and Belgian holidays and consumer habits differ, which directly affects demand forecasts.

## A Concrete Scenario of Cross-Border Logistics Complexity

A distribution centre in Roosendaal ships goods daily serving both the Dutch and Belgian markets, with different regulations, public holidays and delivery expectations on either side of the border. A forecasting model for this operation must account not only for standard seasonal effects, but also for how Belgian and Dutch consumer patterns differ — a layer of complexity that doesn't exist at a purely domestic distribution centre, and which makes the work here more interesting than the title "supply chain planner" suggests.

## A Common Misconception About Why Border Regions Would Be Less Attractive

Candidates sometimes assume working in a border region brings complications that make it less attractive than working in a purely Dutch region — extra regulations, possible language barriers. In practice, this complexity is exactly what makes the work technically more interesting, and companies dealing with it every day often develop more refined, robust modelling processes than comparable companies serving a single domestic market.

For a candidate who eventually wants a broader, internationally oriented career in logistics analytics, experience with the specific complexity of cross-border operations in Roosendaal offers a concrete, distinguishing point on a CV — a skill not every Dutch logistics employer can offer, and one that remains relevant if you later want to work more internationally.

## A Concrete Example: Planning for Promotions on Both Sides of the Border

Imagine a distribution centre in Roosendaal supplying supermarkets in both the Netherlands and Belgium. A retailer announces a promotion that runs in Dutch stores one week and in Belgian stores the following week, with different discount mechanisms. The demand forecast must separate the effect of each promotion from normal seasonality, account for Belgian public holidays that don't exist in the Netherlands, and anticipate stock-piling behaviour before the promotion ends.

The forecast then drives several downstream decisions: how much stock to order from suppliers, how to allocate labour across shifts in the warehouse, and how many truck departures to plan towards each country. If Belgian deliveries must also respect time windows imposed by city distribution rules, route planning becomes more constrained still. A data scientist who builds such a system touches forecasting, optimisation and operational planning at once — which is why these roles are more demanding than their titles suggest.

## Automation and Robotics in Distribution Centres

Distribution centres in West Brabant are investing in automation to deal with labour shortages and rising volumes. That includes automated storage and retrieval systems, goods-to-person picking, autonomous mobile robots and automated truck loading. These systems generate large amounts of operational data and create new optimisation questions: which orders to release to the automated system first, how to balance workload between robots and human pickers, and how to predict and prevent system stoppages.

For candidates, this means that logistics data roles increasingly overlap with robotics and control engineering. Experience with simulation — to evaluate automation investments before they are made — and with analysing equipment performance data is becoming a clear advantage.

## Labour Market Context

Logistics in Brabant faces a persistent shortage of both operational staff and specialists. For data professionals, that shortage has two consequences. First, employers value data work that directly reduces the need for scarce labour, such as better labour planning and automation support. Second, candidates with logistics data skills have a relatively strong negotiating position, especially outside the big cities where fewer specialists live. Ask about training budgets, hybrid working arrangements and development paths during negotiations — regional employers are often more flexible than candidates expect.
