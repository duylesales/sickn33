Dutch border regions — Limburg, Twente, the Achterhoek, parts of Brabant, Groningen and Zeeland — sit inside long-established Euregio cross-border cooperation structures with Belgium and Germany, covering everything from infrastructure to healthcare. Job visibility for AI and data roles rarely benefits from that same cooperation, even though the underlying candidate pools genuinely overlap across the border.

## Why This Gap Exists

National job boards default to national borders as a matter of design, even in regions where a candidate's realistic commute radius crosses into a neighbouring country. A candidate in Enschede or Maastricht may have relevant employers just across the border that a Dutch-only search structurally never surfaces.

## The Named Euregios Along the Dutch Border

Cross-border cooperation along the Dutch border is organised in several Euregios, each with its own governance and track record:

| Euregio | Area | Notable characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **EUREGIO** | Twente, Achterhoek and parts of Drenthe with the German Münsterland and Emsland areas; secretariat in Gronau | Founded in 1958, often described as the first Euregio in Europe |
| **Ems Dollart Region** | Northern Netherlands (Groningen, Drenthe, Friesland) and north-west Lower Saxony | Cooperation between the northern provinces and German districts |
| **Euregio Rhine-Waal** | Gelderland and parts of Brabant and Limburg with the Lower Rhine area of North Rhine-Westphalia; based in Kleve | Includes the Arnhem–Nijmegen region |
| **Euregio Meuse-Rhine** | South Limburg, the Belgian provinces of Limburg and Liège, the German-speaking Community of Belgium and the Aachen region | A three-country region around Maastricht, Aachen and Liège |
| **Euregio Scheldemond** | Zeeland and the Belgian provinces of East and West Flanders | Cooperation across the Scheldt estuary |

Several of these regions also host universities with cross-border ties, such as the University of Twente with German partners, Rhine-Waal University of Applied Sciences in Kleve near Nijmegen, and Maastricht University in close proximity to RWTH Aachen University and the University of Liège.

## Existing Instruments That Already Support Cross-Border Work

**Interreg VI.** The EU's cross-border cooperation programmes — including Germany–Netherlands, Flanders–Netherlands and Euregio Meuse-Rhine — fund projects on innovation, labour markets and education. Several past projects addressed cross-border labour market barriers.

**GrensInfoPunten.** Cross-border information points in the border regions give independent advice to cross-border workers and employers on tax, social security, pensions and employment law — the practical questions that often stop cross-border moves.

**EURES.** The European Employment Services network supports job mobility in the EU, including cross-border partnerships in border regions where public employment services, trade unions and employers cooperate.

**Social security coordination.** Under **EU Regulation (EC) No 883/2004**, a worker is generally insured in one country. The **Framework Agreement on cross-border telework**, which the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany have signed, allows teleworkers who work less than half of their working time in their country of residence to remain insured in the employer's country, under conditions — an important enabler now that hybrid work is common.

## What Existing Cooperation Structures Could Extend To

**Shared visibility of vacancies across the border**, in regions where Euregio cooperation already exists for other purposes, would extend a working model rather than create a new one.

**Joint messaging to graduates and job seekers** about cross-border opportunities, coordinated between regional organisations on both sides rather than each working independently.

**A concrete example for existing Euregio cooperation bodies** to point to, since job visibility is smaller and simpler to standardise than the infrastructure or healthcare cooperation these bodies typically manage.

## What This Is Not

This isn't a claim that cross-border job seeking is simple in practice — language, tax and legal differences remain real. It's a narrower point: existing cooperation structures already handle harder cross-border coordination problems, so job visibility specifically is a comparatively easy extension.

## A Deeper Look at Why Job Visibility Fits Euregio Cooperation Well

Euregio cooperation structures typically exist because a problem doesn't respect the administrative border it happens to sit on — traffic, healthcare capacity, environmental management. Talent visibility fits this pattern precisely: a candidate's realistic commute radius in a border region genuinely crosses into Germany or Belgium, regardless of which side of the border their national job board defaults to searching.

## Comparing Border-Region Candidate Pools to Interior Ones

| | Interior Dutch region | Border region (Enschede, Maastricht, Nijmegen, Terneuzen) |
|---|---|---|
| Realistic commute radius | Fits within national job search defaults | Extends across a border national defaults ignore |
| Employer pool relevant to a candidate | Fully captured by a national search | Partially missed by a national-only search |
| Existing cooperation infrastructure | Less relevant to this problem | Already exists for other cross-border coordination |

## The Practical Barriers, Named Honestly

| Barrier | What it involves | Existing help |
|---|---|---|
| **Language** | Dutch, German or French may be required, although many AI teams work in English | Language courses in Euregio projects; English-speaking teams in tech and research |
| **Tax** | Double taxation treaties determine where income is taxed; home-working days can shift taxation | GrensInfoPunten, tax authorities' cross-border teams |
| **Social security** | Insurance in one country, with specific rules for telework | EU coordination rules, Framework Agreement on cross-border telework |
| **Recognition of qualifications** | Degrees may need evaluation, especially for regulated professions | National diploma recognition services; most AI roles aren't regulated professions |
| **Employment law** | Contracts are usually governed by the law of the country where the work is performed | GrensInfoPunten, legal advice |

For AI and data roles, the barriers are often lower than for regulated professions: many teams work in English, and data science isn't a regulated profession. That makes these roles a good test case for cross-border visibility.

## Why Starting Small — One Sector, One Border Segment — Beats a Region-Wide Launch

Rather than coordinating job visibility across an entire Euregio's economy at once, a more tractable starting point is one sector — AI, machine learning and data — in one border segment, proving the concept before expanding. This mirrors how many successful Euregio initiatives in other domains began: narrow and concrete, then expanded once the coordination mechanism worked.

## A Pilot Design in Five Steps

1. **Choose one border segment**, for example Twente–Münsterland or Maastricht–Aachen–Liège.
2. **Map employers on both sides** with AI and data roles, using existing Euregio and regional development contacts.
3. **Make vacancies visible across the border**, for example by encouraging employers on both sides to list roles on a platform that shows exact locations and commuting distance regardless of national borders.
4. **Coordinate messaging** with universities and universities of applied sciences on both sides, and link to GrensInfoPunten for practical questions.
5. **Measure for six to twelve months:** cross-border applications, hires and the questions candidates raise.

## Cross-Border AI and Data Opportunities by Border Segment

Each border segment has its own profile of AI and data employers, which shapes where a pilot is most promising:

| Border segment | Dutch side | Neighbouring side | Overlapping AI and data themes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Twente–Münsterland | University of Twente, high-tech and systems companies around Enschede and Hengelo | Universities and industry in Münster and the Münsterland | Robotics, sensor systems, manufacturing data |
| Nijmegen–Lower Rhine | Radboud University, health and semiconductor companies around Nijmegen | Rhine-Waal University of Applied Sciences in Kleve, logistics along the Rhine | Health data, logistics, agri-food |
| Venlo–Lower Rhine | Logistics and horticulture around Venlo | Logistics hubs in North Rhine-Westphalia | Supply chain analytics, greenhouse data |
| Maastricht–Aachen–Liège | Maastricht University, Brightlands campuses, Chemelot | RWTH Aachen University, University of Liège, industry in both regions | Health AI, materials science, industrial AI |
| Zeeland–Flanders | Terneuzen, Vlissingen, HZ University of Applied Sciences | Ghent University, port of Ghent within North Sea Port | Chemical process data, port logistics, energy transition |

## A Communication Checklist for Candidates

Joint messaging to candidates should answer the practical questions that otherwise stop cross-border moves:

- **Working language:** Is the team English-speaking, or is Dutch, German or French required?
- **Commute:** What is the realistic travel time, and is there public transport across the border?
- **Tax and social security:** Where will I be taxed and insured, and does working from home change that? Link to the relevant GrensInfoPunt.
- **Health insurance:** Which country's health insurance applies, and how does access to care work?
- **Recognition:** Is my degree accepted, and is recognition required for this role?
- **Salary and allowances:** How do salary levels and allowances compare with jobs on my side of the border?

## What Employers on Each Side Need to Know

Employers are often more hesitant about cross-border hiring than candidates. A short employer guide produced by the Euregio or regional development agencies can address the main concerns: payroll obligations when an employee lives abroad, the effect of home-working days on social security, the use of the Framework Agreement on cross-border telework, and where to get independent advice. Employers who understand that most of these questions have established answers are far more likely to open vacancies to candidates across the border.

## Why AI Roles Are a Good First Sector

AI and data teams in research institutions, high-tech companies and scale-ups often work in English, recruit internationally anyway and face tight labour markets on both sides of the border. RWTH Aachen, the University of Twente, Maastricht University and technical universities of applied sciences on both sides train graduates with overlapping skills. A cross-border pilot in this sector therefore faces fewer language and qualification barriers than most, while addressing real shortages.

## Reporting Results to Euregio Boards and Funders

Cross-border pilots often depend on support from Euregio boards, provinces or Interreg programmes, which expect clear reporting. A simple results framework helps:

| Indicator | Example measure |
|---|---|
| Employer participation | Number of employers on each side making vacancies visible across the border |
| Candidate reach | Number of candidates from the other side viewing or applying for vacancies |
| Cross-border applications | Applications where candidate and employer are in different countries |
| Hires | Cross-border hires resulting from the pilot, reported by employers |
| Barriers encountered | Most common practical questions raised, for example about tax or social security |

Reporting the barriers is as valuable as reporting successes: it shows cooperation bodies where further work on rules or information is needed, and it builds a stronger case for follow-up funding.

## Key Takeaways

- Dutch border regions belong to named Euregios — EUREGIO, Ems Dollart Region, Euregio Rhine-Waal, Euregio Meuse-Rhine and Euregio Scheldemond — with decades of cooperation.
- National job boards structurally miss employers just across the border, even though commute radii cross it.
- Interreg VI, GrensInfoPunten, EURES and EU social security coordination, including the telework framework agreement, already support cross-border work.
- Language, tax, social security, qualification recognition and employment law are real barriers, but often lower for English-speaking AI teams.
- Start with a pilot in one sector and one border segment, and measure cross-border applications and hires.
