Dutch border regions — Limburg, Twente, parts of Brabant and Zeeland — sit inside long-established Euregio cross-border cooperation structures with Belgium and Germany, covering everything from infrastructure to healthcare. Job visibility for AI and data roles rarely benefits from that same cooperation, even though the underlying candidate pools genuinely overlap across the border.

## Why This Gap Exists

National job boards default to national borders as a matter of design, even in regions where a candidate's realistic commute radius crosses into a neighboring country. A candidate in Enschede or Maastricht may have relevant employers just across the border that a Dutch-only search structurally never surfaces.

## What Existing Cooperation Structures Could Extend To

**Shared visibility of vacancies across the border**, in regions where Euregio cooperation already exists for other purposes, would extend a working model rather than create a new one from scratch.

**Joint messaging to graduates and job seekers** about cross-border opportunities, coordinated between regional organizations on both sides rather than each side working independently.

**A concrete example for existing Euregio cooperation bodies** to point to, since job visibility is a smaller and simpler thing to standardize than the infrastructure or healthcare cooperation these bodies typically manage.

## What This Is Not

This is not a claim that cross-border job seeking is simple in practice — language, tax and legal differences remain real. It's a narrower point: existing cooperation structures already handle harder cross-border coordination problems, so job visibility specifically is a comparatively easy extension.

## A Deeper Look at Why Job Visibility Specifically Fits Euregio Cooperation Well

Euregio cooperation structures typically exist because a problem doesn't respect the administrative border it happens to sit on — traffic, healthcare capacity, environmental management. Talent visibility fits this pattern precisely: a candidate's realistic commute radius in a border region like Enschede or Maastricht genuinely crosses into Germany or Belgium, regardless of which side of the border their national job board defaults to searching. The mismatch between the administrative border and the functional labor-market border is exactly the kind of gap these cooperation bodies already exist to close in other domains.

## Comparing Border-Region Candidate Pools to Interior Ones

| | Interior Dutch region | Border region (Enschede, Maastricht, etc.) |
|---|---|---|
| Realistic commute radius | Fits within national job search defaults | Extends across a border national defaults ignore |
| Employer pool relevant to a candidate | Fully captured by a national search | Partially missed by a national-only search |
| Existing cooperation infrastructure | Less relevant to this specific problem | Already exists for other cross-border coordination |

## Why Starting Small — One Sector, One Border Segment — Beats a Region-Wide Launch

Rather than attempting to coordinate job visibility across an entire Euregio's full economy at once, a more tractable starting point is one specific sector (AI/ML/Data, as discussed throughout this site) in one specific border segment, proving the concept before expanding. This mirrors how most successful Euregio initiatives in other domains began — narrow and concrete, then expanded once the coordination mechanism was proven to work.
