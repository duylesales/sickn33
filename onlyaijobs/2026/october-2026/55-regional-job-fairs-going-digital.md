An in-person regional job fair has a built-in filter: every employer present is physically located nearby, and every attendee has chosen to show up in that specific place. When such an event moves online — for reach, cost or convenience — that geographic filter often disappears unless someone deliberately rebuilds it.

## What Gets Lost in the Move Online

A generic virtual job fair platform typically shows employers and attendees from anywhere, which dilutes exactly what made the original regional event useful: a curated, local set of employers a nearby job seeker could realistically consider.

## What a Specialist, Address-Level List Restores

Pointing attendees towards a resource that shows AI, machine learning and data vacancies at exact address level, filtered by distance, restores the local specificity a generic virtual fair platform loses — without requiring the organising body to build that filtering capability itself.

## The Named Job Fair Landscape in the Netherlands

Job fairs in the Netherlands come in several forms, each with its own organisers and audiences:

**UWV job markets.** **UWV**, together with municipalities in the labour market regions, organises job markets and recruitment events, often through the regional **Employer Service Points**. These events usually target job seekers broadly and are organised regionally.

**University and student association company days.** Many universities and universities of applied sciences host company days, often organised by student associations, where employers meet students in specific disciplines. Technical universities typically attract many engineering and data employers.

**Sector and regional career events.** Business associations, regional development organisations and sector bodies organise career events focused on particular regions or sectors, such as technology or logistics.

**European online events.** The **EURES** network organises **European Online Job Days**, virtual recruitment events connecting employers and job seekers across Europe — useful for cross-border and international recruitment, but by design not locally filtered.

## How This Pairs With an Existing Event

**Before the event**, as a resource for attendees to research local opportunities beyond the specific employers attending.

**After the event**, as an ongoing reference point once the fair has concluded, since a job fair is a single date but hiring continues.

**As a complement, not a replacement**, for the employer booths and direct conversations that remain the core value of a fair, whether in person or virtual.

## A Deeper Look at Why Geographic Specificity Was the Hidden Value All Along

Organisers moving a job fair online often focus on replicating the visible features of the in-person event — booths, scheduled conversations, a registration system — without noticing that the event's real value partly came from an invisible feature: everyone present was already geographically filtered by having shown up in person. That filter has no obvious digital equivalent unless someone deliberately rebuilds it.

## Comparing Three Ways to Handle the Format Change

| Approach | What it preserves | What it risks losing |
|---|---|---|
| Move directly to a generic virtual fair platform | Scale, convenience | Geographic specificity |
| Stay in person only, accept smaller reach | Geographic specificity | Scale, convenience, wider access |
| Go virtual or hybrid, but add a local, address-level resource | Both scale and geographic specificity | Requires one small deliberate addition |

## Designing a Hybrid Regional Fair

Many organisers now combine formats. A hybrid design that keeps the local filter might look like this:

| Component | Format | Local filter |
|---|---|---|
| Employer presentations | Live-streamed and recorded | Only employers located within the region |
| One-to-one conversations | In person, with video slots for those who can't travel | Candidates indicate home location at registration |
| Vacancy overview | Online, before and after the event | Vacancies shown with exact address and distance from home |
| Workshops (CV, interviews, pay) | In person and online | Content tailored to the regional labour market |
| Follow-up | Email to attendees after two and six weeks | Links to new local vacancies in the relevant categories |

## Legal and Practical Requirements for Digital Events

- **Attendee data.** Registration, matching and follow-up involve personal data under the **GDPR**. Collect only what you need, explain how data is used, get consent before sharing attendee details with employers, and set a retention period.
- **Accessibility.** The **European Accessibility Act** raises accessibility requirements for many digital services, and public organisations in the Netherlands must meet accessibility standards based on the **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)**. Choose platforms with captioning, keyboard navigation and screen reader support, and offer recordings.
- **Pay information.** Under the **EU Pay Transparency Directive**, employers will have to provide pay range information to applicants before the interview. Encouraging exhibiting employers to show salary ranges in their fair materials helps attendees and prepares employers for the new rules.
- **Recording and consent.** Inform attendees and speakers if sessions are recorded, and don't record one-to-one conversations.

## Making AI and Data Employers Visible at General Fairs

Regional fairs often feature large, well-known employers in logistics, healthcare or manufacturing whose data roles aren't obvious from their booths. Organisers can help by:

- **Asking exhibitors in advance** whether they have data, IT or AI-related vacancies, and tagging them in the fair programme.
- **Organising a short "data jobs in our region" session** where two or three employers explain what data roles involve in practice.
- **Linking to category-filtered vacancy lists**, so attendees interested in data work can find relevant roles beyond the exhibitors present.

## Measuring Whether the Digital Format Works

| Indicator | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Share of attendees living within the region | Whether the local filter is preserved |
| Number of conversations booked per employer | Engagement beyond passive viewing |
| Clicks on the local vacancy list after the event | Whether the ongoing resource is used |
| Employer-reported applications from attendees | Real outcomes, collected through a short employer survey |
| Accessibility feedback | Whether the format excludes groups of attendees |

## A Before–During–After Checklist for Organisers

**Before the event**

- Ask exhibitors whether they have data, IT or AI-related vacancies, and whether they can show exact locations and salary ranges.
- Ask registrants for their home region and areas of interest, with a clear privacy statement.
- Send registrants a link to a local, category-filtered vacancy list, so they can prepare questions for employers.
- Check that the platform meets accessibility requirements and offers captions.

**During the event**

- Prioritise one-to-one slots for local candidates when capacity is limited.
- Mention the local vacancy list in opening and closing sessions.
- Offer a short session explaining what data roles involve in regional sectors.
- Monitor technical issues and have a fallback, such as phone contact, for one-to-one conversations.

**After the event**

- Send attendees a follow-up email after two weeks with links to new local vacancies and recordings of presentations.
- Ask exhibitors how many relevant conversations and applications resulted.
- Delete attendee data according to the stated retention period.
- Evaluate the share of local attendees and adjust registration or promotion for the next edition.

## Communicating With Exhibiting Employers

Employers often measure a job fair by the number of conversations, not by their relevance. Organisers can improve outcomes by setting expectations in advance:

- **Explain the audience:** where attendees live, which disciplines and experience levels they represent.
- **Encourage concrete vacancies:** booths or virtual rooms with specific, current roles attract more meaningful conversations than general employer branding.
- **Recommend visible salary ranges:** they save time on both sides and prepare employers for pay transparency rules.
- **Suggest follow-up:** employers who contact promising candidates within a week convert far more conversations into applications.
- **Share results:** a short report after the event helps employers decide whether to return, and helps organisers improve the format.

## Why This Insight Applies Beyond AI and Data Job Fairs

While this site focuses on AI, machine learning and data hiring, the underlying insight — that moving an inherently local event online silently discards its geographic filter unless someone rebuilds it — applies to any regional job fair in any industry. Organisers of fairs for other sectors face the identical structural problem and could apply the same fix.

## A Concrete Scenario

A regional business association in the east of the Netherlands moves its annual technology career fair to a hybrid format. In previous years, the in-person fair drew mainly local visitors. After the first fully virtual edition, organisers notice many registrants live far outside the region and employers report few relevant conversations. For the next edition, they ask registrants for their home region, prioritise local candidates for one-to-one slots, and send all attendees a link to address-level vacancy lists in categories such as Development, Machine Learning and Infrastructure. Employers report more relevant conversations, and the vacancy list continues to generate clicks weeks after the event.

## Questions to Ask Virtual Event Platform Vendors

Choosing a platform determines much of what is possible. Before signing a contract, ask vendors:

1. **Can registrants indicate their home region or postcode area, and can we filter or prioritise by it?** Without this, restoring the local filter is difficult.
2. **Can employers show vacancies with exact locations and salary ranges?** This supports both local relevance and pay transparency.
3. **How does the platform meet accessibility requirements?** Ask specifically about captions, keyboard navigation and screen reader support.
4. **Where is attendee data stored, and can we set retention periods?** Under the GDPR, you remain responsible for how attendee data is handled.
5. **Can we export anonymous statistics after the event?** Aggregated data on attendance, conversations and clicks is needed for evaluation.
6. **Does the platform support hybrid formats?** Many regional events benefit from combining in-person and online elements.
7. **What happens if the platform fails during the event?** Ask about support availability and fallback options.

A platform that answers these questions well makes it much easier to keep the regional character that made the original fair valuable.

## Key Takeaways

- In-person regional fairs contain an invisible geographic filter that generic virtual platforms remove.
- UWV job markets, student association company days, sector events and EURES European Online Job Days serve different audiences; only some are locally filtered by design.
- A hybrid design with local registration, prioritised local conversations and address-level vacancy lists restores specificity.
- Respect the GDPR, accessibility requirements under the European Accessibility Act and WCAG, and encourage pay ranges in employer materials.
- Measure the share of local attendees, booked conversations, post-event vacancy clicks and employer-reported applications.
