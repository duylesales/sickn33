Hengelo has a long history in defence and systems technology, and that history has produced a technical ecosystem around sensor data, signal processing and systems integration — work close to robotics in character, but with a specific focus on defence and security applications.

## What Kind of Work Actually Happens Here

Signal processing, sensor fusion and systems integration for defence and security applications require a combination of machine learning and systems engineering that is rarely presented in AI vocabulary, partly because of the nature of the sector.

## Why This Work Feels Less Accessible

Defence-related work often has stricter screening requirements and less public visibility than an average vacancy, which can create the impression that the sector is inaccessible — while regular technical vacancies do exist.

## The Named Organisations Behind Hengelo's Technology Base

**Thales Nederland.** The Dutch branch of the international Thales group has its headquarters in Hengelo. Thales Nederland is known for naval radar, sensor and combat management systems used by navies worldwide. Radar and sensor systems are among the most demanding signal processing applications that exist: detecting and tracking objects in cluttered environments, separating real targets from noise and interference, and doing so in real time. Machine learning increasingly complements classical signal processing in classification and tracking.

**Siemens Energy.** Hengelo has a long engineering tradition that goes back to the Stork company. Today, Siemens Energy produces industrial compressors and related equipment in Hengelo, with data work in design, testing and condition monitoring of rotating machinery.

**Nobian.** The industrial salt and chlor-alkali producer operates a salt production site in Hengelo, where salt is extracted from underground layers. Process data, subsurface monitoring and energy optimisation are relevant data topics.

**Twence.** The waste-to-energy and sustainable energy company based in Hengelo converts waste into electricity, heat and raw materials — an industrial process with its own optimisation and emissions monitoring needs.

**University of Twente.** Located in Enschede, next to Hengelo, the university has strong research in robotics, electrical engineering, signal processing and data science, with its Digital Society Institute connecting technology with societal applications.

**Saxion University of Applied Sciences.** With campuses in Enschede and Deventer, Saxion trains engineers and ICT professionals and runs applied research in areas including mechatronics and smart industry.

**Kennispark Twente.** The innovation district around the University of Twente hosts spinoffs and technology companies and links academic research to the regional industry, including Hengelo.

**NIDV.** The Netherlands Industries for Defence & Security association connects Dutch defence and security companies with the Ministry of Defence and knowledge institutions.

## The Entity Landscape by Type

**Defence-related systems companies**, with a long history in signal processing and sensor technology, form the core of this market and recruit for both regular technical roles and roles with specific screening requirements.

**Suppliers in precision electronics and sensor technology**, which grew up around these larger systems companies, add a second layer with comparable technical challenges but less strict entry requirements.

**Industrial companies** in energy, process technology and waste-to-energy add a civil industrial layer with sensor and process data work.

**Research collaborations** with knowledge institutions in the wider Twente region connect Hengelo's defence focus with the more academic robotics ecosystem around Enschede.

## What Technically Distinguishes Signal Processing From Regular Data Science

To understand why Hengelo's labour market asks for a different kind of candidate than an average data science role, it helps to look concretely at what makes signal processing different.

Where a classic machine learning problem usually starts with a table of features and labels, signal processing starts with a continuous stream of measurements in which the relevant information often lies not in individual values but in patterns over time and frequency. Transforming that raw stream into something a model can work with meaningfully requires knowledge of techniques — filtering, frequency analysis, noise suppression — that most data science programmes cover only in passing, but which form the core of the discipline here. A candidate who is excellent at training models but has never worked with this kind of preprocessing often discovers that the modelling itself represents only a fraction of the actual difficulty.

Sensor fusion — combining multiple independent sensor sources into one coherent picture — introduces its own class of problems. Each sensor has its own noise characteristics, sampling frequency and moments of unreliability, and merging them requires explicit assumptions about how to handle conflicting measurements. Those assumptions are rarely purely statistical; they also reflect engineering knowledge about how the sensors behave in practice under different conditions.

Finally, real-time processing plays a bigger role here than in almost any other segment discussed on this site. A model that only produces an outcome after several seconds is simply unusable for many applications in this domain, however accurate it is. This hard time constraint forces design choices — simpler models, optimised implementations, sometimes processing directly on embedded hardware — that would never be needed in an environment without that constraint.

## How Screening and Entry Requirements Actually Work in This Sector

For candidates considering this direction, it helps to understand realistically how entry requirements work, because there is a lot of confusion that deters candidates unnecessarily.

Not every role in this domain requires extensive screening. A considerable part of the work — especially in civil applications, general systems engineering and suppliers — has no special entry requirements beyond what is usual at any technical employer. Candidates who consider the whole segment inaccessible because part of it deals with sensitive applications unnecessarily exclude themselves from a large share of the available roles.

For roles that do require additional screening, it is important to know that this is usually a process initiated and managed by the employer after a positive hiring decision, not a condition you must meet yourself before applying. In the Netherlands, positions involving access to classified information can be designated as positions of trust, for which the Military Intelligence and Security Service may carry out a security investigation leading to a **Certificate of No Objection** (Verklaring van Geen Bezwaar). Candidates can generally apply and treat the screening process as part of hiring.

What is realistic to take into account is that such processes take time, so the application process in this sector can be longer than at a regular technical employer. Candidates in talks with several employers at once should factor this timing difference into their planning.

**Export controls add a further layer.** Technology that can be used for both civil and military purposes falls under the **EU Dual-Use Regulation (EU) 2021/821**, and military goods are subject to strict export licensing. For employees, this can affect which projects they may work on, and in some cases nationality or residence may be relevant for access to specific technology.

## The Wider Twente Context and What It Means for Your Career

Hengelo doesn't function as an isolated labour market but as part of the wider Twente technical ecosystem, in which Enschede with its university robotics tradition is the most visible component. For a candidate, this means the relevant search radius almost always includes both cities, and that career moves within the region are possible without moving house.

This interconnection also has a substantive dimension. The underlying technical skills sought in Hengelo's systems technology cluster overlap considerably with what the robotics spinoffs around Enschede look for: perception, sensor fusion, real-time processing, systems integration. An engineer who develops these skills in one context can largely take them to the other.

At the same time, it is honest to note that this is a relatively small, specialised community. The number of employers offering this specific kind of work is limited, which means the professional reputation you build within that community weighs more heavily than in a large, anonymous market.

## Who This Specialisation Suits in the Long Term

The skills this segment requires are deep but relatively narrow, which deserves an honest assessment before investing in them. An engineer who works in signal processing and systems integration for ten years builds expertise that is particularly valuable within this domain, but less directly transferable to, say, a consumer-facing data science role than a broader, more generic profile would have been.

For candidates intrinsically motivated by physical systems, measurement problems and the puzzle of reliable interpretation under uncertainty, that is an excellent trade: the work is rich, the expertise is scarce, and demand is structural rather than cyclical. Increased European defence spending in recent years has further strengthened demand for engineers in this field.

## How to Prepare for an Interview in This Domain

Interviews in systems technology differ from interviews at a software company, and candidates who prepare for that perform noticeably better.

Expect questions that go deeper into your understanding of measurement uncertainty than is usual in a generic technical interview. Where a software-oriented interview would ask how you train a model, an interview here is more likely to ask how you would determine whether an unusual measurement is a real signal or a sensor artefact — a question you can only answer convincingly if you are used to thinking in terms of measurement chains, calibration and systematic errors.

Also prepare concrete examples of working with limited computing power or hard time limits. Even a modest project running a model on embedded hardware, or optimising a processing pipeline to stay within a time budget, gives you something specific to discuss.

Finally, show genuine interest in the underlying physical system. Interviewers in this domain quickly recognise the difference between a candidate who treats sensor data as an abstract series of numbers and one who is curious about what the system actually measures and why.

## The Relationship Between Defence-Related and Civil Work in the Region

A nuance often lost in conversations about this region is that the technical core of the work is largely the same, whether a specific application is civil or defence-related. Signal processing, sensor fusion and systems integration are general disciplines; the application context mainly determines the conditions under which you work, not the fundamental nature of the technical problem.

This has a practical implication for career planning: expertise built in one context is largely transferable to the other. What does differ are the practical aspects around confidentiality and what you can later say about your work. In some contexts, the ability to discuss your work publicly — in a portfolio, at a conference or in an interview with a future employer — is more limited than in civil work. That deserves an explicit question during the application process.

## Why This Region Remains Internationally Relevant Despite Its Size

Twente isn't a large region by Dutch standards, which sometimes leads candidates to underestimate its technical relevance. In narrow, deep fields such as signal processing and systems integration, relevance is determined not by the size of the regional economy but by the concentration of expertise — the same pattern visible in Leeuwarden's water technology cluster and in agricultural research around Wageningen. Expertise built in this region is visible to an international professional field, not only to Dutch employers.

## Accessibility and Search Radius

Hengelo is a railway junction: Enschede is about ten minutes away, Almelo around a quarter of an hour, and the international train between Amsterdam and Berlin stops in Hengelo. The A1 and A35 motorways connect the city with the rest of the Netherlands and Germany. A realistic search radius includes Enschede, Borne, Almelo, Delden and Oldenzaal, and — for cross-border workers — parts of Lower Saxony and North Rhine-Westphalia.
