A contract-to-hire model — first a temporary arrangement, with the intention of moving to a permanent contract if the match is good — can structurally reduce the risk of a wrong AI hire. It only works if both sides are honest beforehand about what the model is and isn't.

## When This Model Actually Makes Sense

**When the precise scope of a new role is uncertain.** If a team isn't yet fully sure what the role will involve in six months, a trial arrangement gives both sides room to work that out together without the weight of an immediate permanent appointment.

**For a first AI hire in a team without experience of this kind of recruitment.** A team hiring its first AI specialist may not yet have a good calibration point for what "good" means in this role — a trial arrangement gives room to discover it.

## Why It Doesn't Fit Every Situation

For a candidate who already has several permanent offers, a contract-to-hire route is often less attractive than a direct permanent appointment. Be realistic about this instead of applying the model everywhere by default.

## What Honest Communication Means Here

Be explicit beforehand about the criteria for moving to a permanent contract, and about the timeline on which that decision will be made. Vagueness here feels to a candidate like a disguised, extended probation period without clarity.

## The Three Contract-to-Hire Structures Used in the Netherlands

"Contract-to-hire" isn't a legal term in the Netherlands. In practice, it takes one of three forms, each with different legal consequences:

| Structure | How it works | Key legal points |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct fixed-term contract** | The employer offers a fixed-term employment contract, for example for six or twelve months, with the intention of converting it to permanent | Probation period rules, the chain rule, transition payment on non-renewal, higher unemployment insurance premium |
| **Via an agency or secondment company** | The candidate is employed by an agency and placed with the client, who may hire them later | The Waadi limits restrictions on the worker joining the client; a reasonable fee between agency and client may apply |
| **Via a freelance assignment** | The candidate works as a freelancer first, then joins as an employee | Risk of false self-employment under the DBA Act if the work is effectively employment |

## The Legal Frame for a Direct Fixed-Term Route

**Probation period.** Under the Dutch Civil Code, no probation period is allowed in fixed-term contracts of six months or less. For contracts longer than six months but shorter than two years, the maximum is one month; for contracts of two years or more and permanent contracts, two months. A contract-to-hire model therefore can't simply be "a long probation period" — the legal probation period is short, and the fixed-term contract itself becomes the evaluation period.

**Chain rule.** Under the chain rule (*ketenregeling*), a series of fixed-term contracts generally becomes permanent after more than three contracts or after a total duration of more than three years, unless a collective labour agreement provides otherwise. Plan the route so that the decision point is clear well before these limits.

**Transition payment.** If an employer decides not to renew a fixed-term contract, the employee is generally entitled to a transition payment. That means ending a contract-to-hire arrangement has a cost, which should be part of the employer's calculation.

**Unemployment insurance premium.** Since the **Balanced Labour Market Act (WAB)** took effect in 2020, employers pay a higher unemployment insurance (WW) premium for flexible contracts than for written permanent contracts, with rules that can lead to retroactive adjustment. A fixed-term start is therefore more expensive in premium terms than a permanent start.

## The Legal Frame for Agency and Freelance Routes

**Agency route.** Under the **Placement of Personnel by Intermediaries Act (Waadi)**, an agency may not prevent a worker from entering into employment with the client after the assignment ends. The agency and client may agree a reasonable fee for that transfer. This makes the agency route a legitimate contract-to-hire structure, provided the client budgets for the fee.

**Freelance route.** Hiring someone first as a freelancer "to see how it goes," while they work under the same conditions as employees, is risky. Under the **DBA Act**, the **Dutch Tax Administration** assesses whether a relationship is in fact employment, and it resumed enforcement from 2025. If the freelancer works under authority, within the team structure and for an open-ended period, the relationship may be reclassified as employment, with consequences for the client.

## A Deeper Look at Why Vague Criteria Do the Most Damage

A contract-to-hire route without clear criteria quickly feels to a candidate like a disguised, extended probation period — exactly the feeling the model should avoid. The damage isn't in the model itself, but in the ambiguity around it: a candidate who doesn't know what they're being assessed on can't prove themselves in a targeted way, and an employer who hasn't set criteria often falls back on a vague gut-feeling decision at the end.

## Comparison Table: Contract-to-Hire With and Without Clear Criteria

| Aspect | With criteria set in advance | Without clear criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate experience | Knows what they're assessed on | Feels left in the dark |
| Decision at the end | Substantiated, verifiable | Often gut feeling |
| Risk of disputes | Lower | Higher |
| Attractiveness to strong candidates | Higher | Lower |

## What Good Conversion Criteria Look Like for AI Roles

Criteria should be concrete, observable and within the candidate's influence. Examples for an AI role:

- **Delivery.** A defined first model, pipeline or analysis delivered and in use by a named stakeholder.
- **Quality.** Code reviewed and accepted according to the team's standards; documentation in place.
- **Collaboration.** Positive feedback from two named stakeholders outside the data team.
- **Scope clarity.** A jointly written description of what the permanent role will involve.
- **Process.** Evaluation moments at, for example, month two and month five, with the final decision communicated at least a month before the contract ends.

Write these down in the offer letter or a separate document, and review them together at each evaluation moment.

## Why This Model Specifically Fits a First AI Hire in a New Team

A team hiring its first AI specialist often doesn't yet have a good calibration point for what "success" means in the role. A contract-to-hire route gives room to discover those criteria with the candidate and make them explicit, instead of inventing criteria nobody in the team has tested against practice.

## The Candidate's Perspective

Strong AI candidates weigh a contract-to-hire offer against the security of permanent offers. Some things make the model more acceptable:

- **Competitive pay from day one.** Under the **EU Pay Transparency Directive**, pay ranges must be shared before the interview; a contract-to-hire offer below market level signals that the employer sees the candidate as a risk.
- **A clear decision date.** Candidates can plan around a known date; they can't plan around vagueness.
- **A meaningful project.** An interesting, well-defined first project makes the temporary phase feel like an opportunity rather than a test.
- **Honesty about why.** "We're hiring our first data scientist and want to define the role together" is a reason candidates accept; "we always start with a trial" isn't.

## When a Permanent Contract With a Probation Period Is the Better Choice

For many AI roles, a permanent contract with the legal two-month probation period is simpler and more attractive. It signals commitment, lowers the unemployment insurance premium and avoids a transition payment on non-renewal. Choose contract-to-hire only when there is a genuine uncertainty that a two-month probation period can't resolve — typically about the scope of the role rather than the candidate's ability.

## Using Categories to Describe the Role Clearly

Whichever structure you choose, describe the role precisely in the vacancy. On **OnlyAIJobs**, vacancies are grouped by type of work, such as Development, Machine Learning, Infrastructure or AI Product. If a role's scope is still uncertain, say so explicitly in the vacancy and explain which categories it may evolve towards — that honesty attracts candidates who enjoy shaping a new role.

## A Sample Conversion Timeline for a Twelve-Month Fixed-Term Contract

| Moment | What happens | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Before start | Criteria, evaluation moments and decision date agreed in writing | Signed offer letter with an annex describing the criteria |
| Month 1 | Onboarding, access to data and systems, first small task | Short check-in on expectations and early obstacles |
| Month 3 | First formal evaluation against criteria | Written summary: on track, concerns, support needed |
| Month 6 | Second formal evaluation | Joint description of what the permanent role will involve |
| Month 9 | Decision conversation | Permanent offer, or honest explanation and transition plan |
| Month 10–12 | Handover or transition to permanent contract | Contract signed, or orderly end with transition payment settled |

Making the decision at month nine gives the employee time to plan and the employer time to arrange the permanent contract before the fixed-term contract ends — and avoids an automatic, unplanned extension.

## A Checklist for the Offer Letter

- The structure used (direct fixed-term, agency or freelance) and why.
- The intended outcome: conversion to a permanent contract if criteria are met.
- The concrete criteria and how they will be assessed.
- Evaluation dates and the decision date.
- Salary, and whether it changes on conversion.
- What happens if the decision is negative, including notice and any transition payment.
- Who the candidate can contact with questions during the arrangement.

A candidate who receives this level of clarity is far more likely to accept a contract-to-hire offer — and far less likely to feel misled if the outcome is not a permanent contract.

## Key Takeaways

- Contract-to-hire suits roles with uncertain scope and first AI hires, not every vacancy.
- In the Netherlands it takes three forms: direct fixed-term contracts, agency placement or freelance assignments — each with different legal rules.
- Probation periods are short, the chain rule limits fixed-term series, non-renewal usually triggers a transition payment, and flexible contracts carry a higher WW premium.
- The Waadi allows agency workers to join the client, possibly with a reasonable fee; the freelance route carries false self-employment risk under the DBA Act.
- Set concrete conversion criteria, evaluation moments and a decision date in writing.
