⚗️ Process industry data work is consistently underrated by engineers who haven't encountered it. Polymer processing in particular: a product defined by properties — molecular weight distribution, crystallinity, mechanical strength — that emerge from processing conditions in ways related to physics but not simply calculable from it.

⏱️ And measurement lags production. Many quality properties need lab testing that takes hours, so by the time you know a batch is off-specification you've made more of it. That's exactly what soft sensors exist for: inferring the lab measurement from continuously available process data, in time for correction to matter.

🔄 The non-obvious insight: steady operation isn't where the value is. A plant running at a known good operating point isn't where problems occur. Grade changes — switching from one product specification to another — generate off-spec material and consume time, and a meaningful share of total waste arises there. It's inherently dynamic rather than steady-state, which makes it both harder and more interesting.

♻️ Newer problem class: recycled feedstock. Virgin polymer has specified properties; recycled material's composition depends on what was collected, how it was sorted, how many times it's been processed and what contaminants came along. Input variability becomes the central challenge rather than an inconvenience.
