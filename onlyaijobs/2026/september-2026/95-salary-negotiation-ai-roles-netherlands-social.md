💬 Salary negotiation in the Netherlands has one question you should ask before anything else, and most candidates don't: is this role under a collective labour agreement, or does the employer set pay freely?

Because the answer changes what conversation you're having entirely. Public sector, healthcare, education and many research institutes operate under agreements that define pay scales. In that case the amount is genuinely not theirs to grant — pushing on it is asking for something the person opposite you cannot give.

What IS negotiable there: which scale you're placed in, which step within it, whether prior experience justifies a higher starting step, scarcity allowances that exist in some agreements and are used less than they could be, training budget, conference attendance, and start date.

💰 The items candidates from other markets consistently omit from comparisons:
· Pension contribution — varies substantially between Dutch employers and can outweigh a salary difference entirely
· Thirteenth month — some pay it, some don't, and it's a material annual difference
· Holiday allowance around 8%, standard rather than a bonus
· Leave days, where five or ten days' difference is real compensation
· The 30% ruling for qualifying incoming employees — verify current conditions rather than second-hand information, the rules have been adjusted repeatedly

🎯 What actually determines your position: scarcity of your specific profile, not years of experience. A competent generalist data scientist isn't scarce. Someone who can formulate and solve a large routing problem, or has taken a medical AI product through regulatory approval, or can do statistical disclosure control properly — that's scarce, and the negotiation follows from it.

🇳🇱 On style: be direct. State a clear figure with short reasoning when asked. Evasion reads as game-playing here rather than strategy, and inflation damages credibility in a way that's hard to recover — Dutch employers check.

⏱️ Most under-used ask: a salary review at six months against defined expectations, rather than the standard annual cycle. Often easier for an employer to grant than a higher starting number, and sometimes worth more.
