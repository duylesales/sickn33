The most interesting ML problem in the Netherlands might not be in a data centre — it could be on a factory floor in Eindhoven.

In Amsterdam, most ML serves a screen. In Eindhoven, a large share serves a machine: a vision system inspecting parts, a model predicting equipment failure. Latency is a physical constraint, not a UX preference. Data is expensive and scarce. Failure has a cost with a unit.

That work is harder, less crowded, and rarely searched for — because most applicants filter it out before finishing the job description.

If you've only worked on screen-facing ML, Brainport's hardware-adjacent AI work is worth a look specifically because the constraints are different.
