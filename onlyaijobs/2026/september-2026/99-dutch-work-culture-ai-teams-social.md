🇳🇱 Every guide says Dutch people are direct. Almost none explains what that means concretely, so international arrivals work it out through a series of small misunderstandings. Here are the specifics.

📌 Directness is about content, not tone. A colleague will tell you your approach has a flaw — plainly, in a meeting, possibly to a room. They're describing the approach, not evaluating you, and that distinction is real even though it takes time to feel real.

It also applies upward. A junior engineer can tell a director a plan won't work, and that's expected rather than brave. If you come from a culture where that's career-limiting, this is the largest adjustment and the largest gain.

⚠️ The misreadings that actually damage working relationships:

· Hearing criticism of your work as criticism of you. Most common, most costly. Responding defensively signals that substantive discussion with you is expensive, and colleagues start routing around you
· Hearing a proposal as a decision. A manager stating a direction is often opening a discussion, not closing one. Stay silent because you heard a decision and you've missed the moment your objection was actively wanted
· Reading respected working hours as low ambition. Long hours signal poor planning here, not dedication — and visible overwork is often read as struggling
· Assuming part-time means less serious. A four-day week is normal at every level, including senior and including management

💬 On feedback: it arrives unprompted and in the moment rather than at review cycles. It's specific rather than a global assessment. Positive feedback is sparser than in some cultures — absence of praise doesn't mean dissatisfaction. And you're expected to give it too, including upward. A colleague who never disagrees is read as disengaged or dishonest.

🗣️ The language point that matters most: colleagues switch to English instantly, which is kind and is exactly why internationals never learn Dutch. But informal conversation — lunch, corridors, the kitchen — defaults to Dutch, and that's where context, relationships and the informal information about how the organisation actually works all live.

✅ What this culture is genuinely good for: technical disagreement is cheap. You can argue a design is wrong without political consequence.
