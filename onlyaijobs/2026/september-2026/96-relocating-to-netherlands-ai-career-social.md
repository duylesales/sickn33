🇳🇱 The Netherlands is one of the more accessible European destinations for an international ML professional — English in most technical workplaces, an established skilled-worker route, employers recruiting internationally at scale. But the thing that actually derails relocations isn't immigration. It's housing.

🏠 Specifically: you must register your address with the municipality to get a citizen service number, which you need for a bank account, health insurance and being paid correctly. Some landlords do not permit registration. An unregistrable address creates a cascade of administrative problems that are genuinely hard to unwind. Confirm before signing that you may register — this single check prevents the most common serious mistake.

⏳ And the sequencing trap: you want housing before you start, landlords want your employment contract first. Resolvable, but it needs planning. Most successful arrivals spend one to three months in temporary accommodation and search properly from inside the country. Costs more per month, dramatically reduces the risk of committing to something unsuitable sight unseen.

📍 The single most useful piece of advice: look outside Amsterdam and Utrecht. Supply is far better, prices are far lower, and the rail network makes many locations genuinely viable. Insisting on central Amsterdam makes the hardest part of relocating harder still.

📋 On the visa route — for non-EU candidates the employer is the applicant, not you. They must be a recognised sponsor. A small company that has never hired internationally may simply not be willing to become one for a single candidate, which is a commercial decision rather than a comment on you. Check sponsor status before investing effort in an application.

🗣️ Cultural things arrivals misjudge in both directions: directness isn't rudeness, working hours are genuinely respected, long hours read as poor planning rather than commitment, and a four-day week is normal at all levels including senior.
