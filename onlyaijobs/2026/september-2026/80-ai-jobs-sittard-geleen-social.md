🧪 Materials informatics is practised at scale in very few Dutch locations. This is the clearest one — a large integrated chemical site and a materials research campus occupying the same grounds.

🔬 The problem shape is unusual and worth understanding. Predicting a polymer's mechanical or thermal properties from its molecular structure and formulation, where the relationship is governed by chemistry but not simply calculable, and experimental measurement is slow and expensive. Formulation optimisation across a combinatorially large design space with expensive experiments — which makes it a sequential experimental design problem, where Bayesian optimisation and active learning are genuinely used rather than merely discussed.

📉 And the datasets are hundreds of formulations, not millions of examples. Every label required laboratory work. That rules out approaches depending on abundant data and makes data efficiency, uncertainty quantification and embedding domain knowledge essential rather than optional.

📚 An immediately valuable and overlooked problem: these companies hold decades of laboratory notebooks and test reports, largely unstructured. Extracting usable structured data from that archive is substantial NLP work with direct payoff.

🏭 On the industrial side, the interesting distinction is site-level versus plant-level optimisation. An integrated site has plants feeding each other, shared utilities, and disturbances that propagate — so optimising one plant in isolation can make the whole site worse. Accounting for the couplings is where the largest value sits.
