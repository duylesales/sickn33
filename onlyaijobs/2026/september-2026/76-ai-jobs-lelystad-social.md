🌾 Here's something non-obvious: the farmland around Lelystad was designed, not inherited. Drained from the IJsselmeer in the twentieth century and laid out according to agricultural engineering principles — large rectangular parcels, relatively uniform soil, systematic drainage, planned access.

🚜 That matters technically. Most European farmland evolved over centuries: irregular fields following old property divisions, with soil varying substantially within a single parcel. Precision agriculture there fights the geometry. Here, autonomous machinery has a far easier operating environment, remote sensing is simpler with regular boundaries, and experimental comparison is more tractable because there's less confounding variation between plots. It's why agricultural robotics trials concentrate in regions like this.

⚖️ The honest counterpoint: it also makes the region less representative. A model developed here may transfer poorly to the fragmented landscapes where most European agriculture actually happens.

🐄 Second domain most people never consider: livestock data. Per-animal time series across hundreds or thousands of animals — activity, rumination, yield, intake. Early disease detection from subtle behavioural change, where labels come from later veterinary diagnosis and are therefore delayed and incomplete. The subjects can't report symptoms, so inference from indirect signals is the only option — closer to machine condition monitoring than to human health data.

🔥 And the strategically growing part: agricultural emissions measurement. Diffuse, weather-dependent, expensive to measure directly — so estimated from proxies by models that must be defensible enough to withstand legal challenge, because individual farm businesses depend on the numbers.
