💊 Oss has an unusual history for a city of 93,000: it was a pharmaceutical research centre for decades. When the multinational owner scaled back, the site became a shared life sciences campus — and that changes what small companies can do.

🔬 Drug discovery needs screening equipment, analytical instrumentation and compound libraries a fifteen-person company cannot normally afford. Sharing them means small companies here do work technically comparable to large-pharma discovery research, which isn't true of most life sciences startup environments. The technical content at small employers is genuinely deep rather than thin.

⚠️ The adjustment that catches commercial ML people out: biological data reproducibility isn't a peripheral concern, it's central. The same assay in two labs, or the same lab in different weeks, can give meaningfully different results. Cell lines drift, reagent batches vary, published results frequently fail to replicate.

So training on aggregated public data means training on measurements that aren't strictly comparable. Apparent performance may reflect batch signatures rather than biology. A model that predicts published results well might be learning publication bias. Validate across independent sources, not random splits — and being able to discuss this credibly signals experience immediately.

🧬 On generative molecular design: synthesisability is the binding constraint. Generating structures that score well on a property model but cannot actually be made is easy and useless.
