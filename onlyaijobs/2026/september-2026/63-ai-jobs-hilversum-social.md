📺 Hilversum has 90,000 residents and contains close to the entire Dutch broadcasting industry — broadcasters, production facilities, the national audiovisual archive and a media tech layer, all within a few kilometres.

🎯 The most interesting constraint in this market: a public broadcaster's recommender cannot simply optimise engagement. It has a legal mandate covering plurality, minority viewpoints and reach across demographics — and the result has to be explainable to an editorial board and a regulator. That is a much harder problem than maximising watch time.

🎙️ Then there's Dutch speech recognition on a broadcast archive spanning nearly a century of changing recording quality and speech conventions. Off-the-shelf models underperform noticeably, so the fine-tuning work is real rather than nominal.

💡 Non-obvious career point: the public side often produces the more distinctive portfolio. Almost every recommendation engineer has optimised engagement. Very few have built a system that had to satisfy a diversity mandate and survive editorial scrutiny — experience that transfers unexpectedly well into regulated finance and healthcare.

⚠️ One honest caveat: Dutch is genuinely required for content-facing roles here. You cannot judge whether an automatic transcript is good without hearing the errors.
