A career service that advises students to "look locally first" before moving to the Randstad gives advice that is hard to act on without a concrete tool. Students hear the advice, but have no easy way to check what "local" actually contains in terms of AI or data vacancies.

## What the Problem Is, Exactly

A general job site shows place names, not the exact distance from home, and ranks by advertising budget instead of relevance to a student in a specific region. A career service that points to such a site is essentially giving the same generic advice the student already knew, without making it more concrete.

## What a Specialist, Address-Level Job Board Adds

A platform that shows AI, ML and data vacancies at exact address level, with the distance to a given location, makes the advice "look locally" checkable for the first time — in under a minute, without the career service having to build or maintain a database itself.

## How This Fits With Existing Work in Practice

**A mention in existing career communication.** A short reference in a newsletter or on a careers page takes little time and requires no new infrastructure.

**Support for existing employer engagement.** If the university already maintains relationships with regional employers, encouraging a free first vacancy listing is a small addition to an existing conversation.

**A concrete aid to career guidance, not a replacement for it.** This doesn't replace personal guidance — it gives students something concrete to check themselves between advice sessions.

## Three Concrete Forms This Partnership Can Take

**A mention on the faculty careers page**, next to existing references to internship offices and job sites — a small addition that doesn't require an approval process from an entire board.

**A short section in the graduation or alumni newsletter**, aimed at recent graduates who are still searching — exactly the moment when the advice "look locally" is most relevant.

**A tool mentioned during a career workshop**, where students are already learning how to apply — a natural moment to introduce a concrete instrument.

## Comparison Table: Generic Advice vs. a Concrete Tool

| Aspect | "Look locally" as generic advice | Reference to an address-level tool |
|---|---|---|
| Concreteness for the student | Low | High |
| Time to implement | None, it has already been said | A few minutes, adding a link |
| Measurability of effect | Not measurable | Potentially measurable via clicks/usage |
| Requires new infrastructure | No, but also no action | No — it already exists |

## Why Timing Matters Here as Much as the Tool Itself

Students are most receptive to this kind of concrete advice in a narrow window: the last months of their studies and the first months after graduation. A career service that only mentions it when first-year students arrive misses the moment it actually becomes relevant. For the greatest effect, link the mention to graduation moments and alumni communication, not to introduction weeks.

## A Concrete Scenario of How This Could Work in Practice

The career service of a university of applied sciences adds a short paragraph to its existing graduation newsletter: "Thinking of moving for your first job? First check which AI and data vacancies are open literally near you, at exact address level." This one paragraph takes the career service five minutes to add, is read by hundreds of graduating students who already open the newsletter, and may change the first step of their job search for some of them — from automatically looking at the Randstad to checking locally first.

## A Common Misconception About the Effort Required

Educational institutions sometimes postpone this kind of partnership because they assume it requires a formal cooperation agreement or an extensive approval process. In practice, the first step can be as small as a single paragraph in existing communication, without any formal agreement needed to start — the threshold to begin is much lower than often assumed.

## Why Small Interventions Like This Add Up to Real Impact Over Time

One paragraph in one newsletter changes little. The same paragraph, repeated year after year for each new graduating cohort, combined with similar mentions in career workshops and on the careers page, builds into a structural pattern in which regional searching becomes a normal, obvious first step instead of an exception — a change that doesn't happen all at once, but does add up.

## How to Combine This With Existing Alumni Policy

Many educational institutions already keep in loose contact with alumni through newsletters or LinkedIn groups, often without a clear, recurring purpose for that communication. Adding an annual or twice-yearly update on regional employment opportunities — including a reference to an address-level job board — gives that existing alumni communication a concrete, useful purpose that benefits both the institution and the alumnus, instead of remaining purely promotional.

## Why Small, Repeated Actions Work Better Here Than One Big Initiative

Institutions that set up a large, one-off partnership project run the risk that the initiative fades as soon as attention shifts to the next project. A small, recurring action — one paragraph, every year, in an existing newsletter — has a higher chance of actually lasting for several years, precisely because it doesn't require a separate, fragile structure that has to be maintained on its own.

## What Data Institutions Already Have About Where Graduates Work

The idea that students leave the region without knowing what is nearby doesn't have to remain a gut feeling. Dutch educational institutions have access to national monitors that show where graduates end up.

| Source | Who runs it | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **National Alumni Survey (NAE)** | Coordinated by the **Research Centre for Education and the Labour Market (ROA)** at Maastricht University, together with the sector | Labour market position of graduates of universities of applied sciences and research universities, including the fit between education and work |
| **National Student Survey (NSE)** | Studiekeuze123 | Student satisfaction, including preparation for the labour market |
| **Own alumni research** | The institution itself | Employers, sectors and places of residence of alumni |
| **Research on stay rates of international students** | Among others **Nuffic** | How many international graduates stay in the Netherlands after their studies |

By looking at these data per programme and region, a career service can substantiate whether graduates of AI, data science and computer science programmes disproportionately leave for the Randstad. That makes the conversation with the board, faculty and regional partners more concrete — and makes it measurable whether a reference to local vacancies makes a difference after a few years.

## A Fixed Place in the Study Cycle

The advice "look locally first" works best when it returns at the moments students make choices. An example of an annual cycle:

| Moment | Target group | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Start of the search for a graduation internship** | Third- and fourth-year bachelor's students, master's students | Reference to regional AI and data vacancies and internships at address level in the internship information |
| **Company days organised by study associations** | All students | Explicitly invite regional employers and point them to visible vacancy listings |
| **Career workshops** | Final-year students | A short demonstration: searching by distance from home instead of by place name |
| **Graduation newsletter** | Graduating students | One paragraph with a direct link |
| **Six to twelve months after graduation** | Alumni | Update on regional employment through alumni channels |

**Study associations as partners.** Many company days, excursions and in-house days are organised by study associations, not by the career service itself. Involve them: they have direct access to students and often already have contact with regional companies.

## International Students: A Separate, Large Opportunity

International students in AI and data programmes form a substantial group in many programmes, and their decision to stay in the region after graduating depends heavily on whether they find work.

- **The orientation year for highly educated persons.** Graduates from outside the EU can, under conditions, apply to the IND for a residence permit for the orientation year, allowing them to work freely and look for a job for a year. Career services that combine this with a concrete overview of regional vacancies increase the chance that this talent stays in the region.
- **Lower salary threshold after graduation.** A lower salary threshold applies to highly skilled migrants who start work shortly after their studies or orientation year. That makes them more accessible to regional SME employers than many employers think — information career services can pass on.
- **Employers with sponsor status.** International students need to know which employers are recognised sponsors. A vacancy text that states this saves them a lot of searching.
- **A lasting connection.** Nuffic's **Holland Alumni network** helps international alumni stay connected to the Netherlands; regional vacancy information fits well in that communication.

## Privacy: What Is and Isn't Possible

A partnership with a job board requires no exchange of student data, and it should stay that way.

- **Refer, don't share data.** A link on a careers page or in a newsletter shares no personal data. Passing on students' names, email addresses or study results to external parties requires a legal basis under the **GDPR** and isn't needed for this purpose.
- **Measure anonymously.** Use tagged links or anonymous click statistics to see whether the reference is used, without tracking individual students.
- **Be transparent.** State in the communication that it concerns an external service.

## An Example Paragraph for the Graduation Newsletter

> **Your first job in AI or data? First see what's nearby.**
> Many graduates assume that serious AI and data work can only be found in the Randstad. Yet our region has companies with exactly those roles — they are just often called something else, such as data analyst, planner or process analyst. Before you move, check which AI, machine learning and data vacancies are open within your commuting distance, at exact address level. International graduates: don't forget the orientation year for highly educated persons at the IND.

## Checklist for the Career Service

- The reference is on the careers page and in the internship information.
- Study associations are informed and invite regional employers to company days.
- The paragraph appears in every graduation and alumni newsletter.
- Usage is measured anonymously.
- There is a short annual evaluation with regional partners.

## Key Takeaways

- The advice "look locally first" only becomes effective with a concrete tool that shows distance from home.
- The National Alumni Survey, the National Student Survey and research on stay rates help substantiate where graduates go to work.
- Link references to fixed moments: internship search, company days, career workshops, graduation and alumni contact.
- International students are a large opportunity; point to the orientation year, the lower salary threshold and employers with sponsor status.
- Refer to vacancies without sharing student data, and measure usage anonymously.
