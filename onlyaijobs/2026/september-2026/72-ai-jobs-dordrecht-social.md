🚢 Dordrecht sits where the Rhine and Meuse systems meet, and that geography produces two technical domains invisible to anyone searching for ML roles by title: a dense maritime and shipbuilding cluster, and a national role in flood risk because the city is among the most flood-exposed urban areas in the country.

📉 One development turned a minor concern into an urgent market. Inland shipping depends on river depth. In dry summers, Rhine levels fall until vessels must cut loading substantially — and a ship loading at 60% carries 60% of the revenue for the same crew and fuel cost. Draught forecasting, loading optimisation under uncertain future water levels and modal shift planning became things companies actively pay for. Climate change converting a background condition into a live market for technical work.

🌊 Water safety work has a property most data work lacks: the events you must estimate are rarer than the observational record, so extrapolation has to be defensible under legal standards. Real-time forecasting carries high costs for false alarms and missed warnings alike.

🔧 And a useful compensation for messy sensor reality — because the physical systems obey well-understood physics, you can validate data against physical plausibility. Water doesn't flow uphill. Conservation of mass constrains what a network of flow meters can jointly report. That's leverage commercial datasets don't give you.
