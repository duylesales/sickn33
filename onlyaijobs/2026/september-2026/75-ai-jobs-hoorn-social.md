📍 Hoorn's local AI market is genuinely thin — a handful of recognisable roles at any time. But there's one thing that changes the picture, and it's easy to miss.

🌱 The area around Hoorn, extending toward Enkhuizen and Andijk, is one of the most important vegetable seed breeding concentrations in the world. Varieties developed here are grown across continents. And plant breeding has become a seriously data-intensive science.

🧬 Genomic prediction: high-dimensional regression where markers vastly outnumber tested individuals, with population structure creating confounding you have to handle properly. Phenotyping across thousands of experimental lines, increasingly by imaging rather than manual assessment. Trial design that separates genetic signal from spatial variation across a plot. And predicting how a variety performs in climates you haven't trialled it in — which is getting harder as climates shift.

🔍 Like the Wageningen agrifood market, it's hidden behind vocabulary. Roles appear as "breeding data specialist", "quantitative geneticist" or "phenotyping researcher" — never as machine learning.

🧮 Useful method that generalises: to check any regional market's real depth, count employers rather than vacancies. Under ten organisations in reasonable travel distance means a thin market regardless of how many roles happen to be open today.
