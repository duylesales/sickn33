🎓 The Netherlands produces far more research-trained people than academia can absorb, so this transition is well-trodden — and routinely handled badly in both directions.

Researchers undersell what genuinely distinguishes them and oversell what doesn't.

✅ What actually transfers and is genuinely scarce:
· Designing an investigation that can answer a question — most people in industry have never been trained in this. They test what's convenient, conclude from insufficient evidence, and don't notice
· Knowing when a result isn't real — multiple comparisons, selection effects, insufficient power, the difference between a finding and an artefact
· Quantifying uncertainty honestly rather than producing a point estimate with unwarranted confidence
· Working on something hard for years without external structure

❌ What actively hurts, and the first one is the most common mistake by a wide margin:
· Treating novelty as a value. A published contribution must be new; an industrial solution must work and be maintainable. Choosing a sophisticated approach where a simple one suffices reads as poor judgement, not capability
· Perfectionism — a good answer now often beats a better answer in three months
· Needing the question pre-defined, when in industry it's often poorly specified and changes
· Expecting academic status to carry. Nobody will be impressed by a venue they don't recognise
· Treating engineering as beneath you. Visible within a week, ends your credibility

✍️ How to translate your record: lead with the problem and outcome, not the method. Not "I worked on variational inference for hierarchical models" but "I built a method to estimate effects where group-level data was sparse, which let us conclude from a much smaller sample." Same work, described by what it achieved.

And foreground the engineering. Almost every research project involves substantial software and data infrastructure. You built it and you probably don't mention it.

🎯 Target sectors where research training is the scarce input rather than an incidental credential: semiconductor metrology, drug discovery, materials informatics, medical imaging, official statistics. Retail and consumer tech value it least — honestly.
