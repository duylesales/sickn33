Posting a vacancy on Indeed takes a few clicks. Wading through the pile of CVs from candidates who have "IT" in their profile but have never trained a model takes half a working day. That second part is rarely counted as a cost, but it is exactly where things go wrong when you post an AI, ML or data vacancy on a general platform.

This is not a criticism of the candidates who apply. It is a predictable consequence of how a general job platform is built — and that immediately explains what needs to change structurally, not just how you write the vacancy text.

## Why This Is a Structural Problem, Not a Writing Problem

A general job platform makes money by creating as many matches as possible between employers and job seekers, because that keeps both sides — paying employers and job seekers who search for free — on the platform. Relevance is subordinate to volume. The result: the matching algorithm treats a word like "AI" or "data" as a way to increase reach, not to filter.

On top of that, visibility depends partly on budget: a vacancy from a small AI team with a modest budget is drowned out by a large company's "Software Engineer" vacancy that happens to mention machine learning in the fifth bullet point. Both vacancies compete for the same pool of applicants, most of whom are not specifically looking for either role.

## What This Actually Costs You

The visible problem is time: wading through a pile of applications of which only a small share is relevant. The less visible problem is worse — candidates who are specifically looking for AI/ML/data work scroll past hundreds of irrelevant IT vacancies on a general platform to find the few that fit. If your vacancy competes on the same results page with backend, DevOps and IT support roles, you are not just fishing in a crowded pond — the fish you want to catch have often already stopped fishing there.

## What a Specialist Platform Does Differently

**The audience has already filtered itself.** Everyone browsing OnlyAIJobs does so because they are specifically looking for an AI, ML or data role. You are not paying for broad reach and hoping relevance sorts itself out — the relevance comes before your vacancy.

**Visibility does not depend on budget.** Every vacancy is shown on equal terms, regardless of company size or advertising budget. A small team with an interesting AI problem is not drowned out by the bigger recruitment budget of a company with an unrelated vacancy that happens to mention the same keyword.

**Location does real filtering work.** Vacancies show the exact address and the distance from the candidate's home, not just a city name. Candidates who click through have already accepted the commute before they apply — fewer drop-outs from people who only discover how far the drive is when they see the address.

## Free Posting in the Netherlands: Which Channels Really Exist

"Post a vacancy for free" means something different on every platform. Sometimes posting is free but visibility is paid; sometimes the first post is free and the rest is not. An honest overview of the channels Dutch employers use for AI and data roles:

| Channel | What is free? | What do you pay for? | Suitability for AI roles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indeed** | Organic job listings | Sponsored jobs for more visibility | Large reach, little filtering by specialism |
| **LinkedIn** | Limited basic job posts | Promoted jobs and recruiter licences | Strong for approaching senior candidates directly, noisy for inbound |
| **werk.nl (UWV)** | Posting vacancies through the employer portal | — | Broad audience, rarely the source of experienced ML engineers |
| **Nationale Vacaturebank** | Limited | Paid packages | General Dutch-language reach |
| **University and university of applied sciences career portals** | Usually free for employers | Sometimes events or career days | Strong for internships, graduates and junior roles |
| **Meetups and communities** | Showing up | Sponsorship or speaking slots | Slow, but good for credibility with engineers |
| **OnlyAIJobs** | First vacancy | Not yet publicly communicated for further vacancies | AI, ML and data candidates only, with distance from home |

The point is not that one channel replaces all others. The point is that "free" is only worth something if the applications that come in are relevant. A free vacancy that produces forty unsuitable applications costs you more in screening time than a paid vacancy that produces three good candidates.

## What an AI Vacancy Must Contain to Attract Serious Candidates

A specialist audience reads a vacancy more critically than a general audience. Experienced AI and data professionals scan for a few concrete signals, and vacancies without those signals are skipped, regardless of the platform.

**A salary indication.** A range or a reference to a salary scale from a collective labour agreement (for example the CAO Metalektro in manufacturing or the CAO Rijk in central government) shows that you are serious. The **EU Pay Transparency Directive (EU) 2023/970** will also require employers to give applicants information about the starting salary or salary range before the interview, and bans questions about salary history. The transposition deadline for member states was June 2026; check the current status of the Dutch legislation.

**An exact location and the hybrid policy.** Not just "Eindhoven region," but the address, the number of office days per week and whether those days are fixed. That is exactly the information candidates filter on at OnlyAIJobs.

**The working language.** Is the working language Dutch, English or both? Many AI teams work in English. An unnecessary requirement such as "native Dutch speaker" excludes international candidates and may also conflict with equal treatment rules.

**Sponsor status for highly skilled migrants.** If you want to be able to hire candidates from outside the EU, your organisation must be a recognised sponsor with the **IND**. State this explicitly: it saves both sides time.

**Concrete work instead of buzzwords.** Which data, which models, which stack, which problem? "Working with the latest AI technology" says nothing. "Predicting maintenance moments based on sensor data from 400 machines, with Python, PyTorch and an MLOps pipeline on Azure" says everything.

**Team context.** Is this the first data scientist in the company, or will the candidate join a team of eight? For senior candidates this is often the most important question.

## Rules to Take Into Account as an Employer

**NVP Recruitment Code.** The recruitment code of the Dutch Association for Personnel Management & Organisational Development (NVP) is a widely used code of conduct for a careful and fair recruitment procedure. Many organisations refer to it in their vacancies.

**GDPR and retention periods.** The **Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens)** advises deleting applicant data no later than four weeks after the procedure ends, unless the candidate consents to longer retention, usually up to one year at most.

**Equal treatment.** The Dutch General Equal Treatment Act and the Equal Treatment in Employment (Age Discrimination) Act prohibit distinctions based on age, sex and origin, among other grounds. Phrases such as "young and dynamic team" create unnecessary risk; the **Netherlands Institute for Human Rights** assesses complaints about this.

**AI in your own recruitment process.** The **EU Artificial Intelligence Act** classifies AI systems used to filter or rank applications or to evaluate candidates as high-risk. Employers who use such systems take on obligations around human oversight and informing candidates as the relevant provisions apply. For an employer recruiting AI talent, it is also a matter of credibility: candidates ask about it.

## Common Mistakes When Posting for Free

- **Only giving a city name.** Without an exact address, the distance-from-home search cannot work, and a candidate only sees the commute in the interview.
- **Choosing the wrong category.** An ML role posted as a general "Development" role misses candidates who filter specifically on machine learning.
- **Leaving an outdated vacancy online.** A position that has been filled but stays online costs candidates time and damages your employer brand.
- **Giving no salary indication.** Experienced AI candidates in particular often skip vacancies without a salary range.
- **Not measuring.** Without a separate application link or a source question, you won't know afterwards which channel produced the good candidates.

### (Scenario: recruiter who just received hundreds of irrelevant applications through a general platform) Why do I get so many unsuitable applications when I post an AI role on a general platform?
General platforms are built to create as many matches as possible across their whole audience, not to filter for relevance to one specific role. A keyword such as "AI" or "data" is used to increase reach, not to narrow it, and ranking depends partly on advertising budget rather than fit.

### (Scenario: small AI team competing with large companies' recruitment budgets) Can a small company's vacancy really compete with large employers on a specialist platform?
Yes, structurally even more than on a general platform. Vacancies are shown on equal terms, regardless of company size or advertising budget, so a small team's vacancy is not systematically drowned out by a larger company paying for placement.

### (Scenario: recruiter unsure whether a new platform is worthwhile alongside Indeed and LinkedIn) Is it worth also posting on a specialist AI platform if I already use Indeed and LinkedIn?
It is a cheap test, not a replacement. The composition of the audience is fundamentally different — everyone visiting a specialist platform is specifically looking for AI, ML or data roles. Because the first posting is free, the only cost of testing is the time it takes to send a link.

### (Scenario: employer who doesn't know what is needed to post) What exactly do I need to post a vacancy?
A link to the vacancy on your own website or careers page, and the city or exact address where the role is based. The address powers the distance-from-home search for candidates, so being specific improves the match, not just the reach.

### (Scenario: recruiter wondering why an exact address matters) Why does showing an exact address instead of just a city change the applications I get?
Because candidates see the real commute before they apply, instead of discovering it at the first interview. That pre-filtering means those who do apply have already accepted the commute — fewer drop-outs at a later stage.
