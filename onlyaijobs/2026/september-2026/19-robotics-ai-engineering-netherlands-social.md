A perception model that works perfectly in a notebook can still fail on a robot — because the notebook doesn't have to run in real time while a physical arm is already moving.

Robotics AI roles combine ML with control systems, sensor fusion and often C++ or embedded constraints. Real-time inference isn't optional. Failure is physical, not just a worse recommendation. That's also why this category is genuinely rarer nationally, concentrated at specialized industrial-automation and research-spinout employers rather than the consumer-tech companies that dominate general job board advertising.

If your background spans both ML and systems engineering, that combination is rarer than either skill alone — say so explicitly.
