🛡️ Defence is a growing part of the Dutch technical market and the sector where candidates most need information they can't easily get. Three things worth stating plainly.

🔐 First, the clearance question. Screening is done by a national authority, not the employer, and it investigates verifiable history — residence, employment, finances — over years. The widespread belief that only Dutch nationals can work in defence is inaccurate as a general statement. What screening needs is a history it can verify. Someone who has lived in three countries in five years presents a harder verification problem than someone who hasn't, regardless of citizenship.

Some roles genuinely do have nationality requirements — those touching specific classified programmes or alliance-restricted material. That's role-specific and stated. And export control is a separate mechanism entirely: you can hold clearance and still be excluded from a project. Timelines are months. Ask in the first conversation; recruiters here answer this daily.

⚖️ Second, the ethical question, answered straight rather than dodged. The substantial majority of defence AI work concerns sensing, situational awareness, navigation, communications, logistics, maintenance, simulation and cyber defence. Work contributing directly to weapons employment exists — and it's identifiable, so you can ask specifically rather than avoiding the sector wholesale. Dual-use is genuine rather than a euphemism: the radar that detects a threat also detects a vessel in distress.

Refusing on principle is a coherent position. So is participating on the view that a country's defence capability should be built by people who take ethics seriously. Worth deciding with accurate information about what the work actually is.

📈 Third, it's expanding faster than its talent supply — so employers increasingly accept candidates without defence backgrounds and train them, because the alternative is unfilled positions.

🚪 Lightest access barriers: cyber security, particularly the commercial layer serving both defence and civil clients. Also the most transferable skills back out.
