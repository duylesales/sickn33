An internship with an AI team almost always produces a thesis. It by no means always produces an offer of a permanent job — and the difference rarely lies in how good the research itself was. It more often lies in what an intern did outside the defined thesis project.

## What Actually Makes the Difference

**Visibility beyond your own supervisor.** Interns who occasionally contribute to a team meeting, or briefly present their work to people beyond their direct supervisor, are remembered in a way a good thesis alone doesn't achieve. A permanent job is rarely decided by one person.

**Building something the team still uses after your internship.** A thesis that disappears into a drawer after your defence leaves few traces. A piece of code, a dashboard or a pipeline the team keeps using is a far more concrete argument for keeping you than a grade.

**Asking about what happens after the internship — early, not in the last week.** Teams often plan budget and headcount months ahead. An intern who only asks about a permanent position in the last week often comes too late for that planning, however good the work was.

## Why This Isn't a Matter of "Working Harder"

Two interns of comparable technical quality can have very different outcomes, purely because one was visible and timely and the other wasn't. That is neither unfair nor fair — it is simply how hiring decisions come about in practice, and it is something you can influence as an intern.

## A Timeline of What Successful Interns Do Differently

**Weeks 1–2:** Asking how previous interns moved on, and who besides their own supervisor is involved in any hiring decision.

**Months 1–2:** Actively looking for a small, well-defined piece of work separate from the thesis itself that the team can use immediately — a cleaned dataset, a small dashboard, a reusable function.

**Months 2–3:** Briefly and informally sharing progress with people beyond the direct supervisor — a short update in a team meeting, a message in a shared channel.

**Last month:** If the conversation about a permanent position hasn't started yet, actively asking about it — not as a demand, but as genuine interest.

## Comparison Table: Intern Who Gets Hired vs. Intern Who Doesn't

| Behaviour | More common among hired interns | More common among interns not hired |
|---|---|---|
| Visibility beyond the supervisor | Regular, small and informal | Rare, only at formal moments |
| Left something useful behind | Yes, also separate from the thesis | No, only the thesis itself |
| Asked about next steps | Early in the internship | Only in the last week, or never |
| Technical quality of the work | Comparable between both groups | Comparable between both groups |

The last point in this table is the most important: technical quality often hardly differs between those who are and aren't hired. The difference lies almost entirely in the other three rows.

## A Concrete Scenario of Two Interns Side by Side

Two interns start the same week in the same AI team, with a comparable technical background. Intern A works hard on the thesis and eventually delivers a technically strong piece of work, but in four months has barely had contact with anyone beyond their own supervisor. Intern B delivers a slightly less polished end result, but in those same four months has briefly presented something in a team meeting three times, left behind a small script the team has kept using, and asked in month two how previous interns moved on. At the evaluation at the end of the internship, it is often intern B who gets the offer — not because the work was better, but because more people in the team were able to form an opinion about them.

## A Common Misconception About What Drives a Hiring Decision

Interns often assume that a hiring decision is driven primarily by the quality of the graduation work, because that is the only tangible, graded result they have at the end of their internship. In practice, a hiring decision is rarely made by one person on the basis of one document — it is a collective impression built from many small interactions, of which the thesis is only one.

## What This Means for How You Should Approach an Internship

Instead of focusing all your energy on perfecting the thesis until the last week, it is more effective to deliberately reserve time from the start for visibility and for leaving something useful behind — not at the expense of the quality of your thesis, but as an equally serious part of how you shape your internship.

## What to Do If You Realise Halfway That You Haven't Been Visible Enough

If you are halfway through your internship and realise you have had hardly any contact with anyone beyond your supervisor, it is not too late to adjust — but it does require a conscious, slightly uncomfortable step: actively ask for a short opportunity to present your work to the wider team, instead of waiting for someone else to arrange it for you. Most teams are receptive if you initiate it yourself, while they rarely think of organising this for an intern who doesn't ask.

## Why This Pattern Keeps Repeating After Your Internship

The lesson described here — that visibility and leaving something useful behind weigh at least as heavily as the quality of your finished work — is not a one-off feature of internships, but a pattern that repeats throughout your later career at every review, promotion or consideration for a new role within the same company. Anyone who learns this lesson early in an internship and acts on it deliberately builds a habit that pays off at every subsequent stage of a career, not just at this one transition from internship to permanent job.

## The Formal Side of an AI Internship in the Netherlands

Moving on starts with an internship that is well arranged. A few Dutch ground rules determine how much room you have.

**The internship agreement.** An internship that is part of your programme is usually recorded in a three-party internship agreement between you, the company and your educational institution. It sets out learning objectives, supervision, duration, working hours and any allowance. An internship agreement is different from an employment contract: the learning objective is central.

**Internship allowance.** An internship allowance is not legally required in the Netherlands, and the statutory minimum wage does not apply to a genuine learning internship. Many companies do pay an allowance. Note: if an "intern" in practice simply does productive work without a learning element, the relationship can legally be regarded as an employment contract — which is exactly why the agreement and the learning objectives matter.

**Duration.** A graduation internship at a university of applied sciences often lasts around a semester; a university graduation project at a company usually runs from a few months to half a year. That window determines how much time you have to build the visibility this article describes.

**Confidentiality and intellectual property.** AI internships often work with company data and code. Companies therefore regularly ask for a non-disclosure agreement, and the internship agreement records who holds the rights to what you build. Sometimes your thesis gets an embargo or a confidential version. Arrange this early with your academic supervisor so your graduation isn't put at risk.

**Personal data.** If you work with customer or patient data, the **GDPR** applies. Ask which data you may use, whether it has been pseudonymised, and whether a record of processing activities or a data protection impact assessment applies. An intern who asks about this unprompted shows exactly the maturity a team looks for in a future colleague.

**International students.** Students from outside the EU who study in the Netherlands can usually do an internship as part of their programme with an internship agreement from their educational institution. Additional rules from the **UWV** and the **IND** may apply in other situations. Nuffic and the international offices of universities provide information on this.

## What Happens When You Get an Offer

The offer after an internship is rarely an open-ended contract straight away. Anyone who knows how Dutch employment law works can negotiate better.

| Topic | How it works in the Netherlands | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed-term or permanent** | Many first contracts are fixed-term. The chain rule usually allows at most three fixed-term contracts within three years before a contract becomes permanent | Ask about the prospect of a permanent contract |
| **Probation period** | Only in writing, at most one month for a contract of up to two years and two months for a permanent contract; not allowed for a contract of six months or less | After a successful internship, a long probation period is hard to justify; discuss this |
| **Non-compete clause** | Must be in writing; in a fixed-term contract only with a written justification of compelling business interests | Read the scope and duration critically |
| **Salary** | Collective labour agreement scale or individual agreement | Compare with other starter offers, not with your internship allowance |
| **Training budget** | Often agreed in a collective labour agreement or company policy | Important for growth in AI, where knowledge quickly becomes outdated |

## Alternative Routes If There Is No Permanent Position

Sometimes the team is enthusiastic, but the budget is missing. Then there are intermediate forms that keep the door open:

- **A working student contract** after your internship, if you are still studying: a part-time employment contract alongside your studies.
- **A traineeship or junior pool** within the same organisation, if it has vacancies elsewhere.
- **A fixed-term project contract** with a clear evaluation date.
- **A warm introduction** to the team's partners, clients or suppliers. A supervisor who has seen your work is your strongest reference.

In that last case, ask explicitly whether your supervisor is willing to act as a reference, and whether they know contacts at organisations that are hiring.

## What to Record in Your Last Internship Week

- A short handover of what you have built, so the team can use it without you.
- Your supervisor's contact details and the question of whether they are willing to act as a reference.
- An agreement about when you will hear whether a follow-up is possible.
- Permission to mention your work — insofar as it isn't confidential — in your portfolio.

## Key Takeaways

- A good internship agreement records learning objectives, supervision, allowance, confidentiality and intellectual property.
- An internship allowance is not legally required; an internship without a learning element can legally be an employment contract.
- If you work with personal data, ask about the GDPR yourself — it shows maturity.
- Know the rules on the chain rule, probation periods and non-compete clauses before you accept an offer.
- No permanent position available? Working student contracts, traineeships, project contracts and introductions keep the door open.

## Where to Start

When you start an AI internship, ask in the first month how previous interns moved on, and actively build something that remains useful after you leave — not just a thesis that ends up on a shelf.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: intern who thinks a good thesis grade is enough) Is a good thesis grade enough to be offered a permanent job?
Not always — a hiring decision is rarely made by one supervisor. Visibility within the wider team and building something that stays useful after the internship often weigh at least as heavily.

### (Scenario: intern who is shy about being visible) Why is visibility beyond my supervisor important?
Because a permanent appointment is usually a team decision, not that of one supervisor. People who have never seen your work can't make a case for keeping you.

### (Scenario: intern unsure when to raise a permanent job) When should I ask about the possibility of a permanent position?
Early in the internship, not in the last weeks — teams often plan budget and headcount months ahead, and a question asked too late often falls outside that planning.

### (Scenario: intern who only works on the thesis) Is it enough to focus entirely on my thesis?
The thesis is necessary for graduating, but says little about how useful your work is to the team after you leave. Building something that keeps running is a more concrete signal towards a permanent position.

### (Scenario: intern wondering whether this is unfair) Isn't it unfair that visibility counts for more than pure technical quality?
It is not a judgement about fairness — it is simply how hiring decisions come about in practice, and something you can influence as an intern by being visible early.
