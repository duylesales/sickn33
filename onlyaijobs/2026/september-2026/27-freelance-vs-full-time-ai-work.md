Freelance AI work in the Netherlands is often compared on hourly rate versus salary, as if that were the core of the choice. It isn't. The core is which kind of work and which kind of risk you are comfortable with — and that differs fundamentally between the two routes, regardless of what arrives in your bank account.

## What Freelance Work Actually Gives You

Variety in projects and clients, more control over your schedule, and often access to interesting, short-term problems that a company doesn't consider worth opening a permanent position for. The price: you are responsible yourself for acquisition, administration and periods without an assignment — things a full-time role simply takes away.

## What Full-Time Work Actually Gives You

Continuity to work on a problem over a longer period than one assignment allows, a team to be part of, and less operational burden outside the work itself. The price: less control over which project you do, and often less variety than freelance work can offer.

## The Question That Makes the Difference

Not "which pays more," but: can you work well without the structure of a team around you, and do you find it acceptable to be responsible for your own acquisition and administration? Anyone who doesn't enjoy that will experience freelance work as stressful regardless of the rate. Anyone with a strong need for variety and autonomy will experience a permanent job as restrictive regardless of the security.

## Two Questions That Make the Choice More Concrete Than a Rate Comparison

**Can you work well without the daily structure of a team around you?** Some people thrive on the freedom to plan their own day; others lose motivation without the social structure and direct feedback of colleagues.

**Do you find it acceptable to be responsible for acquisition and administration alongside your technical work?** This is not a matter of discipline — it is a matter of which kind of work gives you energy and which kind costs you energy.

## Comparison Table: Freelance vs. Permanent, on the Points That Really Matter

| Aspect | Freelance/self-employed | Permanent contract |
|---|---|---|
| Control over project choice | High | Low to medium |
| Income security | Variable, dependent on acquisition | Predictable |
| Administrative burden | Borne yourself | Largely taken over by the employer |
| Social structure | Limited, to be built yourself | Built in through the team |
| Project variety | Often high | Depends on the employer |

## Why This Choice Doesn't Have to Be Permanent

Many AI professionals treat this choice as if it fixes their entire career, while in practice many people switch between freelance and permanent work several times during their career, depending on their life stage, financial situation or need for stability versus variety. A few years of freelancing after a long period in permanent employment, or the other way round, is the rule rather than the exception in the Dutch AI market.

## A Concrete Scenario to Test Your Own Preference

Imagine an ordinary Tuesday a year from now. As a freelancer, you start that day by checking your calendar for three different clients, you have to decide yourself how much time to spend on acquisition for next month, and you work physically alone unless you make an effort to work somewhere with others. As a permanent employee, you start that day with a team meeting, you know exactly what is expected of you this week, and you don't have to think about where next month's income will come from. Which of these two Tuesdays appeals to you more — not in theory, but when you really try to imagine yourself in it?

## A Common Misconception About Financial Security

People often assume that a permanent contract by definition gives more financial security than freelance work. That is true in the short term, but in the longer term freelance income depends on your ability to keep finding new assignments — a skill that, once built, can offer its own form of security that is just as robust as a permanent salary, provided you have built a diverse network of clients instead of depending on one large client.

## Why This Choice Is Less Final Than It Feels at the Start of Your Career

Many professionals make this choice early in their career and then treat it as a permanent identity — "I'm a freelancer" or "I'm in permanent employment" — while the Dutch labour market for AI professionals is flexible enough to switch several times as your life stage, financial situation and personal preferences change. Treat the current choice as suited to your current situation, not as a permanent label.

## A Practical Way to Test Your Preference Without Switching Immediately

If you are torn between both routes without ever having tried the other, there is a middle way: a permanent contract with the option of occasionally doing a small, separate freelance assignment alongside it (where your employer allows it), or conversely a longer freelance assignment with one client that feels close to permanent employment in terms of daily structure. These intermediate forms give you a more realistic sense of which side attracts you, without taking the full, irreversible step before you know what suits you.

## Why Most People Misjudge This Choice Before Actually Making It

People who have never freelanced often overestimate how much they will value the freedom and underestimate how much they will miss the social structure of a team — and conversely, people who have always been in permanent employment often underestimate how much satisfaction they can get from building their own client portfolio. This is not a lack of self-knowledge; it is simply hard to judge an experience accurately before you have actually had it, which is a strong argument for doing a small-scale experiment first where possible before making a final choice.

## The Dutch Ground Rules for Self-Employed AI Professionals

Freelancing in the Netherlands is not just a personal choice, but also a legal and tax one. Anyone who knows the rules makes a more realistic comparison.

**Registration with the KVK.** As a self-employed professional, you register with the **Netherlands Chamber of Commerce (KVK)**. From that moment you are an entrepreneur, with your own obligations for VAT returns and administration with the **Dutch Tax Administration**.

**Entrepreneur tax facilities.** Entrepreneurs for income tax purposes can, under conditions, use the self-employed persons' deduction and the SME profit exemption. The self-employed deduction requires, among other things, meeting the hours criterion of at least 1,225 hours per year spent on your business. Note: the self-employed deduction has been reduced step by step in recent years. Calculation examples from a few years ago therefore paint too rosy a picture.

**False self-employment and the Wet DBA.** The Dutch Tax Administration resumed enforcement against false self-employment on 1 January 2025. Whether you are genuinely self-employed or effectively working in an employment relationship depends on the circumstances: authority, integration into the organisation, free substitution, own risk and the duration of the assignment. For an AI freelancer who works full-time in a fixed team for months, with the same tasks as salaried colleagues, this is not a theoretical risk. Clients have become more cautious as a result, which has affected demand for freelancers in some organisations.

**Liability.** As a freelancer building models that influence decisions, you bear the risk of damage caused by errors yourself. Professional or business liability insurance and clear liability arrangements in the assignment agreement are not a luxury.

**Intellectual property.** Who holds the rights to code, models and derived data? Without agreements, this can be unclear. Record in the assignment agreement what the client receives and what you may reuse yourself, such as generic tools and your own libraries.

## What a Permanent Contract Gives You That Freelancers Have to Arrange Themselves

The "hourly rate versus salary" comparison often goes wrong because an employee receives many provisions that a self-employed professional has to pay for or do without.

| Provision | Employee in the Netherlands | Self-employed professional |
|---|---|---|
| Holiday allowance | At least 8% of gross salary | Reserve it yourself |
| Continued pay during illness | Employer in principle continues paying for up to two years (at least 70% of salary, within statutory limits) | Insure yourself through disability insurance or reserve it yourself |
| Pension | Often an employer pension with employer contribution | Build it yourself, for example through an annuity product |
| Unemployment benefit | Entitlement to unemployment benefit under conditions | No unemployment benefit |
| Training budget | Often through the employer or collective labour agreement | Pay yourself and free up time |
| Non-billable hours | Paid | Unpaid: acquisition, administration, training |

A commonly used rule of thumb is that a freelancer can bill far from all available working hours, because acquisition, administration, holidays and illness all reduce them. So calculate with a realistic number of billable hours per year instead of 40 hours times 52 weeks.

## The AI Freelance Market in Practice

**Through intermediaries.** Many AI and data assignments at large organisations run through intermediaries, secondment agencies or procurement platforms. That gives access to assignments, but costs margin and often means a procurement process with framework contracts.

**Directly with clients.** Direct assignments usually come from your network: former colleagues, previous clients and communities. A strong network is at least as important for an AI freelancer as technical knowledge.

**What work freelancers often do.** Well-defined projects such as a proof of concept, a model audit, setting up an MLOps pipeline, a temporary expansion of a team, or specialist knowledge an organisation doesn't need permanently.

**Regulation as a new market.** Because of the **EU Artificial Intelligence Act**, organisations are looking for temporary expertise for inventories of AI systems, risk classification and documentation. Freelancers with demonstrable knowledge of AI governance find a growing niche here — provided they clearly define what they are responsible for.

## Doing an Honest Calculation

1. **Determine your gross annual salary equivalent**, including holiday allowance, bonus, pension contribution and training budget, from your current or intended permanent job.
2. **Add the costs you bear yourself as a self-employed professional**: disability insurance, pension, insurance, hardware, software, accounting and training.
3. **Estimate realistically how many hours you can bill**, taking into account holidays, illness, acquisition and periods without an assignment.
4. **Divide the total by those billable hours**. That is the minimum hourly rate at which freelancing is financially equivalent to permanent employment.
5. **Then weigh the non-financial factors** from this article: structure, variety, autonomy and risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Freelancing in the Netherlands means KVK registration, your own VAT and tax obligations, and entrepreneur tax facilities that have been scaled back in recent years.
- The resumed enforcement of the Wet DBA makes long, embedded assignments riskier for both client and freelancer.
- A self-employed professional arranges holiday allowance, disability cover, pension and training themselves; so calculate with billable hours.
- The AI Act creates temporary demand for governance and compliance expertise.
- Do an honest calculation, and then decide based on how you prefer to work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: engineer who only compares rates) Is freelance AI work always financially more attractive than full-time?
Not necessarily, once you include acquisition time, administration and periods without an assignment. The comparison is about more than an hourly rate versus a salary.

### (Scenario: engineer unsure whether freelance work suits them) Who is better off as a freelancer than in a permanent job?
People who can work well without the structure of a team, and who find the operational burden of acquisition and administration acceptable in exchange for autonomy and variety.

### (Scenario: engineer who thinks full-time work is always more stable) Is a full-time job always more stable than freelance work?
Usually in terms of income, but not necessarily in terms of project variety or autonomy — a full-time role gives less control over which problem you solve than freelance work often offers.

### (Scenario: engineer considering switching) Can I easily switch between freelance and full-time work during my career?
Yes, that happens more often in the Dutch AI market than people think — the two routes are not mutually exclusive, and many engineers switch depending on which life stage or type of work suits them at the time.

### (Scenario: engineer unsure how to decide) What should I pay attention to when making this choice?
How you prefer to work — with or without a team structure, with or without your own acquisition — more than a direct rate comparison, because the two routes have structurally different risks that no rate removes.
