⚡ Grid data work in the Netherlands is currently one of the most consequential technical problems in the country — and most candidates have no idea.

🔌 Connection capacity is fully allocated across large parts of the country. New industrial connections, sometimes new housing, cannot get the grid capacity they need. Physical expansion takes years. So the gap has to be managed with what already exists — which turns grid operation into a forecasting and optimisation problem with immediate economic consequence.

📊 The direct link is unusual: a congestion forecast that is better by a meaningful margin allows connections that would otherwise be refused. Very little commercial data work has a causal chain that short.

🧩 And the historical patterns are actively breaking. Electrification of heating and transport, plus millions of distributed solar sources feeding power backwards through a network designed for one-way flow. You are forecasting on a system whose behaviour is changing while you model it.

🚪 Entry without energy experience is realistic. Time series forecasting, constrained optimisation and sensor anomaly detection all transfer. Utilities expect to teach the physics and regulation — but they screen for whether you're curious about the physical system or treating it as an abstract time series. A few hours understanding the capacity constraint puts you ahead of most outside applicants.

🚆 Honest note on Alkmaar itself: Amsterdam is 35–45 minutes, so this isn't an isolating market. But 90 minutes of daily travel is a real cost, and candidates who treat it as free usually revise that within six months.
