A fully remote offer solves the problem candidates complain about most: the commute disappears, the radius of employers you can consider widens enormously, and the day belongs to you. It also removes things that are hard to notice until they are gone — the incidental conversation that reveals why a dataset looks strange, the colleague who would have reviewed your approach before you spent a week on it, the visibility that determines who is considered for the interesting project. Fully remote work suits some people and some roles extremely well. The decision is worth making on the specifics of this role rather than on a general preference.

## What "Fully Remote" Actually Means Here

The phrase covers several arrangements, and vacancies rarely distinguish them.

**Remote-first company.** Everyone works remotely, processes are written down, decisions happen in documents and meetings assume nobody is in a room together. This is the version that works.

**Remote exception in an office company.** Most colleagues are together, you are not. Everything informal happens without you, and you hear about decisions after they are made.

**Remote with occasional presence.** Fully remote in name, with a monthly or quarterly gathering. Workable, provided the expectation is honest and the travel is reimbursed.

**Remote for the employer's convenience.** The organisation has no office near you and no intention of building remote practice. You get isolation without the benefits of a designed remote culture.

The first and third are genuinely attractive. The second and fourth are where regret comes from.

## The Legal Position in the Netherlands

The **Flexible Working Act** allows employees of employers with at least ten employees to request changes to working hours, working times and workplace. For workplace requests, employers must consider the request and explain their reasoning, but retain more discretion than for hours. A proposal to strengthen the right to work from home, the "Work Where You Want" bill, was rejected by the Senate in 2023.

Practically, this means remote work in the Netherlands rests on what is agreed rather than on a statutory right. Get the arrangement into the contract or a written annex, because a policy can change when a manager does.

Employers also have obligations for your working conditions under the **Working Conditions Act**, including the home workstation — equipment, a suitable chair and desk, and attention to health. Many employers provide a setup budget and a **home working allowance**, the tax-free level of which is set annually; check the current amount rather than assuming.

## What Remote Costs in an AI Role Specifically

**Data context is transmitted informally.** Why a column changed meaning in 2023, which plant records downtime differently, why nobody trusts one particular report. This knowledge lives in conversations, and remote teams must work deliberately to write it down.

**Review friction rises.** A quick "can you look at this" becomes a scheduled call, so it happens less. Over months, less review means slower growth.

**Stakeholder relationships need effort.** The planner or underwriter who would have explained their process over coffee now needs a meeting invitation, which they may decline.

**Visibility affects opportunity.** Interesting projects go to people who are present in the minds of those allocating them. Remote professionals must create that presence deliberately.

**Onboarding is where most remote roles fail.** The first three months are exactly when informal learning matters most, and exactly when a remote joiner has the least access to it.

## When Fully Remote Works Well

- The company is genuinely remote-first, with written decisions and asynchronous norms.
- You are experienced enough not to need constant review.
- The work has long, self-contained blocks rather than continuous coordination.
- You have a good workspace at home and a life that provides social contact outside work.
- The team includes other remote people at your level.
- Occasional in-person gatherings are scheduled and paid for.

## When to Be Careful

- You would be the only remote person in an office-based team.
- You are early in your career, or new to the domain.
- The role is a first data hire, where relationship-building is most of the job.
- The organisation has no written documentation culture.
- Your home situation makes concentrated work difficult.
- You rely on work for most of your social contact.

## The Cross-Border Complication

If the employer is outside the Netherlands, or if you intend to work from another country for part of the year, the arrangement becomes more complicated than it looks.

Social security coordination within the EU is governed by **Regulation 883/2004**, and where you are insured depends on where you work rather than where your employer sits. A framework agreement on cross-border telework allows, in participating states and under conditions, a substantial share of telework from the country of residence while remaining insured in the employer's state. Tax treatment follows separate rules and treaties.

The practical advice is simple: if any part of your arrangement crosses a border, ask the employer to confirm in writing how social security, tax and applicable labour law are handled, and take advice. Do not rely on a recruiter's assurance that "it is all arranged".

## What to Ask Before Accepting

- **How many people work fully remotely, and for how long?** Existing examples matter more than policy.
- **Where are decisions recorded?** A documentation culture is the single best predictor of remote success.
- **What does onboarding look like for a remote joiner?** Ask for specifics — a buddy, structured introductions, a first project designed for it.
- **How often would I travel to the office or to colleagues, and who pays?**
- **What equipment and home working arrangements are provided?**
- **Is the remote arrangement in the contract or in a policy?** Policies change.
- **How are promotions and project allocation decided?** Listen for whether presence plays a role.

## How to Make It Work If You Accept

Front-load relationships. In the first two months, arrange short individual conversations with everyone you will depend on, including domain experts outside your team. It feels excessive and it is the highest-return thing you can do.

Write more than feels natural. Decisions, assumptions, open questions, what you tried and discarded. In remote teams, writing is both the work and the visibility.

Create deliberate presence. A short weekly summary of what you did and what you learned, sent where people read it, substitutes for the corridor.

Protect the boundary. Fully remote work erodes the separation between work and everything else, and Dutch working time rules do not enforce it for you.

Arrange contact outside the company. Meetups, a peer group, a coworking day. Professional isolation creeps up on people who are otherwise content.

## The First Six Months Decide It

Remote roles rarely fail in year two. They fail in the first months, quietly, and the pattern is consistent enough to plan against.

**Weeks one to four.** You are given access, a laptop and a backlog. Nobody realises how little context you have, because in an office your confusion would be visible. This is when to ask far more questions than feels comfortable, and to ask them in writing so the answers are reusable.

**Weeks five to twelve.** You deliver something small, and the team forms its impression of you. Remote joiners who ship one genuinely useful thing early buy themselves months of goodwill. Those who spend the same period on an ambitious project nobody sees become an unknown quantity.

**Months four to six.** Either you have become someone colleagues route questions to, or you have become someone they forget to include. This is the fork, and it is largely determined by how visible your work has been rather than how good it was.

Two practices protect against the bad branch. First, a standing weekly written update in a channel people actually read — three lines on what moved, what you learned and what you need. Second, deliberate one-to-one contact with at least one person outside your immediate team each month, which is how remote professionals hear about things before they are announced.

If the employer offers an onboarding period with more presence — a first week on site, a fortnight of daily calls with a buddy — take it, even if it is inconvenient. It compresses months of drift into days.

## If the Team Is Not Remote-First, Negotiate This

Sometimes the role is worth taking even though the organisation is office-centred. In that case, ask for four specific things rather than for goodwill.

**Meetings that include you properly.** Everyone dials in individually, or the room has decent audio and a chair who brings you in. Hybrid meetings where six people share one laptop are how remote colleagues become invisible.

**A named buddy for the first three months,** with a scheduled short call rather than an open invitation to ask questions.

**Written decisions in a shared place.** Even a simple channel where outcomes are posted. You are asking for something the whole team benefits from, which makes it an easy request.

**Scheduled presence, reimbursed.** A day a month or a few days a quarter, planned in advance so you can arrange your life around it and the team can plan around your being there.

These four cost the employer very little and remove most of the risk. An employer who refuses all four is telling you how the arrangement will actually work.

## Real example

### An ML engineer in Friesland who negotiated four days a quarter

An ML engineer living in Friesland is offered a role at a company based in Utrecht, advertised as fully remote. The salary is good and the alternative jobs near home are limited.

He asks the questions. Three of eleven team members are fully remote, two of them for more than two years. Decisions are recorded in a shared repository of short documents. Onboarding includes a named buddy and a first project scoped for a remote joiner.

He negotiates one addition: four full days a quarter in Utrecht, reimbursed, scheduled in advance, plus a home office budget.

The first months are harder than expected — he underestimates how much he relied on incidental conversation — so he adds a weekly written update and a standing monthly call with two stakeholders. A year later he is leading a project. The quarterly days, he says, do most of the relationship work; the writing does the rest.

## Key Takeaways

- Distinguish remote-first companies from office companies with a remote exception; the second is where most remote roles disappoint.
- Dutch law lets employees request a change of workplace but gives employers discretion, so the arrangement must be agreed in writing rather than assumed.
- Remote costs in AI roles are concentrated in informal data context, review friction, stakeholder access, visibility and onboarding.
- Cross-border remote work raises social security, tax and applicable-law questions that must be confirmed in writing.
- If you accept, front-load relationships, write more than feels natural, create deliberate visibility and arrange professional contact outside the company.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: candidate offered a remote role) Do I have a right to work from home in the Netherlands?
No. The Flexible Working Act allows you to request a change of workplace, but employers retain discretion, and a bill to strengthen that right was rejected in 2023. Agree the arrangement in writing.

### (Scenario: candidate assessing a company) How can I tell whether a company is genuinely remote-friendly?
Ask how many people already work fully remotely and for how long, where decisions are written down and what onboarding looks like for a remote joiner.

### (Scenario: candidate concerned about career) Does remote work affect promotion?
It can, because project allocation follows visibility. Ask how promotions and projects are decided, and compensate with written updates and deliberate relationship building.

### (Scenario: candidate working from another country) What if I want to work remotely from abroad?
Social security and tax depend on where you work, with EU coordination rules and a cross-border telework framework agreement in play. Get the employer's written confirmation and take advice.

### (Scenario: candidate setting up at home) What should the employer provide?
Employers have working conditions obligations covering the home workstation; many provide equipment, a setup budget and a home working allowance, whose tax-free level is set annually.
