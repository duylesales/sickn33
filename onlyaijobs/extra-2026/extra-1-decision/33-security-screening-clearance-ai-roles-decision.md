Some of the most interesting sensor, autonomy and signal processing work in the Netherlands sits behind a line most candidates never cross. Defence organisations, intelligence services, parts of critical infrastructure and certain government functions designate roles as positions of confidence, and filling one requires passing a security investigation rather than simply handing over a certificate of conduct. For candidates this raises practical questions that rarely get answered before an application: what is actually examined, how long it takes, what happens if you have lived abroad, and what it means for the rest of your career. The decision is worth making with facts rather than with assumptions borrowed from television.

## Two Different Things Often Confused

**A Certificate of Conduct (VOG).** Issued by **Justis**, this states that no objections were found in your judicial history relevant to the specific risk profile of the role. The employer sees a yes or a no, not your history. It is common for roles with access to sensitive data or financial systems, takes weeks rather than months, and is not a clearance.

**A security investigation for a position of confidence.** Conducted under the **Security Screening Act (Wet veiligheidsonderzoeken)** by the **AIVD** for civil functions or the **MIVD** for military ones, resulting in a Declaration of No Objection if it is granted. This is a far more extensive process, applies to designated positions only, and is what people mean when they say clearance.

Knowing which one a vacancy requires changes the decision entirely. Many AI roles at defence-adjacent suppliers require only a VOG.

## What a Security Investigation Looks At

The exact scope depends on the level of the position, and the services do not publish a checklist. In general terms, an investigation examines judicial data, information held by the intelligence services, financial circumstances and personal history, with the aim of assessing whether someone can be relied upon to handle sensitive information under pressure.

Two practical points matter more than the details.

**Verifiable history.** Investigations rely on being able to check a person's background over a period of years. Long periods spent abroad can make an investigation more difficult or lengthen it considerably, because information must be obtained from other jurisdictions. This affects internationals and Dutch nationals who have lived elsewhere.

**Honesty is the decisive factor.** Undisclosed debts, an omitted conviction or a concealed relationship weigh far more heavily than the underlying fact usually would. Screening is designed to identify vulnerability to pressure, and something you hid is exactly that.

## Timelines, and Why They Matter to Your Decision

Security investigations take substantially longer than ordinary recruitment — a period measured in months rather than weeks, varying with the level and complexity of the case. Employers usually cannot start you in a designated position before the declaration is granted, though some can offer interim work on non-sensitive tasks.

For you this has three consequences. A competing offer with a normal timeline may force your hand. Resigning from a current job before a declaration is granted is risky, and most employers will advise against it. And if your situation is complex — recent time abroad, dual nationality, a financial history worth explaining — ask early about expected duration rather than assuming the standard case applies.

## Who These Roles Suit

**People who want work that is genuinely unavailable elsewhere.** Sonar and radar classification, autonomy for mine countermeasures, maritime situational awareness, certain kinds of anomaly detection on critical systems — this work exists in very few places.

**People comfortable with restricted publishing.** You will often be unable to describe what you do in detail, publish results or discuss your work at meetups.

**People who value stability.** Defence and government employers tend to offer long-term employment and structured conditions.

**People who plan to stay in the Netherlands.** Clearance is jurisdiction-specific and does not transfer, and some positions have nationality requirements.

## Who Should Think Twice

**Anyone who needs to start quickly.** The timeline is the single most common reason candidates withdraw.

**People whose careers depend on public visibility.** If conference talks, open-source work and a public portfolio are how you build your reputation, a role where none of that is possible has a real cost.

**Internationally mobile professionals.** A period in a cleared role can complicate later work in some countries, and the reverse is also true: extensive foreign ties can complicate the investigation itself.

**People uncomfortable with ongoing obligations.** Positions of confidence usually involve periodic re-investigation and duties to report certain changes in personal circumstances, including some foreign contacts and travel.

**Anyone who would find refusal devastating.** A declaration can be refused, and while it is not a judgement on your character, it feels like one.

## What Refusal Actually Means

If a declaration is refused, you cannot occupy that designated position. The decision is administrative and can generally be objected to and appealed through the applicable legal routes, and reasons may be limited because they can involve intelligence information.

It does not make you unemployable. The vast majority of AI and data work in the Netherlands — including at defence suppliers, in critical infrastructure and at government organisations — involves no clearance requirement at all. It is, however, a reason not to build your entire search around roles that depend on one.

## Related Restrictions Worth Knowing

**Export control.** Defence and dual-use technologies fall under export control rules such as the **EU Dual-Use Regulation**, which can restrict who may access certain technical information regardless of clearance status, including on nationality grounds.

**Classified handling.** Working with classified material often means dedicated systems, no personal devices, limited remote work and restrictions on where you may work from — practical constraints that shape daily life more than the screening itself.

**The AI Act's military exclusion.** AI systems used exclusively for military, defence or national security purposes fall outside the AI Act's scope, though international humanitarian law and national policy on responsible military AI apply. Dual-use and civilian applications at the same employer may still be in scope.

## Questions to Ask the Employer Before Applying

- **Is this a position of confidence, or does it only require a Certificate of Conduct?**
- **Which level of investigation applies, and what is the typical duration for this role?**
- **Can I start on non-sensitive work while the investigation runs?**
- **Are there nationality or residence-history requirements?**
- **What happens to my employment if the declaration is refused or later withdrawn?**
- **Which parts of my work could I describe publicly, on a CV or in future interviews?**
- **Are there restrictions on travel, foreign contacts or secondary activities?**

An employer accustomed to these roles answers all of this without hesitation. One that improvises may not have thought through what they are asking of you.

## How It Affects Your Next Job

This is the question candidates ask least and regret most.

The constraint is describability. You may be unable to name systems, results or even projects, which makes interviews harder — not impossible, since interviewers in the sector understand the situation, but harder outside it. Mitigate it by keeping a record of transferable specifics you *can* describe: methods, scale, constraints, the type of problem, the role you played.

The advantage is scarcity. Engineers who combine machine learning with signal processing, safety-critical validation and experience of working under security constraints are genuinely rare, and defence budgets in Europe have been rising. Within the sector, cleared experience is an asset that competitors cannot quickly replicate.

## What Daily Life Actually Looks Like

The screening is a one-off event; the working conditions that follow are permanent, and candidates underestimate them because they are mundane rather than dramatic.

**Separate systems.** Sensitive work often happens on dedicated machines in dedicated rooms, which means no personal laptop, no phone at the desk and frequently no ability to work from home on those tasks. If hybrid working matters to you, ask specifically which parts of the role can be done remotely.

**Slower tooling.** Software on secured networks is approved rather than downloaded. The newest library may not be available, and getting it can take weeks. Engineers used to installing whatever they need find this the biggest adjustment.

**Compartmentalisation.** You may not know what colleagues two desks away are working on, and they may not know what you do. It makes the informal knowledge-sharing that normally accelerates learning considerably harder.

**Reporting obligations.** Positions of confidence usually carry duties to report certain changes in personal circumstances, and periodic re-investigation applies.

**Careful conversations.** Explaining your work to friends, family or a future interviewer in general terms becomes a skill you practise.

None of this is unbearable, and people who enjoy the work describe it as a fair exchange. But it is the part worth picturing concretely before you accept, because it is what you live with every day rather than once.

## Real example

### An engineer in Twente who asked about the timeline first

A signal processing engineer is approached about a role at a defence-adjacent employer working on sensor classification. The work is exactly what she wants, and the vacancy mentions a security investigation.

She asks the practical questions before applying. It is a designated position; the investigation typically takes several months; she could start on non-sensitive research in the meantime; she would be unable to publish, which matters to her because she speaks at conferences; and periodic re-investigation applies.

She also raises her own complication: three years working in another country, which she expects will lengthen the process. The employer confirms it probably will.

She applies anyway, starts on unclassified work after two months and receives the declaration five months later. She stops speaking publicly about her work, which she misses, and gains access to problems she could not have worked on anywhere else. Two years later she is one of a handful of people in the country with that combination of experience — and says the honest trade was visibility for depth.

## Key Takeaways

- Distinguish a Certificate of Conduct, issued by Justis and common for sensitive data roles, from a security investigation for a designated position of confidence conducted by the AIVD or MIVD.
- Investigations examine judicial, financial and personal history to assess reliability; honesty about difficult facts matters more than the facts themselves.
- Timelines run to months, long periods abroad complicate them, and most employers cannot start you in the position before the declaration is granted.
- Expect restrictions: limited publishing, dedicated systems, constrained remote work, periodic re-investigation and duties to report certain changes.
- Refusal blocks that specific position, not your career — but it argues against building an entire job search around cleared roles.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: candidate seeing a screening requirement) What is the difference between a VOG and a security clearance?
A Certificate of Conduct is a judicial background check issued by Justis for a specific risk profile. A security investigation for a position of confidence is far broader and is conducted by the AIVD or MIVD.

### (Scenario: candidate who lived abroad) Does time spent abroad affect the investigation?
It can. Investigations depend on verifiable history, so periods in other jurisdictions may lengthen the process or complicate it, which is worth raising early with the employer.

### (Scenario: candidate needing to start soon) How long does a security investigation take?
Typically months rather than weeks, depending on the level and complexity. Ask whether you can start on non-sensitive work while it runs.

### (Scenario: candidate worried about refusal) What happens if the declaration is refused?
You cannot occupy that designated position, and objection and appeal routes exist. It does not affect the large majority of AI and data roles, which require no clearance.

### (Scenario: candidate thinking ahead) How does a cleared role affect my next job application?
The main constraint is that you may not be able to describe your work in detail; keep a record of transferable specifics such as methods, scale and constraints that you can discuss.
