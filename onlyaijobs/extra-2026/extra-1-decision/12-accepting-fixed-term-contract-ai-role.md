The offer is good: interesting work, a reasonable salary, a team you liked. Then you reach the contract type — one year, fixed term, "with the intention of extending". In the Netherlands this is entirely normal, especially for a first hire in a data team or for a role funded by a specific project. It is also a materially different proposition from a permanent contract, in ways that reach beyond work into mortgages, rental applications and, for international candidates, residence permits. The question is not whether fixed-term is acceptable in principle, but whether it is acceptable in your situation and on these terms.

This article provides general information, not legal advice. For your specific situation, consult an employment lawyer, your union or the free information service **Juridisch Loket**.

## What Dutch Law Actually Says

**The chain rule (ketenregeling).** An employer can offer a maximum of three consecutive fixed-term contracts within a maximum of three years. Exceed either limit and the contract becomes permanent by operation of law. Contracts count as consecutive if the gap between them is six months or less. Collective agreements can deviate from these rules in certain situations, so check whether one applies to you.

**Ending and notice.** A fixed-term contract ends automatically on the agreed date. For contracts of six months or longer, the employer must inform you in writing at least one month before the end date whether the contract will be continued and on what terms; failing to do so triggers a compensation obligation. Interim termination is only possible if the contract explicitly allows it.

**Probation period.** No probation period is allowed in a contract of six months or less. For longer fixed-term contracts under two years, the maximum is one month.

**Transition payment.** Employees are in principle entitled to a transition payment when the employer ends the employment, including when a fixed-term contract is not continued at the employer's initiative.

**Unemployment benefits.** If a fixed-term contract ends and you meet the conditions, you may be entitled to unemployment benefits through the **UWV**. Ending a contract yourself, or being dismissed for cause, affects entitlement.

## Why Employers Offer Fixed-Term Contracts for AI Roles

Understanding the reason behind the offer tells you how much risk you are actually taking.

**First data hire uncertainty.** An organisation hiring its first data professional often does not yet know whether the role will deliver value, so it limits its commitment.

**Project or grant funding.** Research institutes, universities and some public organisations fund roles from specific budgets with defined end dates.

**Genuine trial.** Some employers use a year as an extended assessment, particularly for candidates switching fields.

**Hiring freezes and caution.** Economic uncertainty pushes organisations towards temporary commitments.

**Policy, not judgement.** Some employers simply offer everyone a one-year contract first, regardless of the candidate.

The last reason is common and relatively benign. The first is the one to probe: if the role exists because someone hopes data will help but nobody has defined what success looks like, a year may end with an honest "we did not get value" that had little to do with your work.

## What It Costs You Beyond Job Security

**Mortgages.** Dutch lenders assess income stability. A fixed-term contract usually requires an employer's declaration of intent regarding continuation, and conditions vary between lenders. Buying a home during a fixed-term contract is possible but more complicated.

**Rental applications.** Private landlords and agencies frequently ask for proof of income and contract type, and a temporary contract can weaken an application in a tight housing market.

**Residence permits.** For non-EU candidates on a highly skilled migrant permit, residence depends on employment. If the contract ends, a search period applies within which new sponsored employment must be found. Check current conditions with the **IND** before accepting.

**Negotiating position.** Employees on temporary contracts are often more hesitant to raise concerns, ask for development budget or push back on scope. This is worth being aware of in yourself.

**Planning.** Parental leave, long holidays and training commitments are all affected by an end date on the horizon.

## What to Ask Before Signing

- **Why is this contract fixed-term?** The answer distinguishes policy from doubt about the role.
- **What has to be true after twelve months for this to become permanent?** Vague answers are a warning; specific ones give you something to work towards.
- **How many people were offered a fixed-term contract last year, and how many were extended?** A track record is the most reliable signal available.
- **Is the role funded from a project budget with an end date?** Common in research and public organisations, and important to know.
- **Does a collective agreement apply, and what does it say about contract chains?** Sector agreements can deviate from the statutory rules.
- **What is the notice arrangement for interim termination?** If the contract cannot be ended early by either side, you are locked in too.

## When to Accept

Accepting is usually sensible when the work is clearly good, the employer answers the questions above concretely, and your personal situation can absorb a possible end date — no imminent mortgage application, no permit dependency you cannot manage, some savings.

It is also reasonable when the alternative is nothing. A year of relevant experience improves your position substantially more than a year of searching, and the Dutch chain rule means that continuation, if it happens, moves you towards a permanent contract on a defined timeline.

## When to Push Back or Decline

**When you are leaving a permanent contract.** Giving up permanent employment for a temporary one is a real risk, and it is entirely reasonable to ask for a permanent contract or a longer term in exchange.

**When the role is undefined.** "We want to do something with AI" plus a one-year contract is a combination that frequently ends without extension.

**When your housing or permit situation cannot absorb it.** These are practical constraints, not preferences, and employers usually understand them when explained plainly.

**When the employer will not discuss it.** An employer unwilling to explain the reasoning or the path to permanence is telling you something about how they treat employees generally.

## Reading the Employer's Track Record

The most useful evidence about a fixed-term contract is not in the contract; it is in what the employer has done with previous ones.

Ask directly how many people joined on temporary contracts in the last two or three years and how many were continued. A specific answer — "five of seven, the two who left went of their own accord" — tells you far more than any assurance about intentions. Hesitation or a vague answer is itself informative.

You can also check indirectly. During interviews, ask colleagues how long they have been there and how they started; people answer this honestly. If everyone on the team began on a one-year contract and is still present three years later, the policy is genuinely a formality. If the team has visibly rotated, ask why.

Look at the reason for the role too. A position funded from a defined programme budget carries different risk than one on a permanent budget line where the contract type is simply house policy. Neither is disqualifying, but they justify different negotiations.

## If the Contract Is Not Extended

Plan for the outcome you do not want, because it is easier to arrange now than later.

Ask when the decision about continuation will be communicated. For contracts of six months or more, the employer must inform you in writing at least one month before the end date, but an earlier informal conversation is normal and lets you start searching in time.

Use the final months deliberately: make sure your work is documented, get a written reference or a recommendation, and keep a record of what you delivered in terms the next employer will understand. If the role ends, entitlement to unemployment benefits through the UWV depends on meeting the conditions, and a transition payment applies in principle when the employer ends the employment.

Candidates who treat the end date as a fixed planning point — rather than as something to worry about later — usually find the transition uneventful, whichever way it goes.

## What You Can Negotiate Instead

If the contract type is genuinely fixed by policy, other terms often are not.

A longer initial term — eighteen months or two years rather than twelve — reduces uncertainty while staying within the chain rule. A written statement of the criteria for continuation gives you something concrete. A higher salary compensates for the risk you are carrying, and with pay transparency rules employers must in any case provide pay information before the interview. A guaranteed training budget protects your market value if the role ends. Explicit agreement on a reference and a notice conversation well before the end date costs the employer nothing and is worth having.

## Real example

### A data scientist in Leiden who asked one extra question

A data scientist in Leiden receives an offer from a research-adjacent organisation: interesting work, a one-year contract, a vague promise of extension. She is currently employed on a permanent contract and is about to start looking at apartments.

She asks three questions. Why fixed-term? Because the role is funded from a two-year programme budget. What happens after the programme? The organisation intends to continue the function but cannot guarantee it. How many programme-funded colleagues were extended in the last three years? Four out of six.

With that information she negotiates: an eighteen-month initial contract rather than twelve, a written note of the criteria for continuation and a training budget. The employer agrees to the term and the training budget, and explains the criteria honestly rather than putting them in the contract.

She accepts, postpones the apartment search by a year and keeps her network warm. Eighteen months later the contract becomes permanent. The decision was not that fixed-term is fine or not fine; it was that she found out what she was actually being offered.

## Key Takeaways

- Dutch law limits fixed-term contracts to three consecutive contracts within three years, after which the contract becomes permanent.
- Employers must notify you in writing at least one month before the end of contracts of six months or longer, and a transition payment applies when they end employment.
- The main costs are outside work: mortgages, rental applications, residence permits and your own negotiating confidence.
- Ask why the contract is temporary, what continuation depends on and what the employer's extension track record is.
- If the contract type is fixed, negotiate the term, salary, training budget and written criteria instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: candidate offered a one-year contract) Is a fixed-term contract normal for AI roles in the Netherlands?
Yes, particularly for first data hires, project-funded roles and employers with a standard policy of starting everyone on a temporary contract.

### (Scenario: candidate wondering about permanence) When does a fixed-term contract become permanent?
After more than three consecutive fixed-term contracts, or once the chain exceeds three years, with gaps of six months or less counting as consecutive; collective agreements can deviate.

### (Scenario: candidate planning to buy a home) Can I get a mortgage on a fixed-term contract?
It is possible but more complicated; lenders usually ask for an employer's declaration of intent regarding continuation, and conditions differ per lender.

### (Scenario: international candidate) What happens to my residence permit if the contract ends?
Highly skilled migrant permits depend on sponsored employment, with a search period applying if employment ends; check current conditions with the IND before accepting.

### (Scenario: candidate leaving a permanent job) Should I give up a permanent contract for a temporary one?
Only with good reason and after negotiating — a longer initial term, higher salary or written continuation criteria — because you are carrying the risk the employer is avoiding.
