Two offers, apparently for the same work. In one you are employed directly by the organisation whose data you will be working on. In the other you are employed by a staffing or payroll company and placed with that organisation, sometimes with a slightly higher hourly figure to make up for it. Candidates often treat this as an administrative detail, because the day-to-day job looks identical: same desk, same team, same manager. It is not a detail. It changes who can end your employment, how quickly, what terms apply to you, what you accrue and how a lender sees you. In the Netherlands these arrangements are regulated in some detail, and the details are worth knowing before you sign.

## The Arrangements You May Be Offered

**Direct employment.** A contract with the organisation itself, fixed-term or permanent, under whatever collective agreement applies to them.

**Temporary agency work.** A staffing agency employs you and makes you available to a client. The agency recruits, and the client directs your work.

**Payroll employment.** You were recruited by the client, but employed by a payroll company that handles the formal employment. The recruitment function is the client's; only the employment sits elsewhere.

**Secondment.** A firm employs you, usually on a longer-term contract, and places you with clients. Closer to consultancy in character.

The Dutch legislature treats these differently on purpose. Payroll employment in particular was tightened under the **Balanced Labour Market Act**, precisely because it had been used to place employees on weaker terms while the client did the actual recruiting.

## What the Rules Give You

You do not have to rely on the agency's description of your position; several protections apply by law.

**Equal terms.** Under the **Waadi**, workers placed with a client are in principle entitled to the same essential employment conditions as the client's own employees in comparable roles, including pay and allowances. Ask which terms are being applied to you and on what basis.

**No fee to the worker.** An intermediary may not charge you for placing you.

**Payroll employees.** Under the Balanced Labour Market Act, payroll employees are broadly entitled to the same terms as the client's own staff, and payroll employment is excluded from the lighter regime that applies to genuine agency work. This is a substantial protection, and it is the reason the distinction between agency work and payrolling matters.

**Sector agreements.** Staffing work is generally covered by a collective labour agreement for the sector, which governs phases of employment, pay development, sick pay arrangements and pension. Ask which one applies to you and read what it says about your phase.

**Right to be told about vacancies.** Placed workers should be informed about vacancies at the client, so an internal move is not something you have to discover by accident.

The rules change periodically, including on agency work specifically, so verify current details for your situation rather than relying on an older summary.

## The Phase System, in Plain Terms

Agency employment in the Netherlands is structured in phases. In the early phase, the arrangement is the most flexible: an **agency clause** may apply, meaning the contract can end when the assignment ends, and there is little notice on either side. As you accumulate time, you progress to phases with more security — a sequence of fixed-term contracts, and eventually an open-ended arrangement with the agency.

Two practical consequences follow. First, early-phase agency work is genuinely insecure: an assignment ending can mean employment ending, quickly. Second, time accrues, so it matters whether previous work with the same agency or at the same client counts towards your position. Ask explicitly which phase you would start in, what the current durations are, and what happens at each transition.

## What Changes in Practice

**Who can end it, and how fast.** In direct employment, ending the contract requires the usual routes and notice. In early-phase agency work, the client ending the assignment can end your employment almost immediately.

**Pension accrual.** Staffing sector pension arrangements typically start after a waiting period and build differently from a client's own scheme. Over a year or two the difference can be meaningful.

**Secondary terms.** Training budgets, thirteenth month, bonus schemes, home working allowances and travel reimbursement may follow the agency's arrangements rather than the client's, subject to the equal terms rules. Ask item by item.

**Mortgage capacity.** Lenders treat agency contracts cautiously. An employer's declaration of intent may help; a fixed-term direct contract is generally viewed more favourably, and a permanent one more favourably still.

**Belonging.** Placed workers are sometimes outside internal training, development conversations and team budgets, not by design but by administrative default. This matters more in AI roles than in many others, because development is a large part of the value of the job.

**Career visibility inside the client.** Being on an agency contract can quietly place you outside promotion and succession conversations.

## When the Agency Route Is Reasonable

- **It is a genuine route in.** Some employers hire almost exclusively through a trial period on an agency contract, particularly for career changers, and convert afterwards. If that is the pattern, ask how often conversion actually happens.
- **You are new to the Dutch market** and need first local experience and references.
- **You want flexibility yourself** — a defined period, a project, a bridge between roles.
- **The work is genuinely temporary** — parental leave cover, a project with an end date — and the client is honest about it.
- **The equal terms position is clear and in writing.**

## When to Push for Direct Employment

- The role is structurally permanent and the agency arrangement exists only to shift risk.
- You were recruited by the client, in which case you are likely in payroll territory and entitled to their terms.
- A mortgage application is coming.
- The training budget, which you need, sits with the client and not with you.
- The client has a pattern of extending agency arrangements without converting anyone.
- You are senior enough that the market gives you the choice.

Asking is normal. A reasonable question is simply: is direct employment possible now, and if not, what would need to be true for it to happen, and when would we review it?

## Questions to Ask Before Signing

- **Which arrangement is this exactly — agency, payroll or secondment?**
- **Which collective agreement applies, and which phase do I start in?**
- **Which employment terms are matched to the client's own employees, and which are not?**
- **What happens if the client ends the assignment early?**
- **When does pension accrual start?**
- **Who pays for training, and who approves it?**
- **Is there an intention to convert to direct employment, on what timeline and on what conditions?**
- **Does time already worked here or with this agency count towards my phase?**

## How to Use the Arrangement Well If You Take It

Get the conversion conversation on the calendar from the start, with a date rather than a vague intention. Perform as an employee rather than as a guest — ask to join the team's rituals, reviews and knowledge sessions, since exclusion is usually administrative rather than deliberate. Keep your own record of what you build, because agency workers are frequently left out of formal evaluation processes and you will need the evidence. And keep an eye on the market throughout, since the arrangement's flexibility runs in both directions.

## Spotting the Arrangement Before the Offer Stage

Vacancies do not always make this clear, and candidates frequently reach the offer conversation before discovering who the employer would actually be. A few signals let you establish it early, which is when questions are easiest to ask.

**Who is advertising?** If the vacancy is posted by a company whose own business is staffing, secondment or recruitment, the employment will usually be with them rather than with the organisation you would work at.

**The language used.** Wording around secondment, placement, assignment, hourly rates or "our client" points to an intermediary. Wording about joining the team, the collective agreement and the organisation's own terms points to direct employment.

**Whether the client is named.** A vacancy describing "a leading financial organisation in Utrecht" without naming it is almost always an intermediary. That is not disqualifying, but it tells you what to ask.

**Who conducts the interviews.** An initial conversation with a consultant, followed by a meeting with the actual manager, is the standard intermediary pattern.

The question to ask in the first conversation is simply: who would my employment contract be with, and which collective agreement applies? It is a normal question, it costs nothing, and the answer determines everything in this article. A recruiter who is vague about it is worth pressing, because the difference between agency work and payroll employment carries real consequences for your terms — and the person arranging it should be able to say which one this is.

## Real example

### A career changer in Arnhem who set a conversion date

A career changer moving from finance into analytics receives an offer to join a regional insurer through a staffing agency, with the expectation of a direct contract "if it goes well". It is his first analytics role and he is inclined to accept anything.

He asks anyway. Which collective agreement applies, which phase, which terms match the insurer's own staff, when pension accrual starts, and how many people were converted last year.

The answers are mostly good: five of seven placed workers were converted within a year, and the equal terms position is documented. Pension accrual starts later than it would directly, and the training budget sits with the agency and is smaller.

He accepts with two things agreed in writing: a review of conversion at nine months, and the insurer covering a specific course the agency budget would not stretch to.

At ten months he is offered a direct contract. His conclusion is that the agency route was the only realistic way into his first analytics job, and that asking the questions was what turned it from a hope into a date in the calendar.

## Key Takeaways

- Agency, payroll, secondment and direct employment look identical day to day and differ substantially in security, terms and accrual.
- The Waadi provides for equal essential terms with the client's comparable employees, and payroll employees are broadly entitled to the client's terms under the Balanced Labour Market Act.
- Early-phase agency work is genuinely insecure, since the assignment ending can end the employment; ask which phase you start in and what counts towards it.
- Pension accrual, training budgets, secondary terms and mortgage capacity are where the difference is felt in practice.
- If you accept, agree a conversion review date in writing, keep your own record of your work and ask to be included in team development.

## Where to Start

Vacancies rarely state whether the contract is direct or through an intermediary, so ask early — and check which employers near you are hiring directly for comparable work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### (Scenario: candidate offered an agency contract) Is an agency contract worse than direct employment?
It is less secure, particularly in the early phases, and secondary terms and pension accrual often differ. As a route into a first Dutch role it can still be the right choice.

### (Scenario: candidate comparing terms) Am I entitled to the same pay as the client's own staff?
In principle, the Waadi provides for equal essential employment conditions with comparable employees of the client, and payroll employees are broadly entitled to the client's terms. Ask how this is applied to you.

### (Scenario: candidate worried about security) What happens if the assignment ends early?
In early-phase agency work with an agency clause, employment can end with the assignment at short notice. In later phases and in direct employment, the normal rules on notice and termination apply.

### (Scenario: candidate planning a house purchase) Does an agency contract affect a mortgage?
Usually yes. Lenders view agency contracts more cautiously; an employer's declaration of intent can help, but a direct contract is generally viewed more favourably.

### (Scenario: candidate hoping to convert) How do I improve the chance of a direct contract?
Ask how many placed workers were converted last year, agree a review date in writing, keep evidence of your work and ask to be included in the team's normal development processes.
