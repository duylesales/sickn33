Grenoble is a mid-sized city surrounded by mountains, but in science and technology it plays in the top tier. It is one of France's most important research and innovation centres, with a dense cluster of laboratories, a leading position in microelectronics and a long tradition in applied mathematics and computer science. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Grenoble offer an unusual profile: machine learning applied to chips, sensors, energy systems and scientific instruments, often in close collaboration between research institutes and industry. This guide explains the Grenoble ecosystem, the entry-level paths available and how to make the most of the city's research-to-industry pipeline.

## The Grenoble Ecosystem

**Université Grenoble Alpes and Grenoble INP.** The Université Grenoble Alpes (UGA) brings together the city's universities and schools, including Grenoble INP, with engineering schools such as Ensimag, which specialises in computer science and applied mathematics. The Grenoble Computer Science Laboratory (LIG) and the Jean Kuntzmann Laboratory of applied mathematics are major research labs.

**MIAI Grenoble Alpes.** The Multidisciplinary Institute in Artificial Intelligence was created as one of France's interdisciplinary AI institutes under the national AI strategy. It brings together researchers from the university, national research organisations and industry, with themes including embedded and hardware-efficient AI, AI for health, environment and energy, and societal aspects of AI.

**Inria.** Inria's centre at Université Grenoble Alpes hosts research teams in machine learning, computer vision, robotics, optimisation and high-performance computing.

**CEA-Leti and the GIANT campus.** CEA-Leti is one of the world's leading research institutes in microelectronics and nanotechnology, based on the GIANT campus (Grenoble Innovation for Advanced New Technologies) on the Presqu'île. It works on chips, sensors and components, including hardware for AI. The same campus hosts international research facilities such as the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility (ESRF) and the Institut Laue-Langevin, which generate huge amounts of scientific data.

**Industry.** STMicroelectronics operates major sites in the Grenoble area, including semiconductor manufacturing in Crolles. Soitec, a semiconductor materials company, is based nearby in Bernin. Schneider Electric has a significant presence in Grenoble in energy management and automation. Many other companies and startups work on semiconductors, sensors, energy, medical technology and software.

As AI moves from data centres onto devices — sensors, cars, phones, industrial equipment — the ability to design models that run with limited power and memory becomes valuable. Grenoble's combination of chip expertise and ML research makes it one of Europe's centres for this field.

## Entry Paths for Graduates

**Internships (stages).** French engineering and master's programmes include internships, typically five to six months for the final one. In Grenoble, many students do their final internship at research institutes or companies, and a significant share leads to job offers.

**Work-study (alternance).** Many French programmes allow students to alternate between study and work in a company under an apprenticeship or professional contract. This builds substantial experience before graduation and is common in engineering and IT.

**CIFRE PhDs.** The CIFRE scheme lets a company hire a doctoral student whose research is supervised by an academic lab, co-funded by the state. Grenoble's close industry–research links make CIFRE PhDs common, particularly in embedded AI, semiconductors and energy.

**Research engineer positions.** Research institutes hire engineers on project contracts to develop software, run experiments and build prototypes — an excellent first role for graduates who enjoy research but do not want a PhD yet.

**Junior roles in industry.** Companies hire junior data scientists, ML engineers and embedded software engineers, often from local schools.

**VIE.** The Volontariat International en Entreprise allows young French and EU graduates to work abroad for French companies for a limited period — relevant if you want international experience with a Grenoble employer.

## Language

French is essential for daily life and most local employers. Research labs and some international teams work in English, and many research groups have international members. For international students and graduates, investing in French significantly broadens opportunities in Grenoble's industry.

## Living in Grenoble

Grenoble sits at the foot of the Alps, surrounded by the Chartreuse, Vercors and Belledonne ranges. Skiing, hiking and climbing are part of daily life for many residents. The city is flat, compact and well suited to cycling, with a tram network connecting the centre, the university campus in Saint-Martin-d'Hères and the GIANT campus. Housing is more affordable than in Paris or Lyon, and the large student population creates a lively atmosphere. Lyon is about an hour and a half away, and Geneva is also within reach.

## Embedded AI: Skills Worth Building

If Grenoble's embedded AI focus interests you, these skills will make you stand out:

- **Model compression:** quantisation, pruning and knowledge distillation to reduce model size and computation.
- **Hardware awareness:** understanding memory hierarchies, accelerators, energy consumption and how neural network operations map to hardware.
- **Programming for constrained devices:** C and C++, embedded toolchains and frameworks for deploying models on microcontrollers and edge processors.
- **Neural architecture search and efficient architectures:** designing models that balance accuracy with latency and energy.
- **Signal processing:** many edge applications process sensor signals — audio, vibration, radar or images.
- **Evaluation under constraints:** measuring accuracy, latency and energy together, and understanding trade-offs.

Student projects that deploy a model on a real low-power device — for example keyword spotting on a microcontroller or anomaly detection on sensor data — are strong portfolio items for Grenoble employers.

## AI in Semiconductor Manufacturing

Semiconductor fabs are among the most complex manufacturing environments in the world. Hundreds of process steps, extremely tight tolerances and expensive equipment generate vast amounts of data. Machine learning helps in several ways: predicting yield from process measurements, detecting defects in wafer images, identifying the root causes of process drift, scheduling equipment maintenance and optimising process recipes. Data scientists work closely with process engineers, and domain knowledge is essential. For graduates, a role in a fab offers intensive learning about a strategically important industry — Europe's efforts to strengthen its semiconductor capabilities, including through the European Chips Act, underline its importance.

## Scientific Data at the Research Facilities

Grenoble's international research facilities produce enormous volumes of experimental data. The ESRF, a synchrotron light source used by scientists from many countries, and the Institut Laue-Langevin, a neutron research centre, both need data scientists and software engineers to process, analyse and store data. Machine learning helps reconstruct images, detect features in experimental data, automate experiments and control instruments. These roles suit graduates with strong physics or mathematics backgrounds who want to apply ML to fundamental science in an international environment, often in English.

## Energy and Automation

Grenoble has a long history in hydroelectric power and electrical engineering, and energy remains an important sector. Companies in energy management, automation and electrical equipment use AI for building energy optimisation, load forecasting, predictive maintenance of electrical equipment and optimisation of industrial processes. The energy transition increases demand for these skills, as grids must integrate more renewable energy and buildings become smarter. For graduates, energy offers stable employers and work with clear environmental impact.

## Preparing Your Applications

French employers expect a concise CV (one page for graduates), a motivation letter tailored to the company and, for internships and work-study, clear information on dates and duration. Highlight projects and internships with concrete results. In interviews, expect questions about your technical choices, your understanding of the company's domain and your motivation for the specific role. For research positions, prepare to discuss your academic projects in depth and to show curiosity about the lab's work. School and university career services in Grenoble offer CV reviews and interview practice — use them.

## Student Life and Networking

Grenoble's large student population and research community create many opportunities to network. MIAI and the labs host seminars and events, schools organise company forums and hackathons, and local meetups on AI, data science and embedded systems bring together students and professionals. Being active in student associations related to AI or robotics also helps you meet companies, since many sponsor or support these associations.

## Understanding French Graduate Offers

When you receive your first job offer in France, a few elements are worth understanding. The contract type — a permanent CDI or a fixed-term CDD — affects stability and access to housing and loans, since landlords often prefer CDI holders. Engineering graduates are usually hired with executive (cadre) status, which often comes with a days-based working-time arrangement and additional rest days (RTT). Salaries are expressed as annual gross amounts, sometimes including a thirteenth month; net pay is lower after social contributions. Many employers apply a collective agreement, such as the Syntec agreement for engineering and IT services or the metallurgy agreement for industry, which sets minimum conditions. Benefits commonly include meal vouchers or a subsidised canteen, supplementary health insurance, reimbursement of half the cost of public transport passes and sometimes profit-sharing schemes.

## Your First Two Years

Use your first years to build deep expertise in one domain — embedded AI, manufacturing data, energy or scientific data — while strengthening engineering fundamentals: clean code, testing, version control and working with hardware or process engineers. Keep in touch with your school and lab, because Grenoble's ecosystem rewards people who move between research and industry. And take advantage of the mountains: the work-life balance is one of the city's greatest assets.

## Beyond Grenoble: The Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes Region

The wider region offers further options. Lyon, about an hour and a half away, has a large economy with health, biotech, gaming, software and industrial companies, as well as universities and research centres. Annecy and Chambéry have technology and industrial firms in attractive mountain settings. Geneva, across the Swiss border, hosts international organisations and companies, though Swiss rules for work permits and cross-border commuting apply. Many Grenoble graduates build careers across this region, benefiting from its combination of research, industry and quality of life.

## Real example

### An Ensimag graduate who chose embedded AI

An Ensimag student specialising in data science completed her final internship at a research institute working on compressing neural networks for low-power chips. She enjoyed the combination of ML and hardware constraints, but hesitated between a data science job in Paris and staying in Grenoble.

She was offered a CIFRE PhD with a semiconductor company, supervised by a university lab, on hardware-aware neural architecture search. She compared the options: the Paris job paid more immediately, but the CIFRE PhD offered a salary, deep expertise in a growing field and strong connections to industry. She chose the PhD. Three years later, she joined the company's AI team as an engineer specialising in edge AI — a profile that is in demand across Europe.
