Toulouse is Europe's aerospace capital. Airbus has its headquarters and final assembly lines there, the French space agency CNES runs a major centre in the city, and a dense network of suppliers, laboratories and engineering schools surrounds them. For machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Toulouse offer something few cities can: the chance to apply AI to aircraft, satellites and safety-critical embedded systems, where reliability, certification and physics matter as much as model accuracy. This guide explains the Toulouse AI landscape, the skills that stand out, the practicalities of working in France and how to evaluate an offer.

## The Toulouse Aerospace and Space Ecosystem

Toulouse's economy is built around aerospace and space, and AI work in the city largely follows that structure.

**Aircraft manufacturing and operations.** Airbus uses data and machine learning across design, manufacturing, supply chain, quality and aircraft operations. Predictive maintenance, anomaly detection in sensor data, computer vision for inspection and optimisation of production are recurring themes.

**Space.** CNES's Toulouse centre, Airbus Defence and Space, Thales Alenia Space and many smaller companies build satellites and process Earth observation data. Machine learning for image analysis, onboard processing, mission planning and signal processing is an important field.

**Research.** ONERA, the French aerospace research agency, has a centre in Toulouse. The city's universities and engineering schools — including ISAE-SUPAERO, INSA Toulouse, ENSEEIHT (Toulouse INP) and Université Toulouse III — host laboratories such as IRIT (computer science) and LAAS-CNRS (systems and robotics).

**ANITI.** The Artificial and Natural Intelligence Toulouse Institute was created as one of France's interdisciplinary AI institutes, with a strong focus on trustworthy and certifiable AI for critical systems, in partnership with industry.

**Suppliers and engineering services.** A large ecosystem of equipment suppliers, engineering consultancies and software companies works for the major players, and many AI jobs in Toulouse are with these firms rather than the brand-name primes.

**Beyond aerospace.** Toulouse also has healthcare research, including the Oncopole cancer research campus, as well as agri-tech, mobility and digital companies.

## Trustworthy and Certifiable AI

The distinguishing feature of AI in Toulouse is the emphasis on safety and certification. In aviation, software used in aircraft is subject to strict development and certification processes. Introducing machine learning into such systems raises hard questions: how can you demonstrate that a neural network behaves correctly in all relevant conditions, how do you specify requirements for a learned component, and how do you monitor it over the life of an aircraft?

The European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) has published guidance and roadmaps on AI in aviation, and industry and research bodies in Toulouse are active in this field. For ML engineers, this creates demand for skills that are rare elsewhere:

- **Robustness and verification:** testing model behaviour under perturbations and formal verification of neural networks.
- **Data quality and representativeness:** demonstrating that training data covers the operating domain.
- **Explainability:** providing interpretable evidence for engineers and certification authorities.
- **Uncertainty quantification:** knowing when a model is outside its domain.
- **Documentation and traceability:** tracing from requirements to data, models and tests.

These skills also map closely to requirements of the EU AI Act for high-risk systems, which makes experience gained in Toulouse valuable across industries.

## Skills That Stand Out

Beyond core ML skills, Toulouse employers often value:

- **Signal processing and time series:** sensor data from aircraft, engines and satellites.
- **Computer vision and remote sensing:** satellite and inspection imagery.
- **Embedded and edge ML:** model compression, quantisation and deployment on constrained hardware.
- **Physics-informed and hybrid modelling:** combining physical models with learned components.
- **Systems engineering awareness:** understanding requirements, verification and validation processes.
- **Security:** in defence and space work, security clearance or eligibility may be required for certain roles.

## Working in French

Many international companies in Toulouse use English in technical work, but French remains the language of daily life and often of internal communication in large French organisations and suppliers. Some roles — particularly in defence or public research — require French. If you do not speak French, focus first on teams that explicitly work in English, invest in language learning early and expect it to become important as you grow into roles with more responsibility.

## Permits and the Talent Passport

EU and EEA citizens can work in France without a permit. Non-EU professionals often use the Talent Passport (Passeport Talent), a multi-year residence permit for qualified employees, researchers and other categories, which includes a version corresponding to the EU Blue Card. Researchers have a specific route. Requirements, including salary thresholds, change; always check official French sources and your employer's experience with the process.

## Evaluating an Offer for AI Jobs in Toulouse

- **Gross versus net:** French social contributions are significant; compare net pay and benefits carefully.
- **Collective agreements:** many engineering jobs fall under industry collective agreements (for example the Syntec agreement for engineering and IT services), which influence working time, leave and minimum conditions.
- **Working time:** executive (cadre) status and forfait jours arrangements affect how working hours are counted.
- **Employer type:** working directly for a prime differs from working for a consultancy placed at a prime's site in stability, scope and progression.
- **Location:** Airbus and many suppliers are around Blagnac and Colomiers to the west, while research campuses are in the south-east (Rangueil and the aerospace campus). Traffic around the ring road can be heavy, so housing near your workplace or on a tram and metro line saves time.
- **Hybrid policy:** varies widely; defence-related roles may require on-site work.

## Living in Toulouse

Toulouse — "la ville rose" for its pink brick buildings — is a large student city with a warm climate, a lively centre and access to the Pyrenees and the Mediterranean. Housing costs are generally lower than in Paris, and the metro, tram and bus network covers the main areas, with a third metro line under construction to connect the aerospace zones. For families, the region offers international schools linked to the aerospace industry. The quality of life is one reason many engineers stay long-term.

## A Closer Look at Typical Projects

To understand what AI jobs in Toulouse involve day to day, it helps to look at the kinds of projects that recur across the ecosystem.

**Predictive maintenance for aircraft fleets.** Modern aircraft generate large volumes of sensor and maintenance data. Teams build models to detect early signs of component degradation, prioritise inspections and reduce unscheduled maintenance. The challenges are typical of industrial ML: rare failure events, heterogeneous data from different aircraft types and operators, long feedback loops and the need to explain alerts to maintenance engineers who must act on them.

**Visual inspection in manufacturing.** Computer vision systems help detect defects in composite parts, surface treatments or assembly. Because false negatives can have serious consequences, these systems are usually designed to assist human inspectors rather than replace them, and much of the engineering work concerns measuring performance on rare defect types and integrating results into quality processes.

**Earth observation analytics.** Satellite imagery is used for agriculture, forestry, urban planning, maritime surveillance and climate monitoring. ML engineers work on segmentation, change detection, cloud removal and fusion of optical and radar data. Toulouse's space ecosystem provides both data and domain expertise.

**Onboard processing.** Processing data onboard satellites or aircraft reduces the amount of data that must be transmitted and allows faster decisions. This requires compact, energy-efficient models, radiation-tolerant hardware and very careful testing, because updates after launch are limited.

**Supply chain and production planning.** Aerospace manufacturing involves complex supply chains with long lead times. Forecasting, optimisation and simulation help plan production and anticipate disruptions.

## Career Paths in the Toulouse Ecosystem

The structure of the Toulouse market shapes career paths. Many engineers start at a consultancy or supplier, gain domain knowledge on projects for large primes and later move to a prime, a startup or a research institute. Others start with a PhD — often a CIFRE industrial doctorate, co-funded by a company and a laboratory, which is a common French model — and then join the partner company. Research engineers at institutes such as ONERA or at ANITI-affiliated laboratories can move into industry with rare expertise in trustworthy AI.

Because aerospace programmes last decades, domain knowledge accumulates value over time. An engineer who understands both ML and the constraints of avionics, certification or space missions becomes difficult to replace, and that is reflected in career opportunities.

## Questions to Ask in Toulouse Interviews

- Will the models I work on be used in operations, in engineering support or in certified systems?
- Who are the internal users, and how do they receive and act on model outputs?
- What data access do I have, and how long does it take to obtain it?
- Is the role with the company directly or placed at a client site?
- What language are meetings, documentation and code reviews conducted in?
- Does the role require security clearance or nationality restrictions?

Clear answers help you understand whether the role fits your expectations for impact, autonomy and growth.

## Building a Network in Toulouse

Toulouse's aerospace community is large but interconnected. Many engineers know each other from engineering schools, previous projects or supplier relationships. To build a network:

- Attend seminars and public events at ANITI, ISAE-SUPAERO and the university laboratories, many of which are open to industry participants.
- Join local data science and Python meetups, which bring together people from aerospace, healthcare and digital companies.
- Follow trade events and industry days organised by the aerospace cluster and regional bodies, where suppliers and startups present their work.
- Contribute to open-source tools or public datasets in Earth observation, which are widely used by Toulouse teams.

A good network helps you learn which teams are growing, which projects are starting and which employers offer the kind of work you want — information that rarely appears in job postings.

## Beyond the City: Occitanie

The wider Occitanie region adds further opportunities. Montpellier has a strong university, digital companies and health research, and some aerospace suppliers operate in smaller towns around Toulouse. Remote and hybrid arrangements make it possible for some roles to combine a Toulouse employer with living elsewhere in the region, though on-site presence remains important in manufacturing and defence work.
