Turin is the historic heart of Italian industry. It was the birthplace of FIAT, it remains a centre of automotive engineering and design, and it hosts major aerospace and space activities, one of Italy's largest banking groups and a strong technical university. In recent years, the city has positioned itself as a hub for industrial artificial intelligence, including as the home of AI4I, the Italian Institute of Artificial Intelligence for Industry. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Turin combine engineering depth, industrial applications and research at a cost of living considerably lower than Milan. This guide explains the landscape and what to consider before moving.

## The Industrial and Corporate Base

**Automotive and mobility.** Stellantis, formed from the merger of FCA and PSA, maintains significant operations in Turin, including the historic Mirafiori complex, and the region has a large ecosystem of automotive suppliers, design houses and engineering firms. AI applications include driver assistance and vehicle software, manufacturing quality, predictive maintenance, battery and electrification analytics, supply chain optimisation and design support.

**Aerospace and space.** Turin is one of Italy's leading aerospace centres. Leonardo has aeronautics activities in the region, and Thales Alenia Space has a major site in Turin working on space infrastructure, including modules for space stations and exploration missions. AI work covers satellite data analysis, onboard processing, testing and manufacturing analytics.

**Banking and insurance.** Intesa Sanpaolo, one of Italy's largest banking groups, has its registered office in Turin and a major presence in the city, and insurers and financial service providers add further roles. Data science in banking covers credit risk, fraud, AML, customer analytics and language models for documents and customer service.

**IT consulting and services.** Reply, a large Italian IT consulting and services group, was founded and is headquartered in Turin, and many consulting and system integration companies employ data scientists and ML engineers working for clients across Italy and Europe.

**Food and consumer goods.** Companies in coffee, confectionery and food production in Piedmont use data for demand forecasting, supply chain and quality.

**Innovation hubs.** OGR Torino, a former railway workshop transformed into a cultural and innovation centre, hosts startup programmes and technology activities.

## Research and Education

**Politecnico di Torino** is one of Italy's leading technical universities, with strong research in computer engineering, data science, machine learning, telecommunications, automotive and aerospace engineering. It collaborates extensively with industry and attracts international students.

**University of Turin** contributes computer science, mathematics, physics and life sciences.

**AI4I.** The Italian Institute of Artificial Intelligence for Industry was established in Turin as a national research institute focused on AI applied to industry, with the goal of connecting research with Italian manufacturing and services.

**ISI Foundation.** The ISI Foundation in Turin conducts research in complex systems, network science and data science, including applications in public health and social systems.

Other research organisations, such as the LINKS Foundation, work on applied digital research with industry.

## Italian Employment Conditions

Italian employment is governed by national collective agreements (CCNL) by sector — for example the metalworking agreement for many industrial employers, or banking and commerce agreements. Salaries are expressed as gross annual amounts (RAL), typically paid in 13 or 14 instalments, and employees accrue a severance fund (TFR). Many employers offer meal vouchers, supplementary health insurance and welfare benefits. Salaries in Turin are generally lower than in Milan, but so is the cost of living, particularly housing. Italy's tax regime for workers relocating to Italy ("impatriati") was reformed in 2024 with reduced benefits; check current eligibility with a tax adviser.

## Language

Italian is essential for daily life and for most workplaces in Turin, especially in industry, banking and public organisations. Research groups, some international teams and consulting projects for foreign clients use English. Learning Italian is a necessary investment for a long-term career in the city.

## Living in Turin

Turin is an elegant city with long arcades, baroque squares, a strong café culture and views of the Alps. It has a metro line, extensive trams and buses and fast trains to Milan (under an hour) and to France. Housing is considerably more affordable than in Milan, and the city offers a high quality of life with access to mountains and the Langhe wine region. Many industrial sites are located on the city's outskirts or in surrounding municipalities, so check commute times to the exact site.

## Automotive AI in a Changing Industry

The automotive industry is undergoing deep change — electrification, software-defined vehicles, new competitors and cost pressure — and Turin feels these changes directly. For AI professionals, this creates both opportunity and uncertainty. Demand for software, data and AI skills remains significant as manufacturers and suppliers build capabilities in vehicle software, battery analytics, connected services and digital manufacturing. At the same time, restructuring can affect individual business units. When evaluating an automotive role, ask how central the team is to the company's strategy, how it is funded and how stable its roadmap is. Diversifying your experience across automotive, aerospace, energy or industrial AI makes your career more resilient.

Typical automotive AI projects in Turin include vision-based inspection of paint and body parts, anomaly detection on assembly lines, predictive maintenance of production equipment, analysis of vehicle test data, battery state-of-health estimation and demand forecasting for parts. These projects require close collaboration with engineers and a solid understanding of quality and safety processes.

## Space and Aerospace Data

Turin's space industry builds pressurised modules, satellites and exploration systems, and aerospace companies in the region design and manufacture aircraft components. AI supports several activities: analysis of test data during integration, anomaly detection in telemetry, processing of Earth observation data, autonomous operations for spacecraft and quality control in manufacturing. Work in this sector follows rigorous standards for verification and documentation, and security considerations may apply to some projects. For ML engineers who enjoy long-horizon engineering and extreme reliability requirements, space and aerospace offer rare opportunities in Italy, concentrated in Turin.

## Banking AI at Scale

Turin's banking sector gives data scientists access to large, diverse datasets and mature data platforms. Beyond classic risk and fraud models, banks are applying language models to documents, internal knowledge management and customer interactions, with careful governance. Regulators expect model validation, documentation and monitoring, and under the EU AI Act creditworthiness assessment of natural persons is classified as high-risk. Engineers who combine ML skills with governance expertise are in demand, and banking roles offer stability and structured career paths that suit many professionals.

## Consulting as a Career Accelerator

Turin's IT consulting and services sector employs many data professionals, often working for clients in automotive, banking, energy, telecom and retail across Italy and Europe. Consulting offers variety, fast learning, exposure to different technologies and a broad network. The trade-offs are less ownership of long-term products and project-driven workloads. Many engineers spend several years in consulting before moving into an in-house role in the domain they enjoy most — a path that works well in Turin, where consulting firms and industrial clients are close together.

## Research Careers

For research-oriented engineers, Turin offers PhD and research positions at Politecnico di Torino and the University of Turin, applied research roles at AI4I and research on complex systems and data science at the ISI Foundation. National research programmes and European projects fund much of this work. As elsewhere in Italy, early research careers typically involve fixed-term positions, and competition for permanent academic posts is strong. The city's industrial base, however, makes transitions from research to industry comparatively easy, and industrial PhDs in collaboration with companies are common at Politecnico di Torino.

## Questions to Ask Turin Employers

- At which site will I work, and how is it connected to the city?
- Which collective agreement applies, and is the salary paid in 13 or 14 instalments?
- What is the working language, including for documentation?
- How central is the AI team to the company's strategy, and how is it funded?
- How do models move from prototype to production on the factory floor or in operations?
- Does the company support applications for the impatriate tax regime?

## Settling In

Newcomers need an Italian tax code (codice fiscale), registration of residence, enrolment in the national health service and a bank account. Procedures can take time, so start early and ask your employer for support.

## Networking in Turin

Turin's AI and data community meets at Politecnico di Torino events, AI4I seminars, OGR Torino programmes and local meetups on machine learning, data science and Python. Industry associations in automotive and aerospace organise events where AI is increasingly on the agenda. Personal relationships matter in Italian professional life, so attending events regularly and following up with people is one of the best ways to learn about opportunities, many of which are filled through recommendations.

## Who Turin Suits Best

Turin suits engineers who enjoy industrial, automotive or aerospace AI, want to work close to physical systems, speak or are ready to learn Italian and value a more affordable, elegant city with the Alps on the horizon.

## Evaluating an Offer in Turin

When comparing AI jobs in Turin with offers elsewhere, calculate the full annual package: gross salary across 13 or 14 payments, TFR accrual, meal vouchers, welfare benefits, supplementary health insurance and any variable pay. Then compare realistic housing costs. A Turin salary somewhat below a Milan or foreign offer can still leave you with more disposable income, because rents are lower and commutes shorter. Also weigh non-financial factors: the depth of the engineering work, the stability of the employer and the chance to own systems end to end, which are often strong points of Turin's industrial employers.

## Turin and the Wider North-West

Turin's location opens further options. Milan is under an hour by high-speed train, Genoa and the Ligurian coast are close, and the French border region is within reach. Some professionals live in Turin and work hybrid for Milan employers, combining Turin's lower living costs with Milan's larger job market — a practical arrangement worth discussing with employers.

## A Final Tip

Visit the actual site before accepting an industrial role: meeting the team where the machines are tells you more than any interview.

## Real example

### A vision engineer choosing Turin over Milan

An Italian computer vision engineer working in Germany on manufacturing inspection wants to return to Italy. He receives offers from a Milan consultancy and from an automotive engineering company in Turin working on quality inspection and vehicle perception.

The Milan offer pays slightly more, but he compares rents and finds Turin considerably cheaper. The Turin role also matches his expertise more closely and offers ownership of inspection systems across several plants. He checks his eligibility for the reformed impatriate regime and accepts the Turin role. After a year, he says his disposable income is higher than it would have been in Milan, and he values the combination of engineering depth and the city's quality of life.
