Tampere, set between two lakes in southern Finland, is the largest inland city in the Nordic countries and one of Finland's most popular places to study and live. It has a proud industrial history and today combines machinery and automation companies, telecom R&D, software and game studios with a large, merged university and a university of applied sciences. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Tampere offer entry into industrial AI, robotics and automation, signal processing, telecom and software, often in English-speaking teams, in a city with affordable housing and an active student culture. This guide explains where to look and how to get started.

## Tampere University and TAMK

Tampere University, formed in 2019 by merging the University of Tampere and Tampere University of Technology, offers programmes in computing, electrical engineering, automation, mechanical engineering, physics and health sciences, many in English. Its research covers signal processing, machine learning, computer vision, robotics and automation, photonics and health technology. The Hervanta campus, in the south of the city, is a traditional technology hub where university research and companies meet.

Tampere University of Applied Sciences (TAMK) trains practically oriented engineers and IT professionals, often through projects with local companies.

## Industrial Machinery and Automation

Tampere has a long tradition of heavy industry and machine building. Companies in mining and rock excavation equipment, such as Sandvik's operations in Tampere, and in pulp, paper and energy technology, such as Valmet's operations in the region, develop machines that are increasingly automated and connected. Other companies build cranes, forestry machines and industrial automation systems. AI applications include autonomous mobile machines, remote operation, predictive maintenance, process optimisation and computer vision for machine perception. Tampere University's research on autonomous heavy machinery and robotics connects directly with this industry.

## Telecom and Electronics

Nokia has a significant site in Tampere, and the city has a history in mobile phone development. Telecom and electronics companies work on network software, radio technologies and devices. AI roles include network automation, signal processing and embedded software.

## Software and Games

Tampere has a lively software sector, including consultancies, product companies and game studios. The Finnish game industry is internationally known, and Tampere has its own game companies and education programmes. Software companies work on digital services for industry, health and public sector clients, increasingly incorporating AI.

## Entry Paths

1. **Summer jobs.** Finnish companies hire many students each summer, often leading to thesis projects and permanent roles.
2. **Master's thesis in a company**, typically paid.
3. **Research assistant and PhD positions** at Tampere University.
4. **Junior roles** in machinery, telecom, software and games.

## Working in Finland

Finnish workplaces are flat, trust-based and focused on reliability and work-life balance. Collective agreements shape pay and conditions, and benefits often include lunch and sports benefits and occupational healthcare. English is widely used in tech companies and research; Finnish helps in daily life and with some employers. EU citizens can work freely; non-EU graduates of Finnish universities may be able to stay to look for work, and specialists can apply for permits including fast-track services.

## Living in Tampere

Tampere consistently ranks as one of Finland's most popular cities to live in, with a lively centre, lakes on both sides, saunas — it calls itself the sauna capital of the world — and a new tram network. Housing is more affordable than in Helsinki, and trains reach the capital in about 1.5 hours. The student community is large, and the city has an active cultural scene.

## Autonomous Heavy Machinery

Autonomous and remotely operated heavy machines — in mining, construction, forestry and ports — are a Finnish speciality, and Tampere is a centre for this work. These machines operate in harsh, unstructured environments: dust, darkness, mud, snow and changing terrain. Perception systems must detect obstacles and people reliably, localisation must work where GPS is unavailable, and planning must account for heavy loads and safety. Machine learning supports perception and prediction, while control engineering ensures safe motion. Safety is paramount, and the EU Machinery Regulation and the EU AI Act set requirements for AI used as safety components. For graduates, this field offers a rare combination of robotics, ML and real industrial impact.

## Process Industry and Paper Technology

Pulp, paper and energy technology companies apply AI to process optimisation, quality control and predictive maintenance in large industrial plants around the world. Data scientists work with sensor data from machines that run continuously and must be tuned for efficiency and quality. These roles teach graduates how data science creates value in capital-intensive industries.

## Signal Processing and Vision Research

Tampere University has a strong tradition in signal processing, computer vision and image processing, with research on audio, video, imaging and sensor data. Graduates with this background find roles in telecom, electronics, medical devices and machinery. Research groups offer PhD positions in areas such as deep learning for signals, 3D vision and photonics, often in collaboration with companies.

## Building a Profile for Tampere Employers

- **Signal processing, control and ML fundamentals**.
- **Programming in Python and C++**, plus ROS for robotics roles.
- **Hands-on projects** with sensors, robots or embedded systems.
- **A thesis with a company** or applied research group.
- **English communication** and, ideally, some Finnish.

## Game Development and Player Analytics

Finland's game industry is internationally known, and Tampere hosts game studios and game education programmes. Data roles in games involve player analytics, balancing and live operations. For graduates who enjoy creative products, game studios offer a lively, informal environment.

## Questions to Ask Tampere Employers

- What will I work on in my first months, and who will mentor me?
- Will I work with real machines or mostly in simulation?
- What is the working language of the team?
- Is the thesis position paid, and can it lead to a permanent role?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what benefits are included?

## Networking

Tampere has an active tech community, with events on the Hervanta campus, startup programmes, game jams and software meetups. Student associations organise company visits and career fairs.

## Your First Two Years

Build foundations in engineering practices, ML and your chosen domain — autonomous machines, signal processing, telecom or software. Ask for feedback and take ownership of small deliverables. Tampere's ecosystem makes it easy to move between research and industry later.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews for junior roles in Tampere usually include technical questions on programming, signal processing or ML, discussion of your thesis or projects and conversations about teamwork. Finnish interviewers value honesty and clarity over self-promotion. Prepare to explain your projects concisely and to ask thoughtful questions about the team and projects.

## Staying in Tampere

Many graduates stay in Tampere because of its quality of life, affordable housing and growing tech sector. Experience from Tampere's machinery and automation industry is valued internationally, and Helsinki remains within easy reach for later moves.

## A Simple Action Plan

Apply for summer jobs early, choose a thesis with a company, attend campus events and follow up with engineers you meet.

## Mining and Rock Excavation Technology

Mining equipment developed in Tampere operates in mines around the world, from underground tunnels to open pits. Modern mining machines are increasingly automated: drill rigs position themselves, loaders navigate tunnels autonomously and fleets are monitored remotely from control rooms far from the mine. Data science supports these systems in several ways — perception and navigation for autonomous machines, predictive maintenance using sensor data from engines, hydraulics and drills, optimisation of drilling patterns and fleet scheduling, and analysis of energy use as machines move toward battery-electric power. The work combines ML with mechanical engineering, hydraulics and safety, and it contributes to making mining safer by keeping people away from hazardous areas. Graduates who join these teams gain experience with some of the most demanding real-world autonomy problems anywhere, as well as the chance to see their work deployed on machines used globally. Tampere University's research on heavy machinery automation and the companies' own R&D centres create a steady stream of thesis and junior opportunities.

## Health Technology

Tampere also has health technology research and companies, supported by the university's health sciences faculty and Tampere University Hospital. Projects include medical imaging, biosignal analysis, rehabilitation technology and digital health services. Finland's comprehensive health data and strict data protection framework make careful methodology essential. Graduates interested in health can combine signal processing and ML with clinical collaboration.

## Software Consultancies

Tampere's software consultancies build digital services and data solutions for industrial, public and commercial clients. They offer graduates varied projects, strong craftsmanship cultures and exposure to many technologies, making them a good starting point before specialising.

## Reading Graduate Offers

Finnish graduate salaries in engineering and IT are often defined by collective agreements, with benefits such as lunch and sports vouchers and occupational healthcare included. When comparing offers, look at salary, benefits, mentoring, training and the type of work you will do in your first year. For your first role, learning and mentoring matter more than small salary differences, and a well-supported thesis project can be the best possible start.

## Responsible AI in Industrial Autonomy

Autonomous machines operate near people and in safety-critical settings. Engineers must document how systems are tested, understand failure modes and design safe fallbacks. The EU AI Act and machinery regulations require risk management and human oversight for AI used as safety components, and Finnish companies take these obligations seriously. Graduates who understand safety engineering alongside ML stand out.

## Life in Tampere

Beyond work, Tampere offers lakes for swimming and skating, many public saunas, a strong music and theatre scene and easy access to nature. The city is walkable and bikeable, with trams connecting key areas including Hervanta. Many international students find Tampere friendly and easy to settle into, and the large student population keeps it lively. Winters are dark but snowy and bright compared with coastal cities, and summers are long and warm by Nordic standards.

## A Final Tip

Visit a company's test site or lab before choosing a thesis; seeing real machines in action makes it easier to choose the right path.

## Staying Connected

Keep in touch with your summer job colleagues and thesis supervisors after graduating; Tampere's engineering community is close-knit, and recommendations often lead to the next role.

## One More Tip

Join a student team or game jam in your first year; it builds skills and contacts quickly.

## Real example

### An automation graduate working on autonomous machines

An automation engineering student at Tampere University did a summer job at a machinery company, testing sensor systems on mobile machines. Her master's thesis, also with the company, used machine learning to improve obstacle detection for autonomous operation in harsh environments.

After graduation, she joined the company's autonomy team as a junior engineer. She works with lidar and camera data, collaborates with control engineers and tests systems on real machines. She says Tampere's combination of heavy industry and strong automation research made it possible to work on autonomous systems without leaving her home city.
