Prague is one of Central Europe's most attractive cities for technology professionals. Its historic beauty and cultural life are well known, but the city also has a mature software industry, strong technical universities and a track record of home-grown companies with global reach — from cybersecurity software used by hundreds of millions of people to document AI and hospitality platforms. Data scientist jobs in Prague span consumer security, product analytics, e-commerce, search and language models, banking, automotive and research. This guide explains where the work is, what employers expect and what experienced professionals should consider before moving.

## The Czech Technology Landscape

**Cybersecurity and consumer software.** Avast, founded in Prague, became one of the world's best-known antivirus companies and later merged with NortonLifeLock to form Gen Digital, which maintains a large engineering presence in Prague. Cybersecurity at this scale relies heavily on ML for malware detection, phishing and scam detection, threat intelligence and user analytics.

**Document AI.** Rossum, founded in Prague, develops AI for processing business documents such as invoices, combining computer vision and language models. It is an example of Czech deep-tech expertise turned into an international SaaS product.

**Search and language models.** Seznam.cz, the Czech internet company and search engine, has long developed its own search and language technologies for Czech, including language models. This makes Prague one of the few places in Europe where a local company competes in search with its own ML stack.

**E-commerce and quick commerce.** Rohlik Group, founded in Prague, operates online grocery delivery in several European countries. E-commerce data science covers demand forecasting, logistics optimisation, pricing, personalisation and warehouse automation.

**SaaS and product companies.** Prague has a growing number of software companies selling internationally, such as Mews in hospitality software and Productboard in product management tools, which use data science for product analytics, recommendations and AI features.

**Automotive and industry.** Škoda Auto, headquartered in Mladá Boleslav north-east of Prague and part of the Volkswagen Group, and many suppliers apply data science to manufacturing, quality, logistics and vehicle software. The Czech Republic has a strong industrial base in which AI adoption is growing.

**Banking and finance.** Czech banks and financial institutions headquartered in Prague use data science for credit risk, fraud detection, customer analytics and digital banking.

**Shared service and technology centres.** International companies operate technology and business service centres in Prague, employing data engineers, analysts and data scientists.

## Research and Universities

**Czech Technical University in Prague (CTU).** CTU is the country's leading technical university. Its Faculty of Electrical Engineering hosts an AI Center with research in machine learning, planning, multi-agent systems and computer vision, and CTU's Czech Institute of Informatics, Robotics and Cybernetics (CIIRC) conducts research in AI, robotics and industrial automation, including a testbed for Industry 4.0.

**Charles University.** The Faculty of Mathematics and Physics at Charles University is known for strong computer science, including natural language processing — its Institute of Formal and Applied Linguistics has a long track record in Czech and multilingual NLP — as well as machine learning and theoretical computer science.

**Other institutions.** The Czech Academy of Sciences has institutes working on computer science and information theory, and universities in Brno — including Brno University of Technology and Masaryk University — add a second strong tech centre in the country.

## Language and Culture

Czech is the language of daily life and many local organisations. In international product companies, cybersecurity firms and technology centres, English is the standard working language. Prague has a large international community, and daily life in central areas is manageable in English, but learning basic Czech helps with bureaucracy and social integration. Czech workplace culture in tech is generally informal, pragmatic and direct, with a strong engineering focus.

## Pay, Currency and Taxes

Salaries in Prague are among the highest in the Czech Republic and have grown quickly in tech, though they generally remain below Western European levels. Salaries are paid in Czech koruna (CZK), so currency movements affect their value in euros. The Czech tax system includes personal income tax and social and health insurance contributions; some experienced professionals work as contractors on a trade licence, which has different tax and social security implications. Compare net income and protections carefully, and consider the cost of living: rents in Prague have risen significantly and are among the highest in Central Europe relative to local salaries.

## Permits

EU citizens can work freely and register their residence if staying long-term. Non-EU professionals typically use the Employee Card, a combined work and residence permit, or the EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers, under the Czech implementation of the recast Blue Card Directive. The Czech Republic has run programmes to speed up procedures for highly qualified employees of certain employers. Check official Czech sources for current rules and processing times.

## Living in Prague

Prague offers a beautiful historic centre, excellent public transport with metro, trams and buses, abundant parks and a lively cultural life. The city is safe and well connected by train and air to the rest of Europe; Vienna, Berlin, Dresden and Munich are within reach. Many tech companies are located in districts such as Karlín, Holešovice, Smíchov and the centre. Housing costs have risen, so many people live further out along metro and tram lines.

## Cybersecurity Data Science in Depth

Prague's cybersecurity sector offers some of the most challenging data science work in Europe. Security products installed on very large numbers of devices generate telemetry on files, URLs, network activity and user interactions. Data scientists and ML engineers work on:

- **Malware classification:** identifying malicious files and behaviours, including new variants.
- **Phishing and scam detection:** analysing websites, messages and emails, increasingly with language and vision models.
- **Threat intelligence:** clustering and attributing campaigns, detecting emerging threats.
- **Privacy-preserving analytics:** extracting insight while respecting user privacy and GDPR.
- **Evaluation under adversarial conditions:** attackers adapt, so models must be continuously monitored and updated.

The adversarial nature of the work, the need for very low false-positive rates and the scale of deployment make it intellectually demanding. Engineers who enjoy these challenges build expertise that is valuable across security, fraud and trust and safety roles.

## Document AI and Vision-Language Models

Document AI — extracting structured information from invoices, receipts, contracts and forms — has become a significant field, and Prague has notable expertise. The work combines layout understanding, optical character recognition, vision-language models and domain knowledge of business processes. Challenges include handling many document formats and languages, learning from limited labelled data, integrating human review for uncertain cases and meeting enterprise requirements for accuracy, security and auditability. Recent advances in multimodal language models have transformed the field, and companies in Prague are applying them to real business workflows.

## Czech Language Technology

Czech is a highly inflected Slavic language with rich morphology, which makes it an interesting challenge for NLP. Prague has a long tradition in Czech language technology: Charles University's linguistics and NLP researchers have produced widely used resources, including treebanks and tools, and Seznam.cz has developed its own search and language models for Czech. For NLP engineers, this creates opportunities in search ranking, language model training and evaluation for Czech, machine translation and domain-specific applications in banking, government and e-commerce. Expertise in morphologically rich, lower-resource languages is transferable to many other European languages.

## E-commerce and Logistics

Prague's e-commerce companies, including online grocery, face demanding data science problems: forecasting demand for perishable goods, optimising delivery slots and routes, managing warehouse automation and personalising assortments. Waste reduction is a key objective in grocery, which makes accurate forecasting both a business and a sustainability issue. These companies often expand into several countries, so data scientists work with data from multiple markets and must handle differences in customer behaviour and logistics.

## Brno as an Alternative

Brno, the Czech Republic's second-largest city, is about two and a half hours from Prague by train and has its own strong tech scene. Brno University of Technology and Masaryk University produce many graduates, and companies such as Kiwi.com, the travel technology company, and many international technology centres are based there. Living costs are lower than in Prague. For data scientists who prefer a smaller city, Brno is worth considering alongside data scientist jobs in Prague.

## Questions to Ask Prague Employers

- Is the salary set in koruna or euros, and how are adjustments handled?
- Is the contract an employment contract or a trade-licence arrangement?
- What is the working language, including for documentation and company meetings?
- How does the team evaluate models and handle production incidents?
- What relocation support is offered, including permits and housing?

## Industrial AI and the CIIRC Testbed

The Czech Republic is one of Europe's most industrialised economies, with a large share of employment in manufacturing, especially automotive. CTU's CIIRC operates a testbed for Industry 4.0 technologies, where researchers and companies experiment with robotics, digital twins, AI-based quality control and flexible production. For data scientists interested in industrial applications, collaborations between CIIRC and manufacturers offer interesting projects, and roles at automotive and industrial companies around Prague provide hands-on experience with production data, predictive maintenance and supply chain optimisation.

## Networking in Prague

Prague has an active data science community, with regular meetups on machine learning, data science, Python and MLOps, as well as conferences and hackathons. University seminars at CTU and Charles University are often open to professionals. The international community is large and welcoming, and meetups are a good way to learn about the culture of different employers, understand local salary levels and make friends.

## Settling In

Newcomers need to register their residence, obtain health insurance, open a bank account and, for non-EU citizens, complete permit procedures. Many employers and relocation agencies help with these steps; start early, as appointments and processing can take time.

## Who Prague Suits Best

Prague suits data scientists who want product-focused, technically deep work in English-speaking teams, combined with a beautiful, well-connected city at the heart of Europe.

## Real example

### A security data scientist moving from Amsterdam to Prague

A data scientist working on fraud detection at an Amsterdam fintech receives an offer from a Prague cybersecurity company to work on scam detection for consumer products. The gross salary is lower, but the role involves models protecting a very large user base and working with an experienced research team.

He calculates net income in koruna, considers currency risk and compares Prague rents with his Amsterdam costs. He finds that his disposable income would be similar and that the technical challenge — adversarial, high-volume detection — is exactly what he wants. He accepts, and after a year says the combination of technical depth, English-speaking teams and Prague's quality of life made the move worthwhile, although learning basic Czech proved necessary for dealing with authorities.
