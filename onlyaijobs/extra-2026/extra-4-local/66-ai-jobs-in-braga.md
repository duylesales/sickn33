Braga, Portugal's third-largest city and one of its oldest, has become a surprising technology centre. Its University of Minho is one of Portugal's leading engineering universities, a large automotive electronics R&D centre employs hundreds of software and hardware engineers, an intergovernmental nanotechnology laboratory sits on the city's edge and a lively startup scene has grown in recent years. With a young population and Porto less than an hour away, Braga offers graduates a realistic and affordable place to start an AI career. This guide explains where AI jobs in Braga are found and how to get started.

## The University of Minho

The University of Minho (UMinho) has campuses in Braga (Gualtar) and Guimarães (Azurém). Its School of Engineering offers programmes in informatics, electronics, industrial electronics, biomedical engineering and data science, and its research centres work on AI, machine learning, computer vision, robotics, software engineering and cybersecurity. The university has a strong tradition of collaboration with industry, especially automotive, textiles and manufacturing companies in the Minho region, and many students do their master's dissertations inside companies.

## Automotive and Embedded Software

Bosch's Car Multimedia centre in Braga is one of the company's important locations for automotive electronics, combining production and a large R&D centre working on in-vehicle systems, sensors and software. Collaboration with the University of Minho has supported research on topics such as automated driving, sensors and human–machine interfaces. For graduates, this environment offers roles in embedded software, computer vision, sensor data processing, testing and validation, often with international teams. Other automotive and electronics suppliers in the region add opportunities.

## Nanotechnology Research

The International Iberian Nanotechnology Laboratory (INL), founded by Portugal and Spain, is an intergovernmental research organisation based in Braga. It works on nanotechnology for health, environment, food, energy and ICT, including nanoelectronics and sensors. INL uses data science in materials research, microscopy image analysis and sensor development, and it runs programmes for young researchers. Working at INL offers an international research environment, typically in English.

## Software Companies and Startups

Braga has a growing number of software companies and startups, supported by Startup Braga, a municipal innovation hub that runs acceleration programmes and connects founders with investors. Business software, health tech, e-commerce and industrial software companies are present, and several international software companies have engineering teams in the city. Guimarães, 25 minutes away, has textile, manufacturing and technology companies linked to UMinho's Azurém campus.

## Entry Paths

**Master's dissertation in a company.** Portuguese engineering master's programmes end with a dissertation, and many UMinho students do theirs inside companies. This is one of the most effective routes into a first job.

**Research fellowships.** Research centres and INL hire graduates on research fellowships (bolsas) funded by projects, which offer research experience and can lead to PhDs or industry roles.

**Internships and professional traineeships** at software companies and startups.

**Graduate recruitment** at larger employers, which run programmes for recent graduates.

## Pay and Cost of Living

Salaries in Portugal are generally lower than in Northern and Western Europe, and this applies to entry-level AI roles. Braga's cost of living is lower than Porto's or Lisbon's, although housing has become more expensive. Portugal has introduced tax measures for young people in the first years of their careers; check the current rules. Compare offers on net pay, meal allowances (commonly paid in Portugal) and learning opportunities.

## Language

Portuguese is essential for daily life and for many local employers. International R&D teams, INL and many software companies work in English. International graduates can find English-speaking roles, especially in R&D, but learning Portuguese helps with integration.

## Automotive AI: What the Work Involves

Automotive R&D in Braga covers in-vehicle systems, sensors and software. Graduates working in AI-related roles might develop algorithms that process camera, radar or lidar data, build tools that analyse large recordings of test drives, validate perception functions against safety requirements or optimise models to run on embedded hardware. Automotive software follows strict development processes and safety standards, so documentation, testing and traceability are part of daily work. This discipline is valuable preparation for any career in safety-critical AI, and it is increasingly relevant under the EU AI Act, which classifies AI systems used as safety components in certain regulated products as high-risk.

## Building a Profile for Braga Employers

Braga employers value graduates who combine solid engineering with AI skills:

- **Programming**: Python for ML, and C or C++ for embedded and automotive roles.
- **Computer vision and signal processing**: especially for automotive and sensor work.
- **Software engineering discipline**: version control, testing and documentation.
- **A dissertation with real-world relevance**, ideally in a company or applied research project.
- **English communication** for international teams, and Portuguese for local employers.

Student associations, robotics teams and hackathons at UMinho are good ways to build projects and meet employers. Startup Braga's events connect students with founders and investors.

## Research Fellowships and PhDs

Research fellowships in Portugal allow graduates to work on funded research projects at universities, research centres and institutes such as INL. They offer valuable experience, publications and networks, and they can lead to PhD positions or industry roles. Fellowships are often fixed-term and paid as stipends rather than employment contracts, so it is worth understanding the conditions, social security coverage and duration before accepting. For graduates who enjoy research, a fellowship followed by a PhD in collaboration with industry is a common path in Braga.

## Textiles, Manufacturing and Industry 4.0

The Minho region has a strong manufacturing tradition, including textiles, footwear, metalworking and electronics. Companies are modernising with data and AI: quality inspection with computer vision, predictive maintenance, production planning and energy optimisation. UMinho's research centres and technology institutes support these companies with applied projects. For graduates, industrial data roles offer concrete problems and visible impact, often in companies that export worldwide.

## Living in Braga

Braga combines a historic centre with baroque churches, a young student population and a relaxed pace of life. Housing is more affordable than in Porto or Lisbon, although prices have risen. The city is compact, with buses and growing cycling infrastructure, and trains connect it to Porto in under an hour. The Peneda-Gerês National Park and the Atlantic coast are within easy reach for weekends.

## Questions to Ask Braga Employers

- What will I work on in my first six months, and who will mentor me?
- What is the working language of the team?
- Is the position an employment contract or a fellowship, and what are the conditions?
- What training and certification opportunities are offered?
- Is hybrid work possible?

## Braga or Porto?

Many graduates in northern Portugal weigh Braga against Porto. Porto has a larger job market, more international companies and a larger startup scene, but higher housing costs. Braga offers strong automotive R&D, close university–industry links and lower living costs. Because the two cities are well connected, some graduates live in Braga and work in Porto, or vice versa. Comparing concrete offers — the team, mentoring, pay and commute — is more useful than choosing a city in the abstract.

## Your First Two Years

Use your first years to build engineering foundations, depth in one area such as computer vision, embedded ML or data engineering, and experience with production systems. Stay connected to UMinho and your classmates. These foundations prepare you for senior roles in Braga, Porto, Lisbon or abroad.

## Staying in Portugal or Moving Abroad

Many Portuguese engineers consider working abroad for higher salaries, and some return later with international experience. Remote work has also made it possible to work for foreign companies while living in Braga. For a first job, however, in-person mentoring in a strong local team usually matters more than a higher salary elsewhere. Later, compare concrete offers on net pay, cost of living and career growth.

## A Simple Action Plan

Choose three Braga employers and one research centre that interest you, attend their events, ask about dissertation or internship opportunities and follow up personally. In a city of Braga's size, genuine interest gets noticed quickly.

## Networking in Braga

UMinho organises job fairs and company presentations, Startup Braga hosts events and demo days and tech meetups in Braga and Porto bring together students and professionals. Attending regularly helps you discover roles before they are advertised and meet engineers who can recommend you. Because the local tech community is still relatively small, people remember those who show genuine curiosity, and a single good conversation at a meetup can lead to an internship or a dissertation project.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews for junior AI roles in Braga usually include a conversation about motivation and background, a technical interview covering programming, algorithms and machine learning fundamentals, and a discussion of your dissertation or projects. Automotive and embedded companies may ask about C or C++, real-time constraints, signal processing or testing practices, while software companies focus on Python, SQL, cloud basics and product thinking. Research centres and INL look closely at your academic work, your understanding of methods and your ability to work independently. Prepare by revisiting your projects in depth: explain why you chose certain methods, how you evaluated them, what went wrong and what you would do differently. Practise explaining your dissertation in five minutes to a non-specialist. Portuguese interviewers generally appreciate humility, curiosity and clear reasoning more than self-promotion, and asking thoughtful questions about the team, mentoring and how models reach production shows that you take the role seriously.

## Responsible AI Skills

Graduates who understand GDPR, bias evaluation and documentation practices bring practical value to Braga employers building AI for European customers, especially in automotive and health applications.

## Reading Graduate Offers Carefully

When you receive an offer, check the contract type, gross monthly salary and number of payments (Portuguese salaries are often paid in 14 instalments), meal allowance, health insurance, training budget and hybrid policy. Ask about the first-year learning plan and who will review your work. For graduates, mentoring and learning opportunities matter more than small salary differences.

## Staying Connected

After graduating, keep in touch with your dissertation supervisor and classmates; in Braga's close-knit ecosystem, those relationships often lead to your next opportunity, whether in a company, a research centre or a startup.

## Real example

### A UMinho graduate who stayed in Braga

A UMinho master's student in informatics engineering did her dissertation on sensor fusion for driver assistance in collaboration with an automotive R&D team in Braga. Many classmates planned to move to Lisbon or abroad.

The company offered her a junior engineer role in its perception team, and a university research centre offered a research fellowship with the possibility of a PhD. She chose the company, where her dissertation work continued directly and the team was international. She rents an apartment near the city centre at a price far below Lisbon levels. After two years, she works on validation of perception algorithms and says Braga gave her an international career without leaving the north of Portugal.
