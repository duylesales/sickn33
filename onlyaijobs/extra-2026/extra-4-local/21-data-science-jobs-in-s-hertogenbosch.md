's-Hertogenbosch — better known as Den Bosch — is a good illustration. It is the capital of Noord-Brabant, home to the Jheronimus Academy of Data Science (JADS), and surrounded by employers in energy, water, food, health, government and industry that need data talent. Data science jobs in 's-Hertogenbosch and the surrounding region are real, varied and often overlooked. This guide is written for students and graduates who want to understand what the region offers before deciding to move away.

## JADS: A Data Science Academy in the Heart of Den Bosch

The Jheronimus Academy of Data Science is a joint initiative of Tilburg University and Eindhoven University of Technology, located in 's-Hertogenbosch. It offers bachelor's, master's and doctoral education in data science, combining technical depth with business and entrepreneurship. JADS students work on projects with companies and public organisations, and the academy has become a meeting point between the region's universities and employers.

For graduates, JADS matters in two ways. First, it produces a steady flow of data scientists in the city, which in turn attracts employers who want to hire them. Second, its collaborations with regional organisations mean that students often discover local employers through projects — exactly the kind of connection that keeps talent in the region. Avans University of Applied Sciences, with a campus in 's-Hertogenbosch, adds practically trained IT and business graduates.

## Employers Behind Data Science Jobs in 's-Hertogenbosch

**Energy.** Enexis, one of the main Dutch electricity and gas distribution network operators, has its headquarters in 's-Hertogenbosch. Grid operators face major challenges from the energy transition — congestion, solar panels on roofs, heat pumps and electric vehicles — and rely on data science for forecasting, network planning, asset management and customer processes.

**Water.** Brabant Water, the drinking water company for most of the province, is also headquartered in the city. Water companies use data for leak detection, demand forecasting, asset management and water quality monitoring.

**Government and public services.** The Province of Noord-Brabant has its provincial government building in 's-Hertogenbosch, and municipalities, water boards and public agencies in the region employ data analysts and scientists for policy analysis, spatial planning, mobility and environmental monitoring.

**Health.** The Jeroen Bosch Ziekenhuis is a large teaching hospital in the city. Hospitals and healthcare organisations use data for capacity planning, clinical prediction models and quality improvement.

**Food and consumer goods.** Den Bosch has a brewing tradition and food production in the surrounding area. Food companies use data for production optimisation, quality control, forecasting and marketing.

**Finance, insurance and services.** Financial service providers, insurers and consultancies have offices in the city, often serving clients across the south of the Netherlands.

**Startups and scale-ups.** The JADS ecosystem and regional innovation programmes support data-driven startups.

## Entry-Level Roles to Look For

- **Junior data scientist** in energy, water, health or public sector organisations.
- **Data analyst** roles with a path toward modelling and ML.
- **Data engineer** roles, often the most numerous entry points.
- **Traineeships** in data or IT at larger organisations and through regional traineeship programmes.
- **PhD or research assistant** positions at JADS, TU/e or Tilburg University.
- **Consultancy roles** serving regional clients.

Many regional employers publish vacancies in Dutch and use titles such as "data specialist", "data-analist" or "BI-specialist" for roles that involve data science. Search broadly and read descriptions carefully.

## Why Regional Employers Struggle to Be Seen

Understanding why regional employers are overlooked helps you find them. Large tech companies invest in employer branding, career fairs and online visibility. A grid operator, water company or provincial government has a different primary mission and often a smaller recruitment budget. Their vacancies may be published on their own websites or general job boards, in Dutch, with functional titles. Students scrolling through listings for "machine learning engineer" rarely come across them.

Yet these organisations often offer exactly what graduates say they want: meaningful problems, real data, stable employment, reasonable working hours and the chance to grow quickly because teams are smaller. Many also offer good employment conditions under collective labour agreements, including pensions, training budgets and flexible working. The mismatch is informational, not substantive — and that is something you can fix by changing how you search.

## What Regional Employers Look For in Graduates

Employers offering data science jobs in 's-Hertogenbosch and the surrounding region often look for a combination of technical skill and practical attitude:

- **Solid fundamentals:** Python or R, SQL, statistics and machine learning basics.
- **Data preparation:** real-world data in energy, water or healthcare is messy; willingness to clean and understand it is essential.
- **Communication:** explaining results to engineers, policy advisers, doctors or managers who are not data specialists.
- **Domain curiosity:** interest in how the grid, water network, hospital or supply chain actually works.
- **Business sense:** JADS graduates are often valued for combining data science with business understanding.
- **Dutch language:** helpful or required for many regional roles, especially in public organisations.
- **Responsible data use:** understanding of GDPR and, increasingly, the EU AI Act, particularly in public sector and health settings.

If you are still studying at JADS, use your remaining time strategically. Choose projects with regional organisations, attend events where companies present their data challenges and ask project partners about their teams and vacancies. A thesis or project with a regional employer is one of the most effective ways to secure a first job, because the organisation already knows your work. Alumni of JADS working in the region are also a valuable source of information — many are happy to talk about their experience.

## Housing and Living in Den Bosch

's-Hertogenbosch is a lively historic city with a well-preserved centre, canals and a strong cultural life, including the annual Carnival and events around Jheronimus Bosch, the painter after whom JADS is named. Housing is in demand, as throughout the Netherlands, but rents are generally lower than in Amsterdam or Utrecht. Surrounding towns such as Rosmalen, Vught, Oss and Boxtel offer further options with good connections. For many graduates, the combination of lower costs, a strong local identity and access to a wide regional job market makes Den Bosch an attractive base for the first years of a career.

## Typical First-Year Projects

To make the opportunities concrete, here are examples of work graduates might do in their first year at regional employers.

**At a grid operator.** Building forecasts of electricity demand and solar generation at neighbourhood level; analysing where congestion is likely as heat pumps and electric vehicles spread; supporting planners who decide where to reinforce cables and transformers; improving data quality in asset registries.

**At a water company.** Detecting leaks from flow and pressure patterns; forecasting drinking water demand during heatwaves; predicting which pipes are most likely to fail so that replacement can be prioritised; monitoring water quality measurements for anomalies.

**At a provincial or municipal government.** Analysing mobility data to support traffic and public transport planning; combining spatial data to evaluate nature, housing or energy policies; building dashboards that help policy advisers track progress; supporting responsible algorithm use through documentation and registers.

**At a hospital.** Forecasting bed occupancy and emergency department demand; supporting clinical researchers with data preparation and statistical analysis; evaluating prediction models together with clinicians, with careful attention to privacy and validation.

**At a food or manufacturing company.** Optimising production planning; predicting quality outcomes from process data; forecasting demand for products with seasonal patterns.

These projects share a common trait: they connect data science to tangible results that matter to people living in the region. For many graduates, that sense of purpose is a strong reason to choose a regional employer.

## Planning Your Career From Den Bosch

A first job in the region does not limit your future. The skills you build — forecasting, optimisation, working with messy real-world data, communicating with non-specialists — are valued everywhere. Many professionals in Brabant build their entire careers in the region, moving between energy, high-tech, logistics and public organisations as their interests develop. Others gain experience locally and later move abroad or to the Randstad, with a stronger profile than if they had started there. Either way, starting close to home with meaningful work and manageable living costs is a sound foundation.

## Traineeships and Graduate Programmes in Brabant

Traineeships are a common route into the Dutch labour market, and Brabant has many of them. Larger organisations such as grid operators, water companies, the provincial government and consultancies run their own programmes, and regional traineeship organisations place trainees with several employers over one or two years. A good data traineeship combines real project work with training in technical and professional skills, a mentor and a clear perspective on a permanent role afterwards.

When comparing traineeships, ask how much time is spent on actual data work versus general training, which teams you will rotate through, whether you will work with experienced data scientists and what happened to previous trainees. Traineeships at public organisations often follow collective labour agreement pay scales, which makes salaries transparent and predictable.
