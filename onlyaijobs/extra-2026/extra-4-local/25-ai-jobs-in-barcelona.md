Barcelona attracts people for its architecture, beaches and Mediterranean lifestyle, but it has also become one of Southern Europe's most important technology hubs. The city hosts one of Europe's leading supercomputing centres, strong universities and research institutes, a vibrant startup ecosystem and engineering hubs of international companies. Every year, the Mobile World Congress brings the global telecommunications industry to the city. AI jobs in Barcelona range from research on large-scale computing and language models to product ML at scale-ups and data science in industry. This guide explains the landscape and the practicalities for experienced engineers considering a move.

## The Research and Computing Base

**Barcelona Supercomputing Center (BSC-CNS).** The BSC is Spain's national supercomputing centre and hosts MareNostrum 5, one of the EuroHPC supercomputers and among the most powerful in Europe. The BSC conducts research in computer science, life sciences, earth sciences and engineering, and has been active in developing language models and resources for Spanish, Catalan and other Iberian languages. It employs researchers, research engineers and HPC specialists.

**Universities.** The Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC) has strong computer science, telecommunications and engineering schools. Universitat Pompeu Fabra (UPF) is known for research in information and communication technologies, including machine learning, natural language processing and audio and music technology. The Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona (UAB) hosts the Computer Vision Center (CVC), a research centre dedicated to computer vision. The Universitat de Barcelona adds mathematics, data science and health research.

**Research institutes.** The Institut de Robòtica i Informàtica Industrial (IRI), a joint centre of the Spanish National Research Council (CSIC) and UPC, works on robotics and AI. Biomedical research institutes in Barcelona apply data science to genomics and health.

## Skills in Demand

- **Product ML:** recommendations, search, ranking, pricing and experimentation for consumer platforms.
- **Logistics and optimisation:** routing, demand forecasting and dispatch for delivery and mobility companies.
- **NLP and LLMs:** multilingual models, including Spanish and Catalan, retrieval and evaluation.
- **Computer vision:** manufacturing inspection, retail, health imaging.
- **MLOps and data engineering:** reliable pipelines and model serving at scale.
- **HPC and distributed training:** for research and large-model work at the BSC and similar organisations.

## Language

Spanish and Catalan are both official in Catalonia. Catalan is widely used in public administration, education and daily life; Spanish is also spoken by everyone. Many tech companies and startups work in English, and Barcelona's international community is large. For roles in local companies and public institutions, Spanish (and sometimes Catalan) is important. Learning Spanish is valuable for daily life, and some Catalan is appreciated.

## Pay, Taxes and Contracts

Salaries in Barcelona are generally lower than in Northern Europe, although international tech hubs and well-funded scale-ups often pay competitively by Spanish standards and may offer equity. Spanish salaries are often expressed as annual gross amounts divided into 12 or 14 payments. Spain has had a special tax regime for certain inbound workers, commonly called the "Beckham law", which was updated by the Startups Law; eligibility depends on current rules and personal circumstances. Collective agreements shape conditions in many sectors. Compare net pay and total compensation carefully.

## Permits

EU and EEA citizens can work in Spain and must register as residents. Non-EU professionals often use the highly qualified professional visa or residence permit under Spain's law for entrepreneurs and their internationalisation, or the EU Blue Card. Spain's digital nomad visa allows non-EU remote workers to live in Spain while working for foreign companies under certain conditions. Requirements change; check official Spanish sources and ask employers about their experience with sponsorship.

## Living in Barcelona

Barcelona offers a high quality of life, a mild climate, the sea, mountains nearby and excellent food and culture. The downside is housing: rents in Barcelona have risen sharply, driven by demand from residents, international newcomers and tourism, and the city has introduced measures to address housing pressure. Finding a flat can be competitive. Public transport is extensive, with metro, buses, trams and regional trains, and cycling has grown. Many people live in neighbouring municipalities or further along the coast to find more affordable housing.

## Scale-Up Machine Learning in Practice

Barcelona's scale-ups have produced a generation of engineers experienced in ML at scale. Delivery platforms, for instance, depend on models that estimate preparation and delivery times, assign orders to couriers, forecast demand by area and hour and set dynamic fees. Marketplaces rely on search ranking, recommendations, fraud detection and image understanding. Travel and expense platforms use ML for pricing, recommendations and document processing. SaaS companies build AI features into their products, increasingly using language models.

These environments share characteristics: large volumes of real-time data, a strong experimentation culture, close collaboration with product and operations teams and rapid iteration. For experienced engineers, they offer ownership and visible impact. The trade-offs include intense pace, frequent reorganisations as companies grow or restructure, and dependence on funding cycles. When evaluating a scale-up, ask about funding, profitability path, team stability and how ML priorities are set.

## Language Technology for Iberian Languages

Barcelona has become a centre for language technology serving Spanish, Catalan and other languages of the Iberian Peninsula. Research groups at the BSC, UPF and other institutions have built datasets, benchmarks and models for these languages, supported by public initiatives aimed at ensuring that AI works well for them. For NLP engineers, this creates opportunities in research and in companies building multilingual products. Working on languages with fewer resources than English involves distinctive challenges: data collection and curation, evaluation in multiple varieties of a language, tokenisation and cultural context. It is a field where Barcelona has genuine expertise.

## International Tech Hubs

Many international companies have established engineering and data hubs in Barcelona, attracted by the talent pool, lifestyle and costs. These hubs range from teams working on global products to regional operations centres. For engineers, they combine international career paths and English-speaking environments with Barcelona living. When evaluating a hub role, ask the same questions as in any satellite office: which products and models the team owns, where decisions are made, how the Barcelona office is positioned within the company and whether there are growth opportunities locally.

## Industry and Health Applications

Beyond tech companies, Catalonia has a significant industrial base. Automotive manufacturing near Martorell, chemical and pharmaceutical companies, food production and logistics around the port and airport all use data science and AI. Typical applications include quality inspection, predictive maintenance, process optimisation, supply chain planning and energy management. Health is another strength: hospitals and biomedical research institutes in Barcelona work on genomics, medical imaging and clinical data, and data scientists in these settings work within strict ethics and data protection frameworks.

## Neighbourhoods and Commuting

Barcelona is dense and relatively compact within its city limits, but the metropolitan area is large. Tech companies cluster in 22@/Poblenou, the Eixample and around the port, while research centres are spread across the city and campuses in Bellaterra (UAB) and elsewhere. Popular neighbourhoods for tech professionals include Gràcia, Poblenou, Sant Antoni and the Eixample, but rents there are high. Municipalities such as Badalona, L'Hospitalet de Llobregat, Sant Cugat del Vallès and towns along the coast offer alternatives with train or metro connections. Check commute times to the exact office before signing a lease.

## Building a Network

Barcelona has an active tech community with frequent meetups on data science, machine learning, Python and MLOps, as well as conferences and startup events. The Mobile World Congress and related events bring international visitors each year. Participating in these communities helps newcomers find roles, learn about companies and build friendships — important in a city where many international residents arrive without existing connections.

## Research Careers at the BSC and Universities

For research-oriented engineers, Barcelona offers a combination of large-scale computing and academic depth that is rare in Southern Europe. The BSC recruits research engineers, software engineers and scientists for projects in computer architecture, programming models, AI, life sciences and climate modelling, often funded by European programmes. Working at a supercomputing centre gives exposure to distributed systems, performance engineering and large datasets — skills that transfer directly to large-model training in industry.

University research groups at UPC, UPF, UAB and the University of Barcelona offer PhD and postdoc positions, supported by Spanish and Catalan research funding and European grants. Catalonia has also used programmes to attract international research talent. As elsewhere, early-career research positions are often fixed-term; ask about funding horizons and career paths.

## Questions to Ask Barcelona Employers

- What does the team own, and how are ML priorities decided?
- How is the company funded, and what is its path to profitability?
- What is the working language in practice, including informal communication?
- Is relocation support offered, including temporary housing?
- How many office days are expected, and where exactly is the office?
- Is the company familiar with the special tax regime for inbound workers and with permit procedures?

## Evaluating Equity Offers

Equity is common in Barcelona scale-ups. Understand the type of instrument, the vesting schedule, what happens if you leave and the company's latest valuation. Treat equity as a potential bonus rather than guaranteed income, and base your decision primarily on salary, role and learning.

## Settling In

Newcomers to Barcelona need to obtain a foreigner identification number (NIE), register with the local town hall (empadronamiento), register with social security and open a Spanish bank account. Appointments with authorities can be difficult to obtain, so start early and ask your employer for help. Temporary furnished housing for the first weeks gives you time to find a longer-term flat near your workplace.

## Is Barcelona Right for You?

Barcelona suits engineers who value lifestyle, international community and scale-up energy, and who can accept lower salaries and a competitive housing market. Visiting the city, meeting the team and talking to recent arrivals will help you decide.

## Real example

### A logistics ML engineer moving from Amsterdam to Barcelona

An ML engineer working on delivery-time prediction at an Amsterdam logistics company is approached by a Barcelona delivery scale-up. The gross salary is lower than in Amsterdam, but the role includes ownership of dispatch optimisation for several markets and equity.

She calculates net pay, including the potential application of the special tax regime for inbound workers, and compares rents in Barcelona neighbourhoods near the office with her Amsterdam costs. Housing turns out to be the biggest uncertainty, so she negotiates temporary accommodation for the first two months. She accepts the offer and starts Spanish lessons. After a year, she finds that the work is more intense than in Amsterdam but the scope much broader — and that the lifestyle compensates for the lower salary.
