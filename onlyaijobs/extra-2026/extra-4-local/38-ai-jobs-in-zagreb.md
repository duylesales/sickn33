Croatia is better known for its coastline than for its technology sector, but over the past decade Zagreb has produced a surprising number of internationally successful tech companies. Rimac built high-performance electric vehicles and drivetrain technology near the capital, Infobip grew from a small Croatian startup into a global communications platform, and Photomath, the maths-solving app, was acquired by Google. Computer vision companies, game studios and software firms add to the mix. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Zagreb offer a chance to start a machine learning career in a growing ecosystem, often at companies with international products. This guide explains where to look and how to get your first role.

## The Academic Foundation

**FER.** The Faculty of Electrical Engineering and Computing of the University of Zagreb is Croatia's leading institution for computer science and electrical engineering. It has research groups in machine learning, computer vision, natural language processing, robotics and data science, and its graduates form the backbone of Croatia's tech industry. FER students often take part in competitions, research projects and internships with local companies.

**Other faculties.** The University of Zagreb's Faculty of Science (mathematics and physics) and Faculty of Economics and Business contribute graduates with quantitative and analytical skills. Other universities in Split, Rijeka and Osijek add further talent.

**Ruđer Bošković Institute.** Croatia's largest research institute works across the natural sciences and has research groups applying machine learning and data science, including in areas such as bioinformatics and materials science.

## Croatian Technology Companies

**Electric vehicles and automotive technology.** Rimac, founded by Mate Rimac, develops electric vehicle technology, including battery systems, drivetrains and software, and has built a large campus near Zagreb. The broader group has been involved in hypercars and electric vehicle technology supplied to other manufacturers. Automotive technology offers work on battery analytics, vehicle software, simulation and potentially autonomous systems.

**Communications platforms.** Infobip, founded in Croatia, has become a global cloud communications platform with a significant engineering presence in Zagreb. Communications platforms use ML for message routing, fraud detection, spam filtering, conversational AI and analytics.

**Computer vision.** Microblink, based in Zagreb, develops computer vision technology for document scanning and identity verification, used in mobile and web applications worldwide. This is a notable example of deep ML expertise in Croatia.

**Education technology.** Photomath, founded in Zagreb, developed an app that recognises handwritten and printed maths problems and explains solutions step by step; it was acquired by Google. Its success demonstrated the strength of Croatian ML engineering in computer vision and education.

**Games.** Nanobit, a Zagreb mobile game studio, and other game companies use data science for player analytics, monetisation and live operations.

**Software services and consultancies.** Many Croatian software companies build products for international clients, with growing AI and data practices.

**Banks, telecom and retail.** Croatian banks, telecom operators and retailers employ data scientists for risk, fraud, customer analytics and forecasting.

## Entry-Level AI Jobs in Zagreb

| Role | Typical employers | What you learn |
|---|---|---|
| ML or data science intern | Product companies, software services, startups | Real projects, team practices |
| Junior ML engineer | Computer vision, communications, automotive tech | Production ML, deployment |
| Junior data scientist | Banks, telecom, games, retail | Analysis, modelling, business communication |
| Data engineer | Most tech companies | Pipelines, cloud platforms |
| Research assistant | FER, Ruđer Bošković Institute | Research methods, publications |
| Software engineer moving into ML | Product companies | Engineering foundations, gradual ML exposure |

Many Croatian graduates start as software engineers or data engineers and move into ML within a few years. Given the size of the market, being flexible about your first role is often wise.

## Building a Profile That Stands Out

- **A solid thesis:** if possible, do your thesis on an ML topic with a company or research group.
- **Portfolio projects:** end-to-end projects with clean code, documentation and evaluation.
- **Competitions and hackathons:** FER and local companies organise competitions and hackathons that help you learn and get noticed.
- **Internships:** summer internships at Zagreb tech companies are among the best routes to a first job.
- **English:** most tech companies work in English, and strong English communication is expected.

## Pay and Cost of Living

Salaries in Croatia are generally lower than in Western Europe, though international tech companies and product firms pay competitively by local standards. Croatia adopted the euro and joined the Schengen area in 2023, which simplifies cross-border life and comparisons. Zagreb's cost of living is moderate compared with Western European capitals, although rents have risen, partly due to demand from students, young professionals and tourism.

## Language

Croatian is the language of daily life and many local organisations. In tech companies with international products, English is the standard working language. For international graduates, English-speaking tech roles are available, but learning some Croatian helps with daily life and integration.

## What Makes Zagreb's Tech Success Stories Instructive

The success of Zagreb's tech companies offers lessons for graduates. Many were founded by engineers who focused on a hard technical problem — recognising handwritten maths, scanning documents reliably on a phone, building powerful electric drivetrains — and built world-class solutions from a small country. This culture values technical depth, persistence and building products for global markets from day one. For graduates, joining such companies means learning from people who have done exactly that, and absorbing a mindset that is valuable wherever your career takes you.

These companies also demonstrate that ML expertise in Croatia is not limited to services for foreign clients. Computer vision, on-device ML, conversational AI and battery analytics developed in Zagreb are used by millions of people worldwide.

## Preparing for Technical Interviews

Interviews for junior AI roles in Zagreb typically include programming tasks (often in Python, sometimes C++ for computer vision or embedded roles), questions on ML fundamentals, a discussion of your thesis and projects and sometimes a take-home assignment. Computer vision companies may ask about image processing basics, convolutional networks and model optimisation for mobile devices. Communications and data roles may focus on SQL, statistics and experimentation. Prepare by revisiting your projects in depth and practising clear explanations of your choices, results and limitations.

## Student Communities and Events

Zagreb's student community is active. FER hosts career fairs where local and international companies recruit, and student associations organise hackathons, coding competitions and workshops, often with company sponsorship. Local meetups on data science, Python, machine learning and product development are held regularly, and technology conferences bring international speakers to the city. Participation in these events is one of the best ways to discover internships and junior roles, meet engineers who can recommend you and learn which teams offer good mentoring.

## Automotive and Battery Analytics

The electric vehicle sector near Zagreb offers distinctive work for ML graduates. Battery systems generate data on cell voltages, temperatures and currents, and ML models help estimate state of charge and health, detect anomalies and predict degradation. Vehicle software and simulation also benefit from data-driven methods. These roles combine ML with electrical engineering and physics, which suits graduates from FER's electrical engineering programmes as well as computer science. The work involves strict quality and safety standards, excellent preparation for careers in automotive or energy technology anywhere in Europe.

## Your First Two Years

Focus your first two years on building strong foundations: clean code, testing, version control and working with experienced engineers who review your work. Learn how models reach production and how they are monitored. Develop one area of depth — computer vision, NLP, time series or MLOps — while maintaining breadth. Ask for regular feedback and document what you build. These habits will make your next career step much easier, whether you stay in Zagreb or move elsewhere.

## Staying in Croatia or Moving Abroad

Many Croatian graduates consider moving abroad for higher salaries. Remote work and the growth of local tech companies have made staying more attractive than in the past. For a first job, in-person mentoring in a strong local team is often more valuable than a higher salary abroad. Later, you can compare concrete offers based on net pay, cost of living, career growth and quality of life. Some Croatian engineers work abroad for a few years and return with valuable experience — a path that Zagreb's growing ecosystem increasingly rewards.

## Conversational AI and Communications

Communications platforms process enormous volumes of messages across SMS, chat apps, email and voice. ML plays a role in many parts of such platforms: routing messages efficiently across networks, detecting fraud and spam, analysing delivery performance and powering chatbots and conversational AI for businesses. The rise of large language models has expanded this field, with work on retrieval-augmented assistants, intent detection, multilingual support and evaluation of conversation quality. For graduates interested in NLP, the communications sector in Zagreb offers practical experience with language technology at scale.

## Computer Vision on Mobile Devices

Zagreb has notable expertise in computer vision that runs directly on phones and in browsers. This requires models that are accurate yet small and fast, robust to varied lighting, angles and devices, and privacy-preserving because data may not leave the device. Graduates who work in this field learn model compression, efficient architectures, deployment on mobile hardware and rigorous testing across many device types — skills that are valuable in many industries, from fintech to healthcare.

## Reading Job Postings Critically

Graduate postings vary in quality. Strong postings describe the product, the team, the technologies and the learning path, and increasingly include salary ranges — something the EU Pay Transparency Directive will make standard across the EU. Signals of a good junior role include mentoring, code review and clear onboarding. If a junior posting demands years of experience with many advanced tools, ask what is actually expected in the first months.

## Living in Zagreb

Zagreb is a green, walkable city with a historic upper town, a lively centre and a strong café culture. Trams cover most of the city, and many people cycle or walk. The Adriatic coast is a few hours away, and Slovenia and Austria are close for weekend trips. The city is safe and has a relaxed pace compared with larger capitals.

## Beyond Zagreb

Split, Rijeka, Osijek and Varaždin also have universities and growing tech companies, and some Zagreb employers allow hybrid work across Croatian cities. Croatia's digital nomad residence permit has also attracted international remote workers, enriching local tech communities with new meetups and contacts.

## A Simple Action Plan

Choose five Zagreb companies whose products you admire, attend their events, apply for internships and follow up personally. In a small ecosystem, genuine interest and persistence are noticed.

## Real example

### A FER graduate choosing between a startup and a large tech company

A FER master's graduate in computer science did her thesis on document image analysis. She receives offers from a Zagreb computer vision company and from a large communications platform's engineering team.

The computer vision company offers work directly related to her thesis and deep ML expertise in a small team; the larger company offers structured onboarding, broader exposure and a clear career ladder. She talks to engineers at both and chooses the computer vision company, because she wants to specialise early. Two years later, she has deployed several models to production and is recognised internally as an expert in document understanding. She says that choosing a team where she could learn from experienced ML engineers mattered more than the size of the company.
