Lyon is France's second-largest metropolitan economy and, for many French engineers, the most attractive alternative to Paris. The city combines a historic centre listed by UNESCO, a renowned food culture and a strong economy built on life sciences, diagnostics, chemicals, industry, digital services and video games. It hosts international organisations such as Interpol and the World Health Organization's International Agency for Research on Cancer. For experienced professionals, data science jobs in Lyon offer serious work — particularly in health and industry — at a lower cost of living than Paris and with a two-hour train ride to the capital. This guide maps the opportunities and explains what to consider.

## The Sectors Behind Data Science Jobs in Lyon

**Life sciences and diagnostics.** Lyon is one of Europe's leading life science regions. bioMérieux, a global in vitro diagnostics company, is headquartered in Marcy-l'Étoile near Lyon. Sanofi has major vaccine activities in the Lyon area, and many biotech and medtech companies belong to the Lyonbiopôle health cluster. Data science applications include analysis of diagnostic data, microbiology and genomics, clinical trial analytics, manufacturing quality and process optimisation, and AI-based diagnostic tools.

**International health research.** The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), part of the World Health Organization, is based in Lyon and conducts epidemiological research that relies on statistics and data science.

**Security.** Interpol's General Secretariat is headquartered in Lyon, and the organisation works on data and technology for international police cooperation.

**Chemicals and industry.** The "Vallée de la chimie" south of Lyon is one of France's main chemical clusters, and the region has manufacturers in trucks, household appliances, electrical equipment and industrial systems. Data science supports process optimisation, predictive maintenance, energy efficiency and supply chains.

**Video games.** Lyon has a long history in video games, with studios of international publishers and independent developers. Game studios use data for player analytics, balancing and live operations.

**Digital services, banking and retail.** Software companies, IT consultancies, regional banks and retailers employ data scientists and engineers. Lyon's startup ecosystem includes health tech, industrial tech and B2B software.

## Research and Education

Lyon has a strong academic base. Université Claude Bernard Lyon 1 covers sciences, medicine and computer science. INSA Lyon and École Centrale de Lyon are major engineering schools, and ENS de Lyon is known for mathematics, computer science and physics. The LIRIS laboratory works on computer vision, data science and AI, and Inria has a research centre in Lyon with teams in machine learning, networks and computational biology. Business schools such as emlyon add programmes in data and management. Hospitals of the Hospices Civils de Lyon, one of France's largest university hospital groups, work with researchers on medical data.

## Health AI in a Regulated Environment

Much of Lyon's data science work happens in regulated health settings. Diagnostic tools and software used for medical purposes fall under the EU In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation or Medical Device Regulation, and AI components of such products are generally classified as high-risk under the EU AI Act. This means rigorous validation, documentation, traceability and quality management. Pharmaceutical manufacturing follows good manufacturing practice, which requires validated computerised systems. Data scientists in these environments work closely with quality, regulatory and clinical experts. The work is slower than in consumer tech, but it has direct impact on patients.

## French Employment Conditions

Most engineers in France are hired on permanent contracts (CDI) with executive (cadre) status, often with a days-based working-time arrangement (forfait jours) and additional rest days (RTT). Collective agreements such as the Syntec agreement for IT and engineering services or the chemicals and pharmaceutical agreements shape conditions. Benefits commonly include meal vouchers or subsidised canteens, supplementary health insurance, profit-sharing schemes and reimbursement of half the cost of public transport passes. Salaries in Lyon are generally somewhat lower than in Paris, but the lower cost of living often compensates.

## Language

French is essential for daily life and for most workplaces in Lyon, especially in health, industry and public organisations. International organisations, research groups and some tech companies work in English, but French remains important for career growth.

## Living in Lyon

Lyon sits at the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers, with a lively old town, extensive parks and a famous gastronomic tradition. Public transport — metro, trams, buses and a funicular — is efficient, and cycling has grown significantly. Housing is more affordable than in Paris, though prices have risen. The Part-Dieu business district hosts many offices, while life science and industrial sites are spread around the metropolitan area; check commute times to the exact site. The Alps and the Mediterranean are within a few hours, and high-speed trains connect to Paris in about two hours.

## Diagnostics and Microbiology Data Science

Diagnostics is one of Lyon's most distinctive data science fields. Diagnostic companies develop instruments and tests that identify pathogens, measure biomarkers and determine antibiotic susceptibility. Data science contributes at several stages: analysing experimental data during development, building algorithms that interpret instrument signals, predicting antimicrobial resistance from genomic data, monitoring product performance in the field and optimising manufacturing. Because diagnostic results influence treatment decisions, models must be validated across diverse samples, documented in detail and monitored after release. Engineers who combine ML skills with an understanding of biology and regulated development are in short supply, which makes this a strong niche for career growth.

## Epidemiology and Public Health Research

Lyon's international health research institutions analyse data from large cohorts and cancer registries around the world. Statisticians and data scientists in this field use survival analysis, causal inference, multilevel models and increasingly machine learning, with strong emphasis on methodological rigour and reproducibility. Roles in international organisations follow their own recruitment procedures and conditions. For data scientists motivated by public health impact and international collaboration, this is a rare opportunity outside the major capitals.

## Industrial Data Science in the Lyon Region

The Lyon region's industrial base — chemicals, trucks, household appliances, electrical equipment and materials — offers data scientists concrete engineering problems. Typical projects include process optimisation in chemical plants to improve yield and reduce energy use, predictive maintenance of production equipment, quality control with computer vision, demand forecasting and supply chain optimisation. The energy transition adds urgency: chemical and industrial sites are working to reduce emissions, and data-driven optimisation plays a role. Industrial roles usually require French and regular presence on site, but they offer stable employers and visible impact.

## Games and Player Analytics

Lyon's game studios, from large publishers' studios to independent developers, employ data scientists who analyse player behaviour, support game balancing, run experiments on live games and forecast engagement and revenue. Studios increasingly explore machine learning for content tools, matchmaking and anti-cheat systems. Game data roles combine statistics with product thinking and close collaboration with designers. For data scientists who enjoy creative products, this is one of the few places in France outside Paris with a substantial game industry.

## Research-to-Industry Paths

France's CIFRE scheme, which lets companies employ PhD students supervised by academic laboratories, is widely used in Lyon, particularly in health, industry and digital technologies. Inria's Lyon centre and university laboratories collaborate with companies on applied projects. For experienced engineers, part-time teaching at engineering schools or collaborations with research groups are ways to stay connected to research while working in industry.

## Questions to Ask Lyon Employers

- At which site will I work — Part-Dieu, Gerland, a suburban campus or an industrial site?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what is the cadre working-time arrangement?
- For health roles: which regulations apply to the models, and how is validation organised?
- What is the working language for the team and for documentation?
- How do models move to production, and who maintains them?
- What benefits are included, such as profit-sharing, meal vouchers and health insurance?

## Networking in Lyon

Lyon has an active data and AI community with meetups on machine learning, data science and Python, events organised by the health cluster and university seminars at Inria, INSA and ENS. The city's size makes it easy to meet people across sectors. Attending events regularly helps newcomers understand which teams are growing and how different employers work.

## Settling In

Newcomers need a French bank account, registration with social security (usually initiated by the employer) and a carte Vitale for healthcare reimbursements. Non-EU residents must complete residence permit procedures. Temporary furnished housing gives time to find a longer-term flat near the workplace.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare net pay after social contributions, cadre status and RTT days, profit-sharing, health insurance and the value of shorter commutes and lower rents compared with Paris. For health and industrial roles, weigh the stability and long-term impact of the work, which are often strong points of Lyon's employers. A Lyon offer slightly below a Paris salary can leave you with more disposable income and more time.

## Who Lyon Suits Best

Lyon suits data scientists who want serious work in health, diagnostics or industry, speak or are ready to learn French and value a large but manageable city with an exceptional quality of life between the Alps and the Mediterranean.

## Skills That Stand Out in Lyon

Beyond core machine learning, Lyon employers value several specific skills. In health, biostatistics, survival analysis, genomics and experience with validated software development are prized. In industry, time series analysis, process knowledge and the ability to deploy models close to operations matter most. Across sectors, strong data engineering, reproducible workflows and clear communication with non-specialists — biologists, clinicians, process engineers or game designers — make the difference between a model that stays in a notebook and one that changes decisions. Knowledge of GDPR and the EU AI Act is increasingly expected, especially in health and public research, where data protection and documentation requirements are strict. Building one of these specialisms alongside your core skills makes you considerably more competitive for data science jobs in Lyon.

## Beyond Lyon

The wider Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region adds Grenoble's research and semiconductor ecosystem, Saint-Étienne's industry and Clermont-Ferrand's tyre and manufacturing base, all reachable from Lyon for hybrid or regional roles.

## A Final Tip

Ask to meet the scientists or engineers who will use your models; in Lyon's health and industrial sectors, those relationships decide whether your work succeeds.

## Real example

### A biostatistician moving from Paris to Lyon

A biostatistician working for a Paris pharmaceutical company wants a better balance between work and family life. She receives an offer from a diagnostics company near Lyon to lead data science for a new generation of diagnostic tests.

The salary is similar to her Paris salary, and housing costs in Lyon are noticeably lower. The role involves working with microbiologists, software engineers and regulatory specialists to validate machine learning models for diagnostic software. She accepts. After a year, she says the regulated environment requires patience and meticulous documentation, but seeing her work contribute to products used in hospitals is deeply satisfying — and her family enjoys Lyon's quality of life.
