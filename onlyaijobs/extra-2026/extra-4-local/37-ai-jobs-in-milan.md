Milan is Italy's financial, commercial and design capital. It hosts the Italian stock exchange, the headquarters of major banks and insurers, global fashion and luxury houses, a large manufacturing hinterland in Lombardy and two of Italy's most prestigious universities for engineering and economics. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Milan combine corporate scale in finance and industry with applications in fashion, design and retail that few other cities offer. This guide maps the landscape, explains Italian employment conditions and helps you decide whether Milan is the right move.

## The Economic Landscape

**Banking, insurance and finance.** Milan is home to Borsa Italiana, now part of Euronext, and to the headquarters or major operations of Italy's largest banks and many insurers and asset managers. Data science and AI in finance cover credit risk, fraud detection, anti-money-laundering, customer analytics, trading and increasingly language models for document processing and customer service. Fintech companies, such as the mobile payments company Satispay, founded in Milan, add product-focused roles.

**Fashion, luxury and design.** Milan is one of the world's fashion capitals and a centre of design. Fashion and luxury companies use AI for demand forecasting, inventory allocation, pricing, personalisation, visual search, trend analysis and supply chain transparency. Design and furniture companies use data for product development and e-commerce. These applications combine data science with creativity and brand sensitivity.

**Manufacturing and industry.** Lombardy is one of Europe's most industrialised regions, with thousands of manufacturing companies in machinery, automotive components, chemicals, pharmaceuticals and food. Pirelli, the tyre manufacturer headquartered in Milan, is one example of an industrial company investing in digital technology. AI applications include predictive maintenance, quality control, process optimisation and smart products.

**Retail, media and telecom.** Retailers, media groups, telecom operators and consumer goods companies based in Milan use AI for recommendations, forecasting, marketing and customer analytics.

**Consulting and tech.** Consultancies, IT services firms and the Italian offices of global technology companies employ many data scientists and AI engineers.

**Life sciences.** Human Technopole, a research institute for life sciences located in the MIND Milano Innovation District on the former Expo 2015 site, works on genomics, computational biology and health data, and hospitals and pharmaceutical companies in the region add further data roles.

## Universities and Research

**Politecnico di Milano** is Italy's leading technical university, with strong research in computer engineering, machine learning, robotics, data science and operations research. Its graduates are highly sought after, and its research groups collaborate extensively with industry.

**Bocconi University** is renowned for economics, finance and management, and has invested in computing and data science, including research on machine learning theory and AI. Its programmes combine quantitative skills with business understanding.

**University of Milano-Bicocca** and the **University of Milan** contribute computer science, statistics, physics and life science research. Together, these institutions create a steady flow of graduates and a research base connected to Milan's economy.

## Italian Employment Conditions

Italian employment is shaped by national collective agreements (CCNL), which set pay scales, working hours, holidays and other conditions by sector — for example commerce, metalworking, banking or telecommunications. Salaries are typically expressed as gross annual amounts (RAL) divided into 13 or 14 monthly payments, depending on the agreement. Employees accrue a severance fund (TFR), paid when employment ends or transferred to a pension fund. Permanent contracts (contratto a tempo indeterminato) are standard for experienced roles. Many employers offer meal vouchers, supplementary health insurance and welfare benefits.

Salaries in Milan are among the highest in Italy but are generally lower than in Northern European hubs. Italy has had a special tax regime for workers who move their tax residence to Italy ("impatriati"), which was significantly reformed in 2024 with reduced benefits and stricter conditions. Check current eligibility carefully with a tax adviser.

## Language

Italian is essential for daily life and for most corporate environments in Milan. International companies, some fintechs, research groups and tech teams work in English, but meetings with business stakeholders, documentation and social interaction at work are often in Italian. Learning Italian significantly broadens your opportunities and helps you integrate into the workplace culture, which values personal relationships.

## Permits

EU citizens can work freely in Italy and register their residence. Non-EU professionals can use the EU Blue Card or other work permit routes, which are subject to procedures involving the employer and immigration authorities. Italy also introduced a digital nomad visa for highly qualified remote workers. Check official Italian sources for current requirements.

## Living in Milan

Milan is dynamic, international and well connected, with an extensive metro network, trams and high-speed trains to Turin, Venice, Florence, Rome and Switzerland. The city has transformed in recent decades, with new districts such as Porta Nuova and CityLife, and a thriving food, fashion and design scene. It is also Italy's most expensive city, especially for housing; rents have risen significantly. Many professionals live in neighbourhoods along metro lines or in towns connected by suburban rail. The Alps and the lakes — Como, Maggiore, Garda — are within easy reach for weekends.

## AI in Fashion and Luxury

Fashion and luxury present distinctive AI challenges that make Milan particularly interesting:

- **Demand forecasting for new products:** many items are new each season, without sales history. Models must use attributes, images, similar past products and early signals.
- **Allocation and replenishment:** deciding how many units of each size and colour to send to each store and channel, balancing sales against the risk of markdowns.
- **Pricing and markdowns:** luxury brands protect brand value and avoid discounts, while premium and fast-fashion brands optimise markdown strategies.
- **Personalisation:** recommendations and clienteling tools that help sales associates serve high-value clients.
- **Visual AI:** image search, attribute tagging, trend detection from images and generative tools for design support.
- **Sustainability and traceability:** tracking materials through supply chains to meet customer expectations and emerging EU requirements on product sustainability.

Working in this sector requires sensitivity to brand identity and creative processes. Data scientists collaborate with designers, merchandisers and store teams, and the best solutions support rather than override human judgement.

## Banking and Insurance AI

Milan's banks and insurers are major employers of data scientists. Beyond classic credit risk and fraud models, they are applying language models to documents, contracts and customer communications, building AI assistants for employees and customers and modernising data platforms. Regulation shapes this work: model risk management, supervisory expectations from the Bank of Italy and the European Central Bank and the EU AI Act's high-risk requirements for creditworthiness assessment and certain insurance uses. Engineers who combine ML skills with governance expertise are especially valued.

## Industrial AI in Lombardy

Lombardy's manufacturing base — machinery, components, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, food and textiles — is increasingly adopting AI. Many companies are medium-sized, family-owned firms that are global leaders in niche markets. For data scientists, these companies offer projects such as predictive maintenance of machines they sell to customers worldwide, quality control with computer vision, optimisation of production parameters and smart features in industrial products. Italian and European support programmes for industrial digitalisation have encouraged investment. Roles in industrial AI often require Italian and a willingness to spend time on the factory floor, but they offer tangible impact and a strong engineering culture.

## Research Careers

For research-oriented engineers, Politecnico di Milano and Bocconi offer PhD and postdoc positions in AI, machine learning and data science, often with industry collaborations. Human Technopole recruits computational researchers in life sciences. Italy's national AI research initiatives connect universities across the country. Research careers in Italy follow a structure of fixed-term positions before permanent roles, and competition is strong. Many researchers move between academia and industry, and Milan's corporate density makes such transitions easier than in many Italian cities.

## Startups and the Milan Tech Scene

Milan is the centre of Italy's startup ecosystem, with the largest share of the country's venture capital activity. Fintech, e-commerce, proptech, foodtech and B2B software startups use AI in their products, and accelerators and innovation hubs support new companies. Startup roles offer broad scope, English-speaking teams in some cases and equity, but involve the usual uncertainties. When evaluating a startup offer, look at funding, business model and team, and understand equity terms.

## Questions to Ask Milan Employers

- Which collective agreement applies, and is the salary paid in 13 or 14 instalments?
- What is the working language, and is Italian needed for stakeholders?
- How many office days are expected, and where exactly is the office?
- What welfare benefits, meal vouchers and health insurance are included?
- Does the company support applications for the impatriate tax regime?

## Where Employers Are Located

Milan's employers are spread across several districts. Banks, insurers and consultancies cluster in Porta Nuova, CityLife and the historic centre. Fashion houses have headquarters and showrooms in central areas and in districts such as Tortona and Porta Romana. Technology companies and startups are found around the centre, in Isola and in innovation hubs, while industrial companies and some research facilities are in the wider metropolitan area and the Lombardy provinces. The MIND district, north-west of the city, hosts Human Technopole and other research and innovation organisations. Commute times vary significantly, so check the exact address and your route before choosing where to live; the metro and suburban rail (S lines) connect much of the area.

## Networking in Milan

Milan has an active data and AI community, with meetups on machine learning, data science, Python and MLOps, as well as conferences and events at Polimi and Bocconi. Industry events in fashion, design and finance increasingly include AI themes. Personal relationships matter in Italian professional life, so attending events regularly and following up with people you meet is particularly valuable.

## Settling In

Newcomers need an Italian tax code (codice fiscale), registration of residence at the municipal registry office, enrolment in the national health service and an Italian bank account. Procedures can take time, so start early and ask your employer for support.

## Who Milan Suits Best

Milan suits engineers who want corporate-scale AI in finance, fashion or industry, speak or are ready to learn Italian and enjoy a cosmopolitan city close to the Alps and the lakes.

## Real example

### A data scientist returning to Milan from London

An Italian data scientist working on pricing models for a London e-commerce company wants to move back to Italy. He receives an offer from a Milan fashion group to lead demand forecasting and inventory allocation across its stores and online channels.

He compares his London salary with the Italian offer, checks his eligibility for the tax regime for relocating workers after the 2024 reform and compares rents in Milan with London. The Italian gross salary is lower, but his net position is closer than expected once taxes, housing and the value of benefits are included. He also appreciates working at the intersection of data science and a creative industry. He accepts and, after a year, says the role combines technical depth with business impact — though adjusting to more hierarchical decision-making took time.
