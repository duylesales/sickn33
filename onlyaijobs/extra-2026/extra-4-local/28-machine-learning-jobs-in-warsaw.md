Warsaw has transformed over the past two decades into one of Central Europe's largest business and technology centres. The Polish capital hosts the headquarters of the country's biggest banks, product companies with international reach, R&D centres of global technology firms and a growing AI research community. Poland also has a strong tradition in mathematics and algorithmic programming, and Polish engineers are well represented in the global AI community. Machine learning jobs in Warsaw therefore range from credit risk and fraud models in banking to recommendation systems in e-commerce, product ML in marketplaces and research on language models for Polish. This guide explains the landscape and the practicalities for experienced engineers.

## Why Warsaw Matters for Machine Learning

**Mathematical talent.** Polish universities have a long tradition in mathematics and computer science, and Polish students have performed strongly in international programming and algorithmic competitions. The University of Warsaw's Faculty of Mathematics, Informatics and Mechanics is especially well known, and Warsaw University of Technology trains large numbers of engineers.

**A large corporate market.** Warsaw is Poland's financial and corporate centre. Banks, insurers, telecom operators, energy companies and retailers have headquarters or large operations in the city, and many have significant data and AI teams.

**Product companies.** Warsaw has produced product companies with international users, such as Docplanner, which operates medical appointment platforms in several countries, and Booksy, a booking platform for beauty and wellness services. Allegro, Poland's largest e-commerce marketplace, has a major presence in Warsaw alongside its headquarters in Poznań, and has invested in ML research.

**International R&D centres.** Several global technology companies operate R&D and engineering centres in Warsaw, including Samsung R&D Institute Poland, as well as cloud and software companies.

**Language models for Polish.** Poland has developed its own language model initiatives, including PLLuM, a national project to build large language models for Polish, and community-driven models such as Bielik. These efforts have created work on data curation, training and evaluation for the Polish language.

## Contracts: Employment or B2B

A defining feature of the Polish tech market is the choice between an employment contract (umowa o pracę) and a B2B contract, where the engineer operates as a self-employed business and invoices the company. B2B contracts are common among experienced engineers because they can result in higher net income through different tax and social security arrangements, but they offer fewer protections: no statutory paid leave unless negotiated, no sick pay in the same form and less protection against termination. Employment contracts provide full employee rights, paid leave and social security coverage.

When comparing machine learning jobs in Warsaw, look at both forms if offered, calculate net income and consider what protections matter to you. International engineers moving to Poland should get professional advice on taxes and social security before choosing B2B, because obligations and eligibility depend on personal circumstances.

## Pay Levels

Salaries for experienced ML engineers in Warsaw are high by Polish standards and have grown significantly, although they generally remain below levels in Western European hubs such as Amsterdam, Munich or Dublin. Many Polish job postings include salary ranges, often separately for employment and B2B contracts, which makes comparison easier. The EU Pay Transparency Directive will make pay information before interviews a requirement across the EU. Living costs in Warsaw, particularly rents, have risen but are generally lower than in Western European capitals.

## Language and Culture

Polish is the language of daily life and many corporate environments. In product companies, international R&D centres and many software houses, English is the working language. Banks and insurers increasingly have international teams, though Polish remains important for stakeholder communication. Polish workplace culture in tech is generally professional and direct, with a strong emphasis on technical competence.

## Permits

EU citizens can work in Poland and register their stay if staying longer. Non-EU professionals typically need a work permit and a temporary residence permit, or they can use the EU Blue Card if they meet the qualification and salary requirements under Poland's implementation of the recast Blue Card Directive. Processing times can be significant, so employers and candidates should start early. Check official Polish sources for current rules.

## Living in Warsaw

Warsaw is a dynamic city with a rebuilt historic old town, modern business districts, extensive parks and a growing cultural and food scene. Public transport — metro, trams, buses and suburban trains — is efficient and affordable. Many tech companies are located in the city centre around the Rondo Daszyńskiego and Wola business district, in Mokotów's office areas or near the centre. Housing costs have risen, but a wide range of neighbourhoods is available. Warsaw's Chopin Airport connects to much of Europe.

## Banking ML in Poland

Polish banks are known for their advanced digital banking: mobile apps, instant payments and digital onboarding are widely used. This digital maturity creates rich data and demanding ML applications:

- **Credit risk:** scoring models for consumer and business lending, early warning systems and collections optimisation.
- **Fraud detection:** real-time scoring of card, transfer and online banking transactions.
- **Anti-money-laundering:** transaction monitoring and network analysis.
- **Customer analytics:** next-best-offer, churn prediction and personalisation in mobile apps.
- **Document and language AI:** processing loan documents, customer messages and call transcripts, increasingly with language models for Polish.

Banking ML takes place within a strong governance framework. Regulators expect model validation, documentation and monitoring, and the EU AI Act classifies creditworthiness assessment of natural persons as a high-risk use. Engineers in Warsaw banks often work alongside model validation and risk teams, and experience with governance is valued.

## E-commerce and Marketplace ML

Poland's e-commerce market is large and competitive. Marketplaces and online retailers use ML for search ranking, recommendations, advertising, pricing, fraud detection, logistics and customer service. Allegro, for example, has publicly shared research and engineering work on ML for e-commerce, including search and language models for Polish. Such companies offer experienced engineers the chance to work on high-traffic systems with strong experimentation cultures, often with the resources of a large organisation.

## Software Houses and Consultancies

Warsaw and other Polish cities have a large software services industry that builds products for clients in Western Europe, the United States and elsewhere. Many of these companies now have AI and ML practices delivering projects such as recommendation engines, computer vision systems, language model applications and data platforms. For engineers, software houses offer variety, exposure to different industries and often English-speaking teams. The trade-offs include less long-term ownership of products and dependence on client projects. Many engineers spend a few years in a software house to gain breadth and then move to a product company, or vice versa.

## Polish Language Models and NLP

Work on Polish language technology has accelerated. The national PLLuM project has brought together research institutions to develop language models for Polish for use in public administration and beyond. The SpeakLeash community has developed open datasets and the Bielik family of models. Companies in banking, e-commerce and public services increasingly need models that handle Polish well — including its rich morphology, formal registers and domain vocabulary. For NLP engineers, this creates opportunities in data curation, fine-tuning, evaluation and deployment, as well as in research. Polish-speaking engineers with NLP expertise are particularly sought after.

## Research and Academia

The University of Warsaw's mathematics and computer science faculty and Warsaw University of Technology are the main academic centres, with research groups in machine learning, computer vision, NLP and theoretical computer science. Research institutes and Polish Academy of Sciences institutes contribute further. Poland has also invested in AI research centres in recent years. Many Polish researchers have built international careers, and connections between the Warsaw research community and global AI labs are strong. For experienced engineers interested in research, Warsaw offers PhD and research positions, as well as industrial research roles at R&D centres.

## Questions to Ask Warsaw Employers

- Which contract forms do you offer, and what does each include?
- Does the team own models end to end, including deployment and monitoring?
- What is the working language, and is Polish needed for stakeholders?
- For international hires, what support is available for permits and relocation?
- How is model governance organised, especially in regulated sectors?

## International R&D Centres

Global technology companies have established R&D centres in Warsaw to access Polish engineering talent. These centres work on products used worldwide: speech recognition, computer vision, on-device AI, cloud services and developer tools. For experienced engineers, they combine research-oriented work with the resources of large organisations. As with any satellite office, ask which products the Warsaw team owns, how decisions are made across locations and whether career progression to senior technical and leadership roles is possible locally. Centres that own complete products or research areas offer more growth than those implementing work designed elsewhere.

## Interviews in the Warsaw Tech Market

Hiring processes at Warsaw tech companies typically include a recruiter screen, a technical interview covering ML fundamentals and coding, a system design or case discussion and a conversation with the hiring manager or team. Some companies use take-home tasks. Given Poland's algorithmic tradition, coding interviews can be rigorous. Prepare to discuss your past projects in depth, including trade-offs, metrics and failures. Many companies publish salary ranges, so salary discussions are often straightforward, but negotiation is expected, especially for experienced candidates.

## Networking in Warsaw

Warsaw has an active tech community with meetups on machine learning, data science, Python and MLOps, as well as conferences that attract international speakers. University seminars and hackathons are open to professionals. Joining these events helps newcomers learn about companies, understand local salary levels and build friendships in a new city.

## Beyond Warsaw

Kraków, Wrocław, Poznań, Gdańsk and Łódź also have strong technology sectors, universities and ML teams, often with lower living costs. Some employers operate in several Polish cities, which gives flexibility on where to live.

## First Steps After Moving

Obtain a PESEL number, open a Polish bank account and register with the tax office if you choose B2B. Your employer's HR team can usually help with these steps.

## Real example

### A recommendation engineer choosing between Berlin and Warsaw

A Polish ML engineer working on recommendation systems in Berlin considers returning to Warsaw. He receives an offer from a Warsaw marketplace to lead ranking model development, with a choice between an employment contract and B2B.

He compares net income under both contract forms with his Berlin salary, consults a tax adviser about B2B obligations and weighs the protections of an employment contract. He also considers the role: in Berlin, he was one of many engineers on a large team; in Warsaw, he would own the ranking system for a large user base. He chooses an employment contract for stability during the transition and accepts the role. After a year, he says the technical scope is larger than in Berlin, his living costs are lower and being close to family matters more than he expected.
