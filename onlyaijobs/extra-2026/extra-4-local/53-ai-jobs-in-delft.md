Delft is a small historic city with an outsized reputation in engineering. Its technical university, TU Delft, is one of the best in Europe, and around it have grown national research institutes, a well-known startup incubator and a cluster of technology companies. Delft also sits between Rotterdam and The Hague, which puts two large job markets within a 15-minute train ride. For students and graduates, AI jobs in Delft are shaped by engineering: machine learning for robotics, water management, energy systems, mobility, aerospace and industrial design, as well as software companies and deep-tech startups. This guide explains where to look, how to build a profile that works and why searching by distance is especially powerful here.

OnlyAIJobs already lists vacancies in this part of the Netherlands, including in Capelle aan den IJssel on the eastern edge of Rotterdam, alongside Amsterdam, Utrecht, Eindhoven, Groningen and Ede.

## TU Delft and Its AI Labs

TU Delft offers programmes in computer science, electrical engineering, aerospace, mechanical, civil and industrial design engineering, among others, and its research spans almost every engineering discipline. To strengthen AI across the university, TU Delft launched a large set of interdisciplinary AI labs in which AI researchers work with colleagues from domains such as robotics, water, energy, materials, health and mobility. This structure means that AI research in Delft is often driven by concrete engineering problems rather than by benchmarks alone.

The university also has a strong robotics tradition, including research on autonomous systems, drones and robot perception, and a growing focus on responsible AI and human-centred design, supported by its faculty of Technology, Policy and Management.

## Research Institutes

**TNO**, the Netherlands Organisation for Applied Scientific Research, has major locations in Delft and works with companies and government on applied research, including AI for defence and security, mobility, industry, health and energy. It hires graduates as research scientists and engineers, often in multidisciplinary teams.

**Deltares**, an independent institute for applied research in water and subsurface, is based in Delft. Its work on flood risk, water management, coastal engineering and subsurface resources increasingly uses data science and machine learning — for example to accelerate simulations, forecast water levels and analyse satellite data.

## Startups and Companies

YES!Delft, TU Delft's startup incubator, is one of Europe's best-known university incubators and has supported many deep-tech startups in robotics, drones, energy, medical technology and software. Delft Technology Park hosts companies and spin-offs, and software company Exact has its headquarters in Delft. Many graduates also work for companies in Rotterdam and The Hague — in port logistics, energy, government, security and e-commerce — while living in Delft or vice versa.

## Entry Paths

1. **Master's thesis with a company, TNO or Deltares.** Common at TU Delft and often a direct route to a job.
2. **Internships** during your master's programme.
3. **PhD positions** at TU Delft, which in the Netherlands are paid employment positions.
4. **Traineeships** at larger organisations and government in the region.
5. **Startup roles** at YES!Delft companies, suitable for graduates who like broad responsibility.

## Language and Practicalities

Most TU Delft master's programmes and research groups work in English, and many startups and international companies do too. Government and some regional organisations require Dutch. International graduates of Dutch universities can use the orientation year to look for work, after which the highly skilled migrant scheme — with a lower salary threshold for recent graduates — can apply. Check current rules with the IND.

## Robotics and Autonomous Systems

Robotics is one of Delft's signature fields. TU Delft research groups and startups work on drones, warehouse and agricultural robots, autonomous vehicles and marine robotics. Machine learning supports perception, navigation, manipulation and learning from demonstration. Graduates in this field combine ML with control theory, embedded systems and hardware, and they often work in small interdisciplinary teams where software meets physical prototypes. The Netherlands' strengths in logistics, horticulture and maritime industries create real demand for robotics applications, and Delft is one of the best places in Europe to enter the field.

## Water, Climate and Infrastructure

The Netherlands' relationship with water makes Delft a global centre for hydraulic engineering and water management. AI supports flood forecasting, early warning systems, analysis of dikes and infrastructure, coastal morphology, groundwater modelling and climate adaptation. A common theme is using machine learning to accelerate or complement physics-based simulations — so-called surrogate modelling — and to combine models with satellite and sensor data. For graduates interested in climate impact, this field offers meaningful work with international relevance, as Dutch water expertise is applied around the world.

## Energy and Mobility

TU Delft and TNO work extensively on the energy transition: electricity grids, offshore wind, hydrogen, batteries and energy-efficient buildings. Data science helps forecast renewable generation, manage grid congestion, optimise charging of electric vehicles and plan infrastructure. Mobility research covers traffic management, public transport, cycling and autonomous vehicles. Employers in the region — grid operators, energy companies, public transport operators, consultancies and government agencies — hire graduates with these skills, often for roles combining data science with systems thinking.

## Building a Profile for Delft Employers

Delft employers value graduates who can connect ML with engineering practice:

- **A thesis rooted in a real system** — a robot, a water network, a grid, a vehicle — rather than a purely academic benchmark.
- **Programming and engineering discipline**: clean code, testing, version control and, for robotics, ROS or embedded experience.
- **Physics and domain understanding**: knowledge of the systems your models describe.
- **Interdisciplinary communication**: explaining ML to civil, mechanical or aerospace engineers and learning from them.
- **Responsible AI awareness**: TU Delft's emphasis on values and design means many employers expect graduates to think about safety, fairness and societal impact.

Student teams — such as those building solar cars, drones, rockets or autonomous boats — are a Delft tradition and provide excellent evidence of teamwork and engineering skills.

## Living in Delft

Delft is compact, historic and bicycle-friendly, with canals, a lively centre and a large student population. Housing is in high demand, especially for students, but it is generally more affordable than Amsterdam. Many graduates stay in Delft or move to Rotterdam or The Hague, all within a short train or tram ride. The coast, with beaches near The Hague, is close, and the Randstad's rail network puts most of the western Netherlands within an hour.

## PhDs and Research Careers

In the Netherlands, PhD candidates are usually employees with a salary, pension contributions and holiday allowance, which makes a PhD at TU Delft an attractive career step. Many PhD projects are linked to industry or government partners, particularly in the AI labs and applied engineering fields. TNO and Deltares offer applied research careers with a strong link to practice. For graduates who enjoy research but want impact beyond publications, these paths are especially appealing.

## Questions to Ask Delft Employers

- What will I build in my first six months, and who will mentor me?
- For startups: how is the company funded, and how long is the runway?
- What is the working language, and is Dutch needed for clients or partners?
- Are you an IND-recognised sponsor if I need a permit?
- What is the salary range, and which collective agreement applies?
- How often will I work with hardware, field data or on-site systems?

## Your First Two Years

Focus on building strong engineering foundations, depth in one application domain and the ability to take a model from prototype to reliable system. Stay connected to TU Delft through events and collaborations, and consider learning Dutch if you plan to stay. These habits prepare you for senior roles in Delft's research institutes, its startups or the wider South Holland job market.

## The Startup Route

Joining a YES!Delft startup as a graduate offers rapid learning, broad responsibility and close contact with founders. You may work on data pipelines, model development, hardware integration and customer demos in the same week. The trade-offs are uncertainty, lower initial pay in some cases and less structured mentoring. Before joining, ask about funding, the technical leadership in the team and how the company supports juniors. Many graduates find that two or three years in a Delft startup build a profile that opens doors across the Dutch tech ecosystem.

## A Weekly Search Routine

Set your home address, choose a commuting radius that includes Rotterdam and The Hague, and review new AI and data vacancies weekly. Keep a shortlist of employers you want to work for, attend one YES!Delft or TU Delft event per month and follow up personally with people you meet.

## Aerospace and Defence Research

TU Delft's Faculty of Aerospace Engineering is one of the largest in Europe, and applied research organisations in the region work on aerospace, space and defence technology. AI applications include predictive maintenance for aircraft, analysis of flight data, autonomous drones, satellite data processing and decision support. Some defence and security projects require security screening or nationality conditions, which graduates should clarify early. For those interested in aerospace, Delft offers a rare combination of academic depth, applied research institutes and startups building drones and space technology.

## Dutch Graduate Conditions

Dutch employment packages for graduates typically include a monthly salary, a holiday allowance of around eight percent paid once a year, pension contributions, a travel allowance and often a training budget. Many contracts start as fixed-term, with a path to permanent employment. Collective labour agreements set pay scales in many sectors, including research institutes and government. Salary ranges are increasingly published, and the EU Pay Transparency Directive will require pay information before interviews across the EU. When comparing offers, consider learning opportunities and mentoring as seriously as salary: the first two years shape your career far more than a small difference in starting pay.

## Networking in Delft

TU Delft hosts frequent seminars, career fairs and AI lab events, YES!Delft organises startup showcases and meetups, and Rotterdam and The Hague add data science and robotics communities within minutes. Attending regularly is the fastest way to find opportunities that never appear in public postings.

## A Final Tip

Ask to see the hardware, data or field site you would work with; in Delft's engineering culture, that conversation reveals more about a role than any job description.
