Oulu, on the northern Gulf of Bothnia, is Finland's fifth-largest city and one of Europe's most surprising technology centres. It has a long history in wireless communications, hosts major telecom R&D, pioneered research on 6G and has produced internationally known wearable technology companies. A health technology cluster connects the university, hospital and companies. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Oulu offer work in telecom and wireless, wearables and sensor data, health technology and software, often in English, in a city where engineering talent is valued. This guide explains where to look and how to get started.

## The University of Oulu and 6G Research

The University of Oulu has a strong tradition in electrical engineering, telecommunications, computer science and medicine. It leads the 6G Flagship, a research programme launched as one of the world's first large efforts focused on sixth-generation wireless systems, exploring how future networks will work and how AI will be integrated into them. The university's Centre for Machine Vision and Signal Analysis is known for research in computer vision, biometrics and affective computing. Oulu University of Applied Sciences trains practically oriented engineers, and VTT, Finland's technical research centre, has a site in Oulu.

## Telecom and Wireless R&D

Nokia has a large R&D site in Oulu, historically important for mobile radio technology and today working on 5G and future networks. Many smaller companies in wireless, electronics and software grew from this ecosystem. AI in telecom covers radio network optimisation, signal processing, energy efficiency, network automation and research on AI-native networks for 6G. For graduates with electrical engineering or signal processing backgrounds, Oulu offers roles rarely found in other cities of its size.

## Wearables and Sensor Data

Oulu has produced notable wearable technology companies. Oura, known for its smart ring that tracks sleep, activity and physiological signals, was founded in Oulu. Polar Electro, a pioneer of heart rate monitors and sports watches, is based in nearby Kempele. Wearables generate continuous sensor data — heart rate, movement, temperature — and machine learning turns this data into insights about sleep, recovery, fitness and health. Data scientists and ML engineers work on signal processing, algorithm development, personalisation and validation, often in collaboration with health researchers.

## Health Technology

OuluHealth is a health technology ecosystem that connects the University of Oulu, Oulu University Hospital, the city and companies. Its goal is to develop and test new health technologies in real clinical environments. Data science applications include medical imaging, remote monitoring, clinical decision support and hospital operations, within Finland's strict data protection framework and its specific legislation on secondary use of health data.

## Entry Paths

1. **Summer jobs and internships.** Finnish companies commonly hire students for summer positions, which often lead to thesis projects and permanent roles.
2. **Master's thesis in a company.** Many Finnish engineering students complete their thesis inside companies, often paid.
3. **Research assistant and PhD positions** at the University of Oulu, including in 6G and vision research.
4. **Junior roles** in telecom, wearables, health tech and software.

## Working in Finland

Finnish workplaces are flat and trust-based, value reliability and honesty, and protect work-life balance. Collective agreements shape conditions, and benefits often include lunch and sports benefits and occupational healthcare. English is widely used in Oulu's tech companies and research; Finnish helps in daily life and in some organisations. EU citizens can work freely; non-EU graduates of Finnish universities may be able to stay to look for work, and specialists can apply for permits including fast-track options. Check the Finnish Immigration Service's information.

## Living in Oulu

Oulu is a compact, bicycle-friendly city — famous for year-round cycling, even in winter — with a large student population and affordable housing compared with Helsinki. Winters are long and dark, summers bright with midnight sun. Oulu was a European Capital of Culture in 2026, adding to its cultural life. Nature, sea and islands are close, and flights connect to Helsinki in about an hour.

## AI in Future Networks

Research on 6G explores networks that are faster, more reliable and more energy-efficient than today's, with AI built into their design. Machine learning is expected to manage radio resources, predict traffic, optimise beamforming, detect faults and even design parts of the communication system itself. Future networks may also sense their environment, blending communication and sensing. For graduates, this field combines signal processing, wireless theory, optimisation and machine learning, and it offers the chance to contribute to technologies that will shape the next decades of connectivity. Oulu is one of the best places in the world to start such a career.

## Wearable Data: What the Work Involves

Working with wearable data means dealing with noisy, continuous signals from sensors worn by people in everyday life. Data scientists design algorithms that estimate sleep stages, heart rate variability, activity levels and body temperature trends, and they validate these algorithms against clinical reference measurements. Personalisation adapts insights to each user. Privacy is essential, because physiological data is sensitive, and health-related claims may bring products under medical device regulation and, for AI components, the high-risk requirements of the EU AI Act.

## Computer Vision and Affective Computing

The University of Oulu's research in machine vision and signal analysis has a long history, including work on facial analysis, biometrics and affective computing — recognising emotions and physiological states from signals such as video. This research connects with health, human–computer interaction and security applications, and it raises ethical questions that are increasingly regulated; the EU AI Act restricts certain uses of emotion recognition and biometric identification. Graduates interested in vision can join research groups or companies applying these techniques responsibly.

## Building a Profile for Oulu Employers

- **Signal processing and mathematics**, especially for telecom and wearables.
- **Python, C or C++ and MATLAB** for algorithm development and embedded work.
- **A thesis or project with real data**, such as sensor or radio data.
- **Engineering habits**: version control, testing and documentation.
- **English communication** for international teams.

## Summer Jobs: The Finnish Way In

Finnish companies hire many students for summer positions, often starting in the first or second year of studies. These jobs provide paid experience, contacts and often lead to thesis projects and permanent roles. In Oulu, telecom, wearables and software companies run summer programmes; applications usually open in the winter, so plan ahead.

## Questions to Ask Oulu Employers

- What will I work on in my first months, and who will mentor me?
- Is the thesis position paid, and can it lead to a permanent role?
- What is the working language of the team?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what benefits are included?
- How do algorithms reach products, and how are they validated?

## Networking

Oulu's tech community is close-knit, with events at the university, the technology village areas and startup programmes, as well as meetups on software and AI. The city's size makes it easy to meet engineers from many companies.

## Your First Two Years

Build strong foundations in signal processing, ML and software engineering, learn how products are validated and consider whether research or industry suits you best. Oulu's ecosystem makes it easy to move between the two.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews for junior roles usually include technical questions on signal processing, programming and ML fundamentals, discussion of your thesis or projects and conversations about teamwork. Finnish interviewers value honesty, modesty and clear reasoning. Prepare to explain your work concisely and ask thoughtful questions about the team and projects.

## Staying in Oulu or Moving

Many graduates consider Helsinki or abroad, but Oulu's telecom, wearables and health tech companies offer world-class work in a smaller, affordable city. Experience from Oulu is recognised internationally, especially in wireless and wearables.

## A Simple Action Plan

Apply for summer jobs early, choose a thesis with a company or research group, attend local events and follow up with people you meet. In Oulu, initiative is noticed quickly.

## Health Technology in Practice

OuluHealth brings together researchers, clinicians and companies to develop and test new health technologies in real clinical environments. For graduates, this means projects such as analysing data from remote patient monitoring, building models that predict deterioration in hospital patients, supporting imaging diagnostics or optimising hospital logistics. Finland's national framework for secondary use of health and social data enables research while protecting privacy, and projects must follow strict ethics and data protection procedures. Working in health technology teaches graduates rigorous validation, collaboration with clinicians and responsible handling of sensitive data — skills that are valued throughout the health sector in Europe. Combining Oulu's sensor and wearable expertise with clinical research is a particular strength of the city, and some graduates find their niche exactly at that intersection.

## Software Companies and Startups

Oulu's software sector includes companies building products for industry, health, games and consumer markets, as well as startups spun out of the university and former telecom teams. Startups often work on sensors, IoT, health tech and AI applications, supported by local business services and investors. For graduates, startup roles offer broad responsibility and close contact with customers.

## Reading Graduate Offers

Finnish salaries for graduates are defined by collective agreements in many sectors, with benefits such as lunch and sports vouchers and occupational healthcare. Compare offers on salary, benefits, learning opportunities and mentoring rather than salary alone.

## Responsible AI

Wearables, health technology and biometric research all involve sensitive personal data. Graduates who understand GDPR and the EU AI Act — including restrictions on some biometric and emotion recognition uses — bring clear value to Oulu's employers.

## Surviving and Enjoying Northern Winters

Oulu's winters are long, cold and dark, while summers bring nearly endless daylight. Locals embrace winter with cycling on well-maintained paths, skiing, ice swimming and saunas. Newcomers benefit from a daylight lamp, regular outdoor activity and social routines during the darkest months. Many international students and engineers say that after the first winter, Oulu's calm, nature and community become a major reason to stay.

## A Final Tip

Apply for summer jobs in your first or second year of studies; in Finland, early work experience is the most reliable path to a permanent role.

## Staying Connected

Keep in touch with your thesis supervisor and summer job colleagues after graduating; Oulu's engineering community is tightly connected, and many roles are filled through recommendations rather than public postings.

## One More Tip

Visit a company open day or university lab tour before choosing your thesis topic.

## Real example

### An electrical engineering graduate who joined 6G research

An electrical engineering student at the University of Oulu did a summer job at a telecom R&D centre, working on test automation for radio equipment. Her master's thesis, also with the company, explored machine learning for beam management in 5G.

After graduation, she received a job offer from the company and a PhD position in the 6G research programme. She chose the PhD, keeping close contact with industry through joint projects. She says Oulu gave her access to world-leading wireless research and industry without moving to a large capital.
