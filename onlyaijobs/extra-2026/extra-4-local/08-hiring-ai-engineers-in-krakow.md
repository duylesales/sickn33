Kraków has become one of the most important engineering locations in Central Europe. Over the past two decades, international companies established software, finance and shared-service centres in the city, and a generation of engineers built careers there. Today, hiring AI engineers in Kraków is attractive for many employers: strong universities, an experienced technical workforce and a city people want to live in. It is also competitive. This playbook is written for hiring managers and recruiters who want to build or grow a machine learning team in Kraków, and explains where the talent comes from, what candidates compare and how to run a hiring process that actually closes.

## Why Hiring AI Engineers in Kraków Makes Sense

Several factors make Kraków stand out for AI hiring.

**Universities.** Jagiellonian University, one of Europe's oldest universities, has strong mathematics, computer science and physics faculties. AGH University of Krakow is one of Poland's leading technical universities with a large computer science and data science programme. Cracow University of Technology adds engineering graduates. Together they produce a steady flow of graduates with solid quantitative foundations.

**Computing infrastructure.** Cyfronet, the academic computer centre at AGH, operates some of Poland's largest supercomputers, which supports research and high-performance computing skills in the region.

**An established engineering ecosystem.** Many international companies run engineering or technology centres in Kraków. This means an experienced pool of software engineers, data engineers and architects, and a professional culture familiar with international teams, English as a working language and global delivery.

**Quality of life.** Kraków's historic centre, cultural life and relatively compact size make it attractive for both locals and people relocating from elsewhere in Poland or abroad.

## Understanding the Kraków Talent Pool

When hiring AI engineers in Kraków, it helps to segment the market rather than treating it as one pool.

| Segment | Typical background | What they look for |
|---|---|---|
| Graduates and juniors | Jagiellonian, AGH, Politechnika; theses in ML or data | Mentoring, real projects, growth path |
| Software engineers moving into ML | Several years in backend or data engineering | Structured transition, ML exposure on real systems |
| Experienced ML and data scientists | Built and deployed models in industry | Ownership, interesting problems, competitive pay |
| MLOps and platform engineers | Cloud, Kubernetes, data pipelines | Scale, modern tooling, reliability challenges |
| Researchers | PhD or research experience | Research freedom, publication, compute access |

The largest and most accessible segment is often software engineers moving into ML. Many employers build strong teams by combining a few experienced ML specialists with engineers who bring production discipline and learn modelling on the job.

## What Kraków Candidates Compare

Experienced engineers in Kraków typically have several options at once. They compare offers on dimensions that go well beyond salary.

**Contract form.** In Poland, many technology professionals work either on an employment contract (umowa o pracę) or on a B2B contract as a self-employed contractor. Each has different implications for net pay, social security, paid leave and protection. Many candidates have strong preferences; be clear which forms you offer and what each includes.

**Scope of the role.** Candidates who have worked in large delivery centres often want more product ownership. A role where they design and own ML systems end to end is more attractive than one where they implement specifications from elsewhere.

**Team and decision-making location.** Candidates ask whether key decisions are made in Kraków or at headquarters abroad. A local team with real autonomy is a strong selling point.

**Technology and compute.** ML engineers want access to modern tools, GPUs or cloud compute budgets, and a sensible deployment path.

**Hybrid policy and office location.** Commuting in Kraków can be slow at peak times. Office location matters: an office near the centre or well served by trams is more attractive than one in a distant business park for many candidates.

## Writing a Vacancy That Works in Kraków

Kraków candidates read many similar postings. A vacancy that stands out is specific.

1. **Title that matches the work.** "Machine Learning Engineer (recommendation models)" is better than "AI Specialist".
2. **Describe the problem.** What data, what models, what users, what is already in production.
3. **Name the stack.** Languages, frameworks, cloud platform, orchestration and monitoring tools.
4. **State the contract forms and pay range.** Many Polish postings already include ranges for both employment and B2B contracts; omitting them makes your vacancy look less serious.
5. **Give the exact office address and hybrid policy.** Candidates calculate their commute immediately.
6. **Explain the interview process.** Number of rounds, types of tasks and expected duration.
7. **Language requirements.** Say whether Polish is needed. Many teams work in English, which widens the pool to international candidates.

The EU Pay Transparency Directive will require employers across the EU to give pay information to candidates before interviews and prohibit asking about pay history, so including ranges now also prepares you for the new rules.

## Running an Efficient Hiring Process

Experienced candidates in Kraków rarely stay available for long. A slow process loses candidates to faster employers.

- **Keep the process short:** a screening call, a technical conversation, a practical task or case and a final meeting are usually enough. Aim for a decision within a few weeks.
- **Make tasks relevant and bounded:** a small modelling or system design exercise that reflects real work, with a time limit that respects candidates' evenings.
- **Involve the hiring manager early:** candidates want to meet the person they will work with.
- **Give feedback quickly:** even rejected candidates talk to colleagues; a respectful process builds reputation in a close-knit community.
- **Prepare the offer in advance:** know your range, contract options and flexibility before the final round.

## Hiring International Candidates

Kraków attracts engineers from other EU countries and from outside the EU. EU citizens can work without a permit but must register their stay if staying long-term. Non-EU hires usually need a work and residence permit; highly qualified candidates may be eligible for the EU Blue Card, which the recast Blue Card Directive (EU) 2021/1883 made more flexible across member states. Processing times for Polish permits can be significant, so start early and consider working with a relocation specialist. Always check current rules with official sources.

## Building a Sustainable ML Team

Hiring is only the start. Kraków employers that retain AI engineers tend to:

- Give engineers ownership of meaningful problems and let them see the impact of their models.
- Invest in learning: conference budgets, internal reading groups, collaboration with Jagiellonian or AGH on projects or theses.
- Offer clear career paths for both technical and managerial growth.
- Keep compensation reviews regular and transparent.
- Maintain a healthy engineering culture: code review, documentation, reasonable on-call arrangements for production models.

With the EU AI Act, ML teams increasingly also need governance practices — documentation of data and models, risk assessments for high-risk uses and human oversight. Building these practices in from the start makes your team more attractive to engineers who care about quality.

## Partnering With Universities

Collaborating with Kraków's universities is one of the best long-term investments for an AI team. Options include sponsored theses, internships, guest lectures, joint research projects and participation in student hackathons and science clubs. AGH and Jagiellonian have active student research groups in AI and data science; being visible there helps you reach talented students before they graduate. Treat internships as real projects with mentors, because good students compare internship experiences and share them with peers.

## Onboarding: The First Ninety Days

A strong hiring process can be undone by a weak start. AI engineers who join a new Kraków team form their view of the employer in the first weeks, and early disappointment often leads to departures within a year.

**Before day one.** Make sure hardware, accounts, data access and cloud permissions are ready. ML engineers who spend their first two weeks waiting for access to data lose motivation quickly, and it signals that the organisation does not take the work seriously.

**First month.** Give each new hire a buddy, a clear map of the data and systems, and a small but real task that reaches production — for example improving a feature pipeline or adding monitoring to an existing model. Early delivery builds confidence and trust.

**Second month.** Involve the engineer in a larger piece of work with a clear owner and a clear definition of success. Review progress regularly and make expectations explicit.

**Third month.** Hold a structured conversation about how the role matches what was promised in the interviews, what the engineer wants to learn next and where they see their contribution growing. Adjust scope where there is a mismatch.

## Competing Without Outbidding

Not every employer can match the highest offers in the Kraków market. Smaller companies and new entrants can still compete effectively by emphasising what larger centres often cannot offer:

- **Ownership and visibility:** engineers work directly with product and business, and see the impact of their models.
- **Breadth:** exposure to the full lifecycle, from data collection to deployment and monitoring.
- **Speed of decisions:** fewer layers between an idea and its implementation.
- **Flexibility:** hybrid arrangements, flexible hours and room to shape the team's practices.
- **Learning budget:** conferences, courses and time for experimentation.

Be honest about trade-offs. Candidates in Kraków are experienced and will notice if a posting overstates what the company offers. An honest description of the challenges, such as limited data today or a young platform, attracts people who enjoy building rather than maintaining.

## Measuring Your Hiring Funnel

Track a few simple metrics for each AI vacancy in Kraków: number of applications, share that pass screening, time from application to first contact, time to offer, offer acceptance rate and reasons for declined offers. These numbers reveal where the process leaks. A low screening pass rate usually means the posting is too vague; a low acceptance rate often points to pay, contract form or role scope; long time-to-offer means candidates are accepting elsewhere before you decide.

## Retention Signals to Watch

Engineers rarely leave without warning. Signals worth noticing in a Kraków AI team include declining engagement in design discussions, requests to move away from ML work, frustration about infrastructure or data access, and questions about pay compared with the market. Regular one-to-one conversations, transparent pay reviews and visible investment in tooling address most of these before they become resignations. When someone does leave, an honest exit conversation provides information that improves the next hire.

## A Note on Employer Branding in Kraków

Kraków's technology community is well connected, and word travels fast between engineers. Speaking at local meetups, publishing technical blog posts about your ML work and treating every candidate respectfully all build a reputation that makes the next hire easier.

## Real example

### A logistics software company opening an ML team in Kraków

A European logistics software company decides to build a forecasting and optimisation team in Kraków. Its first vacancy, titled "AI Engineer", attracts many applications but few with relevant skills.

The hiring manager rewrites the posting: "Machine Learning Engineer — demand forecasting", with a description of the data (shipment histories, seasonality, external signals), the stack, pay ranges for both contract types, the exact office address near a tram line, a two-day hybrid policy and a four-step interview process. She also partners with an AGH student research group for a sponsored thesis on forecasting.

The rewritten vacancy attracts fewer but much better-matched applications, and the process is shortened from two months to under four weeks. Within a year, the team has two experienced ML engineers, two software engineers who moved into ML and a graduate from the thesis project. Retention is strong because the team owns its models and sees the impact on customers.
