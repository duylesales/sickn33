Friesland is known for its lakes, its own language, its dairy farms and its open landscapes. It is rarely mentioned in discussions about AI careers. Yet the province has a growing knowledge economy around water technology, dairy and agri-food, energy and public services, supported by a university campus, universities of applied sciences and a European research institute for water technology. The challenge for the region is not that AI jobs in Friesland don't exist, but that too few graduates know about them — and that many young people leave for Groningen, Amsterdam or Utrecht. This guide is written for regional organisations — the province, municipalities, educational institutions, development agencies and employer networks — that want to attract and keep data talent.

## Where AI Jobs in Friesland Are Found

**Water technology.** Leeuwarden hosts Wetsus, the European centre of excellence for sustainable water technology, and the Water Campus, where companies, researchers and students work on water treatment, desalination, sensors and resource recovery. Data science supports sensor analytics, process control, modelling of treatment processes and monitoring of water networks. Water boards and drinking water companies in the region add operational data roles.

**Dairy and agri-food.** Friesland is one of Europe's most important dairy regions. Dairy processors, feed companies, cooperatives and agri-tech firms use data for herd management, milk quality, production planning and supply chain optimisation. The Dairy Campus in Leeuwarden brings together research, education and practice in dairy farming, where sensors, robotics and data analytics are increasingly common.

**Energy.** The northern Netherlands is central to the Dutch energy transition, with wind, solar, hydrogen initiatives and grid challenges. Energy companies, grid operators and startups use data for forecasting, grid management and planning.

**Public services.** The Province of Fryslân, municipalities, water boards, healthcare organisations and educational institutions employ data analysts and data scientists for policy analysis, planning, mobility, environmental monitoring and service delivery.

**SMEs and manufacturing.** Friesland's economy has many SMEs in maritime, manufacturing, construction and services, some of which are beginning their first AI projects.

## The Knowledge Institutions

**Campus Fryslân**, the University of Groningen's faculty in Leeuwarden, offers programmes with a focus on data science and society, sustainability and governance, and conducts research that connects data with regional challenges.

**NHL Stenden University of Applied Sciences** trains ICT, data and engineering professionals and has applied research groups working with regional companies, including on computer vision, data science and serious gaming.

**Van Hall Larenstein University of Applied Sciences** focuses on agriculture, food, water and the environment — domains where data science is increasingly important.

**Wetsus** combines research on water technology with a graduate school and collaboration with companies from across Europe.

Groningen, with its university, Hanze University of Applied Sciences and a strong IT sector, is about 35 minutes away by train, and OnlyAIJobs already lists vacancies there.

## Why Talent Leaves Friesland

- **Visibility**: regional employers rarely use the job titles and channels that data graduates search.
- **Perception**: students assume that AI careers require a big city.
- **Critical mass**: graduates worry about few employers and limited career paths.
- **International students** at Campus Fryslân, Wetsus and NHL Stenden may not know which employers hire in English.

These are largely information problems, which regional organisations can address.

## What Regional Organisations Can Do

**Make employers findable.** Encourage employers to publish AI and data vacancies with exact addresses, clear descriptions and language requirements, and encourage job seekers to search by distance rather than by city label.

**Connect education and practice early.** Theses, internships and challenge-based projects with water, dairy, energy and public organisations are the most effective way to keep graduates.

**Show career paths.** Publish concrete stories of data professionals who built careers in Friesland, from first job to senior roles.

**Treat the North as one labour market.** For many graduates, living in Leeuwarden and working in Groningen — or vice versa — is realistic. Cooperation with Groningen and Drenthe widens the market and makes the region more attractive.

**Support SMEs.** Help small companies define realistic first AI roles and write vacancies that compete.

**Address international graduates.** Identify employers that hire in English and help them with information on permits and the orientation year.

## Water Technology Data in Practice

Water technology generates rich data problems that suit graduates in data science, chemistry and engineering. Treatment plants and pilot installations produce continuous sensor data on flows, pressures, conductivity, turbidity and chemical composition. Data scientists build models to predict membrane fouling, optimise energy use, detect sensor faults and control dosing of chemicals. Water networks require leak detection, demand forecasting and asset management. Research at Wetsus and its partner companies explores new technologies whose development benefits from data-driven experimentation. These are niche but internationally relevant skills, because water scarcity and quality are global challenges.

## Dairy, Agri-Food and Precision Farming

Modern dairy farms are data-intensive. Milking robots, activity sensors, feed systems and milk analysers generate continuous information about each animal. Data science helps detect health problems early, optimise feeding, predict fertility and improve milk quality. Processors use data to plan production, manage quality and optimise logistics from farm to factory. Agri-tech companies develop decision-support tools for farmers. Research and education at the Dairy Campus connect these applications with practice. For graduates interested in combining AI with sustainability and animal welfare, Friesland offers work that few other regions can match.

## Energy and the Northern Netherlands

The northern Netherlands is a focal point of the Dutch energy transition, with large-scale wind energy, solar parks, hydrogen initiatives and the phase-out of gas production in Groningen. Grid operators and energy companies face congestion and planning challenges that require forecasting, optimisation and scenario modelling. Regional energy cooperatives and municipalities also need data expertise to plan local energy systems. Data professionals who work in this field contribute directly to the region's economic future.

## Measuring Progress

Regional organisations should track a few indicators: the share of data and AI graduates from Frisian institutions who take a first job in the North, the number of regional AI and data vacancies published with exact addresses and clear descriptions, time to fill for regional data roles and the number of thesis and internship projects with regional employers. These indicators show whether initiatives work and help justify continued investment.

## Working With SMEs

Many Frisian SMEs are interested in AI but unsure where to start. Regional organisations can help them by offering practical support: advice on whether they need a data engineer, analyst or ML specialist first; templates for good vacancy texts; shared internship programmes in which several SMEs host students; connections with NHL Stenden and Campus Fryslân for applied projects; and information on GDPR and the EU AI Act. A single successful first hire often turns an SME into a strong advocate for regional data talent, creating a positive cycle.

## Language and International Talent

Frisian and Dutch are both official in Friesland, and many regional organisations work in Dutch. At the same time, Wetsus, Campus Fryslân and several companies are international and use English. Regional organisations can help by making clear which employers welcome English speakers, supporting language courses for international graduates who want to stay and informing employers about the highly skilled migrant scheme and the orientation year for graduates of Dutch institutions.

## Housing and Quality of Life as Talent Arguments

Friesland offers what many young professionals in the Randstad cannot find: affordable housing, short commutes, nature and a strong sense of community. Regional organisations can use these advantages deliberately in talent attraction, for example by linking housing programmes near stations to young professionals and by highlighting the combination of meaningful work and quality of life in concrete stories.

## A One-Year Programme for Regional Organisations

1. **Map the market**: collect AI and data vacancies from regional employers and identify organisations doing data work without visible vacancies.
2. **Improve vacancy texts**: run workshops on specific, transparent vacancies with exact addresses and clear language requirements.
3. **Engage students**: organise sessions where students search by distance and visit regional employers.
4. **Build project pipelines**: match employers with thesis students and interns for the next academic year.
5. **Communicate**: publish career stories and indicators, and plan the next year together with Groningen and Drenthe partners.

## Responsible AI as a Regional Theme

Campus Fryslân's focus on data science and society gives the region a natural profile in responsible AI. Public organisations, water boards and healthcare providers must use algorithms carefully, and the EU AI Act classifies several public-sector uses as high-risk. A region known for doing AI responsibly can attract professionals who care about societal impact.

## A Message for Job Seekers

If you live in Friesland and want a data career, start by searching within your commuting radius, including Groningen. The role you are looking for may be closer than you think.

## The Role of Educational Institutions

Educational institutions shape retention more than any other actor. When programmes include projects with regional employers, when career services actively present regional organisations and when lecturers use regional data challenges in their courses, graduates are far more likely to stay. NHL Stenden and Van Hall Larenstein have long traditions of practice-oriented education with regional partners, while Campus Fryslân connects academic research with regional questions and Wetsus trains doctoral researchers in close collaboration with companies. Coordinating these efforts — for example through a shared overview of data and AI thesis opportunities in the province — makes it easier for employers to engage and for students to find meaningful regional projects. Institutions can also invite alumni who stayed in the region to speak to students about their careers, which is often more persuasive than any campaign.

## Common Pitfalls

Regional initiatives sometimes fail because they focus on branding rather than practical tools, involve only large employers, ignore international graduates or measure activities instead of outcomes. The most effective programmes are concrete, inclusive of SMEs and public organisations and focused on the moment when a graduate decides where to apply. They also acknowledge honestly that Friesland will not offer every type of AI role, and emphasise instead the kinds of work where the region is genuinely strong: water, dairy, energy and public services.

## Telling the Frisian Story

Generic slogans about innovation rarely persuade graduates. Concrete stories do: a data scientist who predicts membrane fouling at a water technology company, a graduate who builds herd-health models for dairy farmers, an analyst who helps a water board prepare for extreme weather, an international graduate who joined an English-speaking team in Leeuwarden. Combined with practical tools for finding jobs by distance, such stories show that meaningful AI careers exist in Friesland — and that leaving is a choice rather than a necessity.

## Final Advice

Start small, measure honestly and cooperate across the North: a few well-run programmes that connect students with real regional employers achieve more than broad campaigns.

## Real example

### A regional initiative connecting water tech and graduates

A group of water technology companies and knowledge institutions in Leeuwarden noticed that data-oriented graduates from local programmes left the region, while companies struggled to find people who could analyse sensor and process data.

They organised a joint programme: companies formulated real data challenges, students from the university campus and universities of applied sciences worked on them in teams, and each company published its data roles with exact addresses, English-language descriptions where relevant and salary ranges. Students were also shown how to search AI and data vacancies by distance. Several graduates took jobs with participating companies, and the companies reported better-matched applications. The programme did not create new jobs; it connected existing jobs with people who didn't know about them.
