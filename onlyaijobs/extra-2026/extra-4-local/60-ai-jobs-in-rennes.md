Rennes, the capital of Brittany, is a lively university city of around 225,000 people with a young population and a strong technology base. It has become one of France's most important centres for cybersecurity, combining defence, research and industry, and it has a long tradition in telecommunications and digital video. Inria and the IRISA laboratory make it a major site for computer science research outside Paris. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Rennes offer work in security, networks, media technology, automotive and agri-food, in a city with a lower cost of living than Paris and a reputation for quality of life. This guide explains the landscape and what to consider before moving.

## Rennes as a Cybersecurity Hub

Rennes and its surroundings host a concentration of cybersecurity activity that is unique in France. The DGA Maîtrise de l'information, the French defence procurement agency's centre for information and cybersecurity expertise, is located in Bruz near Rennes and employs a large number of engineers. The Pôle d'excellence cyber, a national initiative bringing together defence, industry and academia, is based in Brittany. Defence and security companies, startups and research groups have built teams in the region.

AI and cybersecurity meet in many ways: detecting intrusions and malware with machine learning, analysing threat intelligence, securing AI systems against adversarial attacks and data poisoning, and applying NLP to security documents. Some roles in defence require French nationality or security clearance; civilian companies and research groups are generally open to EU and international candidates.

## Telecom and Media Technology

Brittany has a long history in telecommunications, and the Rennes area still hosts innovation sites of telecom operators and suppliers. b<>com, an institute of research and technology in Cesson-Sévigné, works on digital technologies including AI, immersive media, networks and cybersecurity. Rennes also has a strong heritage in video and image processing; research teams, including InterDigital's research activities in the city, work on video compression, computer vision and immersive media. For ML engineers interested in image and video, this is one of the most relevant places in Europe.

## Research and Education

**Inria and IRISA.** Inria's Rennes centre and IRISA, a large joint research laboratory of the university, CNRS, Inria and engineering schools, cover machine learning, computer vision, data management, security, networks, robotics and bioinformatics. Many researchers collaborate with industry, and the labs offer PhD, postdoc and research engineer positions.

**Universities and schools.** The University of Rennes, INSA Rennes, CentraleSupélec's Rennes campus, IMT Atlantique and other schools train engineers and computer scientists, with specialised programmes in cybersecurity and data science.

## Other Sectors

**Automotive.** Stellantis operates a car plant at La Janais near Rennes, and automotive suppliers and mobility companies are present in the region, with AI in manufacturing and vehicle technology.

**Agri-food.** Brittany is one of France's leading agricultural and food-processing regions. Data science supports production planning, quality control, supply chains and precision agriculture.

**Digital services and startups.** Software companies, IT services firms and startups in Rennes work on SaaS, digital health, fintech and B2B software.

**Banking and insurance.** Regional banks and insurers in Brittany employ data scientists for risk and customer analytics.

## French Employment Conditions

Most engineers in France are hired on permanent contracts (CDI) with cadre status, often with a days-based working-time arrangement and additional rest days (RTT). Collective agreements such as Syntec for IT and engineering services apply in many companies. Benefits commonly include meal vouchers, supplementary health insurance, profit-sharing schemes and reimbursement of half the cost of public transport passes. Salaries in Rennes are generally lower than in Paris, but housing and living costs are also considerably lower.

## Language and Permits

French is essential for daily life and for most workplaces in Rennes, especially in defence, industry and public institutions. Research labs and some tech companies work in English. EU citizens can work freely; non-EU professionals often use the Talent Passport, including its EU Blue Card category, or the researcher route. Defence-related roles may have nationality requirements.

## Living in Rennes

Rennes combines a medieval centre with timber-framed houses, a large student population and an active cultural life. It has two metro lines, buses and extensive cycling infrastructure, and high-speed trains reach Paris in about one and a half hours. Housing is more affordable than in Paris, though demand has grown. The Breton coast — Saint-Malo, the Emerald Coast and Mont-Saint-Michel nearby — is within an hour.

## AI for Cybersecurity in Practice

Security teams face enormous volumes of logs, network flows and alerts, and machine learning helps them focus on what matters. Typical projects in Rennes include detecting anomalous network behaviour, classifying malware from static and dynamic features, correlating alerts across systems, identifying phishing campaigns and extracting indicators from threat reports with NLP. The challenges are distinctive: attackers adapt, labelled data is scarce, false positives are costly for analysts and models must be explainable enough for analysts to trust them. At the same time, AI systems themselves need protection. Research groups and companies in Rennes work on adversarial robustness, data poisoning, model extraction and privacy attacks — topics that the EU AI Act makes directly relevant by requiring robustness and cybersecurity for high-risk AI systems.

## Video, Images and Immersive Media

Rennes' expertise in video goes back decades, to research and industry work on digital television and compression standards. Today, teams in the region apply deep learning to video compression, super-resolution, quality assessment, content analysis and immersive formats such as volumetric video. Learned compression — using neural networks to encode images and video more efficiently — is an active research area where Rennes teams contribute. Engineers with backgrounds in signal processing and computer vision find a rare concentration of relevant roles in the city, combining research depth with industrial standards work.

## Research Careers at Inria and IRISA

For research-oriented engineers, Rennes offers PhD, postdoc, research engineer and permanent research positions. Inria recruits researchers through competitive processes and hires engineers on project contracts, often linked to European or industrial collaborations. IRISA's many teams cover a wide range of topics, making it possible to find a group that matches almost any AI specialisation. France's CIFRE scheme lets companies employ PhD students supervised by academic labs, which is widely used in Rennes, particularly in security, telecom and media technology.

## Agri-Food and Industry in Brittany

Brittany's agricultural and food industries are among the largest in France. Data science helps plan production, predict demand, optimise logistics, monitor quality and reduce environmental impact. Precision agriculture uses sensors, satellite imagery and models to support farmers. These roles usually require French and domain curiosity, and they offer stable employers and tangible impact on a sector central to the regional economy.

## Questions to Ask Rennes Employers

- Does the role involve defence projects, and are there nationality or clearance requirements?
- What is the working language of the team and of clients?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what is the cadre working-time arrangement?
- At which site will I work, and how is it connected by public transport?
- How do models reach production, and who operates them?
- What benefits are included, such as profit-sharing and health insurance?

## Networking in Rennes

Rennes has an active tech community with meetups on cybersecurity, machine learning and data science, events organised by the Pôle d'excellence cyber and regular conferences in security and digital technology. Inria and IRISA host seminars that are often open to professionals. Attending these events is the best way to understand the ecosystem and learn about opportunities early.

## Settling In

Newcomers need a French bank account, registration with social security and a carte Vitale for healthcare reimbursements. Non-EU residents must complete residence permit procedures. Temporary housing for the first weeks gives time to find a flat near your workplace.

## Skills That Stand Out

Rennes employers value security-aware ML engineers, signal and video processing expertise, strong software engineering, and the ability to work with analysts, operators and researchers. Knowledge of privacy-preserving techniques and robustness testing is increasingly sought after. For defence and critical infrastructure roles, rigorous documentation and testing practices matter as much as model performance.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare net pay after social contributions, cadre status and RTT days, profit-sharing and benefits, and weigh the lower housing costs and shorter commutes compared with Paris. For many engineers, a Rennes offer slightly below a Paris salary leaves more disposable income and more free time.

## Beyond Rennes: Brest, Lannion and Nantes

Brittany's technology ecosystem extends beyond the capital. Brest has naval, maritime and defence activities and IMT Atlantique's main campus; Lannion has a long telecom history and photonics companies; and Nantes, in the neighbouring region, has a large digital sector and is about an hour away by train.

## Who Rennes Suits Best

Rennes suits engineers who want AI work in security, networks or media technology, value a research-rich environment and appreciate a lively, affordable city close to the sea.

## Telecom Networks and AI

Beyond video, the telecom heritage of Brittany continues in network research and operations. Operators and suppliers use machine learning to optimise radio networks, predict equipment failures, manage traffic, detect fraud and automate network operations. Research on future networks explores AI-native architectures, energy efficiency and security. b<>com and university labs work with industry on these topics, and engineers with networking, signal processing or reinforcement learning backgrounds find relevant projects. Telecom AI offers a combination of systems engineering and data science that many engineers find intellectually rewarding, with direct impact on infrastructure used by millions of people.

## Working in Defence-Related Projects

Rennes' security ecosystem includes defence-related work. Such projects offer technically demanding problems and stable funding, but they involve specific constraints: security clearances, restrictions on publishing, work in secured facilities and sometimes nationality requirements. Candidates should clarify these conditions early in the process. Civilian cybersecurity companies, research labs and dual-use projects offer alternatives with fewer restrictions, and many engineers move between these environments during their careers.

## Automotive Manufacturing Data

The car plant near Rennes and its suppliers apply data science to paint and body inspection, predictive maintenance of production equipment, energy management and logistics. These projects require close work with production teams and offer tangible, measurable results.

Ask in the first interview whether the role touches defence work; the answer shapes clearance needs, publication rights and timelines.
