The Basque Country, in northern Spain, is one of Europe's most industrial regions and one of the wealthiest in Spain. Its economy rests on manufacturing — machine tools, automotive components, railway equipment, aerospace parts and energy equipment — supported by a dense network of technology centres and a cooperative tradition unique in Europe. The region has invested heavily in applied research and digital industry, and it hosts major energy and banking companies. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in the Basque Country are strongly oriented toward industrial AI, energy and applied research, in cities known for their quality of life, food culture and proximity to both the sea and the mountains. This guide maps the opportunities and explains what to consider before moving.

## The Industrial Foundation

**Machine tools and advanced manufacturing.** The Basque Country is Spain's leading region for machine tools, with many companies concentrated in Gipuzkoa and Bizkaia. AI applications include predictive maintenance of machines, process monitoring, quality prediction, adaptive control and smart features in machines sold worldwide.

**Automotive and components.** Suppliers of automotive parts operate plants and engineering centres in the region, with AI used in quality inspection, process optimisation and supply chain planning.

**Railway and transport.** CAF, headquartered in Beasain, manufactures trains and railway systems. Data science supports predictive maintenance of rolling stock, energy optimisation and fleet management.

**Energy.** Iberdrola, one of the world's largest electricity companies, has its headquarters in Bilbao. Energy companies and suppliers in the region use AI for renewable forecasting, grid operations, asset maintenance and trading. The region also has a strong cluster of energy equipment manufacturers.

**Banking.** BBVA has its historical roots and registered office in Bilbao, and regional savings banks and financial institutions add data roles in risk and customer analytics.

**Cooperatives.** Mondragon Corporation, based in the town of Arrasate-Mondragón, is one of the world's largest groups of worker cooperatives, active in industry, retail, finance and education. Its companies are increasingly adopting digital technologies, and Mondragon University trains engineers closely linked to industry.

## Applied Research Centres

The Basque Country has one of Spain's densest networks of technology centres, which are central to AI employment in the region.

**Tecnalia** is one of Europe's largest applied research and technology organisations, working with companies on digital technologies, industry, energy, health and mobility, including AI and data analytics.

**Vicomtech**, based in San Sebastián, specialises in visual computing, interaction and AI, including computer vision, speech and language technologies and data analytics for industry, health and media.

**BCAM**, the Basque Center for Applied Mathematics in Bilbao, conducts research in applied mathematics, including machine learning, statistics and optimisation.

Other centres and cooperative research institutes work on manufacturing, materials and energy. The region has also invested in quantum computing as part of its science and technology strategy. For ML engineers, these centres offer applied research roles with direct industry impact — a middle ground between academia and product companies.

## Universities

The University of the Basque Country (UPV/EHU) is the main public university, with campuses in Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa and Álava and research groups in computer science, AI, NLP (including Basque language technology) and applied mathematics. The University of Deusto, in Bilbao and San Sebastián, has engineering and business programmes, and Mondragon University offers engineering education closely tied to cooperative industry.

## Language

Spanish and Basque (Euskara) are both official in the Basque Country. Spanish is spoken by everyone and is essential for most workplaces; Basque is widely used in public administration, education and daily life in many areas, and it may be required for some public sector roles. Research centres and some international teams work in English, but industrial companies usually operate in Spanish. For international engineers, learning Spanish is essential, and some Basque is appreciated.

## Pay and Cost of Living

Salaries in the Basque Country are among the highest in Spain, although generally below Northern European levels. Collective agreements shape conditions in industry, and the region has its own tax system through the provincial foral treasuries, which can differ in detail from the rest of Spain. Housing costs are relatively high by Spanish standards, particularly in San Sebastián, while Bilbao and Vitoria-Gasteiz are more moderate. Compare net pay, benefits and housing carefully.

## Industrial AI in Practice

Industrial AI in the Basque Country has specific characteristics that shape daily work:

- **Machine data at the edge.** Machine tools and production lines generate high-frequency signals from spindles, motors, sensors and controllers. Models often run on industrial PCs or embedded hardware close to the machine, with limited compute and strict reliability requirements.
- **Scarce failure data.** Real failures are rare and expensive, so engineers use anomaly detection, physics-informed features, simulation and careful labelling with maintenance experts.
- **Domain collaboration.** Success depends on working with machine builders, process engineers and operators, who understand the physics and the practical constraints.
- **Product integration.** Machine builders increasingly sell AI-enabled features — monitoring, diagnostics, optimisation — as part of their products, which requires robust software engineering and long-term support.
- **Safety and standards.** Industrial machinery is subject to safety regulations, and the EU AI Act classifies AI systems used as safety components in certain regulated products as high-risk.

Engineers who enjoy the combination of signals, physics and software find this work rewarding, and it builds expertise that is valued across European manufacturing.

## Technology Centres as Employers

Working at a Basque technology centre differs from working at a company or a university. Centres carry out applied research and development projects funded by companies, regional and national programmes and European grants. Engineers typically work on several projects at once, often with industrial partners, combining prototyping, experimentation and knowledge transfer. Advantages include variety, exposure to many industries and the opportunity to publish. Trade-offs can include project-based funding cycles and less long-term product ownership. Many engineers move from technology centres into industry after several years, bringing with them a strong network across the region.

## Energy and the Green Transition

Energy is a major part of the Basque economy, from Iberdrola's global operations to manufacturers of wind turbine components, electrical equipment and energy storage. The region has an energy cluster that brings together companies and research organisations. AI applications include forecasting wind and solar generation, optimising grid operations and storage, predicting failures in turbines and transformers, supporting energy trading and improving industrial energy efficiency. As electricity systems integrate more renewable generation, demand for data scientists with energy knowledge grows. For engineers who want their work to contribute to decarbonisation, energy roles in Bilbao offer both technical depth and purpose.

## Language Technology for Basque

Basque is a language isolate, unrelated to other European languages, with a relatively small number of speakers. Building language technology for Basque — machine translation, speech recognition, language models — is a distinctive research and engineering challenge. Research groups at UPV/EHU and technology centres have developed resources and models for Basque, supported by regional language policy. For NLP engineers interested in low-resource languages, this is one of the most interesting places in Europe, and the methods developed for Basque are relevant to many other smaller languages.

## Three Cities, Three Profiles

**Bilbao** is the largest city and the business centre, with energy and banking headquarters, BCAM, the University of Deusto and a transformed riverfront symbolised by the Guggenheim Museum. **San Sebastián (Donostia)** is smaller, famous for its beaches and gastronomy, and hosts Vicomtech, research centres and technology companies; housing is expensive. **Vitoria-Gasteiz**, the administrative capital, is known for green urban planning and has industrial companies and a university campus. The cities are connected by motorways, buses and trains, and many industrial companies are located in towns and valleys between them — check commute times to the exact site before choosing where to live.

## Cooperatives and Workplace Culture

The Basque cooperative tradition shapes workplace culture in parts of the region. In worker cooperatives, employees can become members with voting rights and a share in results, and pay differences between the highest and lowest earners are typically narrower than in conventional companies. For engineers, joining a cooperative company can mean more participation in decisions and a strong sense of community, but also different compensation structures. More generally, Basque workplaces tend to value seriousness, reliability and long-term relationships. Industrial companies often have stable workforces, and people build careers over many years in the same region.

## Questions to Ask Basque Employers

- At which site will I work, and how far is it from Bilbao, San Sebastián or Vitoria?
- In which language are meetings and documentation conducted?
- Is the company a cooperative, and how does that affect compensation and membership?
- For technology centres: how are projects funded, and how long are contracts?
- How do models reach production on machines or in operations?
- Which collective agreement applies, and how does the regional tax system affect net pay?

## Settling In

Newcomers from abroad need a foreigner identification number (NIE), registration at the town hall and social security registration. Because the Basque Country has its own provincial tax administrations, tax registration and filing follow regional rules; ask your employer or a local adviser for guidance.

## Networking in Euskadi

The Basque AI community is smaller than those of Madrid or Barcelona but closely connected. Technology centres, universities and industry clusters organise events on AI, Industry 4.0, energy and digital transformation, and meetups on data science and Python take place in Bilbao and San Sebastián. Regional programmes supporting digital industry bring companies and researchers together. Personal relationships and trust matter in Basque business culture, so attending events regularly and following up with the people you meet is one of the most effective ways to discover opportunities, many of which are filled through recommendations rather than public postings.

## Who the Basque Country Suits Best

The region suits engineers who enjoy industrial and energy problems with tangible results, value applied research close to companies and appreciate a high quality of life between the sea and the mountains. It suits less well those looking for consumer tech at global scale or English-only workplaces.

The salary is lower than in Munich, but she calculates her net income under the Basque tax system, compares housing costs in Bilbao and considers the role: she would work with several manufacturers on real machines, with a research dimension she enjoys. She says the combination of applied research and industrial impact is exactly what she was looking for — and that Bilbao's quality of life exceeded her expectations.
