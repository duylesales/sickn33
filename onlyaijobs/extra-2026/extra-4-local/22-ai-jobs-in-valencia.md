Valencia, Spain's third-largest city, has become a popular destination for young professionals, remote workers and startups. Its Mediterranean climate, beaches, cycling-friendly streets and relatively affordable cost of living (compared with Madrid or Barcelona, though rising) attract people from across Europe. Behind the lifestyle, the city has a serious technical base: a leading polytechnic university with an AI research institute, a regional AI network, a growing startup ecosystem and industries such as logistics, automotive, retail and agri-food. For graduates, AI jobs in Valencia offer a realistic way to begin a machine learning career. This guide explains where to look, what employers expect and how to build a profile that works.

## The Academic Foundation

**Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV).** UPV is one of Spain's leading technical universities, with strong computer science, telecommunications and industrial engineering programmes. Its Valencian Research Institute for Artificial Intelligence (VRAIN) brings together research groups working on machine learning, pattern recognition, natural language processing, multi-agent systems and other AI fields. UPV's pattern recognition and human language technologies research has a long history, including work on machine translation and speech recognition.

**University of Valencia.** The University of Valencia has strong programmes in mathematics, computer science, physics and data science, and research groups applying AI in areas such as health and image processing.

**ValgrAI.** The Valencian Graduate School and Research Network of Artificial Intelligence brings together universities and research centres in the Valencian Community to strengthen AI research, training and collaboration with industry. It offers scholarships and postgraduate opportunities and connects students with research groups across the region.

Other universities in the region, such as the University of Alicante and Universitat Jaume I in Castellón, add further research and graduates.

## Entry-Level Paths

For graduates, typical entry paths include:

1. **Internships (prácticas).** Many Spanish companies hire graduates through internships, often linked to university agreements. Quality varies: the best offer real projects, mentoring and a path to a contract.
2. **Junior data analyst or data engineer roles.** Common first jobs that build the foundation for ML work.
3. **Junior data scientist or ML engineer roles** at startups and tech companies.
4. **Research scholarships and PhD positions** through VRAIN, ValgrAI or other groups.
5. **Consultancies and IT services**, which hire many graduates and offer exposure to various clients.

## Pay and Cost of Living

Salaries in Spain are generally lower than in Northern and Western Europe, and entry-level AI roles in Valencia reflect this. International companies and well-funded startups may pay more. The cost of living is lower than in Madrid or Barcelona, but rents in Valencia have risen noticeably as the city has become popular with international residents. When comparing offers, look at net pay (Spanish salaries are often paid in 12 or 14 payments, with two extra payments in summer and December), the contract type, working hours (some companies use intensive summer schedules) and benefits such as meal vouchers or health insurance.

## Language

Spanish is essential for daily life and for most local employers. Valencian, a co-official language in the Valencian Community, is used in regional administration and education; it is required for some public sector positions. Many startups and international companies work in English, but speaking Spanish significantly broadens opportunities. For international graduates, investing in Spanish early pays off.

## Building a Portfolio That Works in Valencia

Valencia employers, especially startups, want evidence that you can deliver. A strong graduate portfolio includes:

- **One end-to-end project:** from data collection to a deployed model, even a simple one served through an API or a small web application.
- **One project using real, local data:** for example Valencia's open data on mobility, air quality or bicycles, Spanish public datasets or agricultural data. Local relevance makes your project memorable in interviews.
- **Clear documentation:** a README explaining the problem, approach, results and limitations.
- **Evidence of collaboration:** group projects, open-source contributions or hackathons.

Hackathons and datathons organised by universities, companies and public bodies in Valencia are an excellent way to build portfolio projects and meet employers at the same time. Many graduates found their first role through contacts made at such events.

## Understanding Spanish Employment Contracts

Spanish labour law distinguishes between permanent contracts (contrato indefinido) and temporary contracts, which labour reforms in recent years have restricted. Internship contracts for recent graduates (contratos formativos) have specific rules on duration and pay. Many companies apply a collective agreement (convenio colectivo) for their sector, which sets minimum salaries, working hours and holidays. When you receive an offer, check the type of contract, the applicable collective agreement, the gross annual salary and how many payments it is divided into, the probation period and the working schedule. Don't hesitate to ask questions — understanding these elements helps you compare offers accurately.

## Research Careers Through VRAIN and ValgrAI

For graduates drawn to research, Valencia offers accessible paths. VRAIN at UPV brings together groups working on topics including machine learning, computer vision, natural language processing, speech technologies and intelligent systems. Research groups regularly hire research assistants and doctoral candidates funded by European, national and regional projects, and ValgrAI has offered scholarships and programmes to attract and train AI researchers in the region.

A research path in Valencia offers exposure to international collaborations, publications and advanced techniques. It can lead to an academic career or to industry roles where research experience is valued, such as speech, language or computer vision teams in tech companies. Before committing to a PhD, talk to current doctoral students in the group about supervision, funding stability and the typical careers of graduates.

## Industry Applications in the Region

The Valencian Community's economy offers AI applications beyond software:

**Logistics and the port.** The Port of Valencia is one of the busiest container ports in the Mediterranean. Data science supports terminal operations, container flow forecasting, truck scheduling and customs processes.

**Automotive.** Car manufacturing and a network of suppliers around Valencia use machine vision for quality inspection, predictive maintenance on production lines and supply chain optimisation.

**Retail and food.** Large retailers and food companies based in the region use data for demand forecasting, store and warehouse operations and pricing.

**Agriculture.** The region's citrus and horticulture sectors increasingly use satellite and drone imagery, sensors and ML for crop monitoring, irrigation optimisation and yield prediction.

**Tourism.** Valencia and the coast attract many visitors, and tourism companies use data for demand forecasting, pricing and personalisation.

**Ceramics and manufacturing.** The Castellón area has a major ceramics industry, where process optimisation and quality control benefit from data and AI.

## Remote Workers and the International Community

Valencia has attracted many international remote workers, including people using Spain's digital nomad visa, introduced under the Startups Law, which allows non-EU remote workers to live in Spain while working for foreign companies under certain conditions. This international community has enriched the city's tech scene with meetups, coworking spaces and networking events, many in English. For graduates, this community can be a source of mentors and contacts, and some remote workers eventually found local companies or hire locally.

At the same time, be realistic: working remotely for a foreign company as a first job, without in-person mentoring, is harder than joining a local team. For your first role, prioritise learning opportunities over location flexibility.

## Neighbourhoods and Getting Around

Valencia is flat and compact, with an extensive network of cycle lanes and the Turia Gardens — a long park built in the former riverbed — crossing the city. Metro, trams and buses connect the centre, the university campuses, the port and the beaches. UPV's main campus in Vera and the University of Valencia's campuses are in the north-east, while the Marina de Empresas is in the port area. Many young professionals live in neighbourhoods such as Benimaclet, Ruzafa or El Cabanyal, though rents in popular areas have risen significantly. Check commute times to your employer's exact address before choosing where to live.

## Your First Year in a Valencia AI Role

Focus your first year on engineering fundamentals, understanding the business domain, improving your Spanish and building relationships inside and outside your company. Ask for regular feedback, take ownership of small deliverables and document what you learn. These habits build the foundation for a strong AI career, whether you stay in Valencia or move on later.

## Interviews for Junior AI Roles in Valencia

Interview processes in Valencia vary by employer. Startups often combine a conversation with founders or the tech lead, a technical exercise — for example a short take-home task on a dataset — and a discussion of your projects. Larger companies and consultancies may add aptitude tests, group dynamics or several interview rounds. Interviews at local companies are usually conducted in Spanish, while international companies and some startups interview in English.

Prepare to explain your thesis and projects clearly: why you chose a method, how you evaluated it and what you would improve. Employers want to see reasoning, curiosity and the ability to learn, not encyclopaedic knowledge. Asking thoughtful questions about the team, the data and how models reach production also leaves a good impression and helps you assess whether the role offers the learning you need.

## A Note on Responsible AI

Spain has set up a national AI supervisory agency, AESIA, headquartered in A Coruña, in the context of the EU AI Act. Graduates who understand data protection, fairness and documentation requirements will find these skills increasingly valued by Valencia employers.

## Real example

### A UPV graduate who stayed in Valencia

A UPV graduate in computer engineering completed a master's thesis on speech recognition at a VRAIN research group. Many of her classmates moved to Madrid or abroad for higher salaries.

She considered her options: a research scholarship to start a PhD, an internship at an international company in Madrid and a junior ML engineer role at a Valencia startup working on voice interfaces for customer service. She chose the startup, which let her apply her thesis work directly, and negotiated a hybrid arrangement. Within two years, she became responsible for the speech pipeline. Her salary remained below what classmates earned in Madrid, but her living costs were lower and her quality of life higher. She stays in touch with her former research group and collaborates on a joint project — a connection that has helped both the startup and the lab.
