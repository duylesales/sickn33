Málaga has changed remarkably over the past two decades. The city on the Costa del Sol, long associated with beaches and tourism, has developed a technology sector anchored by a large technology park, a university with strong engineering programmes and a cybersecurity cluster with international visibility. Global companies have opened technology hubs in the city, and its climate, airport connections and quality of life attract engineers and remote workers from across Europe. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Málaga offer work in cybersecurity, software, telecommunications and digital services, with a lifestyle few European tech hubs can match. This guide explains the landscape honestly, including its limits.

## Cybersecurity: Málaga's Signature Sector

Málaga's cybersecurity profile has deep roots. VirusTotal, the online service for analysing suspicious files and URLs, was founded in Málaga and acquired by Google in 2012, and its team remained in the city. Building on that presence, Google opened a Safety Engineering Center focused on cybersecurity in Málaga, one of its centres dedicated to security engineering. Around these, cybersecurity companies, startups and training programmes have grown, and the University of Málaga has strong research and teaching in computer security.

AI plays an increasingly central role in cybersecurity: classifying malware, detecting malicious URLs and phishing, analysing threat intelligence, identifying anomalies in network traffic and using language models to support analysts. For ML engineers, Málaga offers exposure to security problems at global scale.

## The Andalusia Technology Park and Tech Hubs

The Andalusia Technology Park (PTA) in Campanillas, west of the city centre, is one of Spain's largest technology parks, hosting hundreds of companies in software, telecommunications, electronics, engineering and services. International technology and telecom companies have established hubs and competence centres in Málaga in recent years, attracted by talent, costs and the city's appeal to international employees. These hubs employ software engineers, data engineers and increasingly ML specialists.

The Polo Nacional de Contenidos Digitales, a national hub for digital content in Málaga, supports companies in video games, animation and digital media, where AI is used in content creation and analytics.

## The University of Málaga

The University of Málaga (UMA) has schools of telecommunications engineering, computer science and industrial engineering, with research groups in AI, machine learning, computer vision, robotics and cybersecurity. Its graduates form the core of the local tech workforce, and the university collaborates with companies at the technology park and in the city.

## Remote Work and the International Community

Málaga has become a popular base for international remote workers, including people using Spain's digital nomad visa, which allows non-EU citizens working for foreign companies to live in Spain under certain conditions. This community has added coworking spaces, meetups and networking events, many in English. For AI engineers, this can be both an opportunity — contacts, events, possible employers — and a consideration: some professionals in Málaga work for employers elsewhere, and local AI roles remain fewer than in Madrid or Barcelona.

## Spanish Employment and Tax

Spanish salaries are usually expressed as gross annual amounts, often in 12 or 14 payments. Permanent contracts are standard after recent labour reforms limited temporary contracts. Salaries in Málaga are generally lower than in Madrid or Barcelona and well below Northern Europe, although international hubs may pay more. Spain's special tax regime for inbound workers, updated by the Startups Law, can reduce taxes for eligible newcomers for several years; check eligibility with a tax adviser.

## Language

Spanish is essential for daily life and for most local employers. International hubs and many startups work in English, and Málaga's international community is large, but Spanish significantly broadens opportunities and helps with integration.

## Permits

EU citizens can work freely and must register as residents. Non-EU professionals can use the highly qualified professional permit, the EU Blue Card or, if working for foreign employers, the digital nomad visa. Check official Spanish sources for current rules.

## Living in Málaga

Málaga offers sunshine for most of the year, beaches, a revitalised historic centre with museums and an international airport with connections across Europe. Housing costs have risen sharply, driven by tourism, international demand and short-term rentals, and finding long-term rentals can be difficult. The technology park lies west of the city, so check commuting options by bus, commuter train or car. Many people live in neighbourhoods near the centre or in towns along the coast.

## Machine Learning for Security at Scale

Security services that analyse files and URLs from around the world process enormous volumes of data. Machine learning helps classify samples, cluster related threats, identify new malware families and prioritise what human analysts should examine. Detecting phishing websites and scam campaigns increasingly relies on combinations of text, image and network features, and language models help summarise and correlate threat reports. The adversarial nature of the field means models must be continuously updated and evaluated against evolving attacks. Engineers working in this area in Málaga gain experience that is valued across security, fraud prevention and trust and safety roles.

## Tech Hubs: Evaluating the Role

Many AI jobs in Málaga are in hubs of international companies. As with any satellite office, ask what the Málaga team owns: complete products, services or research areas, or tasks defined elsewhere? Where are managers and architects located? Can you grow into senior technical or leadership roles locally? How stable is the hub's mandate? Hubs that own significant work usually offer more interesting roles and better career prospects. Talking to current employees at meetups is the best way to learn how a hub really works.

## Tourism, Hospitality and Data

Tourism remains central to the Costa del Sol economy. Hotels, travel companies, airlines and tourism platforms use data science for demand forecasting, dynamic pricing, personalisation and operations planning. Public authorities also analyse tourism flows to manage their impact on the city. These roles combine forecasting and optimisation with domain knowledge of travel, and they are among the most accessible data roles in the region.

## Building a Network

Málaga's tech community is active and welcoming, with meetups on cybersecurity, data science, Python and startups, tech conferences and events at the technology park and the university. The international remote-work community adds informal networking. Regular participation helps newcomers learn which employers are growing and meet potential colleagues.

## Questions to Ask Málaga Employers

- What does the Málaga team own, and where are decisions made?
- What is the working language, and is Spanish needed for any part of the role?
- Is the office in the city centre or at the technology park, and what are the commuting options?
- What relocation support is offered, including temporary housing?
- Does the company support applications for the inbound workers' tax regime?
- How many office days are expected?

## Settling In

Newcomers need a foreigner identification number (NIE), registration at the town hall (empadronamiento), registration with social security and a Spanish bank account. Appointments can be difficult to obtain, so start early.

## Skills That Stand Out

Málaga employers value security-aware ML engineers, strong software engineering, cloud and data engineering skills and the ability to work in international, English-speaking teams. Specialisations in malware analysis, anomaly detection, NLP for threat intelligence or large-scale data pipelines are particularly relevant. For telecom and electronics companies, signal processing and embedded skills are valued. Spanish communication skills help significantly with career progression outside international hubs.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare net pay, the number of salary payments, benefits and the possible effect of the inbound workers' tax regime, then subtract realistic housing costs. A lower gross salary than in Northern Europe can still provide a comparable standard of living, but only if housing is secured at a reasonable price.

## Beyond Málaga: Andalusia's Other Tech Cities

Seville, Granada and Córdoba also have universities and technology companies. Granada's university has well-known computer science and AI research, and Seville hosts aerospace activities and a growing digital sector. Some employers operate across Andalusian cities, and high-speed trains connect Málaga with Córdoba, Seville and Madrid.

## Who Málaga Suits Best

Málaga suits engineers interested in cybersecurity or international tech hubs who value climate and lifestyle highly, speak or will learn Spanish and are prepared for a smaller job market and a tight housing situation.

## Responsible AI and Security

Security products process sensitive data from users worldwide, so privacy, data minimisation and transparency are essential. The EU AI Act requires robustness and cybersecurity for high-risk AI systems, and Spain's national AI supervisory agency, AESIA, oversees implementation. Engineers who understand both security and responsible AI principles are particularly valuable in Málaga's cybersecurity cluster, where these topics meet every day.

## Telecommunications and Electronics

Málaga's technology park has a long history in telecommunications and electronics, and several companies in the sector run engineering and innovation centres in the area. AI work in telecom includes optimising mobile networks, predicting equipment failures, automating network operations, detecting fraud and analysing customer behaviour. In electronics and embedded systems, engineers work on edge AI, sensor data processing and testing. These roles combine ML with systems knowledge and offer stable employers with international connections. The University of Málaga's telecommunications engineering school supplies graduates with strong signal processing and networking foundations, which makes the city a good place for engineers who want to work where AI meets communications infrastructure.

## Startups and Digital Content

Málaga's startup ecosystem includes companies in cybersecurity, travel technology, proptech, fintech and digital content. The national digital content hub supports game and animation companies that increasingly use AI for content creation tools, analytics and personalisation. Startups offer broad scope and ownership, often in English-speaking teams, but with the usual uncertainty about funding. Before joining, ask about runway, business model and technical leadership.

## Remote Work: A Practical Note

If you plan to live in Málaga while working remotely for an employer in another country, clarify in advance which country's employment law, tax and social security rules apply, whether the employer uses a local entity or employer-of-record service and how often travel is expected. These arrangements affect net income and protections, and getting them right from the start avoids surprises later.
