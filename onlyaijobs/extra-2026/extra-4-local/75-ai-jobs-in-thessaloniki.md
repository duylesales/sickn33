Thessaloniki, Greece's second-largest city, is a lively university city on the Thermaic Gulf with a large student population and a long history as a crossroads of cultures. In recent years, it has attracted international companies that opened digital and technology hubs, adding to a research base anchored by Greece's largest university and one of its most important research centres. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Thessaloniki offer a realistic entry into data science and machine learning — often in English-speaking teams — without moving to Athens or abroad. This guide explains where to look and how to get started.

## Universities

**Aristotle University of Thessaloniki (AUTh)** is Greece's largest university, with schools of electrical and computer engineering, informatics, mathematics and physics, and research in machine learning, computer vision, signal processing, NLP and robotics. Its graduates form the core of the local tech workforce.

**University of Macedonia** focuses on economics, business and applied informatics, with programmes in data science and business analytics.

**International Hellenic University** offers programmes in engineering and information technology, including English-taught master's programmes.

## CERTH: Applied Research in AI

The Centre for Research and Technology Hellas (CERTH), headquartered in Thessaloniki, is one of Greece's leading research centres. Its Information Technologies Institute (ITI) works on AI, multimedia analysis, computer vision, robotics, smart cities, health and security, often through European research projects with international partners. For graduates, CERTH offers research assistant and research engineer positions, exposure to European projects and a strong network across academia and industry.

## International Digital Hubs

Several international companies have opened digital and technology centres in Thessaloniki. Pfizer's Center for Digital Innovation, opened in 2020, employs data scientists, software engineers and AI specialists working on digital solutions across the pharmaceutical value chain — from research and clinical trials to manufacturing and commercial operations. Other technology and services companies have established hubs in the city, drawn by university talent and lower costs than larger European cities.

## Software Companies and Startups

Thessaloniki's software companies and startups work on areas such as health tech, logistics, fintech, education and AI applications. The Thessaloniki Technology Park in Thermi and local accelerators support startups and research spin-offs. The city's port and logistics sector, along with manufacturing and agri-food in northern Greece, add data applications.

|---|---|---|
| Pharma and tech digital hubs | Junior data scientist, ML engineer, data engineer | Large-scale enterprise data, regulated environments |
| CERTH and universities | Research assistant, PhD candidate | European projects, research methods |
| Software companies | Junior developer, data scientist | Product development |

## Entry Paths

1. **Internships** at digital hubs and software companies, often during the final year of studies.
2. **Research positions** in European projects at CERTH or university labs.
3. **Graduate and junior programmes** at international hubs.
4. **Startup roles** for graduates who prefer broad responsibility.

## Pay and Cost of Living

Salaries in Greece are generally lower than in Western Europe, including for entry-level AI roles, though international hubs often pay above local averages. Thessaloniki's cost of living is lower than Athens', though rents have risen. Greece has offered a tax incentive for people who move their tax residence to Greece to work, relevant mainly for returnees and newcomers; check current rules.

## Language

Greek is the language of daily life and many local companies; English is the working language in international hubs, research projects and many software companies.

## Data Science in a Pharma Digital Hub

Pharmaceutical companies generate data across research, clinical trials, manufacturing, supply chain and commercial operations. Digital hubs build tools that help scientists analyse experiments, support clinical trial design and monitoring, optimise manufacturing and supply, and help commercial teams understand markets. Machine learning and language models are used to process scientific literature, documents and operational data. Because pharma is regulated, projects require careful validation, documentation and data protection, and AI tools that affect patients may fall under the EU Medical Device Regulation or the high-risk requirements of the EU AI Act. For graduates, working in such a hub offers exposure to enterprise-scale data, international teams and rigorous practices that are valued across industries.

## European Research Projects at CERTH

Much of CERTH's AI work takes place in European research projects that bring together universities, companies and public bodies from several countries. Topics include multimedia analysis and disinformation detection, computer vision for security and smart cities, robotics, digital health and energy. Graduates who join these projects work on real research questions, travel to project meetings, publish papers and build international networks. Positions are often linked to project funding and therefore fixed-term, but they can lead to PhDs, longer research careers or industry roles. The experience of working in multinational consortia is valuable for any career in European AI.

## Building a Profile for Thessaloniki Employers

- **Solid fundamentals** in Python, statistics and machine learning.
- **A thesis with real-world relevance**, ideally with CERTH, a university lab or a company.
- **Engineering habits**: version control, clean code, basic cloud skills.
- **English communication** for international teams.
- **Domain curiosity**, for example in health, logistics or smart cities.

Hackathons, student associations and research projects are effective ways to build a portfolio and meet employers.

## Logistics, Port and Industry

Thessaloniki's port is a gateway to the Balkans, and logistics companies, manufacturers and agri-food producers in northern Greece use data for forecasting, planning and quality. These roles are accessible for graduates and offer practical experience.

## Questions to Ask Thessaloniki Employers

- What will I work on in my first six months, and who will mentor me?
- What is the working language of the team?
- Is the position linked to project funding, and how long is the contract?
- What training and career development opportunities are offered?
- Is hybrid work possible?

## Living in Thessaloniki

Thessaloniki is known for its waterfront promenade, Byzantine monuments, food culture and vibrant nightlife driven by its student population. Public transport includes buses and a metro that opened in recent years, improving connections across the city. Housing is more affordable than in Athens, though rents have risen. Beaches of Halkidiki and mountains such as Olympus are within a few hours.

## Networking

Thessaloniki has a growing tech community with meetups, hackathons and startup events, often hosted by universities, the technology park and digital hubs. Attending events regularly helps graduates discover internships and junior roles.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews for junior roles in Thessaloniki's digital hubs and software companies usually include technical questions on Python, SQL, statistics and machine learning, a case or take-home task and conversations about teamwork and motivation. Research positions focus on your thesis, understanding of methods and ability to work in project teams. Prepare by explaining your projects clearly in English, including limitations, and by asking thoughtful questions about mentoring and the team's work.

## Your First Two Years

Build solid foundations in engineering, data and communication, choose a domain to deepen — health, multimedia, logistics or finance — and learn how models reach production. Stay connected with your university and CERTH. These foundations prepare you for senior roles in Thessaloniki, Athens or abroad.

## Staying in Greece

Many Greek graduates have left the country for work in the past, but the growth of international hubs and research projects has made staying more realistic. A first job in Thessaloniki with strong mentoring and international exposure can be as valuable as a role abroad, with lower living costs and proximity to family. Later, you can compare concrete offers based on net pay, cost of living and career growth.

## A Simple Action Plan

Apply for two or three internships, contact a CERTH lab about thesis or research opportunities, attend local tech events and follow up personally with people you meet. In Thessaloniki's growing ecosystem, initiative is noticed.

## Multimedia Analysis and Disinformation Research

Thessaloniki's research community has a long track record in multimedia analysis: understanding images, video and audio automatically. Applications include searching large media archives, verifying the authenticity of images and videos, detecting manipulated content and deepfakes, and analysing how information spreads on social media. These topics have become central to European policy on disinformation and online platforms, including the Digital Services Act, and the EU AI Act introduces transparency obligations for certain AI-generated content. Graduates who work on such projects gain expertise in computer vision, NLP and network analysis, combined with an understanding of media, journalism and societal impact. Collaborations with news organisations, fact-checkers and platforms make the work concrete. For graduates interested in the social side of AI, this is one of the most meaningful research areas available in the city.

## Health and Smart City Applications

Research groups and companies in Thessaloniki also work on digital health — remote monitoring, clinical decision support and analysis of medical images — and on smart city applications such as traffic management, energy efficiency and environmental monitoring. These projects often involve public authorities and hospitals, so they require careful attention to data protection and responsible use. They offer graduates the chance to see AI applied to problems that affect citizens directly.

## Software Companies, Startups and Remote Work

Thessaloniki's software companies build products and services for clients in Greece and abroad, from enterprise software and e-commerce platforms to health and logistics applications. Startups, some spun out of university and CERTH research, work on AI applications, IoT and digital services, supported by accelerators and the technology park. For graduates, these companies offer broad responsibility and fast learning; startups in particular let you see the whole product, from data to deployment. Some Thessaloniki engineers also work remotely for companies elsewhere in Europe. Remote roles can raise income, but for a first job, in-person mentoring usually matters more. If you consider a remote arrangement, clarify employment status, tax and social security rules in advance, and stay active in the local tech community to keep building your network.

## Reading Graduate Offers

When you receive an offer, check the contract type, gross monthly salary and number of payments, benefits such as private health insurance and meal allowances, training opportunities and hybrid policy. Ask how juniors are mentored and how performance is evaluated. For a first role, learning and mentoring matter more than small salary differences, and a strong team is the best investment in your future.

## Responsible AI Skills

Graduates who understand GDPR, bias evaluation, documentation and the EU AI Act bring practical value to Thessaloniki's employers, especially in pharma, health, public-sector and media projects where AI decisions affect people directly. Adding a course on AI ethics or regulation to your studies is a simple way to stand out.

## Staying Connected

Keep in touch with your professors, CERTH colleagues and classmates after graduation; in Thessaloniki's close-knit tech scene, those relationships often lead to the next opportunity.

## Real example

### An AUTh graduate who stayed in Thessaloniki

An electrical and computer engineering graduate from AUTh worked on a master's thesis on medical image segmentation in collaboration with a CERTH lab. Most of her classmates planned to move to Athens or abroad.

She received offers from a European research project at CERTH and from an international digital hub in Thessaloniki working on data science for clinical operations. She chose the hub, attracted by the scale of its data and the structured training, and stayed close to family. After two years, she leads analyses for a global team and says she never expected to find such international work in her home city.
