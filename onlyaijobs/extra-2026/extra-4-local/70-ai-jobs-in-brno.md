Brno, the Czech Republic's second-largest city and the capital of South Moravia, is a young, affordable university city with a surprisingly strong technology scene. It hosts one of Europe's best-known speech research groups, a large open-source software engineering centre, world-leading electron microscope manufacturing, a well-known travel technology company, the national cybersecurity agency and a busy startup ecosystem. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Brno offer a realistic start in machine learning — often in English-speaking teams — at a cost of living well below Prague and Western Europe. This guide explains where to look and how to prepare.

## Universities and Research

**Brno University of Technology (BUT).** BUT's Faculty of Information Technology (FIT) is one of the leading computer science faculties in Central Europe. Its speech research group has an international reputation in speech recognition, speaker recognition and language identification, and has contributed to widely used research and tools. FIT also has groups in computer vision, graphics, NLP and security.

**Masaryk University (MU).** Masaryk University's Faculty of Informatics has research in AI, NLP, cybersecurity, bioinformatics and human–computer interaction. Its CERIT-SC centre provides computing and data services, and the university's cybersecurity team is well known in the Czech Republic.

**CEITEC.** The Central European Institute of Technology, a research centre shared by Brno's universities and institutes, works in life sciences and advanced materials, including structural biology and microscopy, where data science and image analysis are increasingly important.

## Where AI Jobs in Brno Are Found

**Open-source and enterprise software.** Red Hat has one of its largest engineering offices in Brno, working on Linux, cloud and open-source platforms, including infrastructure used for AI workloads. Engineers gain experience with large open-source projects and global collaboration.

**Travel technology.** Kiwi.com, the travel booking platform founded in Brno, uses data science for search, pricing, route combinations, fraud detection and customer service.

**Scientific instruments.** Thermo Fisher Scientific has major electron microscopy operations in Brno, a legacy of the city's long tradition in microscopy. Machine learning is used for image analysis, instrument automation and data processing in microscopy.

**Cybersecurity.** The National Cyber and Information Security Agency (NÚKIB) is headquartered in Brno, and security companies and university teams work in the city. AI in security includes threat detection and analysis of large security datasets.

**Software companies and startups.** Brno has many software companies and startups, supported by JIC, the South Moravian Innovation Centre, which runs incubation and acceleration programmes. Startups work on SaaS, health tech, AI applications and hardware.

**Industry and engineering.** Engineering and manufacturing companies in the region, including in automation and energy, use data science for quality and maintenance.

## Entry Paths

1. **Internships** at software companies and startups, often available during studies.
2. **Part-time work during studies**, common in Brno's tech sector.
3. **Thesis projects** with companies or research groups.
4. **PhD positions** at BUT, MU or CEITEC.
5. **Junior roles** in software, data or ML teams.

Brno's large companies run internship programmes that frequently lead to full-time offers, and many students start working part-time before graduating.

## Pay, Language and Permits

Salaries in Brno are generally somewhat lower than in Prague, but living costs — especially rent — are also lower. Salaries are paid in Czech koruna. Czech is the language of daily life, but English is the working language in many tech companies and research groups, and Brno has a large international community. EU citizens can work freely; non-EU graduates may use the Employee Card or the EU Blue Card, and Czech universities' graduates may have specific options. Check official sources.

## Living in Brno

Brno is compact and lively, with a historic centre, a castle on a hill, a large student population and a growing café and cultural scene. Trams and buses are efficient and cheap. Housing is more affordable than in Prague, though demand from students is high. Vienna is about 1.5 hours away by train, Prague around 2.5 hours and Bratislava about 1.5 hours, making Brno a convenient base in Central Europe.

## Speech Technology: A Brno Speciality

Speech technology covers automatic speech recognition, speaker recognition and diarisation, language identification, keyword spotting and speech synthesis. Brno's research group has been active in international evaluations and in developing methods and tools used by researchers worldwide. Students who join such a group gain experience with large datasets, deep learning for audio, careful evaluation and international collaboration. These skills are valued by companies building voice assistants, call-centre analytics, accessibility tools and security applications, as well as by research labs. Speaker and voice technologies also raise privacy and ethical questions, and the EU AI Act restricts certain uses of biometric identification, so understanding the regulatory context is part of working responsibly in the field.

## Open-Source Engineering and AI Infrastructure

Working at a large open-source engineering centre gives graduates experience with software used by millions of organisations: operating systems, containers, Kubernetes platforms and tools for running AI workloads in hybrid clouds. Engineers contribute to upstream projects in public, collaborate with developers worldwide and learn rigorous code review and testing practices. For graduates interested in MLOps and AI infrastructure, this is an excellent foundation: the skills needed to deploy and operate models reliably are rooted in exactly this kind of systems engineering.

## Microscopy and Scientific Imaging

Brno's microscopy industry dates back decades and today includes some of the world's leading electron microscope development. Modern microscopes produce enormous image datasets, and machine learning helps with denoising, segmentation, particle picking in structural biology, automated acquisition and defect analysis in semiconductor inspection. Graduates with backgrounds in computer vision, physics or bioinformatics can find roles at instrument makers and at research centres such as CEITEC, where AI supports cryo-electron microscopy and other advanced imaging.

## Travel Technology and Product Data Science

Travel booking platforms solve complex problems: combining flights, trains and buses into itineraries, predicting prices and delays, detecting fraud and handling customer service at scale. Data scientists work on search ranking, pricing, forecasting and experimentation. For graduates, product data science roles offer fast feedback, large datasets and an international environment.

## Building a Profile for Brno Employers

- **Solid programming**: Python, and C or C++ for systems and instrument roles.
- **Linux and open-source experience**: contributions to open-source projects are valued, especially at software companies.
- **A thesis or research project** in speech, vision, NLP or security.
- **Engineering habits**: version control, testing and documentation.
- **English communication**, since many teams are international.

Hackathons, student research programmes and open-source contributions are effective ways to stand out.

## Questions to Ask Brno Employers

- What will I work on in my first months, and who will mentor me?
- What is the working language of the team?
- Is the role full-time or compatible with finishing my studies?
- How are interns and juniors evaluated and promoted?
- What training and conference opportunities are offered?

## Networking and Startups

JIC organises events, incubation programmes and startup competitions, and Brno has regular meetups on Python, machine learning, security and open source. Large companies host tech talks open to students. The Brno tech community is compact and welcoming, and students who attend regularly quickly meet engineers and founders who can help them find internships.

## Your First Two Years

Build strong foundations: good engineering practices, experience with production systems and depth in one area such as speech, vision, infrastructure or security. Stay connected with your university and research groups. These foundations prepare you for senior roles in Brno, Prague, Vienna or elsewhere in Europe.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews for junior roles in Brno usually include a technical screen with programming tasks, questions on algorithms and data structures, ML fundamentals and a discussion of your projects or thesis. Software companies may include Linux, networking or systems questions; research groups focus on your understanding of methods and your ability to work independently; product companies may give a small data analysis task. Prepare by revisiting your projects in depth and practising explaining them clearly in English. Interviewers appreciate honest answers about what you don't know, combined with curiosity and a clear learning plan. Asking thoughtful questions about the team's projects, mentoring and code review culture helps you judge whether the role offers the learning you need.

## Cybersecurity and Public Sector Roles

With the national cybersecurity agency and university security teams in the city, Brno offers graduates paths into security analytics, threat intelligence and incident response. Public-sector security roles may require Czech citizenship or security clearance; private security companies and university teams are generally more open. AI skills are increasingly valued for analysing large security datasets and automating detection.

## Reading Graduate Offers

When comparing offers, check the contract type, gross monthly salary in koruna, meal vouchers, extra holiday, training budgets, hybrid policy and the support offered for finishing studies if you are still a student. For your first role, mentoring and learning matter more than small salary differences.

## A Simple Action Plan

Choose two research groups and three companies that interest you, apply for internships or student research positions, attend JIC and meetup events and follow up personally. In Brno's close-knit tech community, genuine interest and persistence are noticed quickly.

## Brno and Vienna: A Cross-Border Option

Vienna is only about an hour and a half from Brno by train, and some professionals combine Brno's lower living costs with opportunities in the Austrian capital, either by commuting occasionally in hybrid roles or by moving later in their careers. Bratislava is similarly close. For graduates, this proximity means that a first job in Brno does not limit future options: experience gained in Brno's research groups and tech companies is well understood by employers across Central Europe. Cross-border arrangements, however, involve questions of tax residence, social security and health insurance, which should be clarified before accepting a role in another country.

## Life Sciences and Bioinformatics

CEITEC and Masaryk University's life science faculties generate large biological datasets, from genomics to structural biology. Bioinformatics and computational biology groups apply machine learning to analyse these data, and graduates with programming skills and an interest in biology can find research assistant and PhD positions. Biotech and diagnostics companies in the region add industry roles. This path suits graduates who want their ML skills to contribute to medicine and fundamental science.

## A Final Tip

Contribute to one open-source project or research tool used in Brno before graduating; it is one of the most visible signals you can send to local employers.

## Real example

### A BUT graduate who started in speech research

A master's student at BUT's Faculty of Information Technology joined the speech research group as a student researcher, working on speaker recognition. She presented her thesis at an international workshop, which impressed a speech technology company.

After graduation, she was offered both a PhD position in the group and a junior ML engineer role at a company building voice products. She chose the PhD, keeping close contact with industry through joint projects. Three years later, she has publications, industry experience and several job offers across Europe — a path she says Brno's research strength made possible.
