Finland has long had an outsized influence on technology for a country of about five and a half million people. Nokia shaped the mobile era, Finnish game studios built global hits, and the country invested early in AI literacy — the free online course Elements of AI, created by the University of Helsinki and Reaktor, has been taken by a large number of people worldwide. Helsinki and neighbouring Espoo form the core of Finland's AI ecosystem, with strong universities, a national AI research centre, access to one of Europe's most powerful supercomputers and a pragmatic, trust-based working culture. This guide explains where AI jobs in Helsinki are found, what Finnish employers expect and what life in the capital region involves.

## The Research Base

**University of Helsinki.** Finland's largest university has a strong computer science department with research in machine learning, natural language processing and bioinformatics, as well as data science programmes.

**Aalto University.** Located in Otaniemi, Espoo, Aalto combines technology, business and arts. Its computer science department is one of the leading centres for machine learning research in the Nordic countries.

**Finnish Center for Artificial Intelligence (FCAI).** FCAI is a research centre initiated by Aalto University, the University of Helsinki and VTT Technical Research Centre of Finland. It focuses on AI research, including probabilistic machine learning, simulation-based inference and human-AI collaboration, and works with industry and society.

**LUMI and CSC.** The LUMI supercomputer, located in Kajaani in eastern Finland and operated by CSC – IT Center for Science as part of the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking with a consortium of countries, is one of Europe's most powerful supercomputers. It has been used to train large language models, including models for European languages. CSC, based in Espoo, supports researchers and companies with computing and data services.

Its history illustrates the depth of AI expertise in the region: a large team of AI scientists and engineers building models and AI solutions for industrial clients and working on open European language models.

## What Finnish Employers Expect

- **Independence and responsibility:** Finnish workplaces are flat, and employees are trusted to organise their work.
- **Directness and modesty:** communication is concise and honest; exaggeration is not appreciated.
- **Solid engineering:** clean, maintainable code and reliable systems are valued over flashy prototypes.
- **Collaboration:** teams work closely and value reliability — doing what you said you would do.
- **English:** most tech companies and research groups work in English; Finnish helps in daily life and in public sector roles.

## Working Conditions and Pay

Finnish employment is shaped by collective agreements and a strong emphasis on work-life balance. Standard working hours are moderate, and taking holidays — including a long summer break, often in July — is normal. Parental leave is generous for both parents. Salaries for experienced engineers are solid by European standards, though often lower than in Switzerland, Ireland or top-tier big-tech offices, and Finland has progressive income tax. Many employers offer lunch benefits, sports and culture benefits and occupational healthcare, which is common and valued in Finland.

## Permits

EU citizens can work in Finland and register their right of residence. Non-EU specialists typically apply for a specialist residence permit or the EU Blue Card, and Finland has offered a fast-track service for specialists and startup entrepreneurs with shorter processing times. Work in Finland, the national talent attraction service, provides guidance. Requirements change; check the Finnish Immigration Service's official information.

## Living in the Helsinki Capital Region

The capital region includes Helsinki, Espoo, Vantaa and Kauniainen. It is connected by metro, commuter trains, trams and buses, and the new light rail line linking Espoo and Helsinki improves cross-city connections. Housing costs are moderate compared with many Western European capitals, although popular central neighbourhoods are more expensive. Winters are long and dark, and summers are bright with very long days. Nature is everywhere — forests, islands and the sea — and saunas are part of everyday life. Many international residents find Helsinki safe, well organised and family-friendly.

## Games and Machine Learning

Helsinki is one of Europe's leading game development hubs, with Supercell and many other studios. Games generate rich behavioural data, and ML supports many aspects of development and operation:

- **Player analytics:** understanding engagement, retention and churn, and the effects of game changes.
- **Live operations:** planning events and offers, and measuring their impact through experiments.
- **Matchmaking:** creating fair and enjoyable matches based on skill and behaviour.
- **Fraud and cheating detection:** identifying abusive behaviour and payment fraud.
- **Content and tools:** using generative models to support artists and designers, carefully and within the studio's creative direction.

Finnish game studios are known for small, autonomous teams with significant responsibility. Data scientists often work directly with game teams and influence design decisions. The culture values honest analysis — including reporting when a favourite feature does not work.

## Telecom and Networks

Nokia, headquartered in Espoo, is one of the world's leading suppliers of network infrastructure. AI in telecommunications includes optimising radio networks, predicting and detecting faults, automating network operations and research on future network generations, where AI is expected to be deeply integrated. Finland's universities, including the University of Oulu with its 6G research programme, contribute to this field. For engineers interested in systems, signal processing and large-scale infrastructure, telecom offers distinctive AI work.

## The Consultancy Tradition

Finland has a strong tradition of software consultancies — companies that build digital services and AI solutions for clients in industry, finance, retail and the public sector. Reaktor, which co-created Elements of AI, is one well-known example, and many other consultancies of different sizes operate in Helsinki. For ML engineers, consultancies offer varied projects, exposure to different industries and a culture that often emphasises craftsmanship and learning. Consultants frequently work in client teams, which builds communication skills and a broad network. The trade-off is less long-term ownership of a single product; many engineers move between consultancies and product companies during their careers.

## Language Models for Smaller Languages

Finland has been active in developing language technology for Finnish and other European languages. Finnish is a morphologically rich language with relatively little training data compared with English, which creates interesting research and engineering challenges. Researchers in Finland have trained open language models for Finnish and other European languages using the LUMI supercomputer. For engineers interested in multilingual NLP, tokenisation, data curation and evaluation for smaller languages, Helsinki is one of the most relevant places in Europe.

## Settling In and Building a Network

Finns are often described as reserved at first, but friendly and reliable once relationships form. Newcomers can build networks through meetups on AI, data science and Python, university events, industry conferences and startup events such as Slush, the large startup event held in Helsinki. Hobby groups, sports clubs and language courses help with social life. Many employers support Finnish language learning, and even basic Finnish or Swedish — Finland's second official language — makes daily life easier. International House Helsinki offers advice and services for newcomers in one place, which simplifies registration and other practical steps in the first weeks.

## Deep Tech, Space and Health

Beyond software, the Helsinki region has a notable deep-tech sector. ICEYE, based in Espoo, builds and operates synthetic-aperture radar satellites, which can image the Earth's surface through clouds and at night. Data from such satellites is used for flood monitoring, maritime surveillance, insurance and disaster response, and ML plays an important role in interpreting it. Other deep-tech companies work on quantum computing, sensors, batteries and materials, often spun out of Aalto University or VTT. Health technology is another strength, supported by Finland's comprehensive health registers and research tradition, with ML applied to medical imaging, diagnostics and health data analysis under strict data protection rules. Finland has a specific legal framework for the secondary use of health and social data, which enables research while protecting privacy.

## Industry in the Finnish Economy

Finland's economy includes large industrial companies in forestry, paper and packaging, machinery, metals, energy and marine technology, many with headquarters or major units in the capital region. These companies apply AI to process optimisation, predictive maintenance, quality control, energy efficiency and supply chains. Industrial AI roles offer the chance to work on physical processes with measurable impact on efficiency and emissions. Many engineers find these problems as intellectually demanding as consumer tech, with the added satisfaction of tangible results in factories, mills and power plants.

## Questions to Ask Helsinki Employers

- Is the team's working language English in practice, including informal communication?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what benefits are included?
- How much autonomy does the team have in choosing methods and tools?
- Do you use LUMI or other large computing resources, and how is access organised?
- What support do you offer for relocation, housing and Finnish language learning?

## Beyond the Capital: Tampere, Oulu and Turku

Finland's AI ecosystem extends beyond Helsinki. Tampere has a technical university and companies in industrial automation and machinery. Oulu, in the north, has a long history in telecommunications and electronics, with strong wireless and 6G research at the University of Oulu and a notable health technology scene. Turku, on the south-west coast, has universities, marine industry and life sciences. These cities offer lower living costs and close-knit communities, and some employers have offices in several Finnish cities, allowing hybrid arrangements. For engineers who value nature and space even more than the capital offers, they are worth considering alongside AI jobs in Helsinki.

## Preparing for Finnish Winters

Plan for the dark months: a daylight lamp, regular outdoor exercise, social routines and a holiday in the darkest period help many newcomers enjoy their first Finnish winter rather than endure it.

## Real example

### A research engineer choosing between Helsinki and Amsterdam

A research engineer specialising in probabilistic machine learning finishes a postdoc in Germany and receives two offers: one from a Helsinki AI company working with FCAI researchers on simulation-based inference for industrial clients, and one from an Amsterdam fintech.

The Amsterdam offer pays more in gross terms, but the Helsinki role matches her research interests closely and offers a flat, autonomous team. She compares net pay after tax and housing costs, looks at childcare options and parental leave, and considers the Finnish winters honestly. She chooses Helsinki. After a year, she co-authors a paper with FCAI researchers while delivering models to clients — a combination she says would have been hard to find elsewhere.
