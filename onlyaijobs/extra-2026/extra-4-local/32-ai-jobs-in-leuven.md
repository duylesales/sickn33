Leuven is a compact Flemish university city 20 minutes by train from Brussels, but its influence on European technology is disproportionate to its size. KU Leuven is one of Europe's leading research universities, and imec, headquartered in Leuven, is one of the world's most important research centres for semiconductors and nanoelectronics. Around them, a steady stream of spin-offs and corporate R&D teams has formed a deep-tech ecosystem. For experienced machine learning engineers and researchers, AI jobs in Leuven combine research excellence with applications in chips, health, manufacturing and software — in a city where you can cycle to almost everything. This guide explains the landscape and the practical considerations of working in Belgium.

## KU Leuven and Leuven.AI

KU Leuven has a long tradition in artificial intelligence and machine learning research. Its computer science department includes the DTAI group (Declarative Languages and Artificial Intelligence), known for work on machine learning, constraint programming and probabilistic logic, and its electrical engineering department (ESAT) hosts research on computer vision, signal processing and data science. Leuven.AI, the university's AI institute, brings together researchers from many disciplines — engineering, medicine, law, humanities — to work on AI and its applications and societal impact.

The university also participates in the Flanders AI Research Program, funded by the Flemish government as part of its AI policy plan, which supports research across Flemish universities and research centres in areas such as decision-making, edge AI, human-AI collaboration and trustworthy AI, with an emphasis on applications that matter to Flemish industry.

## imec: Chips and AI Hardware

imec is a world-leading research and innovation hub in nanoelectronics and digital technologies, with its headquarters and large cleanroom facilities in Leuven. Semiconductor companies from around the world collaborate with imec on future chip technologies. For AI professionals, imec is relevant in several ways: research on hardware for AI, including energy-efficient computing and neuromorphic approaches; AI applied to semiconductor research and manufacturing, such as analysing process data and inspection images; and applications in health, sensors and smart systems. imec also has research groups at universities across Flanders, including in AI and data science, and has supported many spin-offs.

- **Industrial AI and digital twins** for manufacturing and simulation.

## Language and Culture

Dutch (Flemish) is the official language in Leuven, but the university, imec and many companies are highly international and use English as a working language. In daily life, English is widely understood, but learning Dutch helps with integration, especially if you plan to stay. Belgian work culture tends to be pragmatic and consensus-oriented, with good work-life balance.

## Employment, Taxes and Permits

Belgian salaries are paid monthly, with a holiday allowance and often a thirteenth month under collective agreements. Belgium has high personal income taxes and social contributions, but many employers offer tax-efficient benefits such as company cars, meal vouchers, hospitalisation insurance and group insurance pensions. Belgium introduced a reformed special tax regime for impatriate employees and researchers, which can reduce taxable income for eligible newcomers; eligibility criteria apply. Researchers at universities may be paid through scholarships or employment contracts with different tax treatment.

EU citizens can work freely and register with their municipality. Non-EU professionals can use the single permit or the EU Blue Card, and researchers have specific procedures. Rules are managed at regional level in some respects; check official Flemish and federal sources.

## Living in Leuven

Leuven is a lively student city with a historic centre, many cafés and a strong cycling culture. Most places are within cycling distance, and the train connects to Brussels in about 20 minutes and to Brussels Airport in about 15 minutes, which is ideal for international travel. Housing is in demand due to the large student population, and rents are relatively high for Belgium, but many professionals live in surrounding municipalities such as Heverlee, Kessel-Lo or Herent. Brussels, Antwerp and Ghent are within easy reach for weekends or alternative jobs.

## The Spin-off Culture

Leuven is one of Europe's most successful university cities at turning research into companies. KU Leuven's technology transfer office has supported many spin-offs across medical technology, software, materials, engineering and biotechnology, and imec has spun off companies in chips, sensors and imaging. This creates a steady supply of deep-tech startups that need ML engineers and data scientists.

Working at a Leuven spin-off offers a distinctive experience: close contact with the founding researchers, cutting-edge methods and a strong focus on turning research into reliable products. The challenges are typical of deep-tech startups — long development cycles, regulatory hurdles in medical devices and dependence on funding. Before joining, ask about the funding situation, the regulatory path (for example CE marking for medical devices) and how the company balances research and product development.

## Research Careers in Leuven

For researchers, Leuven offers PhD positions (often fully funded and treated as employment or scholarships), postdoctoral positions and research engineer roles. Funding comes from the Research Foundation – Flanders (FWO), the Flanders AI Research Program, European programmes and industry collaborations. KU Leuven's research groups are international, and English is the working language in most of them. Research careers follow the usual structure of fixed-term positions early on, with competitive tenure-track opportunities later. Many researchers move to imec, spin-offs or corporate R&D after their PhD, taking advantage of the ecosystem's short distances between academia and industry.

## Medical AI in Leuven

Leuven's biomedical research is among the strongest in Europe. University Hospitals Leuven (UZ Leuven) is one of Belgium's largest hospitals, and KU Leuven's biomedical sciences group conducts research across medicine and life sciences. Combined with imec's work on sensors and imaging and the university's AI expertise, this creates a rich environment for medical AI: imaging analysis, digital pathology, wearable sensors, clinical decision support and drug discovery. Medical AI work requires close collaboration with clinicians, rigorous validation and compliance with the EU Medical Device Regulation and, for many AI systems, the high-risk requirements of the EU AI Act. For engineers who want their work to reach patients, Leuven is one of the most interesting places in Europe.

## Industrial AI and Simulation

Leuven also has a strong tradition in engineering simulation and testing, and companies in the region develop software for product design, simulation and testing used by manufacturers worldwide. AI is increasingly integrated into these tools: surrogate models that approximate expensive simulations, anomaly detection in test data, generative design and digital twins that mirror physical systems. Additive manufacturing (3D printing) is another Leuven strength, where AI supports design optimisation, process monitoring and quality control. Engineers with backgrounds in mechanical engineering, physics or numerical methods and ML skills are in demand in this niche.

## Brussels as a Neighbour

Leuven's proximity to Brussels widens the job market considerably. Brussels hosts EU institutions, including the AI Office, consultancies, international companies, startups and industry associations. Many people live in Leuven and commute to Brussels, or vice versa. Antwerp and Ghent, with their own universities and industries, are also within commuting distance. For engineers who want both research-oriented roles and policy or corporate options, the Leuven–Brussels axis offers unusual variety within a small area.

## Questions to Ask Leuven Employers

- Is the position an employment contract or a scholarship, and how does that affect tax, pension and social security?
- For spin-offs: what is the funding runway, and what regulatory milestones lie ahead?
- What is the team's working language, and is Dutch expected for any stakeholders?
- Which benefits are included — company car or mobility budget, meal vouchers, hospitalisation insurance, group insurance?
- Does the employer support applications for the impatriate tax regime?
- How are research results transferred into products, and who owns that process?

## Mobility and Benefits in Belgium

Belgian compensation packages often include elements that are less common elsewhere. Company cars have long been a popular benefit because of their tax treatment, although policies have shifted toward electric vehicles and mobility budgets that can also be spent on public transport, bicycles or housing closer to work. Meal vouchers, eco vouchers, hospitalisation insurance and group insurance pensions are widespread. When comparing a Leuven offer with one from another country, add up the value of these benefits alongside net salary; they can make a meaningful difference. In Leuven specifically, where cycling and trains cover most needs, a mobility budget may be more useful than a car.

## Settling In

After arriving, register at the Leuven city hall to obtain a residence card, arrange health insurance through a Belgian health fund (mutualiteit) and open a bank account. KU Leuven and imec offer support services for international staff, including housing advice and help with administrative procedures. Dutch language courses are widely available in Leuven, including through the university's language institute, and even basic Dutch makes everyday life easier.

## Networking in Leuven

Leuven.AI events, imec technology forums, KU Leuven seminars and local meetups on data science and machine learning bring researchers, founders and engineers together. In a small city, attending a few events regularly quickly builds a network that opens doors across research and industry.

## Who Leuven Suits Best

Leuven suits engineers who value research depth, deep-tech products and a compact, bike-friendly city with fast access to Brussels and international travel.

## Real example

### A computer vision engineer moving from Eindhoven to Leuven

A computer vision engineer working in the Eindhoven high-tech sector is offered a position at a Leuven spin-off developing AI-based analysis of medical images. The spin-off was founded by KU Leuven researchers and collaborates closely with University Hospitals Leuven.

He compares net pay, including the Belgian company car and meal voucher benefits and the potential impatriate tax regime, with his Dutch salary. He also considers the commute: Leuven is about 1.5 hours from Eindhoven by car, so he decides to move. He appreciates the combination of research depth and product focus, and the proximity to Brussels Airport for conferences. After a year, he describes Leuven as a place where academic research and startups mix naturally, which makes the work intellectually rich.
