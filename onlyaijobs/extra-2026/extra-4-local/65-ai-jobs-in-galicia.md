Galicia, in Spain's green and Atlantic north-west, is not usually the first region that comes to mind for AI careers. Yet it combines the headquarters of one of the world's largest fashion retailers, the seat of Spain's national AI supervisory agency, significant automotive and maritime industries, a supercomputing centre and several respected AI research centres. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Galicia span retail and supply chain analytics, AI governance, industrial AI, language technology and research — in cities with a lower cost of living than Madrid or Barcelona and a strong quality of life. This guide explains the landscape and what to consider.

## Fashion Retail at Global Scale

Inditex, the fashion group behind Zara and other brands, has its headquarters in Arteixo near A Coruña. Its business model — rapid design, production and distribution cycles serving stores and online customers worldwide — depends heavily on data. Technology and data teams work on demand forecasting, inventory allocation, logistics, e-commerce personalisation, search, pricing and supply chain traceability. The group's presence has also stimulated a local ecosystem of technology suppliers and startups. For ML engineers, it offers retail and supply chain problems at a scale few companies anywhere can match.

## Spain's AI Supervisory Agency

A Coruña is the headquarters of the Spanish Agency for the Supervision of Artificial Intelligence (AESIA), established as one of the first dedicated national AI supervisory bodies in the EU in the context of the EU AI Act. AESIA's work includes supervising AI systems, supporting implementation of the AI Act and promoting trustworthy AI. For professionals interested in AI governance, the agency's presence creates opportunities — both within public institutions, subject to public-sector recruitment rules, and in companies, consultancies and research groups working on compliance and trustworthy AI.

## Automotive, Maritime and Industry

Vigo, Galicia's largest city, hosts a major automotive plant of Stellantis and a network of component suppliers, as well as one of Europe's leading fishing ports and shipbuilding and maritime industries. AI applications include manufacturing quality control, predictive maintenance, logistics, fishing fleet optimisation, seafood processing and maritime monitoring. Gradiant, a technology centre in Vigo, works on AI, security, communications and connected systems with industry.

## Research Centres and Supercomputing

**CiTIUS** at the University of Santiago de Compostela is a research centre in intelligent technologies, with groups in machine learning, NLP, computer vision and explainable AI. Santiago's researchers have been active in language technology, including for Galician.

**CITIC** at the University of A Coruña is a research centre in information and communication technologies, with work on AI, data science and software.

**atlanTTic** at the University of Vigo focuses on telecommunications technologies, including signal processing, communications and AI.

**CESGA**, the Galicia Supercomputing Center in Santiago, provides high-performance computing resources and has invested in quantum computing.

## Language

Spanish and Galician are both official in Galicia. Spanish is spoken by everyone and is essential for most workplaces; Galician is widely used in public administration, education and daily life, and may be required for some public-sector positions. Large companies' technology teams and research groups often work partly in English, but Spanish is necessary for daily life and most careers in the region.

## Pay and Cost of Living

Salaries in Galicia are generally lower than in Madrid or Barcelona and well below Northern European levels, although large international employers may pay more. The cost of living, particularly housing, is also lower than in Spain's largest cities. Spain's special tax regime for inbound workers may apply to eligible newcomers; check with a tax adviser.

## Permits

EU citizens can work freely and register as residents. Non-EU professionals can use the highly qualified professional permit or the EU Blue Card. Check official Spanish sources.

## Living in Galicia

Galicia's cities are compact and walkable. A Coruña is a coastal city with a long seafront promenade; Santiago de Compostela, the end point of the Camino de Santiago, is a historic university city; Vigo is an industrial port city with beaches and islands nearby. The climate is mild and rainy, the landscape green and the seafood famous. High-speed trains connect the Galician cities with each other and with Madrid.

## Retail and Supply Chain AI in Depth

Fast-fashion retail creates unusual data science problems. Collections change frequently, many products are new without sales history, and decisions about how much to produce and where to send it must be made quickly. Forecasting uses product attributes, early sales signals and similarities to past items. Allocation models decide how many units of each size and colour go to each store and warehouse. Replenishment responds to actual sales. Returns, especially online, must be predicted and handled efficiently. Sustainability and traceability requirements are growing, including emerging EU rules on product information and textile waste. ML engineers working on these problems balance accuracy, speed and business constraints at massive scale, and their decisions affect revenue, customer satisfaction and environmental impact directly.

## Careers in AI Governance

The presence of Spain's AI supervisory agency makes Galicia a natural place for professionals interested in AI governance. Implementing the EU AI Act involves defining how high-risk AI systems are assessed, supervising compliance, running regulatory sandboxes, providing guidance to organisations and cooperating with other national authorities and the EU AI Office. Technical experts who understand model evaluation, robustness, data governance and documentation are needed alongside legal and policy specialists. Beyond the agency itself, consultancies, companies and research groups in the region may develop compliance and trustworthy AI expertise. Public-sector positions follow Spanish public recruitment rules, which can include competitive examinations; consultancies and companies recruit through normal processes.

## Language Technology and Explainable AI

Research groups in Santiago de Compostela have contributed to natural language processing, including resources and models for Galician, and to explainable AI, where the aim is to make AI decisions understandable to people. Explainability is increasingly important in regulated uses under the AI Act, and NLP for smaller languages is part of Europe's effort to ensure that AI works in all its languages. For engineers interested in these fields, CiTIUS and related groups offer PhD, postdoc and research engineer positions and collaborations with industry.

## Industrial AI in Vigo

Vigo's automotive plant and suppliers use data science for quality inspection, predictive maintenance, production planning and logistics. The maritime sector — fishing, seafood processing, shipbuilding and port operations — offers distinctive problems: optimising fishing fleet routes and fuel use, monitoring catches and compliance, predicting demand for seafood products and maintaining vessels and port equipment. Technology centres such as Gradiant work with industry on applied AI projects, and the University of Vigo's telecommunications research supports connected and intelligent systems. These roles usually require Spanish and suit engineers who enjoy practical industrial problems.

## Questions to Ask Galician Employers

- In which city and site will I work, and how many office days are expected?
- What is the working language of the team?
- For public institutions: what is the recruitment process, and are there language requirements?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what is the salary structure?
- Does the company support applications for the inbound workers' tax regime?

## Networking in Galicia

Galicia's AI community is smaller than those of Madrid or Barcelona but closely connected. The research centres in Santiago, A Coruña and Vigo organise seminars and events, and regional initiatives support AI adoption in industry. Tech meetups in the three cities bring together professionals from retail, industry and startups. Personal relationships matter in Galician professional culture, so attending events regularly and following up with people is valuable.

## Settling In

Newcomers need a foreigner identification number (NIE), registration at the town hall, social security registration and a bank account. Appointments can take time, so start early.

## Who Galicia Suits Best

Galicia suits engineers who want global-scale retail problems, interest in AI governance or industrial AI, a lower cost of living and a mild, green Atlantic environment, and who are willing to work in Spanish.

## Supercomputing and Quantum

CESGA in Santiago provides high-performance computing to researchers and companies and has invested in quantum computing infrastructure. For engineers interested in HPC, scientific computing or quantum technologies, CESGA and the research groups using it offer rare opportunities in north-western Spain, including projects that combine AI with large-scale simulation.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare net pay, the number of payments, benefits and possible tax advantages with realistic housing costs in your chosen city. Galicia's lower living costs can compensate for lower salaries, particularly for engineers relocating from expensive cities.

## Skills That Stand Out in Galicia

Employers in Galicia value engineers who combine solid ML with the domain knowledge of their sector. In retail, forecasting, optimisation, experimentation and large-scale data engineering are central; engineers who understand how allocation and replenishment decisions interact with logistics constraints are especially valued. In governance, knowledge of the EU AI Act, model evaluation, robustness testing and documentation practices is essential, together with the ability to write clearly for non-technical audiences. In industry, time series analysis, computer vision for inspection and deployment close to production lines matter most. Across sectors, Spanish communication skills are important, and knowledge of Galician is appreciated in public institutions and regional companies. Engineers who build one of these specialisations alongside strong fundamentals find Galicia a region where they can take on responsibility relatively quickly, because experienced AI professionals are scarcer than in Madrid or Barcelona.

## Startups and the Digital Ecosystem

Galicia's startup ecosystem includes companies in retail technology, health, maritime technology and software, often founded by engineers from the region's universities. Accelerators and public programmes support them. Startups offer broad scope and ownership, while the proximity of large employers creates potential customers and partners.

## Moving Between Galician Cities

A Coruña, Santiago and Vigo are connected by frequent trains and motorways, and travel between them takes well under two hours. Some professionals live in one city and work in another, or work hybrid for employers across the region. Searching by distance across all three cities, rather than limiting yourself to one, reveals a larger job market than any single city suggests.

## A Final Tip

Spend a week in Galicia before deciding; the rain, the seafood and the pace of life are part of the job offer too.

## Real example

### A supply chain data scientist choosing Galicia

A Spanish data scientist working on demand forecasting in London wants to return to Spain but prefers a smaller city to Madrid. He receives an offer from a fashion retail technology team in A Coruña, working on inventory allocation across stores and online channels.

The salary is lower than in London, but his rent in A Coruña is a fraction of what he paid. The scale of the data and the direct impact of allocation decisions on sales and waste make the role technically attractive. He accepts and learns some Galician phrases to connect with colleagues. After a year, he says the combination of global-scale problems and a relaxed coastal city is exactly what he was looking for.
