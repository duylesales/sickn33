Wallonia, the French-speaking southern region of Belgium, is often overlooked in discussions about European tech careers. Yet it combines strong universities, a regional AI programme, one of Europe's most important biopharma clusters, a notable space and aerospace sector and science parks that host startups and research centres. Its cities — Liège, Louvain-la-Neuve, Mons, Charleroi and Namur — are close to Brussels, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Germany and France. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Wallonia offer realistic entry points into machine learning, often with lower living costs than Brussels and a region eager to keep its talent. This guide explains where to look and how to get started.

## Universities and Research

**UCLouvain**, based in the purpose-built university town of Louvain-la-Neuve, has strong engineering, computer science and applied mathematics, with research in machine learning, optimisation, signal processing and AI ethics.

**University of Liège (ULiège)** has well-known research in machine learning, computer vision and biomedical engineering, a science park and close links with regional industry and hospitals.

**University of Mons (UMONS)** has research in AI, signal processing and human–computer interaction, and Mons hosts technology centres and a digital innovation campus.

**Université libre de Bruxelles (ULB)** also has campuses and activities in Wallonia, particularly in Charleroi's Biopark.

The universities collaborate in **TRAIL (Trusted AI Labs)**, a joint initiative of French-speaking Belgian universities and research centres focused on trustworthy AI, and the region's AI efforts are coordinated under Digital Wallonia's AI programme.

## Applied Research Centres

Wallonia has a network of accredited research centres that work closely with companies. Cenaero, in Gosselies near Charleroi, specialises in simulation and AI for engineering. Multitel, in Mons, works on signal processing, AI and telecommunications. Other centres focus on materials, agri-food and industrial technologies. Research engineers in these centres work on applied projects with regional companies, which is an excellent first role for graduates.

## Biotech and Pharma

Wallonia is home to one of Europe's strongest biopharma clusters. Major pharmaceutical and vaccine companies have large operations in the region — for example in the Walloon Brabant province — and the Biopark in Charleroi hosts biotech companies and research. Data science roles include manufacturing analytics, quality, clinical data, bioinformatics and supply chain, within regulated environments.

## Space and Aerospace

Liège and its region have a notable space sector, with companies and research centres in space instrumentation, satellite components and testing, including the Centre Spatial de Liège. The Charleroi area hosts aerospace companies working on engines and components. AI applications include analysis of test data, predictive maintenance and satellite data processing.

## Digital Companies and Science Parks

Science parks near the universities — in Louvain-la-Neuve, Liège, Mons and Gosselies — host software companies, startups and innovation centres. Digital companies in Wallonia work on SaaS, health tech, industrial software and AI applications. Liège's airport is also a major cargo hub, creating logistics data roles.

## Entry Paths

1. **Master's thesis with a company or research centre**, very common in Belgian engineering programmes.
2. **Internships** in companies and startups.
3. **Research engineer positions** at applied research centres.
4. **PhD positions** at universities, often funded by FNRS or regional programmes.
5. **Graduate programmes** at biopharma and larger companies.

## Language and Practicalities

French is the language of daily life and most workplaces in Wallonia. Research groups, biopharma companies and startups often use English as well. Belgian graduate packages typically include monthly salary, holiday pay, often a thirteenth month and benefits such as meal vouchers and insurance. Living costs are generally lower than in Brussels.

## Applied Research Centres as a First Job

Wallonia's accredited research centres play a special role: they help regional companies, especially SMEs, adopt new technologies. A research engineer might spend a year on several projects — predictive maintenance for a metal processing plant, computer vision for quality control at a food producer, simulation-based optimisation for an aerospace supplier — often working on-site with company engineers. For graduates, this offers rapid learning, exposure to many industries and a network across the region. Many engineers later join one of the companies they worked with, or move into R&D roles elsewhere, bringing practical experience that pure academic or large-company paths rarely provide. Regional funding programmes support these collaborations, making research centres stable employers for early-career engineers.

## Biopharma Data Roles

Biopharma companies in Wallonia manufacture vaccines, biologics and other medicines and run clinical and research activities. Data scientists support process analytics, quality control, supply chain planning, clinical data analysis and regulatory reporting. All of this happens under good manufacturing practice and strict data integrity requirements, so validation and documentation are part of daily work. For graduates, biopharma offers stable employment, structured training and experience valued across the life science industry.

## Space Technology in Liège

The Liège region has a long tradition in space technology, with the Centre Spatial de Liège testing space instruments and companies building optical systems, electronics and components for satellites and missions. Data science supports analysis of test campaigns, calibration of instruments and processing of observation data. The Charleroi region adds aerospace engine and component companies. These sectors require precision, documentation and patience, and they offer graduates unusual technical challenges.

## Trustworthy AI in Belgium

TRAIL brings together French-speaking Belgian universities and research centres to work on trustworthy AI: explainability, robustness, fairness, privacy and human oversight. With the EU AI Act setting requirements for high-risk systems, expertise in these topics is increasingly valued by companies and public institutions. Graduates who contribute to trustworthy AI research gain skills that are in demand across Europe.

## Building a Profile for Walloon Employers

- **Solid fundamentals** in programming, statistics and machine learning.
- **A thesis with real-world relevance**, ideally with a company or research centre.
- **French communication skills**, essential for most employers.
- **Domain curiosity**: industry, health, space or logistics.
- **Engineering habits**: version control, clean code, documentation.

## Questions to Ask Walloon Employers

- What will I work on in my first months, and who will mentor me?
- What is the working language of the team?
- Which joint committee applies, and what benefits are included?
- For research centres: how are projects funded, and how long are contracts?
- Is hybrid work possible, and where exactly is the site?

## Living in Wallonia

Wallonia offers green landscapes, historic cities and lower living costs than Brussels. Liège is a lively, working-class city with a strong cultural scene; Louvain-la-Neuve is a car-free university town; Mons and Namur are smaller and relaxed; Charleroi is being revitalised. Trains connect the cities with Brussels in about an hour or less, and Liège has fast rail links to Cologne and Aachen.

## Networking

Science parks, universities and Digital Wallonia organise events, meetups and hackathons. Attending regularly helps graduates discover opportunities in a region where many roles are filled through networks.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews for junior roles in Wallonia usually include a conversation about motivation and background, technical questions and a discussion of your thesis. Research centres may ask how you would approach a company's problem; biopharma employers focus on rigour and data integrity. Prepare to explain your work clearly in French and, where relevant, in English.

## Cross-Border Options

From Liège, Maastricht and Aachen are close; from southern Wallonia, Luxembourg is within commuting distance; and northern France is near Mons and Tournai. Cross-border work requires checking tax and social security rules, but it widens the job market considerably.

## A Simple Action Plan

Choose a thesis with a regional partner, attend science park events, apply to research centres and biopharma graduate programmes and follow up with people you meet.

## Logistics and the Liège Cargo Hub

Liège Airport is one of Europe's major cargo airports, and the surrounding region hosts logistics companies, e-commerce distribution centres and multimodal terminals connecting air, rail, road and waterways. Data science supports demand forecasting, capacity planning, warehouse optimisation, customs processing and route planning. The growth of e-commerce and international trade has increased the complexity of these operations, creating accessible roles for graduates who enjoy operational problems with immediate feedback. Logistics data science combines forecasting, optimisation and simulation, and it offers visible results: better planning reduces delays, costs and emissions. The skills are also highly transferable to logistics hubs across Europe, including those in the Netherlands and Germany, which makes a first role in Liège a good foundation for later moves.

## Industry and the Energy Transition

Wallonia has a long industrial history in steel, glass, chemicals and engineering, and many companies are modernising their processes and reducing emissions. Data science supports process optimisation, energy efficiency, predictive maintenance and quality control. Regional programmes encourage digital transformation in manufacturing, and research centres help companies adopt AI. Graduates who enjoy working close to physical processes find meaningful work in this sector.

## PhDs and Research Careers

Belgian PhD positions are usually funded by the FNRS (the French-speaking community's research fund), regional programmes or industry projects. They offer research experience, publications and international collaborations. Many PhD graduates in Wallonia move into research centres, biopharma or industry R&D, where their expertise is valued.

## Responsible AI

The EU AI Act, GDPR and sector-specific regulation shape how AI is used in biopharma, public services and industry. Graduates who understand documentation, validation and fairness — topics central to TRAIL's work — bring practical value to Walloon employers.

## Startups and Digital Wallonia

Wallonia's startup ecosystem is supported by incubators in the science parks, regional investment funds and Digital Wallonia's programmes. Startups work on health tech, industrial software, AI applications, agri-tech and cleantech. For graduates, startups offer broad responsibility and fast learning, and regional support schemes make it easier for young companies to hire their first engineers. Founders often come from the universities or research centres, so the ecosystem is closely connected to research.

## Reading Graduate Offers

Belgian offers include monthly pay, holiday pay, often a thirteenth month and benefits such as meal vouchers, hospitalisation insurance and group insurance pensions. Compare the total package, the joint committee that applies and the learning opportunities rather than gross salary alone.

## A Final Tip

Spend a day at a science park or research centre open day; meeting engineers in person is often the fastest route to a thesis or first job in Wallonia.

## Staying Connected

Keep in touch with your thesis supervisor, research centre colleagues and classmates; Wallonia's AI community is small and collaborative, and recommendations open many doors.

## Real example

### A UCLouvain graduate who joined a research centre

A computer science graduate from UCLouvain wrote her master's thesis on anomaly detection in industrial sensor data with a Walloon research centre. Many classmates moved to Brussels or abroad.

The research centre offered her a research engineer position working on AI projects for regional manufacturers, and a biopharma company offered a data analyst role. She chose the research centre for variety and learning. After two years, she has worked with five companies on predictive maintenance and quality projects and says the experience gave her a broad, practical foundation — while living affordably close to family.
