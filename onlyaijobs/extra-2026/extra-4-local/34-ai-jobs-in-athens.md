During the economic crisis of the 2010s, many young Greek engineers and scientists left the country to build careers elsewhere in Europe and beyond. Today, a significant number of them are machine learning engineers, data scientists and researchers working in London, Amsterdam, Berlin, Zurich or the United States. Many would like to return — for family, climate, quality of life or a sense of home — but wonder whether the Greek market can offer meaningful work. This guide looks honestly at AI jobs in Athens: the research institutions, the companies, the role of remote work, the tax incentives for relocating residents and how to plan a return that works professionally and financially.

## The Research Ecosystem in Athens

**Athena Research Center.** Athena is one of Greece's leading research centres in information and communication technologies. In recent years it has launched Archimedes, a research unit focused on AI, data science and algorithms, designed to attract top researchers — including Greeks working abroad — and to connect with international research networks.

**NCSR Demokritos.** The National Centre for Scientific Research Demokritos, in the north-east of Athens, has an Institute of Informatics and Telecommunications with research in AI, natural language processing, computer vision and knowledge technologies.

**Universities.** The National Technical University of Athens (NTUA) has a strong School of Electrical and Computer Engineering with well-known research groups in ML, computer vision, signal processing and robotics. The Athens University of Economics and Business (AUEB) is respected for informatics, statistics and data science. The National and Kapodistrian University of Athens contributes computer science, mathematics and medical research. Beyond Athens, the Foundation for Research and Technology – Hellas (FORTH) in Crete and universities in Thessaloniki and Patras add depth.

These institutions participate extensively in European research programmes, which means international collaboration and English-speaking research environments are common.

## Companies Behind AI Jobs in Athens

**Greek-founded product companies.** Several technology companies founded in Greece have built international products. Workable, a recruiting software company founded in Athens, serves customers worldwide. Viva Wallet, a fintech and payments company, grew into a pan-European business. Skroutz, a leading Greek e-commerce and price comparison platform, has a strong engineering team. These companies use ML for search and ranking, recommendations, fraud detection, pricing and automation, and increasingly language models.

**International tech hubs.** Several international companies have opened engineering or R&D teams in Greece to access Greek talent — some in Athens and some in Thessaloniki. Major cloud providers have also announced investments in data centre infrastructure in the country.

**Banks, telecom and energy.** Greek banks, telecom operators and energy companies have invested in data and analytics, with ML roles in risk, fraud, customer analytics and network operations.

**Shipping.** Greece is a global leader in ship ownership, and many shipping companies are based in Piraeus and Athens. Data science in shipping — fuel optimisation, route planning, predictive maintenance, emissions monitoring and chartering analytics — is a growing niche with genuine domain depth.

**Tourism.** Tourism is a major part of the Greek economy, and hospitality and travel companies use data for demand forecasting, pricing and personalisation.

**Startups.** Athens has a growing startup scene, supported by accelerators and venture capital funds, with companies in AI, SaaS, fintech, health and marine technology.

## Remote Work: A Bridge Home

Remote work has transformed the options for Greek engineers abroad. Many continue working for foreign employers while living in Athens, either as employees of local entities, through employer-of-record services or as contractors. This lets them keep international salaries while returning home. It also brings responsibilities: understanding tax residence, social security, employment law and company policies on working from other countries. Some engineers use remote work as a transition — returning while keeping their foreign job — and later move to a Greek company or start their own.

## Tax Incentives for Relocating Residents

Greece has introduced a tax incentive for individuals who move their tax residence to Greece to work, commonly described as a 50% reduction of income tax on employment or business income for a number of years, subject to conditions such as not having been a Greek tax resident for a specified period before the move and working in Greece. This incentive has been widely discussed as a tool to encourage the return of Greeks who left during the crisis. The details, eligibility conditions and duration are set in Greek tax law and can change, so consult a Greek tax adviser before planning your move.

## Pay and Cost of Living

Salaries in Greece are generally lower than in Western and Northern Europe, including for AI roles, though international companies, well-funded product companies and remote work for foreign employers can bring pay closer to international levels. The cost of living in Athens is lower than in cities such as London, Amsterdam or Zurich, but rents have risen considerably in popular neighbourhoods, partly due to short-term rentals and international demand. Many returnees find that family support networks, housing owned by relatives and lower everyday costs make the numbers work, even with a lower salary.

## Planning a Return: A Step-by-Step Approach

Returning home after years abroad is a significant decision. A structured approach helps:

1. **Clarify your priorities.** Family, career growth, salary, research ambitions, lifestyle — rank them honestly.
2. **Map the market.** Identify research groups, product companies, hubs and startups in Athens that match your expertise. Talk to people working there.
3. **Explore remote options with your current employer.** Some employers allow working from another EU country; others do not. Understand the rules before deciding.
4. **Get tax advice early.** The relocation incentive and your tax residence situation significantly affect net income.
5. **Consider timing.** Contracts, school years, housing and notice periods all affect when a move is practical.
6. **Visit and interview in person.** Spend time in Athens meeting teams, not just visiting family.
7. **Plan your network.** Reconnect with university friends, join local meetups and professional groups before you move.

## What Returnees Bring

Greek engineers returning from abroad bring valuable experience: exposure to large-scale systems, modern engineering practices, international product development and professional networks. Greek companies increasingly recognise this and actively recruit returnees. Returnees can also contribute to the ecosystem by mentoring juniors, speaking at events, collaborating with universities and, in some cases, founding startups. Many describe their role as not only taking a job but helping build the Greek tech community.

## Shipping Data Science in Depth

Greek shipping deserves special attention, because it is one of the few sectors where Greece is a global leader. Shipowners and managers based in Athens and Piraeus operate large fleets of tankers, bulk carriers and container ships. Data science in shipping is growing rapidly:

- **Fuel efficiency:** fuel is one of the largest operating costs, and optimising speed, routing, trim and maintenance reduces both costs and emissions.
- **Emissions compliance:** EU and international rules on shipping emissions, including the inclusion of shipping in the EU Emissions Trading System, require accurate measurement and reporting.
- **Predictive maintenance:** monitoring engines and equipment to prevent failures at sea.
- **Chartering and market analytics:** forecasting freight rates and supporting commercial decisions.
- **Voyage optimisation:** combining weather, currents and port information to plan routes.

Many shipping companies are family-owned and traditional, but digitalisation is accelerating, and maritime technology startups have emerged in Athens. For ML engineers, shipping offers domain depth and the chance to work in an industry where Greece has global influence.

## The Athens Tech Community

Athens has a growing tech community with meetups on machine learning, data science, Python and cloud technologies, as well as conferences and startup events. Universities and research centres host seminars open to professionals. Returnee networks and diaspora organisations connect Greeks abroad with opportunities at home. Participating in these communities helps returnees rebuild their professional networks and discover roles that are not widely advertised.

## Challenges to Prepare For

An honest guide must mention the challenges. Bureaucracy in Greece can be time-consuming, although digitalisation of public services has improved many procedures. The number of senior and specialised AI roles is smaller than in major European hubs, so career progression may require patience or remote work. Salaries at traditional Greek employers may not match international levels. Some returnees also experience "reverse culture shock" after years abroad. Being prepared for these challenges — and having a plan for each — makes a return more likely to succeed.

## Thessaloniki as an Alternative

Thessaloniki, Greece's second-largest city, has developed its own technology scene, with the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, research institutes such as the Centre for Research and Technology Hellas (CERTH) and technology hubs of international companies. Living costs are generally lower than in Athens, and the city has a vibrant student and cultural life. For returnees whose families are in northern Greece, or who prefer a smaller city, Thessaloniki is worth considering alongside AI jobs in Athens.

## Questions to Ask Greek Employers

- What is the team's working language, and how international is the product?
- How is compensation structured, and how does it compare with international offers?
- Does the company support remote or hybrid work?
- What ML infrastructure and computing resources are available?
- What opportunities exist for growth into senior or leadership roles?
- Has the company hired other returnees, and how did their transition go?

## Living in Athens

Athens offers sunshine, the sea within reach, a rich cultural heritage and a lively neighbourhood life. The metro, trams and buses connect much of the city, although traffic can be heavy. Many tech companies are located in central areas, the northern suburbs or along the coast, so choosing housing near your workplace or a metro line saves time.

## Real example

### A Greek ML engineer returning from Amsterdam

A Greek ML engineer who moved to Amsterdam a decade ago works on recommendation systems at a large e-commerce company. She and her partner want to raise their children near family in Athens.

She explores three options: continuing remotely for her current employer, joining a Greek product company in Athens and a research position at Athena's AI unit. Her employer allows working from another EU country only under certain conditions, which she discusses with HR. She consults a Greek tax adviser about the relocation tax incentive and calculates net income under each option. She decides to join a Greek product company, whose international business allows it to offer a competitive salary and whose team works in English. With the tax incentive and lower living costs, her net situation is close to what she had in Amsterdam. After a year, she says the return exceeded expectations, though she misses the depth of the Amsterdam tech community and works to build connections in the Athens ML scene.
