Every year, a large group of students graduates from programmes in and around Eindhoven with exactly the skills the AI job market wants — and a surprising number of them pack up and move to Amsterdam, Utrecht or Rotterdam for their first job. Research into regional labour markets has repeatedly shown the same pattern that led to OnlyAIJobs: many job seekers from Noord-Brabant leave for the Randstad not because they want to live there, but because they do not know which interesting companies are in their own region. This guide to AI jobs in Eindhoven is written for students and recent graduates who want to know what is actually available close to home before deciding to leave.

## The Brabant Paradox

Brainport Eindhoven is one of the most technology-intensive regions in Europe. The area around Eindhoven, Veldhoven, Helmond and the surrounding towns is home to global high-tech manufacturers, hundreds of suppliers and engineering firms, and a university of technology with a dedicated AI institute. At the same time, many graduates say they "could not find AI jobs" locally.

Both statements are true, and the explanation is simple: the AI work in Brainport is often not labelled as AI work. It sits inside machine builders, semiconductor equipment makers, medical technology companies and industrial suppliers, under titles such as system engineer, software designer, algorithm developer, data engineer or research scientist. A graduate searching for "AI engineer Eindhoven" on a general job site sees a handful of results and concludes the market is thin. A graduate who knows where to look sees a region full of machine learning, computer vision, control and optimisation problems.

## Where the AI Jobs in Eindhoven Actually Are

It helps to think about the region in layers rather than as a list of big names.

**Global high-tech manufacturers.** ASML in Veldhoven, Philips, NXP Semiconductors and others employ large numbers of engineers working on data-intensive problems: metrology and image analysis, predictive maintenance, yield optimisation, medical imaging and signal processing. Much of this work is applied machine learning combined with deep domain knowledge.

**The supplier ecosystem.** Around the large manufacturers sit hundreds of specialised suppliers and engineering companies. These firms build machines, modules and software for the big players and increasingly need people who can add data analysis, computer vision or smart control to their products. They are often smaller, closer to home and much less visible to graduates.

**High Tech Campus and startups.** High Tech Campus Eindhoven and other innovation sites host startups and scale-ups in areas such as health technology, photonics, robotics and sensing. Startups offer broad responsibility early, which suits graduates who want to learn fast.

**Consultancies and IT service firms.** Several consultancies and software companies in Brabant build AI and data solutions for clients across the region. These roles offer variety and exposure to many industries in a short time.

**Research and education.** TU/e and its Eindhoven Artificial Intelligence Systems Institute (EAISI), Fontys University of Applied Sciences and research organisations in the region hire PhD candidates, research engineers and lab staff.

## Job Titles to Search Instead of "AI Engineer"

For graduates, learning the local vocabulary is half the job search. Titles that frequently hide machine learning or data work in Brainport include:

| Title you might see | What the work often involves |
|---|---|
| System engineer / systems architect | Modelling machine behaviour, data-driven diagnostics |
| Algorithm developer | Signal and image processing, optimisation, ML models |
| Software designer (embedded or data) | ML inference on devices, data pipelines |
| Metrology or vision engineer | Computer vision, measurement analysis |
| Data engineer / data analyst | Pipelines, dashboards, feature engineering |
| Research scientist / research engineer | Prototyping new methods, often with ML |
| Process or yield engineer | Statistical analysis, anomaly detection |

On a board where every listing is already an AI, machine learning or data role, searching by category rather than by title catches these positions. On general sites, search for the problems — "computer vision," "predictive maintenance," "forecasting" — rather than for the word "AI."

## Starting Routes: Internship, Traineeship or Junior Role

Graduates in Brainport typically enter through one of four routes.

**Graduation internship (afstudeerstage).** Many companies in the region host master's and bachelor's graduation projects. A good project at a local company is the most common path to a first job there, because both sides get to know each other for months.

**Traineeships.** Larger employers and some consultancies run traineeship programmes that rotate graduates through teams. They offer structure and training, sometimes at the cost of a slower start in deep technical work.

**Junior roles.** Direct junior positions exist, especially at mid-sized companies and startups. Titles are often "junior data scientist," "junior software engineer (data)" or "medior" roles open to strong graduates.

**PhD or research engineer positions.** For graduates drawn to research, doctoral positions at TU/e and research roles in industry labs are an option — with the advantage that many industry labs sit in the same region.

## Why Distance Matters More Than the City Name

Brabant is a region of mid-sized cities and villages connected by trains, regional buses and cycling routes. "Eindhoven" in a vacancy can mean a campus reachable by bike from the station, or an industrial park that is easy by car and awkward by public transport. For a graduate without a car, that difference decides whether a job is realistic.

## Dutch or English?

In the Brainport region, English is the working language in many high-tech companies and research groups, which have international teams. Dutch becomes more important at smaller suppliers, in customer-facing roles and in companies whose documentation and meetings are in Dutch. International graduates who plan to stay should start learning Dutch early; even basic Dutch widens the set of employers who will consider you, especially outside the largest companies.

## What Employers in Brainport Look For in Graduates

Hiring managers in the region tend to value three things beyond grades.

**Practical projects.** A graduation project, a student team (the region has well-known student teams in areas such as robotics and automotive) or a personal project with real data carries weight.

**Understanding of the domain.** In high-tech manufacturing, a model is only useful if it fits the physics, the machine and the process. Showing curiosity about how systems work — not just how to train models — makes a graduate stand out.

**Communication.** Engineers in Brainport work in multidisciplinary teams with mechanical, electrical and software specialists. Explaining what a model does, and what it cannot do, is a daily task.

## A Graduate's Checklist for Searching AI Jobs in Eindhoven

1. List where you are willing to live and how you will travel (bike, train, car).
2. Search by category and distance, not by "AI" in the title.
3. Build a list of 25 employers across the five layers above, including suppliers you have never heard of.
4. Ask your university's career centre and professors which companies host graduation projects.
5. Apply to a mix of roles: a few at large companies, several at mid-sized firms, one or two startups.
6. For each application, name the specific problem the employer works on and how your project experience relates to it.
7. Decide deliberately whether moving to the Randstad would improve your career — rather than assuming it would.

## The Wider Brabant Map: Den Bosch, Tilburg, Breda and Helmond

Eindhoven is the centre of Brainport, but graduates who limit their search to the city miss a large part of the regional market. 's-Hertogenbosch hosts the Jheronimus Academy of Data Science, a joint initiative of TU/e and Tilburg University, and a mix of financial, construction and service companies building data teams. Tilburg combines a university with strengths in data science and cognitive science with logistics and retail employers. Breda sits close to logistics corridors and has growing IT and data activity, while Helmond is known for automotive and mechatronics. Because these cities are connected by train, a graduate living in Eindhoven or Den Bosch can realistically consider employers across all of them — as long as they check the address of the actual office rather than assuming the station is nearby.

## How to Talk About Your Thesis in Brainport Interviews

Your graduation thesis is often your strongest asset, and Brainport interviewers probe it in a particular way. Expect questions not only about your model's accuracy but about data quality, how you validated results, what you would change with more time and how your approach would behave on real machines or in real clinical settings. Prepare a one-page summary that explains the problem, your data, your method, your evaluation and the limitations in plain language. If your thesis was theoretical, connect it to a concrete industrial problem in the company's domain — for example, how an anomaly detection method could be used for predictive maintenance on production equipment. Showing that you can translate research into engineering value is exactly what regional employers are looking for when they hire graduates into AI and data roles.

## Salary Expectations Without the Guesswork

Graduates often ask what they can expect to earn in an AI or data role in Brainport, and online answers vary widely. Rather than relying on anonymous figures, use three reliable signals. First, many Dutch employers publish salary ranges in their vacancies, and the EU Pay Transparency Directive will make pay information before the interview the norm. Second, collective labour agreements (cao's) apply in several sectors, including metal and electrical engineering, and publish salary scales that tell you where a junior role sits. Third, your university's career service and alumni network can share what recent graduates accepted at regional employers. Compare offers on total package — pension, training budget, travel allowance and flexibility — and remember that the lower housing costs outside the Randstad often make a regional offer stronger than a slightly higher salary in Amsterdam.

Would you still move to the Randstad if you knew three good AI employers within twenty minutes of home? If the honest answer is no, find those three employers first.

## Real example

### A master's graduate who nearly left for Amsterdam

A graduate in data science finishes her master's in Eindhoven and starts applying almost exclusively in Amsterdam, because that is where her searches for "AI engineer" return the most results. She dislikes the idea of paying Amsterdam rent but assumes that is simply the price of an AI career.

A friend suggests searching differently: by category and by distance from her current flat. The results change the picture. Within a comfortable train or cycling distance she finds a machine builder in Helmond looking for someone to build a vision-based inspection system, a medical technology company near the High Tech Campus with a data role in image analysis, and a consultancy in Den Bosch working on forecasting for industrial clients. None of the vacancies uses the title "AI engineer."

She interviews at all three and at one Amsterdam company. She accepts the offer from the Helmond machine builder, keeps her flat and her friends, and starts on a project that combines computer vision with the physical machines she enjoys understanding. Her decision was not about loyalty to the region; it was about seeing the options the region actually had.
