Galway, on Ireland's west coast, is known for its music, festivals, university life and the landscapes of Connemara nearby. It is also one of the world's leading clusters for medical technology, with major manufacturing and R&D operations of global medtech companies and many Irish medtech startups. Software companies, a university with strong data analytics and medical device research, and a technological university add to a technology scene that is remarkable for a city of its size. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Galway are closely linked to medtech — manufacturing analytics, device data, regulatory-grade software — as well as software and research. This guide explains where to look and how to get started.

## The Medtech Cluster

Galway has grown into one of the world's most important medtech hubs over several decades. Global companies such as Medtronic and Boston Scientific have major operations in the city, and many other device manufacturers, suppliers and startups work on cardiovascular devices, catheters, stents, drug delivery and diagnostics. Data science applications include manufacturing analytics and quality control, process optimisation, supply chain planning, analysis of clinical and device data, and increasingly AI features in devices themselves. All of this takes place under strict regulation — the EU Medical Device Regulation and, for AI components, high-risk requirements of the EU AI Act — which makes validation, documentation and quality management central.

## Software Companies

Galway hosts software companies in areas such as customer experience platforms, enterprise software and financial technology, alongside Irish-founded software firms and startups. They employ software engineers, data engineers and data scientists, often in international teams.

## The University of Galway and ATU

**University of Galway** has strong computer science, engineering and medicine. It hosts a node of Insight, the Research Ireland centre for data analytics, and CÚRAM, the Research Ireland centre for medical devices, which develops devices and biomaterials with industry partners. Research combines data science, ML, biomedical engineering and clinical research.

**Atlantic Technological University (ATU)** trains practically oriented engineers and IT professionals, with close links to local medtech and software companies.

## Entry Paths

1. **Graduate programmes** at medtech and software companies, often with rotations.
2. **Internships and work placements**, common in Irish university programmes.
3. **Research assistant and PhD positions** at Insight and CÚRAM.
4. **Startup roles** in medtech and software.

## Permits and Practicalities

EU and EEA citizens can work freely in Ireland and need a PPS number. Non-EU graduates of Irish higher education institutions may be eligible for the Third Level Graduate Programme (Stamp 1G) to stay and look for work, and professionals may use the Critical Skills Employment Permit. Check current rules. English is the working language, which makes Galway accessible for international graduates.

## Living in Galway

Galway is a lively, compact city with a young population, a vibrant arts and music scene and the Atlantic coast on its doorstep. Housing is in short supply and rents are high relative to incomes, as elsewhere in Ireland, so plan your search early and consider towns around the city. Buses connect the city and suburbs, and trains reach Dublin in about 2.5 hours.

## Data Science in Regulated Medtech

Working in medtech teaches graduates a discipline that is valuable everywhere: every model or software tool that affects product quality or patient safety must be validated, documented and controlled. In manufacturing, data scientists analyse process data to reduce defects, support process validation, monitor equipment and predict yields. In product development, they analyse test data, support clinical studies and increasingly develop algorithms that run in or alongside devices — for example signal analysis in monitoring devices or image analysis in diagnostics. Such software may itself be regulated as a medical device, and AI components are generally high-risk under the EU AI Act. Graduates who understand quality systems, risk management and documentation stand out quickly in Galway's medtech companies.

## Research at Insight and CÚRAM

Insight's Galway node works on data analytics, machine learning, knowledge graphs and applications in health, agriculture and smart cities. CÚRAM develops medical devices and biomaterials, from implantable devices to drug delivery systems, in collaboration with clinicians and companies. For graduates, research assistant and PhD positions at these centres offer interdisciplinary work, industry collaboration and international networks. Some graduates combine research experience with later roles in medtech companies, where their expertise in both data and devices is highly valued.

## Medtech Startups

Galway's medtech startup ecosystem is strong, with companies developing novel devices, often founded by engineers with experience at the large manufacturers. Startups need people who can analyse bench and clinical test data, build software tools and support regulatory submissions. Roles offer broad responsibility and exposure to the full product lifecycle, from prototype to regulatory approval.

## Product Analytics in Galway's Software Sector

Software companies in Galway work on customer experience, enterprise platforms and fintech. Data roles include product analytics, ML features and data engineering. They offer graduates modern engineering practices and international teams.

## Manufacturing Analytics in Practice

Medical device manufacturing involves precise processes — extrusion, laser cutting, coating, assembly, sterilisation and inspection — where small variations can affect product performance. Data scientists in Galway's medtech plants work with process parameters, inspection results and equipment data to find the causes of defects, predict yields, optimise settings and support continuous improvement. Computer vision increasingly assists visual inspection of small components, and statistical process control remains a foundation of quality management. Because every change to a validated process requires documentation and approval, data scientists work closely with process engineers and quality teams and learn to present evidence rigorously. Graduates who combine data skills with an understanding of manufacturing processes often move into senior roles quickly, because this combination is scarce. The skills are also highly transferable: medtech companies worldwide value experience gained in Galway's cluster, and many engineers later take roles in other countries or in regulatory and quality functions.

## Clinical and Device Data

As devices become more connected, they generate data about their use and performance in patients. Data scientists analyse this information for post-market surveillance, clinical studies and product improvement, under strict data protection and regulatory requirements. Some companies develop algorithms that interpret physiological signals or images, which may themselves be regulated as medical device software. This work combines ML with clinical understanding and rigorous validation.

## International Graduates in Galway

Galway's universities attract many international students, and the English-speaking environment makes it easier for them to find work locally. Non-EU graduates should check eligibility for the Third Level Graduate Programme and plan applications for graduate programmes early, since many medtech companies recruit a year in advance.

## Responsible AI in Healthcare

AI in medtech directly affects patients. Graduates who understand GDPR, the EU Medical Device Regulation and the EU AI Act, and who approach validation and documentation seriously, bring practical value from day one.

## Reading Graduate Offers

When comparing offers, check salary, pension contributions, health insurance, bonus schemes, training and certification support, and whether the role is part of a structured graduate programme. In medtech, graduate programmes often include rotations across quality, engineering and data teams, which helps you discover where your strengths lie. Ask how juniors are mentored and how performance is evaluated. For your first job, learning opportunities and mentoring matter more than small salary differences.

## Housing and Commuting

Housing in Galway is scarce and rents are high relative to incomes. Many graduates share accommodation or live in towns around the city, such as Oranmore, Athenry, Claregalway or Moycullen, and commute by car or bus. Medtech companies are spread across business parks at the city's edges, so matching your home to your workplace matters. Start your housing search early and ask employers whether they offer relocation support or temporary accommodation for graduates moving from elsewhere.

## Life in the West of Ireland

Galway's atmosphere is one of its biggest attractions: live music in pubs, festivals throughout the year, the Atlantic promenade at Salthill and weekend trips to Connemara, the Aran Islands and the Cliffs of Moher. The weather is mild and often rainy, and the community is friendly and welcoming to newcomers.

## A Final Tip

Visit a medtech plant during an open day or placement before graduating; seeing how devices are made changes how you think about data in manufacturing.

## Staying Connected

Keep in touch with your placement colleagues, lecturers and research supervisors after graduating; Galway's medtech and tech communities are close-knit, and many opportunities come through recommendations rather than public job postings.

## One More Tip

Learn the basics of quality management systems and statistical process control during your studies; medtech interviewers notice.

## And Finally

Galway rewards curiosity: talk to engineers, ask questions and explore the cluster early.
