Ghent is one of Belgium's liveliest cities: a medieval centre, canals, a large student population and a reputation for creativity and progressive politics. It is also a serious technology location. Ghent University is one of the largest universities in the Low Countries, imec runs a major research group in the city, the life science institute VIB has its headquarters there, and a cluster of SaaS scale-ups has grown out of the local ecosystem. For students and recent graduates, AI jobs in Ghent offer a realistic first step in machine learning, often in English-speaking teams, in a city where many young professionals choose to stay after graduation. This guide explains where to look and how to prepare.

## The Academic and Research Base

**Ghent University (UGent).** UGent's Faculty of Engineering and Architecture and Faculty of Sciences have strong programmes in computer science, electrical engineering, statistics and data science. Research groups work on machine learning, computer vision, natural language processing, speech, robotics and bioinformatics. UGent participates in the Flanders AI Research Program, the Flemish government's initiative supporting AI research across Flemish universities.

**imec IDLab.** IDLab is a research group of imec with teams at Ghent University and the University of Antwerp. Its Ghent teams work on AI, data science, wireless networks, IoT and media technology, frequently in projects with companies. For graduates, IDLab offers research engineer and PhD positions with strong industry links.

**VIB.** The Flanders Institute for Biotechnology, headquartered in Ghent, conducts life science research across several Flemish universities. Its data core and computational groups apply machine learning to genomics, proteomics, imaging and drug discovery, and the wider Ghent biotech ecosystem includes spin-offs and companies in agri-biotech and pharmaceuticals.

**HOGENT and Artevelde.** Universities of applied sciences in Ghent train practically oriented IT and data professionals, often with internships in local companies.

## Entry Paths for Graduates

1. **Master's thesis with a company or research group.** In Flanders, many engineering and computer science theses are done with industry partners. It is one of the strongest routes into a first job.
2. **Internships.** Common in universities of applied sciences and increasingly in university programmes.
3. **Junior data scientist or ML engineer roles** at scale-ups and consultancies.
4. **Data engineering roles**, often the most numerous entry positions.
5. **PhD positions** at UGent, imec IDLab or VIB, typically fully funded.
6. **Graduate programmes** at larger companies and consultancies.

## Belgian Graduate Packages

Belgian job offers for graduates are structured differently from many other countries. Salaries are paid monthly, with holiday pay and often a thirteenth month. Taxes and social contributions are high, so employers often add tax-efficient benefits: meal vouchers, eco vouchers, hospitalisation insurance, group insurance pensions and sometimes a company car or mobility budget, even for junior roles in some sectors. When comparing offers, look at the total package rather than gross salary alone, and ask which joint committee (collective agreement) applies.

## Language

Dutch is the official language in Ghent and the language of daily life and the public sector. Many tech companies, scale-ups and research groups work in English, and international students can find English-speaking roles. For long-term careers in the region, and for roles in public organisations or traditional companies, Dutch is a significant advantage, and learning it during your studies pays off.

## Building a Portfolio for Ghent Employers

Ghent's scale-ups and research groups look for evidence that you can deliver working systems, not only good grades. A strong graduate portfolio includes:

- **One end-to-end project** with data collection, modelling, evaluation and a simple deployment, documented clearly.
- **A language model application**, for example a retrieval-based assistant with an honest evaluation of answer quality — highly relevant for SaaS companies adding AI features.
- **A project with local or domain data**: Flemish open mobility data, public biological datasets or industrial sensor data from a course project.
- **Clean, tested code** in a public repository, showing version control habits.
- **Your thesis**, explained in plain language with its limitations.

Hackathons organised by UGent student associations, imec and local companies are excellent opportunities to build such projects and meet employers.

## SaaS Scale-ups and AI Features

Ghent's SaaS companies illustrate how AI work is changing in product companies. Many are adding AI features built on large language models: summarising conversations, generating content, answering questions over customer documents, classifying tickets and automating workflows. For graduates, this creates junior roles that combine software engineering, prompt and retrieval design, evaluation and data engineering. The key skills are pragmatic: building reliable pipelines, measuring quality systematically, controlling costs and handling customer data responsibly under GDPR. Graduates who can show that they know how to evaluate an LLM-based feature — not just build a demo — stand out.

## Computational Biology and Biotech

Ghent's life science ecosystem, anchored by VIB and UGent, offers a distinctive path for graduates with an interest in biology. Machine learning is used to analyse single-cell sequencing data, predict protein structures and interactions, process microscopy images, design experiments and support drug and crop development. Biotech companies and spin-offs in the region hire computational biologists, bioinformaticians and ML engineers, and VIB's research groups offer PhD and research positions. Graduates with a background in computer science can move into this field by taking bioinformatics courses or doing a thesis with a life science group, while biology graduates can strengthen programming and statistics.

## Industry in the Ghent Port Area

The Ghent port area, part of the cross-border North Sea Port with Zeeland in the Netherlands, hosts steel production, car manufacturing, chemicals, bio-based industry and logistics. These industries use data for process optimisation, predictive maintenance, energy efficiency and quality control. Industrial roles often require Dutch and on-site presence, but they offer concrete engineering problems and stable employers. For graduates who enjoy working close to physical processes, they are an attractive alternative to software companies.

## Student Life and Networking

Ghent's student community is large, and student associations organise job fairs, company visits, hackathons and workshops. Local meetups on data science, Python and AI bring together students and professionals, and imec and UGent host public events. Because Ghent is compact, it is easy to attend events and meet people across companies. Use this: talk to engineers about their work, ask which teams mentor juniors well and follow up after events. Many first jobs in Ghent come through such personal connections.

## Living in Ghent

Ghent is compact and bicycle-friendly, with a car-free centre, trams and buses and fast trains to Brussels, Antwerp and Bruges. Housing is in demand because of the large student population, but rents are generally lower than in Brussels. Many young professionals stay in the city after graduation, attracted by its cultural life, festivals and relaxed atmosphere. Brussels is about half an hour by train, which widens the job market considerably: many Ghent residents commute to Brussels for consultancy, finance or EU-related roles, and some Brussels employers allow hybrid work that makes living in Ghent practical.

## Your First Two Years

Use your first years to build strong foundations. Work in a team with experienced engineers who review your code, learn how models reach production and how they are monitored, and develop one area of depth — NLP, computer vision, time series, bioinformatics or MLOps. Improve your Dutch if you plan to stay. Keep contact with your university and research groups, because Flanders' ecosystem rewards people who move between research and industry. These habits will prepare you for more senior roles in Ghent, elsewhere in Belgium or abroad.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews for junior AI roles in Ghent usually include a conversation about motivation and background, a technical interview on programming and ML fundamentals, a discussion of your thesis or projects and often a small case or take-home task. Scale-ups may ask you to reason about a product feature: how you would evaluate a summarisation model, detect a drop in model quality or decide between a simple heuristic and an ML model. Research groups focus on your thesis, your understanding of the literature and your ability to work independently.

Prepare by revisiting your projects in depth. Be ready to explain why you chose a method, how you measured success, what went wrong and what you would change. Practise explaining your thesis in five minutes to a non-specialist. Belgian interviewers appreciate modesty combined with clarity; overselling tends to backfire.

## Reading Graduate Offers Carefully

When you receive an offer, check the contract type (permanent or fixed-term), the joint committee that applies, the gross monthly salary and the number of payments, the benefits included, the training budget, the hybrid policy and the notice period. Ask how performance reviews and salary increases work in the first years. Many graduates focus only on the starting salary, but learning opportunities, mentoring and the team you join matter far more for your long-term career. A slightly lower offer in a strong team is often the better choice.

## Responsible AI Skills for Graduates

As the EU AI Act takes effect, Ghent employers building AI features for European customers need engineers who understand documentation, data governance, evaluation and human oversight. Graduates who can measure bias, document datasets and models and explain GDPR implications of their designs bring practical value from day one. UGent and other Flemish universities offer courses and seminars on AI ethics and law; adding one to your study programme strengthens your profile, especially for roles in health, HR technology, finance or public services where AI systems may be classified as high-risk.

## A Simple Action Plan

Pick three Ghent companies and two research groups that interest you, attend one of their events, ask about thesis or internship opportunities and follow up personally. In a city this compact, genuine interest gets noticed quickly.

## Real example

### A UGent graduate who stayed in Ghent

A UGent master's student in computer science engineering did her thesis on speech recognition with an imec IDLab team. Most of her classmates applied to consultancies in Brussels or to jobs abroad.

She received three offers: a PhD position at IDLab, a junior ML engineer role at a Ghent SaaS scale-up building AI features for customer conversations and a consultancy role in Brussels. She compared the learning opportunities, the total packages and the commute, and chose the scale-up, where her speech expertise applied directly and she could cycle to work. After two years, she owns the company's transcription pipeline and mentors new juniors. She says the thesis gave her both the technical foundation and the network that made the choice possible.
