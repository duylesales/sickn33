Vilnius has built one of the most dynamic technology scenes in Central and Eastern Europe. Lithuania's capital is home to Vinted, the second-hand fashion marketplace that grew into one of Europe's best-known technology companies, and to several other home-grown businesses in cybersecurity and web data. Fintech companies have set up licensed operations there, and international corporations run technology and operations centres in the city. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Vilnius offer product-focused work at companies serving global markets, in a city with a manageable size, a young workforce and a lower cost of living than Western European capitals. This guide explains where the work is, what to expect and what to check before moving.

## The Technology Companies Behind AI Jobs in Vilnius

**Marketplaces and consumer platforms.** Vinted, headquartered in Vilnius, operates a large international marketplace. Marketplaces rely heavily on machine learning for search and ranking, recommendations, pricing suggestions, fraud and trust, image understanding and logistics. Other consumer and e-commerce companies in the city use similar techniques.

**Cybersecurity and privacy.** Nord Security, known for NordVPN and related products, grew out of the Vilnius technology ecosystem. Cybersecurity companies use ML for threat detection, anomaly detection, malware classification and product analytics.

**Web data and infrastructure.** Oxylabs, a company providing web data collection and proxy infrastructure, is based in Vilnius. Data-intensive infrastructure companies employ engineers working on large-scale data processing, parsing and increasingly AI-based extraction.

**Fintech.** Lithuania's central bank, the Bank of Lithuania, has positioned the country as a fintech hub, with a regulatory sandbox and a licensing regime that attracted international companies. Revolut, for example, obtained a specialised banking licence in Lithuania. Fintech firms use ML for fraud prevention, credit risk, anti-money-laundering, transaction monitoring and customer analytics.

**International technology and operations centres.** Several international banks, financial market infrastructure providers and technology companies run centres in Vilnius, employing data engineers, analysts and increasingly ML specialists.

**Startups.** A growing startup scene works on AI applications in fields such as legal technology, health, education and B2B software.

## Universities and Talent

Vilnius University, Lithuania's oldest and largest university, has strong mathematics and informatics faculties. Vilnius Gediminas Technical University (VILNIUS TECH) trains engineers and IT specialists. Kaunas University of Technology in Kaunas, about an hour away, is another important source of engineering graduates, and Kaunas has its own growing technology scene. Lithuania has a young, well-educated technology workforce, many of whom speak excellent English.

## What Employers Look For

Vilnius technology companies tend to be product-driven and value engineers who can take ownership. Common expectations include strong Python and SQL, experience deploying models into production, experimentation and A/B testing (particularly in marketplaces), data engineering skills, and clear communication in English. Fintech roles add knowledge of regulatory requirements and model governance. Because many companies serve international markets, experience with multilingual data and global user bases is an asset.

## Pay and Cost of Living

Salaries in Lithuania have grown quickly, and technology companies with international revenue often pay considerably above local averages, sometimes with equity. Still, pay levels are generally lower than in Western European tech hubs. The cost of living in Vilnius, including rents, has risen but remains below that of cities such as Amsterdam, Munich or Dublin. When comparing offers, look at net pay, the currency (Lithuania uses the euro), equity terms and benefits such as health insurance and learning budgets.

## Permits and Practicalities

EU and EEA citizens can work in Lithuania and register their residence. Non-EU professionals can use the EU Blue Card under Lithuania's implementation of the recast Blue Card Directive, or national work permit procedures; Lithuania has also offered streamlined procedures for certain high-demand occupations and a startup visa for founders. Requirements change, so check official Lithuanian sources and your employer's experience with sponsorship.

Lithuanian is the official language and important for daily life and public services, but English is widely used in technology companies. Learning some Lithuanian is appreciated and helps with integration.

## Marketplace Machine Learning in Depth

Because marketplaces are such an important part of the Vilnius ecosystem, it is worth understanding the ML problems they involve.

**Search and ranking.** Users search in many languages with short, ambiguous queries. Ranking models must balance relevance, freshness, seller quality, price and personalisation, and they are evaluated through offline metrics and online experiments.

**Recommendations.** Homepage feeds, "similar items" and notifications rely on recommendation systems that combine collaborative filtering, content understanding and user behaviour. Cold start — new users and new items — is a constant challenge in marketplaces with many unique second-hand items.

**Image and text understanding.** Listings include photos and short descriptions written by ordinary users. Computer vision and language models extract categories, brands, colours, sizes and conditions, and detect prohibited items.

**Pricing assistance.** Suggesting prices to sellers requires understanding demand, item condition and comparable listings.

**Trust and safety.** Detecting fraud, counterfeit goods, scams and abusive behaviour is essential. This combines ML with human review and policy work, and must be balanced against false positives that frustrate honest users.

**Logistics.** Shipping options, delivery time estimation and returns involve forecasting and optimisation.

For engineers, marketplaces offer the chance to work on many ML problems within one company and to see the impact of changes quickly through experimentation.

## Fintech and Regulation in Lithuania

Fintech roles in Vilnius carry particular responsibilities because licensed institutions are supervised by the Bank of Lithuania within the European regulatory framework. ML models used for anti-money-laundering, transaction monitoring and credit decisions must be explainable, documented and regularly validated. Under the EU AI Act, AI systems used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons are generally classified as high-risk, bringing additional requirements for risk management, data governance, human oversight and documentation. Engineers who combine ML skills with an understanding of these requirements are valuable in the Vilnius fintech sector.

## Living in Vilnius

Vilnius combines a historic old town, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with modern business districts across the Neris river, where many technology companies have their offices. The city is relatively compact, and many people cycle, take buses or trolleybuses or use ride-hailing and scooters. Distances are manageable, so commutes are generally short compared with large capitals.

Housing costs have risen as the technology sector has grown, especially for modern apartments near business districts, but they remain moderate by Western European standards. Winters are cold and dark, and summers are pleasant and long in daylight. The city has a lively cultural scene, many cafés and green spaces, and the Baltic coast and lakes are within reach for weekends. Direct flights connect Vilnius with many European cities.

For families, Vilnius offers international schools and a range of public schools, and Lithuania's healthcare system is funded through compulsory health insurance, often supplemented by private insurance offered by employers.

## Career Growth in the Vilnius Ecosystem

The Vilnius technology community is closely connected. Engineers move between marketplaces, cybersecurity, fintech and startups, and alumni of the larger home-grown companies have founded new startups. This creates a supportive environment for career growth: experience at one company is well understood by others, and networks form quickly. Meetups on data science, Python, ML and product management are held regularly, and technology conferences in Vilnius attract international speakers. Being active in this community helps both in finding roles and in learning from peers who face similar problems at scale.

- What equity do you offer, and on what terms?

## Cybersecurity and Machine Learning

Cybersecurity is one of the areas where Vilnius stands out, and it offers distinctive ML work. Security products process large volumes of network, device and user activity data. Machine learning helps detect malicious domains and files, identify unusual behaviour that may signal account takeover, filter phishing and scams and prioritise alerts for analysts. The challenges differ from consumer ML: adversaries adapt to detection systems, labelled data is scarce and noisy, false positives are costly for users and privacy constraints limit what data can be collected. Engineers in this field combine ML with security domain knowledge, and many find the adversarial nature of the work intellectually engaging.

Privacy-focused products add a further dimension. Because these companies promise users strong privacy, ML teams often need to design models and analytics that work with minimal or aggregated data, which encourages expertise in privacy-preserving techniques and careful data governance.

## Kaunas as an Alternative

Kaunas, Lithuania's second-largest city, is about an hour from Vilnius by car or train. It has a strong technical university, Kaunas University of Technology, a growing number of technology companies and engineering centres, and a lower cost of living than the capital. Some companies have offices in both cities. For engineers who prefer a smaller city or who want to be close to KTU's research, Kaunas is worth considering, and hybrid arrangements can make working with a Vilnius employer from Kaunas possible.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews at Vilnius product companies usually include a technical screen, a coding or ML exercise, a system design discussion — for example designing a recommendation or fraud detection system — and conversations about past projects and collaboration. Prepare to discuss experiments you ran, how you chose metrics and what you learned when results were surprising. Product companies value engineers who think about user impact, not only model accuracy.

## Remote and Hybrid Work

Many Vilnius technology companies offer hybrid arrangements, and some hire remotely across Europe. If you consider a remote role with a Lithuanian employer while living elsewhere, clarify the employment structure, which country's tax and social security rules apply and how often you are expected in the office. For newcomers who relocate, spending time in the office during the first months helps build relationships and understand the product.

## Before You Decide

Visit Vilnius in winter if you can, meet the team in person and speak with an engineer who relocated recently. Their experience is the best guide to what your first year will look like.

## Real example

### A search engineer moving from Warsaw to Vilnius

A search and ranking engineer at a Polish e-commerce company receives an offer from a Vilnius marketplace to work on ranking models serving several European countries. The salary is somewhat higher, and the company offers equity and relocation support.

Before accepting, he checks the team's scope — whether he would own ranking models end to end or implement changes designed elsewhere — and the company's experimentation culture. He also visits Vilnius, compares rents near the office and learns that the team works entirely in English. Satisfied, he accepts. In his first year, he leads experiments on multilingual query understanding, work that draws directly on the marketplace's international user base. He finds the city compact and easy to live in, though he notes that winters are long and dark — something to prepare for.
