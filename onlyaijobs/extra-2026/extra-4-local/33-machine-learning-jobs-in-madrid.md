Madrid is Spain's political and corporate capital and one of the largest metropolitan economies in the EU. The headquarters of major Spanish banks, telecommunications, energy and infrastructure companies are concentrated in and around the city, alongside the Spanish offices of many global technology firms and a growing startup scene. For experienced machine learning engineers, machine learning jobs in Madrid offer scale, variety and international exposure — Spanish companies often operate across Latin America and Europe — in a city known for its energy, culture and long evenings. This guide explains where the ML work is and what to consider before moving.

## The Corporate Landscape

**Banking.** Spain's largest banking groups, including BBVA and Banco Santander, have their corporate headquarters or major operations in the Madrid region. BBVA's headquarters is in northern Madrid, and Santander's corporate campus, Ciudad Grupo Santander, is in Boadilla del Monte, west of the city. Both have invested heavily in data and AI, with teams working on credit risk, fraud detection, AML, customer analytics, personalisation, conversational AI and language models. Their international footprint means ML teams often work on products for customers in several countries.

**Telecommunications.** Telefónica's headquarters, Distrito Telefónica, is in the north of Madrid. Telecom operators use ML for network optimisation, customer churn prediction, fraud detection, service personalisation and increasingly AI-powered customer service.

**Energy and infrastructure.** Energy companies such as Repsol and major infrastructure and construction groups have headquarters in Madrid. AI supports energy trading, demand forecasting, asset maintenance, renewable energy integration and project management.

**Retail, travel and consumer.** Retailers, travel and hospitality companies and consumer brands use data science for demand forecasting, pricing, recommendations and marketing.

**Tech companies and startups.** Global technology companies run offices in Madrid, and local startups and scale-ups work in mobility (Cabify was founded in Madrid), real estate platforms, fintech, insurtech and B2B software.

**Consultancies.** Large consultancies and IT services firms employ many data scientists and ML engineers working for clients across Spain and Latin America.

## Universities and Research

Madrid has several strong universities for AI and data science. The Universidad Politécnica de Madrid (UPM) has large engineering and computer science schools and research in AI, robotics and data science. Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (UC3M) is known for signal processing, statistics and machine learning research. Universidad Autónoma de Madrid (UAM) and Universidad Complutense de Madrid add computer science, mathematics and physics. Business schools such as IE University, IESE (with a Madrid campus) and ESADE (with a Madrid campus) train managers and offer programmes in data and technology. Research institutes of the Spanish National Research Council (CSIC) and others contribute to AI research.

## Spain's AI Policy Context

Spain has pursued a national AI strategy with investments in research, talent and public language models for Spanish and co-official languages. It was also one of the first EU countries to establish a dedicated AI supervisory body, the Spanish Agency for the Supervision of Artificial Intelligence (AESIA), headquartered in A Coruña, in the context of the EU AI Act. For ML engineers, this means growing attention to governance, documentation and compliance in Spanish companies, particularly in banking, insurance and public services.

## Language

Spanish is essential for daily life and for most corporate environments in Madrid. International tech companies, some startups and certain teams in large corporations work in English, but Spanish is the default for stakeholder meetings and documentation in many organisations. Madrid's connections with Latin America mean that many teams include colleagues from across the Spanish-speaking world. For non-Spanish speakers, learning Spanish is one of the most important investments for a successful career in Madrid.

## Pay, Contracts and Taxes

Salaries in Madrid are generally the highest in Spain along with Barcelona, but lower than in Northern European hubs. Large banks, international tech companies and well-funded startups tend to pay the most. Salaries are expressed as annual gross amounts, often divided into 12 or 14 payments. Permanent contracts (indefinidos) are standard after recent labour reforms limited temporary contracts. Spain's special tax regime for inbound workers — often called the "Beckham law" and updated by the Startups Law — can reduce the tax burden for eligible newcomers for several years. Check eligibility carefully with a tax adviser.

## Permits

EU citizens can work freely and register as residents to obtain their identification number. Non-EU professionals can use the highly qualified professional residence permit under Spain's law supporting entrepreneurs and internationalisation, the EU Blue Card or other routes. Spain's digital nomad visa allows remote workers for foreign companies to live in Spain under certain conditions. Check official Spanish sources for current requirements.

## Living in Madrid

Madrid is lively, sunny and culturally rich, with museums, parks such as the Retiro, a vibrant food scene and late-night social life. The metro is extensive and efficient, complemented by Cercanías commuter trains and buses. Housing costs have risen significantly, particularly in central neighbourhoods, though they remain lower than in Paris or London. Many corporate headquarters are located in the north of the city or in business parks on the outskirts, so living near your workplace or along the relevant metro or Cercanías line can save considerable time.

## Banking AI at Scale

Madrid's banking groups are among the most advanced in Europe in their use of data. Their ML teams work on problems at very large scale:

- **Real-time fraud detection** across card, transfer and digital channels in several countries.
- **Credit risk models** for retail and business customers, subject to regulatory validation.
- **Personalisation** of banking apps, offers and financial advice.
- **Conversational AI** for customer service, increasingly based on large language models with retrieval and guardrails.
- **Document processing** for onboarding, lending and compliance.
- **Anti-money-laundering** with network analysis and anomaly detection.

These banks have built internal data platforms, ML tooling and centres of expertise, and some have published research or open-source tools. Working in banking AI in Madrid offers exposure to modern ML at scale combined with rigorous governance — model validation, explainability, fairness testing and documentation — which is increasingly required under the EU AI Act for high-risk uses such as creditworthiness assessment.

## Telecom and Energy Data Science

Telecom operators in Madrid use ML to plan and optimise networks, predict equipment failures, detect fraud, reduce churn and personalise offers. The shift toward software-defined networks and AI-native operations creates demand for engineers who combine ML with systems knowledge.

Energy companies face the transformation of energy systems. Spain has a high share of renewable electricity, particularly solar and wind, which increases the need for accurate forecasting of production and demand, optimisation of storage and trading, and predictive maintenance of assets. Energy data science in Madrid combines time series modelling, optimisation and domain knowledge, and offers work with clear environmental relevance.

## Consulting and Latin American Connections

Madrid is a hub for projects across Spain and Latin America. Consultancies and IT services firms deliver data and AI projects for banks, telcos, retailers and governments in many Spanish-speaking countries. For ML engineers, this offers exposure to diverse industries and markets, sometimes with travel. Spanish companies' Latin American operations also mean that ML teams in Madrid may build models for customers in Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, Argentina or Chile, bringing challenges such as different data availability, regulations and customer behaviour. Engineers who speak Spanish and enjoy international work find this dimension rewarding.

## Startups and the Madrid Tech Scene

Madrid's startup ecosystem has grown steadily, with companies in mobility, proptech, fintech, insurtech, HR tech and B2B software. Accelerators, investors and events support the scene, and the presence of large corporations creates opportunities for B2B startups selling to them. Startup roles offer broad scope and equity but involve more uncertainty. When considering a startup, assess funding, business model and team, and understand the terms of any equity offered.

## Building a Network

Madrid's data and AI community is large and active, with frequent meetups on machine learning, data science, Python and MLOps, as well as conferences, hackathons and university events. Business schools host events on AI and technology strategy. Joining these communities helps newcomers learn about companies, understand local salary levels and make friends in a city where social life is central to everyday culture.

## Questions to Ask Madrid Employers

- Is the team's working language Spanish, English or both, and for which meetings?
- At which campus or office will I work, and how many office days are expected?
- Which collective agreement applies, and how are salary reviews handled?
- Does the company support applications for the special tax regime for inbound workers?
- How mature is the ML platform, and who operates models in production?
- Will I build models for Spain only, or for other countries as well?

## Settling In

Newcomers need a foreigner identification number (NIE), registration at the town hall (empadronamiento), registration with social security and a Spanish bank account. Appointments can be difficult to obtain, so start early and ask your employer for help.

## Who Madrid Suits Best

Madrid suits engineers who want large-scale corporate AI with international scope, are ready to work in Spanish and enjoy a lively, social city with a strong sense of community.

## Real example

### A fraud ML engineer relocating from Dublin to Madrid

A Spanish ML engineer working on fraud detection for a payments company in Dublin wants to return to Spain. He receives an offer from a Madrid-based bank's data and AI unit to work on real-time fraud models for several markets.

The gross salary is lower than in Dublin, but he calculates net pay, compares Madrid and Dublin rents and considers the family support he would have in Spain. He also examines the team: it has modern infrastructure, a strong ML platform and responsibility for models used across several countries. He accepts, choosing a flat near a Cercanías station with a direct line to the office. After a year, he notes that the bank's processes are more formal than in his previous company, but the scale and international scope of the work are comparable, and his overall quality of life has improved.
