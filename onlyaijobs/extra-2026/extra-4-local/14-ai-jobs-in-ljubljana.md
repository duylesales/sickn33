Slovenia is small — around two million inhabitants — but it punches above its weight in artificial intelligence research. Ljubljana, its compact and green capital, hosts the Jožef Stefan Institute, an internationally recognised research institute, and the International Research Centre on Artificial Intelligence under the auspices of UNESCO. The country has also produced software companies with global reach. AI jobs in Ljubljana are therefore a mix of research positions with international visibility, product roles at companies selling worldwide and data roles in pharma, finance and industry. This guide explains the Ljubljana AI landscape honestly, including the limits of a small market, and helps experienced engineers decide whether it fits their plans.

## Ljubljana's Research Strength

**Jožef Stefan Institute (JSI).** The JSI is Slovenia's largest research institute, covering physics, chemistry, biology and computer science. Its departments working on artificial intelligence, knowledge technologies and intelligent systems have a long track record in machine learning, text mining, data analytics and applications in health, environment and industry. The institute participates in many European research projects, which means international collaboration is part of daily work.

**IRCAI.** The International Research Centre on Artificial Intelligence was established in Ljubljana under the auspices of UNESCO. It focuses on AI in support of the UN Sustainable Development Goals and brings international visibility and partnerships to the city's AI community.

**University of Ljubljana.** The Faculty of Computer and Information Science (FRI) is the country's leading computer science faculty, with research groups in machine learning, computer vision, bioinformatics and natural language processing. Other faculties — mathematics and physics, electrical engineering, economics — contribute to data science education and research. The University of Maribor, in Slovenia's second-largest city, adds further research and graduates.

**Computing.** Slovenia hosts Vega, a supercomputer in Maribor that is part of the EuroHPC network, giving researchers access to significant computing resources.

Other Slovenian-founded companies in adtech, fintech and SaaS have similarly built data-driven products for international markets.

## The Honest Limits of a Small Market

Engineers considering AI jobs in Ljubljana should understand the trade-offs.

**Fewer vacancies.** The absolute number of AI roles is much smaller than in large capitals. You may need patience and a network to find the right role.

**Pay levels.** Salaries in Slovenia are generally lower than in Western and Northern Europe, although international product companies can pay competitively. Cost of living, especially housing in Ljubljana, has risen.

**Specialisation.** Highly specialised roles — for example in large-scale LLM training — are rare locally. Research institutes and some product companies offer depth, but breadth of specialist options is limited.

**Career progression.** With fewer large companies, senior and leadership roles are limited. Many experienced engineers combine local roles with international projects or remote work.

## Remote Work and International Employers

A significant number of Slovenian engineers work remotely for companies abroad, either as employees through local entities or employer-of-record services, or as independent contractors. Ljubljana's quality of life, safety and location — close to the Alps, the Adriatic coast, Italy, Austria and Croatia — make it attractive as a base. If you consider this path, clarify employment status, tax residence, social security and how the arrangement affects benefits. Also consider the career effects: fully remote roles can limit mentoring and visibility, so maintain local connections through meetups and the research community.

## Language and Culture

Slovene is the official language, and it is essential for public sector jobs and useful in daily life. However, English is widely spoken, especially among younger people and in the tech sector, and many product companies and research groups work in English. Slovenian workplaces tend to be informal and collegial in tech, with a strong emphasis on work-life balance and outdoor activities.

## Permits

EU and EEA citizens can work in Slovenia and register their residence. Non-EU citizens generally need a single residence and work permit, and highly qualified workers can apply for the EU Blue Card under rules implementing the recast Blue Card Directive. Researchers have specific procedures. Requirements change, so check official Slovenian sources and ask whether your employer has experience with the process.

## Research Careers in Ljubljana

For engineers attracted to research, Ljubljana offers an unusual advantage: access to internationally connected research groups in a city where daily life is relaxed and manageable. Research careers in Slovenia, as in much of Europe, are largely project-funded. Positions at the Jožef Stefan Institute or university often depend on European programmes such as Horizon Europe, national research agency funding or industry contracts. This brings variety — researchers may work on health, environment, media and manufacturing projects within a few years — but also uncertainty about long-term funding.

Practical tips for research candidates:

- **Look at project portfolios:** the projects a department participates in tell you which topics and partners you would work with.
- **Ask about contract structure:** how long positions last, how they are renewed and whether there is a path to permanent employment.
- **Understand PhD options:** many research engineers at institutes pursue a PhD in parallel, often through the Jožef Stefan International Postgraduate School or the University of Ljubljana.
- **Consider industry collaboration:** projects with companies build a network that helps later if you move to industry.

Research groups in Ljubljana have developed tools and datasets used internationally, particularly in text mining, knowledge technologies and data analytics. Contributing to such work gives you visibility well beyond Slovenia.

## Industry Applications Across Slovenia

Although Ljubljana is the centre, Slovenia's industry is spread across the country, and many AI projects happen outside the capital.

**Pharmaceuticals.** Krka in Novo mesto and Lek, part of Sandoz, in Ljubljana are major employers. Data science supports process analytics, quality control, supply chain and commercial analysis, within regulated environments.

**Manufacturing.** Slovenia has a strong manufacturing base, including household appliances, automotive components and electrical equipment. Predictive maintenance, quality inspection and production optimisation are common topics.

**Energy and utilities.** Energy companies and grid operators use forecasting and optimisation, particularly as renewable energy grows.

**Logistics.** The Port of Koper, Slovenia's only seaport, is an important logistics hub for Central Europe, with data applications in operations and supply chains.

Because distances in Slovenia are short, living in Ljubljana while working with employers elsewhere is often feasible with some commuting or a hybrid arrangement.

## Networking in a Small Ecosystem

In a small country, relationships matter even more than in large markets. Many roles are filled through recommendations, and people across research, startups and industry know each other. To build a network in Ljubljana:

- Attend public lectures, workshops and events at the Jožef Stefan Institute, FRI and IRCAI.
- Join local data science and machine learning meetups and startup events.
- Participate in European research networks and conferences that Slovenian groups attend.
- Contribute to open-source projects or datasets developed in Slovenia.
- Engage with the Slovenian diaspora in tech: many engineers abroad maintain close ties and share opportunities.

Newcomers often find that a few genuine connections open doors quickly, because the community is small enough for people to remember and recommend one another.

## Evaluating an Offer in Slovenia

When comparing AI jobs in Ljubljana, look beyond gross salary. Slovenian payslips include significant social security contributions, and net pay can differ considerably from gross. Many employers provide meal and transport allowances, and annual leave and holiday pay (regres) are part of the package. Ask about contract type — fixed-term positions are common in research — and about flexibility, remote work and training budgets. Compare total quality of life as well: short commutes, access to nature and a safe environment are real benefits that many engineers value highly.

## Who Ljubljana Suits Best

Ljubljana tends to suit engineers who value research depth, quality of life and a close-knit community over the sheer number of opportunities available in large capitals. It suits those returning to Slovenia after working abroad, researchers who want international collaboration from a manageable base, and engineers who work remotely for international employers but want a local professional community. It suits less well those who want to change employers frequently or who need very specialised roles that exist only in large technology hubs. Being clear about which group you belong to makes the decision easier.

## Living in Ljubljana

Ljubljana is compact enough that many people walk or cycle to work, and the old town along the Ljubljanica river is largely car-free. The city has been recognised for its green policies, and nature is close: the Alps, Lake Bled and the Soča valley are within easy reach for weekend trips, and the Adriatic coast is around an hour and a half away. Housing has become more expensive as demand has grown, and the rental market can be tight, particularly near the centre and the university. Many people live in surrounding towns and commute by car, bus or train.

For families, Ljubljana offers public kindergartens and schools, and there are international school options. Healthcare is provided through mandatory health insurance, often supplemented with additional insurance. Newcomers generally find the city safe, well organised and welcoming, although building close local friendships may take time — as in many European countries, joining sports clubs, hiking groups or language classes helps.

## Skills That Help You Stand Out

In a small market, versatility is valuable. Ljubljana employers often appreciate engineers who can cover several areas: data engineering and modelling, research and production, technical work and communication with clients or partners. Experience with European research projects, natural language processing for smaller languages, or industrial data can also be a differentiator, given the local research strengths and industry profile.

## First Steps for a Ljubljana Move

Before relocating, secure a job offer or a clear remote arrangement, check your permit route, arrange temporary housing and register your residence soon after arrival. Then build your local network deliberately.

### A data scientist returning to Slovenia

A Slovenian data scientist has worked for five years in Munich on recommender systems. She and her partner want to move back to be closer to family, but she worries about career prospects.

She maps the Ljubljana market: a research engineer role at a JSI department working on European projects in natural language processing, a data science role at a gaming company and remote options with her Munich employer.
