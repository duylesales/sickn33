Gelderland is the largest province of the Netherlands by land area, stretching from the Veluwe forests to the river landscapes around Arnhem and Nijmegen. For students graduating in AI, data science or computer science, it is easy to assume that the interesting jobs are all in Amsterdam or the rest of the Randstad. That assumption is exactly what OnlyAIJobs was built to challenge: talented graduates often leave their region because they simply do not know the local employers doing serious AI work. AI jobs in Gelderland exist across food and agriculture, energy, health, semiconductors and public services — and many of them are a short bike or train ride from where students already live. This guide maps the province for graduates.

## Why Look at AI Jobs in Gelderland

**Strong universities on your doorstep.** Wageningen University & Research is world-renowned for agriculture, food, environment and life sciences, and increasingly for data science applied to those fields. Radboud University in Nijmegen offers bachelor's and master's programmes in artificial intelligence and hosts the Donders Institute for Brain, Cognition and Behaviour, a major research centre in neuroscience and cognition. HAN University of Applied Sciences, with campuses in Arnhem and Nijmegen, trains practically oriented ICT, engineering and data professionals.

**Distinct industry clusters.** The province is not a smaller copy of Amsterdam. It has its own specialisations: food and agri-tech in the Foodvalley region around Wageningen and Ede, energy infrastructure in Arnhem, health and semiconductors in Nijmegen, and public-sector data in Apeldoorn.

**Quality of life and cost.** Housing is scarce throughout the Netherlands, but many parts of Gelderland are more affordable than Amsterdam or Utrecht, and nature is close.

**Existing listings.** OnlyAIJobs already has vacancies in Gelderland, including in Ede, which makes the province a practical example of how location-first searching works.

## The Clusters Behind AI Jobs in Gelderland

### Wageningen, Ede and the Foodvalley region

Wageningen University & Research combines a university with applied research institutes. Its work on precision agriculture, plant and animal breeding, food quality, water and climate generates enormous amounts of data — from satellite and drone images to genomic data and sensor streams from greenhouses and barns. Around it, the Foodvalley region, which includes Ede, hosts food companies, agri-tech startups, research institutes and service providers. Data science and machine learning roles here involve computer vision for crops and livestock, yield prediction, food supply chain optimisation and analysis of consumer and nutrition data.

### Arnhem: energy and infrastructure

Arnhem is home to the headquarters of TenneT, the transmission system operator for the Netherlands and parts of Germany, and Alliander, one of the largest Dutch distribution network operators, is also based in Arnhem. As the energy transition accelerates, grid operators face congestion, a growing share of variable renewable generation and rising electricity demand. Data science is central: forecasting load and generation, predicting congestion, planning grid investments, maintaining assets and analysing large volumes of measurement data. Arnhem also hosts consultancies and engineering firms working on energy.

### Nijmegen: health, cognition and chips

Nijmegen combines Radboud University, Radboudumc (the university medical centre) and a cluster of health and life science companies. Medical imaging, clinical prediction models and health data analysis are prominent. The Donders Institute connects neuroscience with computational modelling and AI. Nijmegen is also home to a significant NXP Semiconductors site, and the semiconductor industry uses data science in manufacturing, testing and design.

### Apeldoorn and the Veluwe

Apeldoorn hosts several public organisations with large data operations, including the Kadaster (the Dutch land registry and mapping agency) and substantial IT operations of the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration. Geospatial data, document processing and public sector analytics are key themes. For graduates interested in public service, these organisations offer traineeships and data roles with societal impact.

## Why Graduates Leave — and Why They Don't Have To

Every year, many graduates from Wageningen, Nijmegen and Arnhem move to the Randstad for their first job. Some go because they want city life or a specific employer, which is a perfectly good reason. Many others go simply because the employers they know by name are there. Career fairs, online job boards and classmates' stories tend to highlight large, well-known companies, most of which have their main offices in Amsterdam, Utrecht, Rotterdam or The Hague.

Regional employers are often less visible. A grid operator, a food processing company, a medical technology firm or a public agency may not advertise at career fairs or may post vacancies with titles like "data specialist" or in Dutch, which students overlook when searching for "machine learning engineer". ## Building a Profile for Regional Employers

Regional employers in Gelderland often look for slightly different qualities than large tech companies in Amsterdam. Many are domain-driven organisations — energy, food, health, government — where data science supports a core business rather than being the product itself. They value:

- **Domain curiosity:** interest in how the grid works, how crops grow or how hospitals organise care. Mention relevant courses, thesis topics or projects.
- **Communication:** the ability to explain results to engineers, agronomists, doctors or policy advisers who are not data specialists.
- **Pragmatism:** choosing a simple model that works and can be maintained over a complex one that impresses on paper.
- **Data engineering basics:** many regional organisations have small data teams, so everyone contributes to data pipelines and quality.
- **Dutch language skills:** helpful for many regional employers, particularly in the public sector and SMEs.

If you are a WUR student, a thesis or internship at a Foodvalley company or research institute is one of the strongest entries into the regional market. Radboud students can look for internships at Radboudumc, NXP or grid operators. HAN students often do graduation projects at regional companies, which frequently lead to job offers.

## Traineeships in the Region

Traineeships are a popular route into the Dutch job market for graduates. Public organisations, grid operators, consultancies and larger companies in Gelderland offer data or IT traineeships lasting one to two years, often combining rotations across teams with training programmes. They provide structure, a network and exposure to different problems, and they are usually well paid by Dutch graduate standards. Some traineeship programmes are run by organisations that place trainees with several employers in the region. When considering a traineeship, ask how much of the time is spent on actual data work, what training is included and what happens at the end of the programme.

## Getting Around the Province

Gelderland is large, and commuting patterns depend on where you live. Arnhem and Nijmegen are connected by frequent trains in about 15 to 20 minutes, making the two cities effectively one labour market. Ede-Wageningen station links the Foodvalley region to Arnhem and Utrecht, though Wageningen itself relies on bus connections to the station. Apeldoorn has direct trains to Amersfoort, Deventer and Zutphen. Cycling works well within cities and increasingly on fast bicycle routes between them, such as the RijnWaalpad between Arnhem and Nijmegen. Many employers reimburse travel costs.

Because distances vary, always check the actual office location. A company listed as "Ede" may be near the station or on a business park, and the difference matters if you depend on public transport. Searching by distance from home — the core of OnlyAIJobs — shows these differences directly.

## Life After the First Job

A first job in Gelderland does not limit your career. The skills you gain in energy, food, health or public sector data are highly transferable, and the Netherlands is small enough that moving to another region later is easy. Many professionals find, however, that the region offers enough variety to build a whole career: from a traineeship to a senior data scientist role, a lead position or a specialised research track at WUR or Radboud.

## Typical First Projects by Cluster

To make the opportunities concrete, here are examples of the kind of work graduates might do in their first year in each Gelderland cluster.

**Energy (Arnhem).** Cleaning and combining smart meter and grid measurement data; building a forecasting model for load at substations; creating dashboards that show where congestion is expected; analysing failure data of cables and transformers to support maintenance planning.

**Food and agri-tech (Wageningen and Ede).** Labelling and training image models to detect plant diseases or count fruit; predicting yields from weather and sensor data; analysing production data in food processing to reduce waste; supporting researchers with data pipelines for field experiments.

**Health (Nijmegen).** Preparing clinical data for research under strict privacy rules; supporting medical imaging projects with data management and model evaluation; building prediction models in collaboration with clinicians, with careful attention to validation.

**Semiconductors (Nijmegen).** Analysing test and yield data from production; detecting anomalies in process measurements; automating reporting for engineers.

**Public sector (Apeldoorn).** Working with geospatial data; classifying and extracting information from documents; improving data quality in large registries; supporting policy analysis with statistics.

These projects share a common feature: they connect data science directly to physical or societal outcomes. For many graduates, that sense of purpose is a strong reason to choose a regional employer over a larger, more anonymous one in the Randstad.

## Responsible AI in Regional Organisations

Many Gelderland employers work in regulated or sensitive areas — health, energy infrastructure, public services. Graduates who understand GDPR, fairness and the EU AI Act's requirements for high-risk systems have an advantage, as these organisations must document and justify their use of algorithms carefully.
