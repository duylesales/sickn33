Linz, the capital of Upper Austria, is an industrial city on the Danube that has reinvented itself as a centre of technology, art and research. It hosts one of Austria's largest industrial companies, a globally successful software company, a software park in nearby Hagenberg and — at Johannes Kepler University — one of Europe's most renowned machine learning institutes, led by the co-inventor of the LSTM. Upper Austria is also Austria's leading industrial region, full of family-owned technology champions. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Linz span fundamental ML research and its commercialisation, industrial AI in steel and manufacturing, software observability and applied research. This guide explains the landscape.

## JKU Linz and Machine Learning Research

Johannes Kepler University Linz (JKU) hosts the Institute for Machine Learning, led by Sepp Hochreiter, whose work on long short-term memory networks shaped modern deep learning. The institute works on deep learning architectures, reinforcement learning, AI for science and AI in industry, and JKU hosts an ELLIS unit and the LIT AI Lab as part of the Linz Institute of Technology. In recent years, researchers from Linz have introduced new recurrent architectures such as xLSTM, and a company, NXAI, was founded in Linz to develop and commercialise such research, particularly for industrial applications.

For researchers and research engineers, Linz offers the rare chance to work close to foundational ML research in a mid-sized city, with strong connections to industry.

## Industry: Steel and Manufacturing Champions

**Steel and materials.** voestalpine, headquartered in Linz, is a global steel and technology group producing high-quality steel products for automotive, rail, energy and aerospace. Steel production is energy-intensive and complex; data science supports process optimisation, quality prediction, predictive maintenance and energy and emissions reduction, especially as the company transitions toward lower-carbon steelmaking.

**Upper Austria's industrial champions.** The region is home to many family-owned technology companies that lead global niches — in injection moulding machines, welding and solar technology, fire-fighting vehicles, motorcycles and more. These companies increasingly use AI for smart products, production optimisation and service.

## Software: Observability and the Hagenberg Park

Dynatrace, a software intelligence and observability company founded in Linz, has large engineering operations in Upper Austria. Observability platforms use AI to detect anomalies, identify root causes and automate IT operations across complex cloud environments — work that combines large-scale data engineering with ML.

The Softwarepark Hagenberg, about 20 kilometres from Linz, brings together the University of Applied Sciences Upper Austria's informatics campus, the Software Competence Center Hagenberg (SCCH) — an applied research centre in software and data science — and many software companies.

## Art and Technology

Linz is also home to Ars Electronica, an internationally known festival and centre for art, technology and society. It regularly explores AI's cultural and societal implications, adding a distinctive dimension to the city's tech scene.

## Austrian Employment Conditions

Austrian employment is governed by collective agreements that set minimum pay; job postings must state the minimum salary and willingness to overpay. Salaries are paid in 14 instalments, and employees receive five weeks' holiday. Salaries in Linz are generally somewhat lower than in Vienna, and housing is more affordable.

## Language and Permits

German is the language of daily life and many industrial companies; research groups, software companies and international teams often work in English. EU citizens can work freely. Non-EU professionals can use the Red-White-Red Card or EU Blue Card.

## Living in Linz

Linz lies on the Danube between Vienna and Salzburg, with trains reaching Vienna in about 75 minutes. It combines industrial heritage with modern culture, parks along the river and access to lakes and mountains in the Salzkammergut. Housing is more affordable than in Vienna or Salzburg, and trams and buses cover the city.

## From Research to Industrial AI

Linz is an interesting example of how fundamental ML research can connect with industry. Researchers develop new architectures for sequence modelling and apply them to industrial time series, simulation and control problems. Industrial partners provide real data and challenges — steel production, manufacturing processes, energy systems — while researchers bring new methods. Spin-off companies aim to turn research into products that industrial customers can use. For engineers, this ecosystem offers roles along the whole path from research to deployment: research scientists developing methods, research engineers scaling them and applied ML engineers integrating them into industrial systems.

## Steel Production and Decarbonisation

Steel production involves blast furnaces or electric arc furnaces, rolling mills, heat treatment and finishing, each generating large amounts of process data. Machine learning helps predict product properties, optimise energy use, schedule production and detect equipment problems early. The transition to lower-carbon steelmaking — for example using electric arc furnaces and, in the future, hydrogen — introduces new processes where data-driven optimisation is especially valuable. Engineers in this field contribute directly to reducing industrial emissions.

## Observability and AI for IT Operations

Modern software systems run on complex cloud infrastructures with thousands of services. Observability platforms collect metrics, logs and traces and use AI to detect anomalies, identify root causes and support automated remediation. ML engineers in this field work on time series anomaly detection at massive scale, causal analysis, language models for log understanding and interfaces that help engineers act quickly. The work combines distributed systems, data engineering and ML, and it is highly transferable across the software industry.

## Industrial Champions and Smart Products

Upper Austria's industrial companies build machines and products sold worldwide — injection moulding machines, welding systems, inverters, vehicles and more. AI helps make these products smarter: predicting maintenance needs for machines at customer sites, optimising process settings automatically, analysing welding quality and offering digital services. These companies often combine long engineering traditions with growing digital teams, and they value engineers who understand both ML and the physical products.

## Applied Research at Hagenberg

The Software Competence Center Hagenberg carries out applied research in software engineering and data science with industrial partners, including work on AI in industrial settings, data quality and trustworthy ML. The University of Applied Sciences Upper Austria's Hagenberg campus trains software and data engineers and conducts applied research, making Hagenberg an important talent and innovation source for the region.

## Networking and Settling In

Linz has an active tech community, with events at JKU, the Hagenberg software park and Ars Electronica, plus meetups on data science and software. Newcomers must register their address, obtain a social insurance number and open a bank account.

## Skills That Stand Out

Linz employers value deep learning expertise, time series and sequence modelling, physics-informed ML, large-scale data engineering and industrial domain knowledge. German broadens options significantly in industry.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare annual net pay across 14 instalments, benefits and housing costs. Linz's lower rents compared with Vienna can make its offers attractive.

## Preparing for Interviews

Research roles in Linz focus on publications, research ideas and deep understanding of ML methods. Software companies include coding, system design and ML fundamentals. Industrial companies discuss past projects, domain understanding and deployment in production environments. Prepare to explain trade-offs clearly and to show interest in the specific domain.

## Who Linz Suits Best

Linz suits engineers who want to work close to foundational ML research, on industrial AI in steel and manufacturing, or on large-scale software AI, in an affordable city with easy access to Vienna, the Alps and the lakes.

## Research Careers

JKU offers PhD, postdoc and research positions in ML, often funded by Austrian and European grants and industry collaborations. ELLIS membership connects Linz researchers with Europe's leading ML labs. Research salaries follow university scales and early positions are typically fixed-term, but the scientific environment is exceptional for a city of Linz's size.

## AI for Science and Engineering

Linz's machine learning researchers have also worked on AI for science: applying deep learning to problems in chemistry, biology, physics and engineering, such as predicting molecular properties, accelerating simulations and modelling physical systems. These applications require careful treatment of uncertainty, generalisation beyond training data and integration of domain knowledge. For engineers with backgrounds in physics, chemistry or engineering, AI for science offers a way to combine domain expertise with state-of-the-art ML, and industrial partners in Upper Austria provide concrete problems in materials, processes and energy.

## Working Culture in Upper Austria

Upper Austrian industrial companies tend to be engineering-driven, with long-term thinking, strong apprenticeship and training traditions and loyal workforces. Decision-making can be thorough and consensus-oriented. Software companies and research groups are more international and informal. Across sectors, work-life balance is generally good, with five weeks of holiday and access to nature close to the city.

## Remote and Hybrid Work

Many Linz employers offer hybrid arrangements, though industrial and lab roles require on-site presence. Some professionals live in Linz and work hybrid for Vienna-based employers, taking advantage of fast trains and Linz's lower housing costs.

## Responsible AI

Industrial AI must be reliable and safe, and the EU AI Act classifies AI used as safety components in certain regulated products as high-risk. Engineers who combine ML with documentation, validation and safety practices are valued by Upper Austria's industrial companies, while research groups contribute to making new architectures efficient and trustworthy.

## Beyond Linz: Salzburg, Wels and Steyr

Upper Austria's industrial towns — Wels, Steyr and others — host manufacturing companies with growing data teams, and Salzburg, about an hour away by train, has technology companies and research institutes. Searching by distance across the region, rather than limiting yourself to Linz itself, reveals many more opportunities, especially in industrial AI where employers are often located outside major cities.

## A Final Tip

Attend a JKU public lecture or an Ars Electronica event before deciding; both show the unusual mix of research, industry and culture that makes Linz distinctive.

## Staying Connected
