Eindhoven is the centre of Brainport, one of Europe's most technology-intensive regions. Global high-tech companies, a dense supplier network, a leading technical university and hundreds of startups compete for engineers. For employers, hiring AI engineers in Eindhoven means competing in one of the tightest technical labour markets in the Netherlands — often against household names with large recruitment budgets. This playbook is written for hiring managers and recruiters at companies of all sizes, and especially for SMEs and scale-ups that need to attract ML talent without matching the largest employers' offers. It covers where talent comes from, what candidates look for, how to write vacancies that work and how to handle international hiring and onboarding.

OnlyAIJobs already lists AI vacancies in Eindhoven, and its design — exact addresses, distance from the candidate's home and no paid placement — was shaped by the Brabant talent market described here.

## Understanding the Brainport Talent Market

**Large high-tech employers.** ASML in Veldhoven, Philips, NXP and other large companies employ thousands of engineers and hire continuously, including for machine learning, computer vision and data roles. They offer strong brands, structured careers and competitive packages.

**Suppliers and engineering firms.** A large network of high-tech suppliers, system integrators and engineering service providers works for the large companies and for customers worldwide, and many of them are building AI capabilities.

**Startups and scale-ups.** High Tech Campus Eindhoven, the TU/e campus and Strijp-S host startups in AI, photonics, semiconductors, health technology and mobility.

**Talent pipelines.** Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e) and its Eindhoven Artificial Intelligence Systems Institute (EAISI) produce AI and data science graduates and researchers, with a focus on AI in engineered systems. Fontys University of Applied Sciences trains practically oriented ICT and data professionals. Tilburg University and JADS in 's-Hertogenbosch add data science graduates nearby.

**International talent.** A large share of Brainport's engineers come from abroad, supported by the Holland Expat Center South and regional talent programmes.

## What AI Engineers in Eindhoven Look For

- **Technically meaningful work**: many candidates are drawn to Brainport precisely because of deep engineering problems — models that run on machines, sensors and devices.
- **Ownership**: the chance to design and own a system rather than a small part of it.
- **Colleagues they can learn from**: strong senior engineers and a healthy technical culture.
- **English as working language**, especially for international candidates.
- **Hybrid flexibility and a reasonable commute**: housing is tight, and candidates care about travel time.
- **Transparent pay** and a clear path to a permanent contract.
- **Support with permits, housing and settling in** for international hires.

## Writing a Vacancy That Works in Brainport

1. **A precise title**: "ML Engineer — defect detection for wafer inspection" rather than "AI Specialist".
2. **The problem and the data**: images, sensor streams, logs, simulations — what will the engineer work with?
3. **Where the model runs**: cloud, on-premise, embedded, at customer sites.
4. **The team**: size, seniority and who the engineer will learn from.
5. **Exact office address and hybrid policy**: Eindhoven, Veldhoven, Best, Helmond and the campuses mean different commutes.
6. **Salary range and contract**: the EU Pay Transparency Directive will require pay information before interviews; publishing now builds trust.
7. **Language**: state clearly whether English suffices.
8. **Sponsorship**: say whether you are an IND-recognised sponsor.
9. **Interview process**: steps and timeline.

## Hiring AI Engineers in Eindhoven Without Outbidding

SMEs and scale-ups rarely win on salary alone against the largest employers. They can win on other dimensions:

- **Breadth and ownership**: engineers see the whole system, from data to deployment.
- **Speed**: shorter decision lines and faster iteration.
- **Visibility**: direct contact with customers and leadership.
- **Flexibility**: hybrid arrangements, flexible hours, room to shape practices.
- **Learning budget and conference time**.
- **Honesty**: candidates in Brainport are experienced and notice exaggeration; describe challenges openly.

Because OnlyAIJobs does not sell higher placement, a well-written SME vacancy appears on equal terms with that of a large corporation — which rewards precise, honest vacancy texts.

## International Hiring

Most international AI engineers in the Netherlands are hired under the highly skilled migrant scheme, which requires the employer to be an IND-recognised sponsor and to meet a salary threshold; graduates of Dutch universities within the orientation year have a reduced threshold. The EU Blue Card is an alternative. Becoming a recognised sponsor takes some preparation but makes future hires much faster. The Dutch tax facility for incoming employees (often called the 30% ruling) has been reduced and changed in recent years; explain the current situation honestly to candidates. The Holland Expat Center South helps international employees with registration and practical matters.

## Housing and Onboarding

Housing is one of the biggest obstacles to hiring in Eindhoven. Employers that help with temporary accommodation, provide information on neighbourhoods and towns along train and bus lines, and allow flexibility in the first weeks make a strong impression. Technical onboarding matters as much: hardware, accounts and data access ready on day one, a buddy, and a first project that reaches a real outcome within weeks.

## Designing AI Roles for High-Tech Contexts

Many Brainport companies struggle because they combine several needs in one vacancy. Before writing, clarify which profile you actually need:

- **ML engineer (vision or signal)**: builds and deploys models on images, sensor data or logs.
- **Embedded or edge ML engineer**: optimises models for constrained hardware close to machines.
- **Data engineer**: builds pipelines from machines, test benches and production systems — often the first hire needed.
- **Applied scientist**: explores new methods, often in collaboration with TU/e.
- **MLOps engineer**: brings models into reliable operation and maintains them across product versions.

A focused role attracts better-matched candidates and shortens time to hire. If you need someone to cover several areas in a small team, say so honestly and set priorities.

## Building Pipelines With TU/e and Fontys

The most reliable long-term source of AI talent in Brainport is the region's own education system. Employers that host thesis projects, internships and graduation assignments build relationships with students long before they enter the job market. TU/e's EAISI and its departments welcome industry collaboration on AI in engineered systems, and Fontys students work on practical projects with regional companies. Treat interns as future colleagues: give them real problems, mentors and feedback. Graduates who have worked on your data and met your team are far more likely to accept your offer than candidates who only know your brand from a job board.

## Running an Efficient Interview Process

- **Screening call**: motivation, experience, salary expectations and permit situation.
- **Technical deep dive**: discuss a past project — data, choices, evaluation, deployment.
- **Practical exercise**: a short, relevant task, such as designing an evaluation for a defect detection model; respect candidates' time.
- **Team conversation**: meet future colleagues and see the lab or production environment.
- **Decision within days**: strong candidates have other offers.

Aim to complete the process within three to four weeks and give feedback quickly, including to rejected candidates; Brainport's engineering community is well connected.

## Retaining AI Engineers

Retention protects your hiring investment. AI engineers in Eindhoven commonly leave when their work becomes routine, when they lack senior colleagues to learn from, when pay falls behind the market or when commuting becomes a burden. Practices that help include regular career conversations, visible impact of models on products and customers, conference and learning budgets, transparent salary reviews and flexible working arrangements. For international employees, support with Dutch language learning and integration helps them build a long-term life in the region.

## Responsible AI in High-Tech Products

Many Brainport products fall under strict regulation: medical devices, machinery, automotive components. The EU AI Act classifies AI systems used as safety components in certain regulated products as high-risk, with requirements for risk management, data governance, documentation and human oversight. Engineers increasingly want to work for employers that take this seriously. Mentioning your quality and governance practices in vacancies signals maturity and attracts candidates who care about building AI properly.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Vague titles and long technology lists** that deter experienced candidates.
- **Missing salary information**, which reduces response rates.
- **Requiring Dutch when the team works in English**, excluding much of the talent pool.
- **Slow processes** that lose candidates to faster employers.
- **Ignoring housing and relocation**, which causes late withdrawals.
- **Underinvesting in onboarding**, which leads to early departures.

## Measuring Hiring Success

Track time to hire, qualified applicants per vacancy, the share of candidates within commuting distance, offer acceptance rates, reasons for declined offers and first-year retention. These metrics show whether changes to your vacancy texts and process actually work.

## Final Advice for Brainport Employers

Treat every AI vacancy as a product for engineers: describe the problem honestly, be transparent about pay and conditions, make the location and commute clear and move fast. In a market where the best engineers can choose, clarity and respect are competitive advantages that do not depend on the size of your recruitment budget.

## Where Candidates Live and Commute

Eindhoven's housing shortage means many engineers live in surrounding municipalities — Veldhoven, Best, Helmond, Geldrop, Son en Breugel, Waalre — or further away in 's-Hertogenbosch, Tilburg, Weert or even across the Belgian border in the Kempen region. Sites differ greatly in accessibility: High Tech Campus is reached by bus and bike, Veldhoven by car and bus, the TU/e campus and Strijp-S by train and bike, and industrial estates often mainly by car. When hiring, look at where your current engineers live and how they travel, publish the exact address and transport options in the vacancy and consider flexible hours that avoid peak congestion on the A2 and A67. Offering a bicycle scheme, public transport reimbursement or a realistic hybrid policy can widen your effective recruitment radius considerably. Candidates increasingly filter vacancies by travel time, so a clear description of the commute is not a detail — it is part of your offer.

## Working With Regional Partners

Brainport Development, the Holland Expat Center South, universities and industry associations offer programmes for talent attraction, international onboarding and SME support. Participating in shared initiatives — joint career events, regional traineeships, internship pools — lets smaller employers reach candidates they would not reach alone.

## Compensation Benchmarks

Benchmark salaries regularly using collective agreement scales, regional salary surveys and your own offer data, and review existing employees' pay when the market moves. In Brainport, where engineers compare notes, internal fairness matters as much as competitive starting offers.

## Real example

### A Brainport supplier filling ML roles against big competition

A high-tech supplier near Eindhoven struggled for months to hire two ML engineers for vision-based quality inspection. Candidates chose larger employers nearby.

The hiring manager rewrote the vacancy: a specific title, a description of the inspection images and production lines, the exact address with parking and bus connection, English as working language, a salary range, a two-day hybrid policy, IND sponsorship and a four-step process completed within three weeks. The vacancy emphasised ownership — the engineers would design the inspection system end to end — and included a short description of the senior engineer they would work with. The company also offered thesis projects to TU/e and Fontys students.

Applications improved, and both roles were filled within two months — one by an experienced engineer who preferred ownership over a larger brand, the other by a TU/e graduate who had done a thesis at the company.
