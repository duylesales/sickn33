Vienna regularly ranks among the most liveable cities in the world, and for machine learning professionals it offers more than coffee houses and concerts. The Austrian capital has strong technical universities, internationally recognised research institutes, a banking sector with Central and Eastern European reach, a lively fintech and software scene and a cluster of international organisations. AI jobs in Vienna therefore span fundamental research, financial services, software products, industry and public institutions. This guide explains the landscape, the particularities of Austrian employment and what experienced engineers should consider before moving.

## The Research Landscape

**TU Wien.** Vienna's technical university has a large informatics faculty with research groups in machine learning, databases, knowledge representation, computer vision and theoretical computer science. Vienna has a notable tradition in logic and knowledge-based AI, and TU Wien's research reflects this alongside modern machine learning.

**University of Vienna.** Austria's largest university has a faculty of computer science with data science and machine learning groups, as well as strong mathematics and cognitive science.

**ISTA.** The Institute of Science and Technology Austria, located in Klosterneuburg just outside Vienna, is a research institute for basic research in the natural and computer sciences. Its machine learning and computer science groups are internationally recognised, and it offers a doctoral programme with an international profile.

**The Linz connection.** Johannes Kepler University Linz, about an hour and a quarter from Vienna by train, is known for its Institute for Machine Learning, where Sepp Hochreiter — co-inventor of the LSTM — leads research. Linz also has a strong industrial base and technology companies.

**Other institutes.** The Complexity Science Hub Vienna studies complex systems with data-driven methods. The Austrian Institute of Technology (AIT) conducts applied research, including in AI and digital systems, and various other institutes contribute to the ecosystem.

## Austrian Employment Conditions

Austria's employment system has some distinctive features that matter when evaluating AI jobs in Vienna.

**Collective agreements.** Almost all employees in Austria are covered by a collective agreement (Kollektivvertrag), which sets minimum salaries by job category and experience, working hours and other conditions. The IT sector has its own collective agreement, and banks, insurers and industry have theirs. Job postings in Austria are legally required to state the minimum salary under the applicable collective agreement and whether overpayment is possible — a useful feature for candidates.

**Fourteen salaries.** Austrian salaries are typically paid in 14 instalments: 12 monthly salaries plus holiday pay and Christmas pay, which are taxed favourably. When comparing with other countries, calculate annual net pay rather than monthly figures.

**Holidays and working time.** Employees are entitled to five weeks of paid holiday, rising to six weeks after longer service. Standard working weeks are around 38.5 to 40 hours, with overtime regulated.

**Language.** German is the language of daily life and many workplaces. Research institutes, international organisations, startups and some tech companies work in English. German skills broaden your options considerably, particularly in banking, industry and public institutions.

## Permits

EU and EEA citizens can work in Austria and must register their residence. Non-EU professionals can use the Red-White-Red Card, a points-based residence and work permit for very highly qualified workers, skilled workers in shortage occupations and other key workers, or the EU Blue Card for highly qualified employees meeting salary requirements. Researchers have specific procedures. Requirements and thresholds change; check official Austrian sources such as the migration portal and your employer's experience.

## Living in Vienna

Vienna combines imperial architecture, extensive green spaces, excellent public transport and a strong cultural life. A notable feature is its large stock of municipal and subsidised housing, which contributes to more moderate rents than in many European capitals, although private rents and new-build apartments have become more expensive and newcomers often start in the private market. The annual public transport pass is affordable, and many people live without a car. The city is safe, family-friendly and well connected by train to Bratislava, Budapest, Prague, Munich and beyond.

## Reading an Austrian Job Posting

Austrian job postings have a distinctive element that helps candidates: the legally required salary statement. A posting might say that the minimum gross monthly salary under the collective agreement is a certain amount, with "willingness to overpay depending on qualifications and experience". The stated minimum is often well below what experienced AI engineers actually earn; it is a floor, not an indication of the offer. Use it as a starting point and ask about the realistic range early in the process.

Other elements to check:

- **Collective agreement and job category:** determines minimum pay progression and some benefits.
- **All-in contracts:** some senior contracts include overtime in the salary ("all-in"); understand how many hours are expected.
- **Hybrid work:** Austria has specific rules for home office, including allowances in some cases.
- **Probation and notice periods:** a one-month probation period is common, and notice periods depend on tenure and contract.

## Banking and Fintech AI in Vienna

Vienna's banking groups serve customers across Austria and Central and Eastern Europe, which gives data scientists access to diverse markets and large datasets. Typical applications include credit scoring, early warning systems for loan defaults, fraud detection, anti-money-laundering, customer segmentation, next-best-offer models and document processing. Banks increasingly use language models for internal knowledge management and customer service support, within strict governance.

Model risk management is central in banking. Models must be validated, documented and monitored, and regulators expect transparency. Under the EU AI Act, AI systems used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons are generally considered high-risk, adding further requirements. Engineers who understand both ML and governance are in demand.

Fintech companies, including crypto and investment platforms, work on risk, fraud, compliance and product analytics, often in English-speaking teams with fast-moving product cycles.

## Research Careers: ISTA, TU Wien and Beyond

For research-oriented engineers, the Vienna region offers a dense cluster of institutions. ISTA's model of independent research groups, an international doctoral school and strong funding attracts researchers from around the world. TU Wien and the University of Vienna offer PhD and postdoc positions funded by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF), European programmes and industry cooperation. The Vienna Science and Technology Fund (WWTF) supports research projects in the city, including in information and communication technology.

Research careers in Austria follow a structure common in German-speaking countries: doctoral positions, postdocs, and then tenure-track or senior positions. Fixed-term contracts are common in the early stages. Many researchers move into industry after a PhD or postdoc, where their expertise in areas such as machine learning theory, computer vision or knowledge representation is valued.

## International Organisations

Vienna is one of the United Nations' headquarters locations, hosting organisations such as the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), the United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO) and the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC), as well as the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) and OPEC. These organisations employ statisticians, data analysts and IT specialists, and increasingly explore AI for analysis and operations. Recruitment follows their own procedures and often involves competitive, lengthy selection processes. Employment conditions, including tax status, differ from those of regular Austrian employers. For data professionals interested in international public service, Vienna offers an unusual concentration of such opportunities.

## Integration and Bureaucracy

Moving to Austria involves several administrative steps: registering your address (Meldezettel) within a few days of moving in, obtaining a social insurance number, opening a bank account and, for non-EU citizens, completing residence permit procedures. Vienna's services for newcomers and many employers' HR departments help with these steps, but patience is needed. Learning German early helps not only at work but in dealing with authorities, landlords and everyday life. Vienna offers many language schools, and some courses are subsidised.

## Questions to Ask Vienna Employers

- Which collective agreement applies, and what is the realistic salary range beyond the stated minimum?
- Is the contract all-in, and how are overtime and on-call handled?
- What is the working language of the team and of the wider organisation?
- How do models move to production, and how is model risk managed?
- What support is offered for relocation, German courses and permit procedures?

Clear answers help you compare Vienna offers with those from other countries on equal terms, and they often reveal how mature and international the employer's AI practice is.

## Beyond Vienna: Linz and Graz

Austria's AI landscape extends beyond the capital. Linz combines JKU's machine learning research with a strong industrial base in steel, chemicals and machinery, and technology companies with significant engineering teams. Research spin-offs from the Linz machine learning ecosystem have attracted attention, including work on new model architectures. Graz, Austria's second-largest city, has TU Graz, a well-known technical university with research in computer vision, security and embedded systems, and a cluster of automotive and semiconductor companies. Both cities offer lower living costs than Vienna and good train connections, and some employers allow hybrid arrangements across locations. For engineers focused on industrial AI or specific research groups, they can be as attractive as the capital.

## Salary Negotiation in Austria

Salary negotiations in Austria usually focus on the annual gross salary divided into 14 payments. Research market levels for your role, consider the collective agreement minimum as a floor and negotiate based on your experience and the value you bring. Benefits such as a public transport pass, meal allowances, training budgets and flexible working are also part of the discussion. Be clear and factual; Austrian employers generally appreciate well-prepared, polite negotiation.

## Building a Network in Vienna

Vienna's AI community meets at university lectures, meetups on data science and machine learning, startup events and conferences. Attending a few regularly is the fastest way to learn which teams are growing and to meet future colleagues.

## Real example

### A fraud detection specialist moving from Frankfurt to Vienna

A data scientist working on fraud detection at a German bank in Frankfurt receives an offer from a Vienna fintech. The monthly salary looks lower, but she learns that Austrian salaries are paid 14 times a year, with favourable taxation of the extra payments.

She calculates annual net pay, compares rents and the cost of Vienna's annual transport pass with her Frankfurt costs, and finds the offers roughly comparable. The fintech works in English, offers a hybrid arrangement and gives her ownership of transaction risk models across several European markets. She accepts, starts German lessons and, after a year, says Vienna's quality of life exceeded her expectations — though she notes that bureaucratic procedures on arrival took patience.
