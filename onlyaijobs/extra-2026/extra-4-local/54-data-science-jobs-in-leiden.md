Leiden is the Netherlands' oldest university city and one of its most important life science locations. Between Amsterdam and The Hague, with Schiphol Airport nearby, it combines Leiden University, the Leiden University Medical Center, the country's largest life science park and — just up the coast in Noordwijk — the European Space Agency's largest establishment. For students and graduates, data science jobs in Leiden are concentrated in biomedical research, pharma and biotech, health data, space and scientific data, with access to the wider Randstad job market. This guide explains where to look and how to build a career from Leiden.

## Leiden University and Its Data Science Research

Leiden University has a long tradition in mathematics, statistics and computer science. The Leiden Institute of Advanced Computer Science (LIACS) conducts research and teaching in AI, machine learning, data science, computer vision, bioinformatics and algorithms, with many English-taught programmes. The Leiden Centre of Data Science connects researchers across faculties — from astronomy and medicine to law and humanities — who use data science in their work. Leiden's Mathematical Institute has strong statistics research, particularly relevant for health data.

The university's campus in The Hague focuses on governance, security and global affairs, including the governance of technology, which connects Leiden to the policy side of AI.

## LUMC and the Leiden Bio Science Park

The Leiden University Medical Center (LUMC) is one of the Netherlands' leading academic hospitals, with research in genetics, immunology, oncology, imaging and clinical epidemiology. Its bioinformatics, biostatistics and medical imaging groups use machine learning extensively.

The Leiden Bio Science Park, adjacent to the university and LUMC, is the largest life science cluster in the Netherlands, hosting many biotech and pharmaceutical companies, research institutes and startups. Companies work on vaccines, cell and gene therapies, drug discovery and diagnostics. Data science roles include bioinformatics, analysis of experimental and clinical data, laboratory automation and manufacturing analytics.

## Space and Scientific Data

The European Space Research and Technology Centre (ESTEC) in Noordwijk, around 15 kilometres from Leiden, is ESA's largest site and its technical heart, where spacecraft and missions are designed and tested. Around ESTEC, companies in the Space Business Park and the wider region provide engineering and data services. Leiden University's astronomy institute, one of Europe's oldest, works with enormous datasets from telescopes and space missions. Naturalis Biodiversity Center, the national natural history museum and research institute in Leiden, applies data science and image recognition to biodiversity collections.

## Entry Paths

1. **Master's research projects** with LUMC, companies on the Bio Science Park or institutes.
2. **PhD positions**, which in the Netherlands are paid employment.
3. **Junior bioinformatics and data science roles** in biotech and pharma.
4. **Traineeships and graduate programmes** at larger organisations in the Randstad.
5. **Space sector roles** through contractors and programmes linked to ESA; staff positions at ESA follow their own recruitment rules and generally require citizenship of member states.

## Language and Practicalities

Research groups, biotech companies and the space sector in the region largely work in English. Hospitals and public organisations often require Dutch for roles involving patient contact or public services. International graduates of Dutch universities can use the orientation year, followed by the highly skilled migrant scheme with a reduced salary threshold for recent graduates. Check current IND rules.

## Health Data Science: What the Work Involves

Health data science at LUMC and in the region spans many activities. Biostatisticians design studies and analyse clinical trials. Bioinformaticians process genomic, transcriptomic and proteomic data. Imaging scientists build models for MRI, CT and microscopy. Clinical data scientists develop prediction models from electronic health records and evaluate them with clinicians. Across all of these, careful methodology matters: understanding bias, confounding, missing data and validation is as important as model performance. Patient data is protected by GDPR and medical ethics rules, and AI tools intended for clinical use may fall under the EU Medical Device Regulation and the high-risk requirements of the EU AI Act. Graduates who combine ML skills with statistical rigour and respect for these frameworks are highly valued.

## Biotech and Pharma Data Roles

Companies on the Leiden Bio Science Park use data science throughout the drug development process: analysing high-throughput experiments, supporting target discovery, modelling biological pathways, analysing clinical data and optimising manufacturing of biologics and vaccines. Many roles involve working closely with laboratory scientists, so curiosity about biology and the ability to communicate with non-programmers are essential. Regulated environments require documentation and validated systems, which gives graduates valuable experience in quality practices that transfer to many industries.

## Scientific Computing and Astronomy

Leiden's astronomy tradition connects to some of the largest datasets in science. Telescopes and space missions generate petabytes of data, and astronomers use machine learning to classify galaxies, detect transient events, analyse spectra and simulate the universe. Graduates with physics, astronomy or computer science backgrounds can find research software engineering and data science roles in these projects, often in international collaborations. The skills — handling very large datasets, efficient computing, rigorous statistics — transfer well to industry, and many astronomy graduates move into data science careers in companies across the Randstad.

## Biodiversity and Image Recognition

Naturalis Biodiversity Center holds one of the world's largest natural history collections. Digitising millions of specimens, recognising species from images and analysing biodiversity data are areas where machine learning plays a growing role. Such projects are smaller in number but offer unusual and meaningful work at the intersection of AI, biology and conservation.

## Building a Profile for Leiden Employers

- **Statistical rigour**: solid understanding of study design, inference and validation.
- **Domain knowledge**: courses or projects in biology, medicine, astronomy or earth sciences.
- **Reproducible workflows**: version control, documented pipelines, containers and workflow tools common in bioinformatics.
- **Programming**: Python and R, SQL and experience with high-performance or cloud computing.
- **Communication**: explaining results to scientists and clinicians.

A master's research project with a company or research group on the Bio Science Park is often the strongest entry point.

## Living in Leiden

Leiden is a compact, historic city with canals, almshouses and a lively student culture. Most places are reachable by bike, and the central station offers fast trains to Amsterdam, Schiphol, The Hague and Rotterdam. Housing is in high demand, particularly for students and young professionals, and rents are relatively high, though generally below Amsterdam levels. Many people live in surrounding towns such as Oegstgeest, Leiderdorp or Voorschoten, or commute from The Hague. The beaches at Katwijk and Noordwijk are close for summer weekends.

## Questions to Ask Leiden Employers

- What data will I work with, and how is access to sensitive data organised?
- Who will mentor me, and how are juniors supported?
- For PhD positions: what is the project, who funds it and what are the collaboration partners?
- What is the working language, and is Dutch needed for any part of the role?
- Are you an IND-recognised sponsor if I need a permit?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what is the salary range?

## Your First Two Years

Use your first years to build depth in one domain — genomics, imaging, clinical data or scientific computing — while strengthening engineering practices and statistical judgement. Stay connected to the university through seminars and alumni networks. These foundations prepare you for senior roles in Leiden's life science ecosystem or anywhere in the Randstad.

## Traineeships and Graduate Programmes

Beyond research and biotech, many Randstad employers — banks, insurers, government, consultancies and grid operators — run data traineeships that suit Leiden graduates. These programmes typically last one to two years, combine rotations with training and offer good conditions by Dutch graduate standards. Because Leiden lies between Amsterdam and The Hague, graduates can apply to programmes in both cities without moving. Comparing such options by distance from your home helps you choose programmes that fit your life as well as your ambitions.

## Dutch Graduate Conditions

Dutch packages usually include a holiday allowance of around eight percent, pension contributions, travel reimbursement and often a training budget. PhD candidates receive salaries on university scales with similar benefits. Many first contracts are fixed-term, with a path to permanent employment. When comparing offers, weigh mentoring and learning as seriously as salary.

## The Space Sector Around Noordwijk

ESTEC in Noordwijk is where many European spacecraft are designed and tested, and it attracts engineers from all ESA member states. Around it, the Space Business Park and companies in the region provide engineering, software, testing and data services. Data science applications include analysis of test campaign data, anomaly detection in spacecraft subsystems, processing of Earth observation imagery and support for mission design. ESA also runs programmes for young graduates, offering temporary positions to recent graduates from member states, which can be an excellent entry into the space sector. Positions with contractors follow normal Dutch employment conditions. For graduates in physics, aerospace, electrical engineering or computer science, the proximity of ESTEC makes Leiden a surprisingly strong base for a space career.

## Research Software Engineering

A growing career path in Leiden's research ecosystem is research software engineering: professionals who build and maintain the software, pipelines and infrastructure that scientists depend on. Research software engineers work at the intersection of software engineering and science, improving code quality, performance and reproducibility. The Netherlands has an active research software engineering community, and universities and institutes increasingly offer permanent positions for this role. For graduates who love programming and science but prefer building tools over publishing papers, it is an attractive and stable path.

## Networking in Leiden

Leiden University and LUMC host frequent seminars, the Bio Science Park organises events for its community and data science meetups in Leiden, The Hague and Amsterdam are within easy reach. Attend regularly, ask researchers and engineers about their work and follow up — many first jobs in Leiden come from these conversations.

## Real example

### A bioinformatics graduate who stayed in Leiden

An international master's student in bioinformatics at Leiden University did her research project at a biotech company on the Bio Science Park, analysing single-cell sequencing data. Many classmates planned to move to Amsterdam.

The company offered her a junior bioinformatician role, and LUMC offered a PhD position in computational immunology. She compared both and chose the company, where her project continued directly and she could cycle to work. After two years, she leads data analysis for one of the company's research programmes. She says Leiden's concentration of life science employers meant she never needed to leave the city to find meaningful work.
