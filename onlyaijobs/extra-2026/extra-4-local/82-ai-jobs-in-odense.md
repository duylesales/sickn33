Odense, Denmark's third-largest city and the birthplace of Hans Christian Andersen, has built one of Europe's most notable robotics clusters. Companies that pioneered collaborative robots and autonomous mobile robots were founded here, and dozens of robotics and automation firms have followed, supported by the University of Southern Denmark and a dedicated cluster organisation. Odense also hosts one of Denmark's largest university hospitals and an industrial port with offshore wind activities. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Odense combine robotics, perception and learning, health technology and industrial automation, in a compact city with a high quality of life. This guide explains the landscape.

## The Odense Robotics Cluster

Odense's robotics story is well known in the industry. Universal Robots, founded in Odense, helped create the market for collaborative robots — robot arms designed to work safely alongside people. Mobile Industrial Robots (MiR), also founded in Odense, develops autonomous mobile robots for logistics in factories and warehouses. Both are now part of the US company Teradyne and retain major operations in the city. Around them, Odense Robotics, the cluster organisation, brings together many companies working on robots, automation, drones and related technologies.

AI and machine learning play a growing role in robotics: perception for picking and navigation, learning from demonstration, motion planning, anomaly detection, fleet management and user interfaces that make robots easier to program. Engineers in Odense work on problems that combine ML with control, mechanics and real-world uncertainty.

## University of Southern Denmark

The University of Southern Denmark (SDU) has its main campus in Odense. Its Maersk Mc-Kinney Moller Institute conducts research in robotics, AI, drones, software engineering and health informatics, and works closely with the robotics cluster. SDU's engineering and science faculties and its health sciences add further research in medical AI, data science and energy.

## Health Technology

Odense University Hospital (OUH) is one of Denmark's largest hospitals and is part of a major new hospital development in the city. Together with SDU's health sciences and companies in health technology, it creates opportunities in medical imaging, clinical data science, hospital logistics and welfare technology — including robotics in healthcare, a field where Odense's two strengths meet. Denmark's comprehensive health registers support research, within strict data protection rules.

## Offshore Wind and Industry

The Lindø industrial park at the Port of Odense, a former shipyard, now hosts companies in offshore wind, heavy manufacturing and logistics. Data science supports maintenance, logistics and production optimisation in these industries. Other manufacturers on Funen and in southern Denmark use automation and AI in production.

## Danish Working Culture and Permits

Danish workplaces are flat, trust-based and balanced, with moderate working hours, generous holidays and extensive parental leave. English is widely used in robotics companies and research; Danish helps with integration. EU citizens can work freely and register for a CPR number. Non-EU professionals typically use the Pay Limit Scheme, the Positive List or fast-track procedures; the EU Blue Card applies differently in Denmark. Denmark has high taxes, with a special scheme for certain foreign researchers and highly paid employees.

## Living in Odense

Odense is compact and bicycle-friendly, with a historic centre, a new light rail line and parks along the river. Housing is more affordable than in Copenhagen. Trains connect Odense to Copenhagen in around 1.5 hours and to Aarhus in about 1.5 hours, making it a central location in Denmark. The island of Funen offers countryside, beaches and small towns.

## Machine Learning in Robotics

Robotics presents some of the most interesting challenges in applied ML. Robots must perceive cluttered, changing environments, handle objects of varying shapes and materials, navigate among people and adapt to new tasks without extensive reprogramming. ML engineers in Odense work on object detection and pose estimation for picking, grasp planning, semantic mapping and navigation for mobile robots, anomaly detection in robot joints and motors, and learning from demonstration so that workers can teach robots new tasks. Safety is fundamental: collaborative robots work near people, and systems must meet safety standards. The EU Machinery Regulation and the EU AI Act, which classifies AI used as safety components in certain products as high-risk, shape how learning-based features are developed and validated. Engineers who combine ML with an understanding of safety and real-world robustness are in high demand.

## Automation Integrators and Industrial Vision

Beyond robot manufacturers, Odense and southern Denmark have many automation integrators that design and install robotic cells and production lines for manufacturers. Their engineers combine robots, vision systems, PLCs and software to automate tasks such as palletising, machine tending, welding and inspection. Machine vision is central, and increasingly uses deep learning for defect detection and flexible picking. These roles are practical and varied, and they offer exposure to many industries.

## Welfare Technology and Health Robotics

Denmark has invested in welfare technology — technology that supports care for elderly and disabled people and relieves pressure on healthcare staff. Odense has been active in this area, combining its robotics expertise with hospitals and municipalities. Examples include robots that assist with logistics in hospitals, disinfection, rehabilitation and care support. AI helps these systems navigate, recognise situations and adapt to users. Projects must address safety, privacy and acceptance by staff and patients, making them as much about human-centred design as about technology.

## Medical AI at the University Hospital

Odense University Hospital and SDU researchers apply AI to medical imaging, pathology, clinical prediction and hospital operations. Denmark's national health registers enable large-scale research, and AI tools intended for clinical use must meet the EU Medical Device Regulation and high-risk requirements under the AI Act. Research and clinical data science roles offer meaningful work with strong methodological standards.

## Questions to Ask Odense Employers

- What will I work on — perception, planning, fleet software or applications?
- How much time is spent on real hardware versus simulation?
- What is the working language of the team?
- How are safety and validation handled for learning-based features?
- What relocation support is offered, including help with housing and CPR registration?

## Networking and Settling In

The robotics cluster organises events, meetups and an annual robotics conference, and SDU hosts seminars and hackathons. The robotics community is close-knit and welcoming. Newcomers register to obtain a CPR number and can access free Danish lessons.

## Skills That Stand Out

Odense employers value computer vision, 3D perception, motion planning, ROS and C++, embedded systems, simulation and a practical, hands-on attitude. Experience with safety standards and testing is an advantage.

## Preparing for Interviews

Robotics interviews often include coding tasks in C++ or Python, questions on geometry, kinematics and perception, system design for a robotic application and discussion of past hands-on projects. Some companies include practical exercises with real robots or simulations. Be ready to explain how you handled real-world messiness — sensor noise, calibration, edge cases.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare net pay after Danish taxes, pension and benefits, and housing costs. Odense's lower rents compared with Copenhagen and short commutes add real value.

## Who Odense Suits Best

Odense suits engineers who love robotics and automation, want to see their code move real machines and value a compact, affordable city with a strong, collaborative tech community.

## Robotics Startups and Scale-ups

The success of Odense's pioneering robot companies has inspired a wave of startups: companies building robots for agriculture, construction, healthcare, inspection and logistics, as well as software for programming, simulating and managing robots. Founders are often former employees of the larger robotics companies or SDU researchers, and the cluster organisation, incubators and investors support them. For engineers, startups offer broad responsibility — you might work on perception, integration and customer pilots in the same month — and equity, with the usual uncertainty about funding. Odense's startup community is collaborative, and knowledge flows easily between companies, which accelerates learning for everyone involved.

## Drones and Autonomous Systems

SDU and companies in the region also work on drones for inspection, delivery and environmental monitoring, supported by test facilities for unmanned aerial systems. AI supports navigation, perception and data analysis from drone flights. Engineers interested in autonomous systems beyond ground robots will find relevant research and industry projects in Odense.

## Responsible Robotics

Robots that work alongside people raise questions of safety, trust and workplace impact. Danish organisations tend to involve employees and unions when introducing automation, and engineers who understand human-centred design, safety standards and the EU AI Act help ensure that robotics benefits both companies and workers.

## Research Careers at SDU

The University of Southern Denmark offers PhD, postdoc and research engineer positions in robotics, AI, drones, health informatics and energy. Danish PhD positions are paid employment with pensions and holidays, and many projects are carried out with companies in the robotics cluster or with the university hospital. SDU's close connection to industry makes transitions between research and companies common: many engineers at Odense robotics firms have SDU backgrounds, and some return to the university for collaborations or part-time teaching. For experienced engineers, research roles provide a chance to explore new methods — for example learning-based manipulation, human–robot interaction or robot safety — while remaining close to real applications.

## Offshore Wind Operations

The Lindø industrial park hosts companies that manufacture and handle components for offshore wind farms. Data science supports production planning, logistics of very large components, quality control and maintenance of heavy equipment. As European offshore wind capacity grows, these operations will continue to need engineers who can optimise complex industrial and logistics processes.

## Remote and Hybrid Work

Robotics work usually requires time in labs and with hardware, so fully remote roles are rare. Many Odense employers nevertheless offer flexible hours and some remote days, in line with Danish work culture. Clarify expectations during the interview process.

## A Final Tip

Ask to see the lab or test area during your interview; in robotics, the quality of the hardware setup says a lot about how seriously a company invests in its engineers.

## Settling Into Danish Life

Newcomers to Odense often find the city easy to navigate and welcoming. Joining sports clubs, associations and language classes helps build friendships, and the robotics community's regular events make it easy to meet peers. Danish winters are dark, so social routines and outdoor activity help, while summers offer long days on Funen's beaches and countryside.

## Staying Connected

Odense's robotics cluster is tightly networked; attend a few cluster events in your first months and you will quickly know people across many companies.
