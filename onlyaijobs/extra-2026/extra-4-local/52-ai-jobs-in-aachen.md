Aachen, at Germany's western edge where the country meets the Netherlands and Belgium, is one of Europe's great engineering cities. RWTH Aachen University is among Germany's largest and most renowned technical universities, and its campus and surrounding institutes form a powerhouse of research in production engineering, mechanical and electrical engineering, computer science and materials. Nearby, Forschungszentrum Jülich operates some of Europe's most powerful supercomputers. For students and graduates, AI jobs in Aachen are strongly oriented toward engineering: machine learning for manufacturing, lasers, vehicles, communications and scientific computing. And because Maastricht and Liège are within easy reach, the job market extends across three countries. This guide explains where to look and how to get started.

## RWTH Aachen and the Research Campus

RWTH Aachen University has a long tradition of close collaboration with industry. Its computer science department has research groups in machine learning, computer vision, natural language processing, robotics and data management, and AI is integrated into many engineering disciplines. RWTH's work on the "Internet of Production", a major interdisciplinary research initiative, explores how data and AI can transform manufacturing by connecting machines, processes and products.

The RWTH Aachen Campus hosts research clusters in which companies and institutes work side by side on topics such as production engineering, automotive, logistics and photonics. Fraunhofer institutes in Aachen — including those for production technology (IPT), laser technology (ILT) and molecular biology and applied ecology — carry out applied research with companies. FH Aachen University of Applied Sciences trains practically oriented engineers and computer scientists.

## Forschungszentrum Jülich and Supercomputing

Forschungszentrum Jülich, about 30 kilometres from Aachen, is one of Europe's largest interdisciplinary research centres. Its Jülich Supercomputing Centre hosts JUPITER, the European exascale supercomputer procured by the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, as well as other large systems. Research at Jülich covers energy, climate, neuroscience, materials and information technology, including AI and quantum computing. For graduates, Jülich offers PhD positions, research software engineering roles and work on large-scale scientific ML and high-performance computing — rare opportunities for those interested in the infrastructure behind modern AI.

## Industry and Companies

The Aachen region hosts automotive research centres, telecom R&D (Ericsson has long-standing research and development activities in the region), engineering service providers, manufacturing companies and many startups spun out of RWTH. Startups work on industrial software, robotics, photonics, energy and mobility. Companies in the wider region, including the Cologne area and the Rhenish mining area undergoing structural change, add further opportunities.

## Machine Learning in Production Engineering

Aachen's defining strength is the application of AI to production. Typical projects include:

- **Process monitoring**: using sensor data from machining, welding, laser processing or injection moulding to detect defects and deviations in real time.
- **Quality prediction**: predicting product quality from process parameters, reducing the need for costly inspections.
- **Optimisation of process parameters**: combining physical models and data to find settings that improve quality and reduce waste.
- **Digital twins**: virtual representations of machines and processes that support simulation, planning and control.
- **Human–robot collaboration and robotics**: perception and learning for flexible automation.

These problems require collaboration with mechanical and production engineers, an understanding of physics and measurement, and robust deployment close to machines.

## Entry Paths

**HiWi and working student positions** at RWTH institutes, Fraunhofer and companies are the most common way to gain experience during studies. Aachen has a very large number of such positions because of the density of research institutes.

**Master's theses** with institutes or companies often lead to job offers.

**PhD positions at RWTH institutes**, particularly in engineering, frequently involve industry projects and are paid as employment on public-sector scales, making them attractive career steps rather than purely academic choices.

**Junior roles** in companies and startups in Aachen, Cologne and the cross-border region.

## Language

Many research groups and international master's programmes at RWTH work in English. Engineering institutes and companies in the region, however, often use German, particularly in industry projects. For long-term careers, German is a significant advantage. In the cross-border region, Dutch and French can also be useful.

## The Border Triangle: One Labour Market, Three Countries

Aachen is part of the Meuse-Rhine Euroregion, together with Dutch South Limburg and the Belgian provinces of Liège and Limburg, including the German-speaking community of Belgium. Maastricht is about 30 minutes away, Heerlen even closer, and Liège less than an hour. Maastricht University and Hasselt University, along with Liège's university, add research and graduates, and the Brightlands campuses in Dutch Limburg host companies in chemicals, materials, health and smart services.

For job seekers, this means that searching by distance rather than by country reveals many more opportunities. For cross-border workers, however, questions about tax, social security, health insurance and recognition of qualifications must be clarified. Bilateral agreements and EU coordination rules apply, and cross-border information points in the region provide advice. It is worth consulting them before accepting a job in another country.

## Supercomputing and Scientific ML

Jülich's supercomputers make the Aachen region one of Europe's best places for graduates interested in high-performance computing and large-scale AI. Research software engineers optimise scientific codes and AI workloads for supercomputers, support researchers in training large models and develop tools for data management and workflow automation. Scientific ML projects include climate and weather modelling, materials simulation, brain research and energy systems. These roles build expertise in distributed computing, GPU programming and performance engineering — skills that are in high demand across industry and research, including in companies training large AI models.

## Building a Profile for Aachen Employers

Aachen employers, especially in engineering, value graduates who combine ML skills with an understanding of physical systems:

- **Signal processing and time series**: many industrial problems involve sensor data.
- **Physics-informed and hybrid modelling**: combining engineering models with data-driven components.
- **Deployment close to machines**: edge devices, industrial communication protocols and real-time constraints.
- **Software engineering discipline**: clean code, testing and documentation.
- **Collaboration with engineers**: the ability to learn domain vocabulary and work in interdisciplinary teams.

A thesis or HiWi experience at a production engineering institute is one of the strongest signals for industrial employers in the region.

## Living in Aachen

Aachen is a historic city — its cathedral, founded by Charlemagne, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — with a large student population that gives it a young, lively atmosphere. Housing is more affordable than in Cologne or Düsseldorf, though demand is high near the university. The city has good bus connections, and regional trains link it to Cologne, Maastricht, Liège and Brussels. The countryside of the Eifel and the Dutch and Belgian borderlands are close for weekends.

## Your First Two Years

Use your first years to build depth in one industrial or scientific domain and strong engineering habits. If you start with a PhD at an RWTH institute, take advantage of industry projects to build a network; if you start in a company, stay connected to institutes through collaborations and events. Improve your German — it opens many doors in the region's industry.

## Questions to Ask Aachen Employers

- Is the position fixed-term, and how is it funded?
- Which pay scale or collective agreement applies?
- What is the working language of the team and of industry partners?
- Will I work on-site with machines or production systems?
- What computing resources are available, including access to Jülich systems?
- For cross-border roles: which country's tax and social security rules apply?

Apply for a HiWi position at an institute whose work interests you, choose a thesis linked to industry, attend events on the RWTH Campus and in the Euroregion and search by distance across the border. In Aachen, a few good connections — and a willingness to look beyond national lines — can open many doors.

## Photonics and Laser Technology

Aachen is one of the world's leading centres for laser technology and photonics, with Fraunhofer ILT, RWTH chairs and companies working on laser-based manufacturing, measurement and medical applications. AI plays a growing role: monitoring laser welding and cutting processes with cameras and sensors, predicting the quality of additively manufactured parts, optimising process parameters and analysing optical measurement data. These applications involve high-speed data, physical understanding of light–material interaction and real-time control. For graduates with backgrounds in physics, electrical engineering or computer science, photonics offers a niche where AI skills are scarce and highly valued, and where research and industry are closely connected.

## Automotive and Mobility Research

The Aachen region has a long tradition in automotive engineering, with RWTH institutes for vehicle technology and combustion engines, automotive research centres and engineering service providers. As the industry shifts toward electric and automated vehicles, AI work includes battery analytics, perception and sensor fusion, simulation-based testing and optimisation of vehicle energy systems. Students can gain experience through HiWi positions at vehicle institutes, theses with automotive companies and projects in the region's testing facilities.

## Structural Change and Energy

The Rhenish mining area near Aachen is undergoing structural change as lignite mining is phased out. Significant public investment supports new research institutes, energy projects and companies in the region, including in hydrogen, digitalisation and AI. For graduates, this creates new opportunities in energy systems, digital infrastructure and applied research — often with the explicit goal of building a sustainable economy in the region.

## German Graduate Conditions

German graduate jobs typically include a probation period of up to six months, at least 20 statutory holiday days (commonly 28 to 30), health, pension and unemployment insurance and notice periods set by law or contract. Industrial employers are often covered by the metal and electrical industry collective agreement, which defines pay groups and annual increases. Research positions at RWTH institutes and Jülich follow public-sector pay scales and are usually fixed-term. When comparing offers, consider net pay, contract duration, learning opportunities and how the role builds toward your long-term goals — in Aachen, an engineering PhD with industry projects can be as valuable a career step as a first industry job.

## Staying Connected

Keep in touch with your institute and classmates after graduation; Aachen's engineering network spans three countries and many industries.

## Real example

### An international RWTH graduate who stayed in production AI

A master's student from Turkey studied computer science at RWTH Aachen and worked as a HiWi at a production engineering institute, preparing sensor data from milling machines. His master's thesis developed a model predicting tool wear from vibration signals.

After graduation, the institute offered him a PhD position in a project with several manufacturing companies. He accepted, attracted by the combination of research and industrial application. During his PhD, he improved his German and published on hybrid models combining physics and machine learning. After finishing, he joined a machine builder in the region as an AI engineer. He says Aachen's density of institutes and companies made it possible to build a career in industrial AI without ever feeling that research and practice were separate worlds.
