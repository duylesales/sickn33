Bucharest is Romania's capital and its largest technology market. Over two decades, the city has grown from an outsourcing destination into the home of internationally successful product companies: UiPath, the automation software company, was founded in Bucharest, and Bitdefender, one of the world's major cybersecurity firms, is headquartered there. The city also hosts Romania's leading e-commerce platform, major banks and telecom operators, international R&D centres and a strong mathematics and computer science tradition. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Bucharest offer automation and document AI, security ML, e-commerce, banking analytics and research, at a cost of living well below Western Europe. This guide explains the landscape.

## Home-Grown Product Companies

**Automation.** UiPath, founded in Bucharest in 2005, became a global leader in robotic process automation and now positions itself around AI-powered automation, including document understanding, communications mining and agentic automation. It retains a significant engineering presence in Romania. Automation software combines machine learning, computer vision for user interfaces, document AI and language models to automate business processes.

**Cybersecurity.** Bitdefender, headquartered in Bucharest, develops security products for consumers and businesses worldwide and has long used machine learning for threat detection. Its research teams work on malware detection, anomaly detection and security for AI systems, and the company has been active in cybersecurity research collaborations.

**E-commerce.** eMAG, Romania's largest online retailer and marketplace, is based in Bucharest and operates in several countries in the region. Its data teams work on search, recommendations, pricing, logistics and fraud.

## Banking, Telecom and Corporate Centres

Major banks in Romania, many of them subsidiaries of international groups, have their headquarters in Bucharest and employ data scientists for credit risk, fraud, AML and customer analytics. Telecom operators use ML for network optimisation and churn prediction. Many international companies run R&D, IT and business service centres in Bucharest, from software and telecom to automotive engineering.

## Research and Education

The National University of Science and Technology POLITEHNICA Bucharest is Romania's largest technical university, with strong computer science and automatic control faculties and research in AI, computer vision and robotics. The University of Bucharest's Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science has a strong tradition in mathematics and machine learning. Institutes of the Romanian Academy, including the Institute of Mathematics and the Research Institute for Artificial Intelligence, contribute research, and Romania's success in international mathematics and informatics olympiads reflects the depth of its quantitative talent.

## Pay and Taxes

Salaries in Romania's IT sector are high relative to national averages but generally below Western Europe. Romania previously offered an income tax exemption for many software professionals, which made net pay attractive; this incentive has been reduced and changed in recent years, so check the current rules before comparing offers. Bucharest's cost of living is higher than in other Romanian cities but well below Western European capitals.

## Language and Permits

Romanian is the language of daily life and public institutions; English is the working language in most tech companies. EU citizens can work freely; non-EU professionals typically need a work permit and residence permit or the EU Blue Card. Check official Romanian sources.

## Living in Bucharest

Bucharest is a large, energetic city with Belle Époque architecture, large parks, a lively restaurant and nightlife scene and an extensive metro network. Traffic congestion can be significant, so living near a metro line and close to your office matters. Housing costs are moderate by EU capital standards. The Carpathian mountains and the Black Sea coast are within a few hours for weekends.

## Automation, Document AI and Agents

Enterprise automation has evolved from rule-based bots that mimic clicks to AI-driven systems that read documents, understand emails, interpret screens and increasingly plan multi-step tasks with language models. ML engineers in this field work on document understanding (layout analysis, extraction, classification), computer vision for user interfaces, natural language understanding of business communications and orchestration of AI agents with human oversight. The challenges include handling diverse document formats and languages, providing confidence estimates so humans can review uncertain cases and ensuring security and governance in enterprise environments. Bucharest is one of the world's centres for this kind of work, thanks to the automation industry's Romanian roots.

## Security ML and AI Security

Cybersecurity companies in Bucharest process large volumes of threat data and apply machine learning to detect malware, phishing, network intrusions and fraud. Research teams also study the security of AI systems themselves — adversarial examples, model extraction and data poisoning — and the use of AI by attackers. The combination of adversarial problems, global scale and deep security expertise makes this one of the most intellectually demanding fields in the city. The EU AI Act's requirements for robustness and cybersecurity of high-risk AI systems make such expertise increasingly relevant across industries.

## E-commerce and Regional Marketplaces

A regional e-commerce leader faces the full range of marketplace ML problems: search in Romanian and other regional languages, recommendations, pricing, demand forecasting, logistics and fraud detection. Operating across several countries adds challenges of multilingual data and different consumer behaviour. For ML engineers, e-commerce in Bucharest offers product impact at regional scale and a strong experimentation culture.

## Banking Analytics

Banks in Bucharest apply ML to credit scoring, fraud detection, AML and customer analytics, under regulatory expectations for model governance. Under the EU AI Act, creditworthiness assessment of natural persons is a high-risk use, bringing documentation and oversight requirements. Engineers who combine ML with governance expertise are in demand.

## Evaluating Corporate Centre Roles

Many AI jobs in Bucharest are in R&D and business service centres of international companies. Before joining, ask which products or models the Bucharest team owns, where architects and managers are located and how career progression works locally. Centres that own significant work usually offer more interesting roles and better growth.

## Questions to Ask Bucharest Employers

- What does the Bucharest team own, and where are decisions made?
- What is the working language, including for documentation?
- Where exactly is the office, and how close is it to the metro?
- How is compensation structured under current tax rules?
- What is the hybrid policy?

## Networking and Settling In

Bucharest has a large tech community with meetups on machine learning, data science, security and startups, and major tech conferences. Universities host seminars and competitions. Newcomers need to register their residence, obtain a tax identification number and open a bank account; employers can help.

## Skills That Stand Out

Bucharest employers value strong mathematical foundations, production ML engineering, document AI and NLP, security ML and data engineering. Experience with language model applications, evaluation and enterprise governance is increasingly sought after, especially in automation. English is expected in tech; Romanian helps with career progression in local organisations.

## Who Bucharest Suits Best

Bucharest suits engineers who want product-focused AI work in automation, security or e-commerce, value a large and lively city with lower costs than Western Europe and can navigate traffic by choosing housing wisely.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews at Bucharest tech companies usually include algorithmic coding tasks — Romania's competitive programming tradition makes these rigorous — ML fundamentals, a system design discussion and a deep dive into past projects. Automation companies may ask about document understanding, evaluation of extraction quality or designing human-in-the-loop workflows; security companies about classification under adversarial conditions and false-positive control; e-commerce companies about experimentation and ranking. Prepare to explain trade-offs clearly in English and to ask about team ownership and deployment.

## Research Careers

Researchers in Bucharest can find PhD and postdoc positions at Politehnica, the University of Bucharest and Romanian Academy institutes, often funded by national and European grants. Collaboration with industry, including security and automation companies, is common, and research experience from Bucharest is well regarded internationally.

## Telecom and Network Analytics

Romania has well-developed telecom infrastructure, with fast fixed broadband and competitive mobile markets. Telecom operators in Bucharest use machine learning to optimise networks, predict equipment failures, detect fraud and reduce customer churn, and they increasingly deploy AI in customer service. International telecom vendors also run R&D teams in the city. These roles combine ML with systems knowledge and offer stable employers with international connections.

## Startups and the Local Ecosystem

Bucharest's startup ecosystem has benefited from the success of its best-known technology companies, whose alumni have founded new ventures and invested in others. Startups work on AI applications, fintech, health tech, developer tools and B2B software, and accelerators and venture funds support them. Startup roles offer broad scope and equity, with the usual uncertainty about funding. Before joining, ask about runway, customers and technical leadership.

## Remote Work

Many Romanian engineers work remotely for foreign companies. This can raise income, but it affects tax, social security and employment protections, and should be clarified in advance. Combining remote work with active participation in Bucharest's tech community helps keep career options open.

## Romanian Language Technology

Researchers and companies in Bucharest work on NLP for Romanian, including language resources, speech recognition and language models. As European initiatives aim to ensure AI works well in all EU languages, and as companies deploy chatbots and document AI for Romanian customers, demand for engineers with Romanian NLP expertise is growing. This is a niche where local engineers have a natural advantage.

## Evaluating an Offer

When comparing AI jobs in Bucharest with offers elsewhere, calculate net income under current Romanian tax and social contribution rules, then add benefits such as private health insurance, meal vouchers, training budgets and stock options at product companies. Subtract realistic housing and commuting costs: a flat near a metro line close to your office can save hours each week in a city where traffic is heavy. For engineers relocating from Western Europe, a lower gross salary can still provide a comparable or higher standard of living, provided the role offers strong technical work and growth. For engineers already in Romania, the key differences between offers are usually team ownership, product impact, mentoring and the stability of the employer rather than small salary gaps.

## A Final Tip

Ask to meet engineers from the team, not only managers; in Bucharest's competitive market, their day-to-day experience tells you most about the role.

## Responsible AI

Automation, security and banking AI all involve sensitive data and decisions. Engineers who understand GDPR, the EU AI Act and practical governance — documentation, evaluation, human oversight — are increasingly valued by Bucharest employers serving European customers.

A Romanian ML engineer working on document processing in London wants to return home. She receives an offer from an automation software company in Bucharest to work on models that extract information from complex documents.

She compares net pay under current Romanian tax rules with her London salary, finds her rent in Bucharest much lower and learns that the team works on the same global product as colleagues in other countries.
