Graz, Austria's second-largest city and the capital of Styria, is a university city with a UNESCO-listed old town and one of Europe's most concentrated clusters of automotive engineering, semiconductor and industrial technology companies. TU Graz is internationally known for research in computer vision, security and signal processing, and the region hosts applied research centres for data-driven business and electronics. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Graz span powertrain and vehicle engineering, sensors and secure chips, industrial machinery, green technology and research, at a lower cost of living than Vienna. This guide explains the landscape.

## Automotive Engineering and Vehicle Manufacturing

Graz is sometimes described as an automotive engineering capital. AVL, headquartered in Graz, is one of the world's largest independent companies for development, simulation and testing in the automotive industry, working on powertrains, electrification, driver assistance and testing systems. Magna Steyr manufactures vehicles under contract for several brands at its Graz plant and provides engineering services. Around them, suppliers and engineering service providers form a large cluster.

AI applications include simulation and virtual testing, analysis of test bench and vehicle data, battery and electric drive analytics, driver assistance validation, manufacturing quality and predictive maintenance. The combination of engineering depth and data makes Graz a strong place for engineers who enjoy physics-informed ML and systems engineering.

## Semiconductors and Sensors

The Graz region is a notable European location for microelectronics. NXP has a site in Gratkorn near Graz focused on secure identification and contactless technologies, and ams OSRAM has its headquarters in Premstätten near Graz, working on optical sensors and light technology. Infineon and other semiconductor companies have development activities in Styria and Carinthia. AI in semiconductors covers edge AI in sensors, manufacturing analytics and design automation.

## Industrial Machinery and Green Tech

Andritz, headquartered in Graz, supplies plants and equipment for hydropower, pulp and paper, metals and other industries, and uses data for digital services and predictive maintenance. Styria promotes itself as a "Green Tech Valley", with companies in renewable energy, recycling and environmental technology that use data for optimisation and monitoring.

## Research and Education

**TU Graz** has strong research in computer vision and graphics, machine learning, signal processing, security and embedded systems. Its security researchers have been internationally recognised for discovering major hardware vulnerabilities in processors.

**Know-Center**, an Austrian research centre for data-driven business and AI based in Graz, works with companies on applied data science, trustworthy AI and knowledge discovery.

**Silicon Austria Labs**, a research centre for electronic-based systems with a site in Graz, works on sensors, embedded systems, intelligent wireless systems and related AI.

**University of Graz** and **FH JOANNEUM** contribute further research and graduates, including in business informatics and applied data science.

## Austrian Employment Conditions

Austrian employment is shaped by collective agreements (Kollektivverträge) that set minimum pay by job category and experience; job postings must state the minimum salary under the applicable agreement and whether overpayment is possible. Salaries are typically paid in 14 instalments, with holiday and Christmas pay taxed favourably. Employees receive five weeks of paid holiday. Salaries in Graz are generally somewhat lower than in Vienna, but housing is also more affordable.

## Language and Permits

German is the language of daily life and many companies, especially in engineering and manufacturing; research centres and international teams often work in English. EU citizens can work freely and register their residence. Non-EU professionals can use the Red-White-Red Card or the EU Blue Card. Check official Austrian sources.

## Living in Graz

Graz has a lively student population, a well-preserved historic centre around the Schlossberg, a strong café and food culture and an efficient tram network. Housing is more affordable than in Vienna. The surrounding Styrian countryside, with vineyards and mountains, offers outdoor activities, and Slovenia and the Adriatic coast are within a few hours.

## Simulation, Testing and Physics-Informed ML

Automotive development relies on simulation and testing: engines, electric drives, batteries and entire vehicles are tested on benches and in virtual environments before they reach the road. Machine learning accelerates this process in several ways. Surrogate models approximate expensive simulations, allowing engineers to explore more designs. Anomaly detection finds unusual behaviour in large test datasets. Calibration of control software can be optimised with data-driven methods. Scenario-based testing of driver assistance systems uses ML to generate and select relevant test cases. These applications combine ML with physics, control engineering and domain expertise, and Graz's automotive engineering companies are among the best places in Europe to work on them.

## Edge AI and Secure Chips

Semiconductor and sensor companies around Graz develop chips for identification, payments, automotive and consumer devices. AI plays a role in two directions: running efficient models on sensors and chips (edge AI) and using data science to improve chip design and manufacturing. Security is a particular strength of the region, reflecting TU Graz's research, and secure hardware for AI applications is a growing field.

## Security Research and AI

TU Graz's security researchers have gained international recognition for work on hardware and software vulnerabilities. For AI professionals, security intersects with ML in several ways: protecting ML systems from adversarial attacks and data leakage, using ML for intrusion and anomaly detection, and ensuring secure deployment of models on devices. The EU AI Act requires robustness and cybersecurity for high-risk AI systems, making this expertise increasingly relevant for industry.

## Trustworthy AI at the Know-Center

The Know-Center works with companies on applied data science and trustworthy AI, including explainability, fairness, privacy and robustness, and it helps organisations prepare for regulatory requirements. Research engineers at applied research centres work on multiple industry projects, gaining variety and a broad network. For engineers who enjoy applied research and knowledge transfer, this is an attractive path between academia and industry.

## Green Technology and Energy

Styria's green technology companies work on renewable energy, recycling, water and environmental monitoring. Data science supports forecasting of renewable generation, optimisation of recycling processes, predictive maintenance of hydropower equipment and monitoring of environmental data. For engineers who want their work to contribute to sustainability, this cluster offers meaningful opportunities.

## Reading an Austrian Job Posting

Austrian postings state the minimum gross monthly salary under the applicable collective agreement, often with a note about willingness to overpay depending on qualifications. For experienced AI engineers, the actual salary is usually higher than the stated minimum. Ask early about the realistic range, and check whether the contract is "all-in" (including overtime) and how hybrid work is handled.

## Questions to Ask Graz Employers

- At which site will I work, and how is it connected by public transport?
- What is the working language of the team and the wider organisation?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what is the realistic salary range?
- How do models move from prototype to production or testing systems?
- What support is offered for relocation and German courses?

## Networking and Settling In

Graz has an active tech community with meetups on data science, AI and software engineering, events at TU Graz and the research centres and automotive cluster events. Newcomers must register their address (Meldezettel), obtain a social insurance number and open a bank account.

## Skills That Stand Out

Graz employers value physics-informed ML, simulation and surrogate modelling, time series analysis of test and sensor data, embedded and edge AI, security-aware ML and strong software engineering. German language skills significantly broaden options in engineering and manufacturing.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews in Graz's engineering companies often include technical discussions of past projects, questions on ML fundamentals and domain-specific topics such as vehicle dynamics, batteries or sensor data, and sometimes practical tasks. Research centres focus on methods and project experience. Prepare to explain trade-offs and to show interest in the engineering domain.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare annual net pay across 14 instalments, benefits and housing costs. Graz's lower rents compared with Vienna, Munich or Stuttgart can make a moderately lower salary attractive.

## Who Graz Suits Best

Graz suits engineers who enjoy automotive, semiconductor or industrial AI, value applied research and a relaxed university city, and are willing to learn German.

## Vehicle Manufacturing Data

Contract vehicle manufacturing produces cars for different brands on flexible lines, which creates complex planning and quality challenges. Data science supports production planning, supply chain coordination, quality inspection with computer vision, analysis of assembly data and predictive maintenance of robots and equipment. Engineers working in this environment learn how large-scale manufacturing operates and how data can improve it, gaining experience valued across the automotive industry.

## Beyond Graz: Carinthia and Upper Austria

Carinthia, south of Styria, hosts semiconductor activities around Villach, and Upper Austria around Linz has a strong industrial and AI research base. Some employers operate across these regions, and Graz is well connected by road and rail.

## Research Careers at TU Graz

TU Graz offers PhD, postdoc and research engineer positions in computer vision, machine learning, signal processing, security and embedded systems, funded by Austrian and European research programmes and industry collaborations. The university's close ties with automotive and semiconductor companies mean that many research projects address industrial problems, and graduates often move into industry after their PhD. Austria's research funding agencies support both basic and applied research, and Christian Doppler laboratories — a model in which companies and universities jointly fund application-oriented research — exist at several Austrian universities, including in Graz. For experienced engineers, research roles offer a way to deepen expertise while staying connected to industry, though salaries follow university scales and early positions are usually fixed-term.

## Working Culture

Graz's engineering companies tend to value precision, thorough documentation and long-term relationships. Hierarchies are moderate, and consensus matters in decision-making. International teams in research centres and larger companies are more informal. Work-life balance is generally good, with five weeks of holiday and flexible working time arrangements common.

## Remote and Hybrid Work

Many Graz employers offer hybrid arrangements, though testing, manufacturing and hardware roles require on-site presence. Some professionals live in Graz while working hybrid for employers in Vienna or elsewhere. Clarify expectations before accepting an offer.

## Responsible AI in Engineering

Automotive and industrial AI often involves safety-critical systems. The EU AI Act classifies AI used as safety components in certain regulated products as high-risk, requiring risk management, documentation and human oversight. Engineers in Graz who combine ML with safety and quality practices are especially valued.

## A Final Tip

Ask to see the test benches or labs during your interview; in Graz's engineering culture, the hardware tells you what the job really is.
