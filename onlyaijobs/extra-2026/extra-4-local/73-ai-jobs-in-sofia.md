Sofia has quietly become one of Southeast Europe's most interesting technology cities. Bulgaria has a long tradition in mathematics and informatics, and Sofia's software industry has produced product companies with global reach in rendering, developer tools and fintech. Since 2022, the city has also hosted INSAIT, a research institute created with the ambition of bringing world-class AI research to Eastern Europe, and Sofia Tech Park houses one of the EuroHPC supercomputers. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Sofia offer research at an ambitious new institute, product ML at international companies and roles in R&D centres, with a flat income tax and one of the lowest costs of living among EU capitals. This guide explains the landscape and its limits.

## INSAIT: Ambitious AI Research

INSAIT, the Institute for Computer Science, Artificial Intelligence and Technology at Sofia University, was founded in 2022 in partnership with ETH Zurich and EPFL. It recruits researchers internationally and offers PhD positions with competitive conditions, aiming to create a top-tier research environment in Sofia. INSAIT has worked on large language models for Bulgarian, among other topics in machine learning, computer vision, robotics and quantum computing. For researchers and research engineers, it represents a rare opportunity to join a young, well-funded institute with international ambitions in Eastern Europe.

## Supercomputing at Sofia Tech Park

Sofia Tech Park, a science and technology park in the city, hosts Discoverer, one of the supercomputers of the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking. Discoverer provides computing resources for research and industry in Bulgaria and across Europe, including for AI workloads. The park also hosts laboratories, an incubator and innovation programmes.

## Software Product Companies

Bulgaria's software industry includes companies that built global products from Sofia. Chaos, known for the V-Ray rendering software used in architecture, design and film, has major development in Sofia and uses AI in rendering and visualisation. Telerik, a Bulgarian developer tools company acquired by Progress Software, left a lasting legacy, and Progress maintains a large Sofia office. Payhawk, a fintech company for corporate spending, grew rapidly from Sofia. Other companies work on hosting, e-commerce, gaming, drones and SaaS. These companies use ML for product features, fraud detection, analytics and automation, often in English-speaking teams.

## International R&D and Service Centres

Many international technology, software and business service companies run R&D and operations centres in Sofia, attracted by talent and costs. These employ software engineers, data engineers and data scientists, with ML roles in analytics, automation and product development.

## Research and Education

Sofia University's Faculty of Mathematics and Informatics and the Technical University of Sofia are the main sources of computer science graduates. The Bulgarian Academy of Sciences' Institute of Information and Communication Technologies conducts research in AI, HPC and data science. Bulgaria's strong results in international informatics olympiads reflect a deep talent pool in algorithms.

## Pay, Taxes and Currency

Salaries in Bulgaria are lower than in Western Europe, although experienced engineers at product companies and international centres earn well above local averages. Bulgaria has a flat personal income tax of 10%, one of the lowest in the EU, plus social contributions. Bulgaria adopted the euro in 2026, which simplifies comparisons with other eurozone countries. Living costs in Sofia are among the lowest of EU capitals, though rents in popular areas have risen.

## Language and Permits

Bulgarian, written in Cyrillic, is the language of daily life and public institutions; English is the working language in most tech companies and at INSAIT. EU citizens can work freely; non-EU professionals typically need a work permit or the EU Blue Card. Check official Bulgarian sources.

## Living in Sofia

Sofia lies at the foot of Vitosha mountain, with hiking and skiing within reach of the city. It has a metro network, trams and buses, a growing café and cultural scene and a relaxed pace compared with larger capitals. Housing is affordable by EU standards. Sofia airport connects to many European cities, and the Black Sea coast is a few hours away.

## Building a Research Institute: What It Means for Engineers

Joining a young research institute differs from joining an established university department. Structures, processes and research directions are still forming, which gives early members influence over how the institute develops. Research engineers help build computing infrastructure, data pipelines and shared tools; researchers establish groups and recruit students; and collaborations with international partners and industry shape the research agenda. The advantages are energy, resources and opportunity; the challenges include uncertainty and the need to build reputation. For engineers who want to be part of building something, this can be a formative career step.

## Language Models for Bulgarian

Bulgarian is a Slavic language written in Cyrillic, with fewer digital resources than major European languages. Efforts to build Bulgarian language models aim to ensure that AI serves Bulgarian speakers well in public services, education and business. Engineers working on such projects deal with data collection and curation, tokenisation for Cyrillic text, evaluation with native speakers and adaptation of multilingual models. These skills are relevant for many other smaller European languages.

## Product ML at Bulgarian Software Companies

Sofia's product companies illustrate how AI is integrated into established software products. In rendering and visualisation, machine learning supports denoising, upscaling, material generation and faster previews, helping artists and architects work more efficiently. In developer tools, AI assists with code generation, UI components and documentation. In fintech, ML detects fraud, categorises transactions and forecasts spending. These companies usually have international customers, English-speaking teams and modern engineering practices, offering ML engineers product impact comparable to that in larger hubs.

## Supercomputing and AI Infrastructure

Access to a EuroHPC supercomputer in Sofia gives researchers and companies computing power for AI training and scientific simulation. Engineers who support HPC systems work on performance optimisation, distributed training, scheduling and data management — skills in high demand as AI models grow. European initiatives to provide AI computing for startups and research may expand opportunities in this area.

## Evaluating R&D Centre Roles

Before joining an international R&D or service centre in Sofia, ask which products or models the local team owns, where decisions are made and how career progression works locally. Centres that own complete products usually offer more interesting work.

## Questions to Ask Sofia Employers

- What does the Sofia team own, and where are decisions made?
- What is the working language, including for documentation?
- What computing resources are available for ML work?
- What is the salary range, and are there equity or bonus components?
- What relocation and permit support is offered?

## Networking and Settling In

Sofia has an active tech community with meetups on machine learning, data science, Python and startups, conferences and events at Sofia Tech Park and INSAIT. Newcomers need to register their residence, obtain a personal number for foreigners and open a bank account; employers experienced with international hires can help.

## Skills That Stand Out

Sofia employers value strong algorithms and mathematics, production ML engineering, computer graphics and vision for rendering companies, fraud and risk modelling for fintech, and HPC skills for infrastructure roles. English is expected in tech; Bulgarian helps with career progression in local organisations.

## The Honest Limits of the Market

Bulgaria's AI job market is growing but still small compared with major European hubs. Expect fewer vacancies and fewer senior specialised roles, and a longer search for highly specific positions. Many engineers combine local roles with international projects or remote work. The advantages are low living costs, a favourable tax system, a close-knit community and the chance to take on responsibility quickly.

## Preparing for Interviews

Interviews at Sofia tech companies typically include algorithmic coding tasks, ML fundamentals, system design and a deep dive into past projects. INSAIT and research roles focus on publications, research ideas and technical depth. Prepare to discuss trade-offs clearly in English and to ask about team ownership, computing resources and mentoring.

## Who Sofia Suits Best

Sofia suits engineers who want to join an ambitious research institute or product company, value low living costs, a flat tax and mountains on the doorstep, and can accept a smaller job market.

## Remote Work and Returning Bulgarians

Sofia attracts many Bulgarian engineers returning from abroad, as well as remote workers employed by foreign companies. Remote arrangements can bring higher salaries, but they affect tax residence, social security and employment protections, so clarify these in advance. Returnees often bring valuable experience to local companies and to INSAIT, and many describe their return as a chance to contribute to building Bulgaria's technology ecosystem.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare net income after Bulgaria's flat tax and social contributions, benefits such as private health insurance and training budgets, equity at product companies and housing costs. For engineers relocating from Western Europe, a lower gross salary can still provide a comparable standard of living, provided the role offers strong technical work.

## Startups and the Sofia Ecosystem

Sofia's startup ecosystem has grown with support from local venture funds, accelerators and European programmes, and from the success of companies that scaled internationally. Startups work on fintech, AI applications, logistics, drones, health tech and B2B software. Founders often have experience at the city's larger product companies, and many startups are international from day one, with English as their working language. For ML engineers, startups offer broad responsibility, direct contact with customers and equity, with the usual uncertainty about funding. Before joining, ask about runway, customers, technical leadership and how ML fits into the product roadmap. Sofia Tech Park's incubator and other hubs host events where founders and engineers meet, which is a good way to explore opportunities.

## Responsible AI

As Bulgarian organisations adopt AI, they must comply with GDPR and the EU AI Act. Engineers who understand documentation, bias evaluation, transparency obligations for AI-generated content and human oversight bring practical value, especially for public-sector projects and for companies selling AI products to European customers.

## A Final Tip

Visit Sofia Tech Park and attend an INSAIT public event before deciding; both give a quick sense of the ambition and community behind Sofia's AI scene.

## Beyond Sofia: Plovdiv and Varna

Plovdiv, Bulgaria's second city, has a growing IT sector and universities, and Varna on the Black Sea coast hosts software companies and a technical university. Living costs are lower than in Sofia, and some employers offer hybrid arrangements that make living outside the capital practical.
