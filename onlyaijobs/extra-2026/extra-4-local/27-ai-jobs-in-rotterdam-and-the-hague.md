The southern half of the Randstad is often overshadowed by Amsterdam in conversations about tech careers, yet it is one of the most interesting regions in Europe for applied AI. Rotterdam has Europe's largest port, a strong logistics and energy sector and a dynamic, modern city culture. The Hague is the seat of the Dutch government and home to international organisations focused on peace, justice and security. Delft, between the two, has one of Europe's leading technical universities. Together they form a polycentric metropolitan region where AI jobs in Rotterdam and The Hague span logistics, energy, e-commerce, government, security, health and engineering. This guide maps the region and explains how to search effectively.

## Why the Southern Randstad Is Distinct

Unlike a single large city, the Rotterdam–The Hague metropolitan region consists of several cities, each with its own profile, connected by trains, trams, the RandstadRail light rail and motorways. Rotterdam and The Hague are about 25 minutes apart by train, Delft lies in between and towns such as Capelle aan den IJssel, Schiedam, Zoetermeer and Rijswijk each host employers. For job seekers, this means the relevant question is never "is the job in Rotterdam?" but "how long does it take to get from my home to this exact office?" ## Rotterdam: Port, Logistics, Energy and E-commerce

**The port.** The Port of Rotterdam is Europe's largest port and a major hub for containers, bulk goods, energy and chemicals. The Port of Rotterdam Authority and terminal operators invest in digitalisation, and AI is applied to vessel arrival prediction, berth and terminal planning, traffic management, predictive maintenance of infrastructure and emissions monitoring.

**Logistics and maritime services.** Freight forwarders, shipping companies, maritime service providers and logistics technology companies in the region use data science for demand forecasting, route and network optimisation, pricing and supply chain visibility.

**Energy and industry.** The port area includes refineries, chemical plants and energy infrastructure undergoing major transitions toward hydrogen, carbon capture and renewable energy. Data science supports process optimisation, maintenance and energy system planning. Energy companies in Rotterdam also work on trading, forecasting and customer analytics.

**E-commerce and consumer brands.** Rotterdam hosts e-commerce companies and consumer goods businesses that use recommendations, forecasting and pricing at scale.

**Health.** Erasmus MC, one of the largest university medical centres in the Netherlands, is a centre for medical research, including medical imaging and clinical data science.

## The Hague: Government, International Organisations and Security

**National government.** The Hague is the seat of the Dutch government, with ministries, the parliament and many national agencies. The government employs data scientists and analysts for policy analysis, digital services and operations, and has invested in responsible algorithm use, including a public algorithm register.

**International organisations.** The Hague hosts international courts and organisations focused on justice and security, as well as agencies such as Europol, the EU's law enforcement agency, and Eurojust. Some of these organisations use data analysis and AI for operational and analytical work, with recruitment following their own procedures.

**Security cluster.** The Hague Security Delta is a network of businesses, governments and knowledge institutions working on cybersecurity and security innovation. AI jobs here include threat detection, security analytics and fraud prevention.

**Legal technology.** The concentration of legal institutions and law firms creates demand for legal tech and document analysis.

## Delft: The Engineering Heart

TU Delft is one of Europe's leading technical universities. It has launched a series of interdisciplinary AI labs that connect AI research with fields such as robotics, energy, water, health and mobility. Delft also hosts TNO, the Netherlands Organisation for Applied Scientific Research, which conducts applied research including in AI and data, and Deltares, an institute for applied research in water and subsurface, where data science supports flood risk and water management. A startup scene around the university includes robotics, drones, energy and deep-tech companies.

## Port AI in Practice

The Port of Rotterdam is a complex system in which many parties must coordinate: shipping lines, terminals, pilots, tug operators, customs, inland shipping, rail and road transport. Data science in this setting deals with:

- **Arrival prediction:** estimating when vessels will arrive, given weather, congestion elsewhere and operational factors, so that berths, pilots and terminal resources can be planned.
- **Terminal optimisation:** planning container stacking, crane sequences and truck appointments.
- **Hinterland logistics:** coordinating barges, trains and trucks that move goods inland to the rest of Europe.
- **Infrastructure maintenance:** predicting when quay walls, bridges, locks and other assets need attention.
- **Energy transition:** modelling energy flows as the port develops hydrogen, carbon capture and renewable energy infrastructure.
- **Emissions and safety:** monitoring emissions and detecting safety risks.

Engineers who enjoy combining ML with optimisation and real operations find port work rich and tangible. Domain knowledge accumulates value quickly, because the port's systems and stakeholders are complex.

## Government Data Science and Responsible AI

The Dutch government has been at the forefront of discussions about responsible algorithm use, partly due to past problems with automated decision-making in public administration that led to serious harm for citizens. As a result, government organisations in The Hague place strong emphasis on transparency, fairness, human oversight and legal compliance. The public algorithm register, impact assessments and supervisory roles for algorithms reflect this. Under the EU AI Act, many public sector uses of AI may be classified as high-risk, adding formal requirements.

For data scientists, government work in The Hague offers significant societal impact and a focus on doing AI carefully. Roles typically require Dutch, and some require security screening. Salaries follow public sector scales, which are transparent, and conditions include good pension arrangements and work-life balance.

## TU Delft AI Labs and Research Careers

TU Delft's AI labs bring together researchers from computer science and application domains such as civil engineering, aerospace, mechanical engineering and design. This creates positions for PhD candidates and postdocs working at the intersection of AI and engineering — for example AI for water management, energy systems, mobility, materials or robotics. TNO and Deltares offer applied research careers with direct links to industry and government. Erasmus University Rotterdam, including its business school and econometrics tradition, and Erasmus MC contribute research in data-driven decision-making and health. Leiden University's campus in The Hague focuses on governance, security and global affairs, including the governance of technology.

For experienced engineers considering a research role, the region offers an unusually practical flavour of AI research: problems come from real infrastructure, water systems, ports and hospitals, and results often feed directly into practice.

## Living in the Region

Rotterdam is known for its modern architecture, rebuilt after the Second World War, its multicultural population and a lively food and culture scene. The Hague combines government buildings, embassies and international organisations with a beach at Scheveningen and green residential neighbourhoods. Delft is a historic student city with canals and a compact centre. Housing is scarce throughout the Randstad, but prices vary: parts of Rotterdam and surrounding towns can be more affordable than Amsterdam or Utrecht. Many people live in one city and work in another, using the frequent train connections or the RandstadRail network.

Because the region is polycentric, your choice of home should follow your employer's exact location. A job in the port area west of Rotterdam, in Capelle to the east, in Delft or in The Hague's government district involves very different commutes. Test your route at rush hour before committing to a lease.

## Permits and Practicalities

EU citizens can work freely in the Netherlands and must register with their municipality. Non-EU professionals are often hired under the highly skilled migrant scheme, which requires an IND-recognised sponsor and a minimum salary, or through the EU Blue Card. Graduates of Dutch universities may use the orientation year. The Rotterdam and The Hague regions have international centres for newcomers that help with registration and practical questions. Check official sources for current requirements.

## Security and Cyber Roles in The Hague

The Hague's security cluster brings together cybersecurity companies, government bodies, research institutes and international organisations. AI plays a growing role in detecting cyber threats, analysing large volumes of logs and network data, identifying fraud and supporting investigations. Work in this field often combines machine learning with domain expertise in security and law, and it places high demands on reliability, explainability and legal compliance — because decisions can affect individuals' rights. Some roles require security clearances and nationality conditions, particularly in government and international organisations. For engineers interested in adversarial problems and public safety, the cluster offers distinctive opportunities.
