Bordeaux is known worldwide for its wine and its elegant eighteenth-century riverfront, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Less well known is its role as one of France's major aerospace and defence centres, a location for space launcher development, a hub for laser and photonics research and the home of a large e-commerce company. Since the high-speed rail line brought Paris within about two hours, Bordeaux has also attracted many professionals seeking a better quality of life. For experienced machine learning engineers, AI jobs in Bordeaux span aerospace, defence, space, e-commerce, photonics, health and agri-food, supported by a strong research base. This guide explains the landscape and what to consider.

## Aerospace, Defence and Space

The Bordeaux area is one of France's leading aerospace regions. Dassault Aviation has major sites in Mérignac, where business jets and military aircraft are assembled. Thales has avionics activities in the area, and ArianeGroup has sites west of Bordeaux working on launchers and propulsion. A dense network of suppliers and engineering companies surrounds them.

AI in aerospace and defence includes predictive maintenance, analysis of flight and test data, computer vision for inspection, simulation and design optimisation, and embedded AI in avionics and systems. Much of this work takes place under strict safety and certification requirements, and defence-related roles may require French nationality or security clearance.

## Lasers and Photonics

The Bordeaux region hosts a notable cluster in lasers and photonics. The CEA's CESTA centre near Bordeaux operates the Laser Mégajoule, one of the world's largest laser facilities, and research institutes, companies and the Alpha Route des Lasers et des Hyperfréquences cluster work on photonics, optics and microwave technologies. AI supports experiment control, data analysis, optical design and manufacturing.

## E-commerce and Digital Services

Cdiscount, one of France's largest e-commerce platforms, is headquartered in Bordeaux. E-commerce data science covers search and recommendations, pricing, demand forecasting, logistics, fraud detection and marketplace quality. Bordeaux also has a growing number of digital companies, IT services firms and startups working on SaaS, health tech, fintech and green tech.

## Wine, Agri-food and Environment

The Bordeaux wine industry and wider agri-food sector increasingly use data: vineyard monitoring with drones and satellites, yield and quality prediction, climate adaptation, supply chain traceability and marketing analytics. Research institutes in the region work on agriculture, forestry and environmental science, where machine learning is applied to remote sensing and climate data.

## Research and Education

**University of Bordeaux and Inria.** The University of Bordeaux and Inria's Bordeaux centre host research teams in machine learning, high-performance computing, computational neuroscience, medical imaging and numerical simulation. LaBRI, the Bordeaux computer science research laboratory, covers algorithms, data science, image processing and software engineering.

**Engineering schools.** Bordeaux INP, including ENSEIRB-MATMECA, trains engineers in computer science, electronics, mathematics and telecommunications. Other schools and business schools add graduates in data and management.

**Health.** The Bordeaux university hospital and research institutes work on neuroscience, cardiology and public health, with growing use of AI in imaging and clinical research.

## French Employment Conditions

Engineers in France are usually hired on permanent contracts (CDI) with cadre status, often with a days-based working-time arrangement and RTT days. Collective agreements such as the metallurgy agreement for aerospace and Syntec for IT services shape conditions. Benefits commonly include meal vouchers, supplementary health insurance, profit-sharing and reimbursement of half the cost of public transport passes. Salaries in Bordeaux are generally lower than in Paris, though housing costs have risen significantly as the city has attracted newcomers.

## Language and Permits

French is essential for daily life and for most employers in Bordeaux, particularly in aerospace, defence and industry. Research labs and some tech companies use English. EU citizens can work freely; non-EU professionals often use the Talent Passport, including its EU Blue Card category. Defence roles may have nationality requirements.

## Living in Bordeaux

Bordeaux combines a beautiful historic centre, a lively food and wine culture and easy access to the Atlantic coast and the Arcachon Basin. Trams, buses and cycling cover the city, although traffic on the ring road can be heavy, particularly toward the aerospace sites in Mérignac and the western suburbs. Housing has become more expensive, but it remains below Paris levels. The high-speed train to Paris makes occasional trips easy.

## AI in Aircraft Design and Maintenance

Aircraft manufacturers and their suppliers generate large amounts of data from design simulations, test campaigns, production and in-service operations. Machine learning supports several activities. In design, surrogate models approximate expensive simulations of aerodynamics and structures, allowing engineers to explore more options. In testing, anomaly detection helps analyse sensor data from ground and flight tests. In production, computer vision assists inspection of composite parts and assemblies. In service, predictive maintenance uses data from aircraft systems to anticipate failures and plan maintenance. All of these must fit into rigorous engineering and certification processes, where traceability and explainability are essential. Engineers who enjoy combining ML with physics and systems engineering find aerospace in Bordeaux rewarding.

## Space Launchers and Propulsion

Launcher and propulsion development involves complex tests, simulations and manufacturing processes. Data science helps analyse test firings, detect anomalies, optimise manufacturing of large composite and metallic structures and support reliability analysis. The European space sector's efforts to reduce launch costs and increase reusability create demand for data-driven engineering. Some of this work falls under defence or export-controlled regimes, so candidates should clarify requirements early.

## E-commerce at Scale

A large e-commerce platform must handle millions of products, many sellers and intense price competition. ML teams work on search ranking, product categorisation, recommendations, dynamic pricing, fraud detection, seller quality, delivery time prediction and warehouse optimisation. Language models help generate and improve product descriptions and support customer service. For ML engineers, e-commerce in Bordeaux offers large-scale data, a strong experimentation culture and product impact, with a cost of living lower than Paris.

## Laser Science and Data

Laser research generates highly complex experimental data. Machine learning supports alignment and control of laser systems, analysis of diagnostic images and signals, optimisation of experimental parameters and simulation of laser–matter interactions. Engineers with physics or optics backgrounds combined with ML skills are rare and valued in this niche.

## Viticulture and Climate Adaptation

Climate change affects vineyards directly through heat, drought, frost and disease pressure. Researchers and wine producers in the Bordeaux region use satellite and drone imagery, weather data and sensor networks to monitor vine health, predict harvest timing and yield, detect disease early and plan adaptation strategies such as new grape varieties or changed practices. Data scientists in this field combine remote sensing, time series and agronomy. The number of roles is modest, but the work is distinctive and connected to one of the region's defining industries.

## Research Careers

For research-oriented engineers, Bordeaux offers PhD, postdoc and research engineer positions at Inria, LaBRI and the University of Bordeaux, with strengths in HPC, numerical simulation, computational neuroscience and medical imaging. France's CIFRE scheme lets companies employ PhD students supervised by academic labs, which aerospace, e-commerce and photonics companies use for applied research.

## Questions to Ask Bordeaux Employers

- Does the role involve defence or export-controlled work, and are there nationality or clearance requirements?
- At which site will I work, and what are the commuting options?
- What is the working language of the team?
- Which collective agreement applies, and what is the cadre working-time arrangement?
- How do models reach production or certified systems, and who validates them?
- What benefits are included, such as profit-sharing and health insurance?

## Networking in Bordeaux

Bordeaux has an active tech community with meetups on data science, machine learning and software engineering, events at Bordeaux Technowest and other innovation hubs, and aerospace industry events. Inria and the university host seminars open to professionals. Attending regularly helps newcomers understand which teams are growing and meet future colleagues.

## Settling In

Newcomers need a French bank account, registration with social security and a carte Vitale. Non-EU residents must complete residence permit procedures. Temporary housing near your workplace gives time to find the right neighbourhood.

## Evaluating an Offer

Compare net pay after social contributions, cadre status and RTT days, profit-sharing and benefits, and weigh housing and commuting costs. Aerospace employers often offer stable careers and strong benefits under industry agreements, while e-commerce and startups may offer faster pace and broader scope. Choose based on the type of work and stability you want.

Bordeaux suits engineers who want AI work in aerospace, e-commerce or photonics, speak or are ready to learn French and value a beautiful city near the Atlantic with fast connections to Paris.

## Skills That Stand Out

Bordeaux employers value engineers who combine ML with physics and engineering — surrogate modelling, time series, computer vision for inspection and embedded deployment — as well as product ML skills for e-commerce. Rigorous documentation and testing practices are essential in aerospace and defence, where models must be traceable and validated. Strong French communication and the ability to work with engineers and certification specialists make a significant difference in career progression. Knowledge of the EU AI Act is increasingly relevant for AI components in regulated products.

## Health and Neuroscience Research

Bordeaux has a strong reputation in neuroscience, with research institutes and university hospital teams studying the brain, neurodegenerative diseases and mental health. Data science and machine learning are used to analyse brain imaging, electrophysiology and large clinical cohorts, and computational neuroscience teams at Inria and the university build models of brain function. Public health research groups work with population data to study ageing, chronic diseases and health services. For engineers interested in health, these environments offer scientifically rich work, usually on public research contracts. When AI tools move toward clinical use, they may fall under the EU Medical Device Regulation and the high-risk requirements of the EU AI Act, which require rigorous validation and documentation. Engineers who combine ML skills with statistical rigour and respect for patient data protection are valued both in research and in the health technology companies that are emerging around the university hospital.
