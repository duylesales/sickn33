Every organisation of any size has a finance function producing numbers that boards, regulators, investors and tax authorities rely on, and an audit profession checking them. Both are document-heavy, rule-bound and full of repetitive work performed by expensive people. AI jobs in audit and finance functions have grown steadily across Europe as a result, and they are among the most accessible applied roles for data professionals who are not put off by accounting vocabulary.

## Where AI Jobs in Audit and Finance Functions Sit

**Audit analytics.** Testing entire populations of transactions rather than samples, identifying unusual journal entries, reconciling ledgers and analysing revenue and expense patterns for indications of error or manipulation.

**Internal controls and risk.** Continuous monitoring of controls, detecting policy breaches, segregation of duties violations and unusual approval patterns.

**Financial forecasting and planning.** Cash flow prediction, revenue forecasting, cost modelling and scenario analysis for budgeting.

**Accounts payable and receivable.** Invoice processing, matching to purchase orders, duplicate payment detection, collections prioritisation and payment behaviour prediction.

**Tax.** Classification of transactions, indirect tax determination, transfer pricing analysis and compliance reporting.

**Treasury.** Cash positioning, foreign exchange exposure and liquidity forecasting.

**Sustainability reporting.** Collecting and assuring the environmental and social data that large European companies now report, which is a rapidly growing data engineering domain.

**Audit firm technology.** Software and data teams inside professional services firms building the tools their auditors use across thousands of engagements.

## The Data Is Structured, and That Changes Everything

Finance data is unusually well organised compared with most operational domains. Ledgers are complete and balanced by construction, transactions have defined structures, and the accounting equation provides built-in consistency checks.

The complications lie elsewhere. Chart of accounts structures differ between entities and change over time, particularly after acquisitions. Multinational groups run several systems in parallel with inconsistent master data. Free text in descriptions and memos carries meaning that structured fields do not. Period-end adjustments and manual journals are exactly the entries most worth examining and the least standardised. And currency, consolidation and intercompany elimination add layers that newcomers underestimate.

The other distinctive property is that being approximately right is not acceptable. A model that classifies ninety-eight per cent of transactions correctly leaves two per cent to be resolved by a person, and the resolution process is the product.

## Audit Analytics in Practice

Audit has shifted from sampling toward testing entire populations, and this shift is what created the technical roles.

Traditionally an auditor examined a sample of transactions and inferred conclusions about the whole. With the full ledger available, the approach becomes different: test every entry against expectations, and direct attention to what stands out.

Typical analyses include three-way matching of purchase orders, goods receipts and invoices; testing journal entries against characteristics associated with error or manipulation; reconciling subledgers to the general ledger; analysing revenue recognition timing around period boundaries; and examining the distribution of transaction values for patterns inconsistent with genuine business activity.

Two constraints shape the engineering. Audit conclusions must be documented and defensible to regulators and to the firm's own quality review, so methodology and reproducibility matter more than sophistication. And the auditor, not the system, reaches the conclusion; the tool directs attention and the professional judges.

Audit regulators across Europe have taken an interest in how technology is used in engagements, which reinforces the emphasis on documentation.

For candidates, this is an area where careful, explainable analysis is valued above modelling ingenuity, which suits people who enjoy rigour.

## Document Processing at Scale

Finance functions handle enormous volumes of documents, and this is the most common entry point for AI engineers into the sector.

Invoices are the canonical case. A mid-sized European company processes tens of thousands annually, arriving as PDFs, scans, emails and portal submissions, in multiple languages and formats, from suppliers who each design their own layout. Extracting supplier, amounts, tax treatment, line items and references, then matching to purchase orders and receipts, is a well-defined problem with measurable value.

The requirements are strict in a specific way. Financial figures must be exactly right, so confidence estimation and routing of uncertain extractions to human review are essential. Tax treatment must be correct, which is jurisdiction-dependent. And an audit trail linking the extracted values to the source document is required.

Beyond invoices: contracts for revenue recognition and lease accounting, expense receipts, bank statements, customs documentation, and correspondence with tax authorities.

European electronic invoicing mandates are changing this landscape, with several member states introducing requirements for structured electronic invoices. Over time this reduces the extraction problem and increases the data integration and compliance problem, which is a shift in the nature of the work rather than a reduction in it.

## Forecasting Inside a Finance Function

Financial forecasting differs from operational forecasting in ways that catch out newcomers.

The forecast is not only a prediction; it is a commitment. Budgets and guidance shape behaviour, and a forecast that a business unit disagrees with will be renegotiated rather than accepted. The political dimension is real and must be worked with rather than around.

Structures are hierarchical and must reconcile: entity, business unit, region, group, with currency conversion and intercompany elimination between levels. This makes coherent forecasting across the hierarchy a genuine technical requirement.

Horizons are driven by the reporting calendar rather than by operational need: month-end, quarter, year-end, and multi-year planning.

Cash flow forecasting is the area where analytical improvement is most visible, because payment timing is predictable from customer behaviour, and treasury decisions depend on it directly.

The judgement input is substantial and legitimate. A sales director knows about a contract that will close next month, and no model can know that. The systems that work incorporate these overrides explicitly, record them, and measure whether they improve accuracy over time — which turns an argument about whose number is right into an evidence-based conversation.

Candidates who understand that this is a collaboration rather than an automation problem do considerably better in interviews.

## Sustainability Reporting as a Data Problem

The fastest growth in finance-adjacent data work across Europe has come from sustainability reporting, and it is fundamentally a data engineering challenge.

Under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive, large companies in the EU report sustainability information according to common standards, with the scope expanding over time and assurance requirements attached. The reported figures must be auditable, which means traceable to source with documented methodology.

The practical difficulty is that the data does not exist in the systems finance already uses. Emissions from energy consumption sit in facilities systems; emissions in the supply chain require supplier data that may not be collected; waste, water and materials data live in operational systems across many sites; and social metrics come from human resources systems in multiple countries.

Consolidating this to a defensible number requires the same discipline as financial consolidation: defined boundaries, consistent methodology, documented estimates where measurement is unavailable, and lineage from the reported figure back to the source.

For candidates, this area combines data integration, methodology and assurance, sits next to the finance function in most organisations, and is funded by obligation rather than discretion. It is one of the clearest growth areas in European corporate data work, and the people who can do it are scarce.

## Language Models in the Finance Function

Text and rules are everywhere in finance, which has made this an active area for language model applications with a strong emphasis on verification.

Established applications include extracting terms from contracts for revenue and lease accounting; classifying transactions and expenses against policy; drafting variance commentary for management reporting, where the numbers come from the ledger and the model drafts the narrative; answering questions over accounting policy, internal procedures and tax guidance; and summarising regulatory changes.

Audit firms apply similar capabilities across engagements: summarising documents, drafting working papers from evidence, and supporting research over standards and guidance.

The constraints are consistent with other high-stakes domains. Figures must never be generated; they must be retrieved from the ledger and quoted. Answers about policy must cite the applicable paragraph. Human review is required before anything reaches a statutory report. And confidentiality restricts where processing may occur, with many European firms requiring EU-hosted or self-hosted models for client data.

For engineers with retrieval and extraction experience, this is a straightforward entry into the sector: the technical patterns are familiar, the evaluation discipline is the differentiator, and the accounting knowledge is acquired alongside qualified colleagues.

## Who Hires Across Europe

**Professional services firms.** The largest audit and advisory firms employ substantial technology and data organisations building the tools used across engagements, plus data specialists who work on client engagements directly.

**Corporate finance functions.** Large companies have finance data and analytics teams supporting controlling, treasury, tax and reporting.

**Shared service centres.** Many multinationals concentrate transaction processing in centres across Europe, particularly in Poland, Portugal, Ireland, Hungary and the Baltics, and these are significant employers of process and data specialists.

**Financial software vendors.** Companies building accounting, consolidation, treasury, tax and expense management software.

**Sustainability reporting and assurance providers**, a growing category.

**Internal audit functions** in large organisations, increasingly employing data analysts directly.

**Regulators and supervisory bodies**, analysing financial reporting at national level.

Conditions vary. Professional services firms are demanding during reporting seasons with strong training and structured progression; corporate finance functions offer steadier rhythms; shared service centres offer process-oriented roles with clear scope.

Geographically, work is distributed across Europe wherever large companies have finance operations, which makes it accessible outside the main technology centres.

## Entering the Field and Interviewing Well

Employers here hire analytical skill and expect to teach accounting, while screening for precision and communication.

Expect questions such as: how would you detect unusual journal entries without generating hundreds of false positives; how would you design a review workflow for extractions the system is unsure about; how would you forecast cash collections; how would you reconcile a figure that differs between two systems; how would you explain an anomaly to a controller who is certain nothing is wrong.

Preparation that helps: learn the structure of a general ledger, what a journal entry is and what a trial balance does; understand the reporting calendar and why period-end matters; and be able to talk about precision at operational volume, because every flag costs someone's time.

The trait most valued is care. In a domain where the output feeds statutory reporting, an engineer who double-checks and documents is worth more than one who iterates quickly.

## Real example

### The anomaly detector that found a process, not a fraud

An internal audit team at a European manufacturer built a system to flag unusual journal entries: large amounts, round numbers, entries posted outside business hours, entries by users who rarely post, and unusual account combinations.

The first run produced several hundred flags. Most resolved to one explanation: a shared service centre in another country posted a batch of adjustments each month, late in the evening local time, from a single account, in round amounts.

It was not fraud. It was an undocumented workaround for a system limitation that had persisted for three years, creating entries nobody reviewed and that occasionally contained errors.

The team documented the process, established a proper control, and refined the detection to account for legitimate patterns.

The audit lead's assessment was that the system had done exactly what it should: not caught a fraudster, but revealed a part of the organisation nobody was looking at. She added that this is the normal outcome, and that teams expecting dramatic discoveries are usually disappointed while teams expecting to find undocumented processes are usually right.
