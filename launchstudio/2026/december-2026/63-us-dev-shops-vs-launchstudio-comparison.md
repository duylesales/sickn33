# US Dev Shops vs. LaunchStudio: A Cost and Time-Zone Comparison for European Founders
A European founder with an AI-built prototype and a real deadline usually starts the vendor search in the same place: Google "software development agency," and the first page fills up with polished San Francisco and New York studios. Their case studies are impressive, their websites are slick, and their sales calls are persuasive. But for a founder based in Amsterdam, Berlin, Dublin, or Lisbon, hiring a US dev shop introduces two compounding problems that rarely show up in the pitch deck: the cost, converted into euros and stacked against actual deliverables, and the time-zone gap, which quietly adds days to every single round of feedback. This article breaks down both, with real numbers, and shows why LaunchStudio's Amsterdam-anchored, EUR-denominated, near-24-hour model was built specifically to solve what US dev shops structurally cannot.

## The Appeal — and the Hidden Cost — of a Silicon Valley Pedigree

There's a reason founders default to US dev shops: brand trust. A studio with a Bay Area address, a portfolio of YC-adjacent logos, and a sales team fluent in startup jargon feels like a safe bet, especially for a first-time founder who has never hired a development team before. The problem is that "safe bet" pricing in San Francisco and New York reflects San Francisco and New York cost structures — office leases, US engineering salaries, and agency overhead that has nothing to do with the actual complexity of hardening an AI-built prototype.

Typical US dev shop pricing for a project resembling "take my Lovable, Bolt, or Cursor prototype and make it production-ready" falls into one of two structures. Hourly-billed studios quote $150-$250/hour for senior engineering time, with no hard cap on total hours — a security-and-payments hardening project that a specialized team could complete in under two weeks can easily run past 150-200 billed hours once discovery calls, scope clarification, and revision cycles are counted, landing the invoice well into five figures before the founder has a firm total. Fixed-fee US studios, meanwhile, commonly quote $30,000-$80,000 for a comparable production-hardening engagement, bundled with a redesign or partial rebuild the founder didn't actually need, since the frontend already exists and works.

Converted to euros at typical exchange rates, that $30,000-$80,000 range lands somewhere between roughly €27,500 and €74,000 — for a category of work LaunchStudio prices between €800 and €7,500 depending on scope, because LaunchStudio isn't rebuilding the product; it's hardening the backend of one that already exists. The gap isn't a rounding error. It's the difference between a founder funding six to twelve months of runway with the money saved, or burning through a seed round on infrastructure work that should have been a fraction of the budget.

Even at the high end of LaunchStudio's range, a European founder pays roughly a fifth to a tenth of what a comparable US fixed-fee engagement costs, in a currency that matches their revenue, their investors' expectations, and their own bank account — with no currency conversion risk if the dollar moves against the euro mid-project, which it regularly does over an 8-10 week engagement.

## The Time-Zone Problem Nobody Puts in the Proposal

The cost gap is the number founders notice first. The time-zone gap is the one that actually kills momentum during the project.

San Francisco (PST/PDT) sits 9 hours behind Central European Time; New York (EST/EDT) sits 6 hours behind. In practice, that means a US dev shop's standard working hours — roughly 9am to 6pm Pacific or Eastern — overlap with a European founder's working day for at most one or two hours, and often not at all once time-zone drift from daylight saving transitions is factored in. A daily standup scheduled at a "convenient" 9am Pacific is 6pm in Amsterdam — the very end of the European founder's day, competing with dinner, family time, or simply the point at which most people's attention is gone. Scheduled at a more European-friendly hour, it falls before the US team's morning coffee, which is why these calls chronically get moved, shortened, or skipped.

The compounding effect is what actually costs founders time: a European founder sends feedback on a build at 5pm CET. The US team, still asleep, doesn't see it until their morning — which, accounting for the 9-hour PST gap, is roughly 1am CET the following day. They respond during their working day, and their reply lands in the founder's inbox around 6pm-3am CET — after the founder has already gone to bed. The founder doesn't see the response and act on it until the next morning. What should have been a same-day fix becomes a 48-72 hour round trip, repeated at every single revision cycle. On an 8-week engagement with weekly feedback rounds, that lag alone can add two to three real weeks to the calendar — invisible in the original proposal, but very visible in a founder's runway.

## How LaunchStudio Closes the Overlap Gap

LaunchStudio's structure was built around this exact failure mode, not as an afterthought but as the core design decision. Account management and project leadership sit in Amsterdam, the Netherlands, operating on CET/CEST — the same working day as the vast majority of LaunchStudio's European client base, with full overlap for calls, Slack threads, and same-day decisions. There is no "convenient for one side" compromise standup, because both sides are already in the same window.

Behind that Amsterdam layer, the primary engineering team operates from Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam — a 5-6 hour time difference ahead of CET/CEST, not behind it. That structure means work handed off at the end of the Amsterdam team's day is picked up by engineers who are just starting theirs, rather than engineers who are asleep. Combined with the Amsterdam-based project layer, LaunchStudio's clients get coverage that spans nearly the full 24-hour cycle: European hours for strategic conversations and sign-off, Vietnam hours for the deep engineering work, with minimal dead time in between. A question raised at 4pm in Amsterdam can have engineering movement on it before the founder is back at their desk the next morning — the inverse of the US-shop pattern, where the same question sits untouched until well into the following European evening.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much cheaper is LaunchStudio than a typical US dev shop?

For comparable production-hardening scope — security, payments, hosting, and monitoring on an existing AI-built frontend — US dev shops commonly charge $30,000-$80,000 in fixed fees, or run $18,000-$30,000+ on an uncapped hourly basis. Converted to euros, that's roughly €27,500-€74,000. LaunchStudio's fixed packages for the same category of work range from €800 to €7,500, meaning most European founders pay a fifth to a tenth of the US shop price for equivalent scope.

### Why does the time-zone gap matter more than it seems on a sales call?

A 6-9 hour gap between US Pacific/Eastern hours and CET leaves at most one to two overlapping working hours per day. Feedback sent at the end of a European founder's day isn't seen by a US team until their morning, and the reply doesn't land back in the founder's inbox until after they've gone to bed — turning a same-day fix into a 48-72 hour round trip that repeats at every revision cycle.

### Does LaunchStudio's team work European hours or Vietnam hours?

Both, deliberately. Account management and project leadership operate from Amsterdam on CET/CEST, giving full working-hours overlap with European founders for calls and decisions. The primary engineering team in Ho Chi Minh City works hours ahead of CET rather than behind it, so work handed off at the end of the European day is already being worked on by the time a founder wakes up — giving LaunchStudio clients near-24-hour coverage instead of a single narrow overlap window.

### Is a US dev shop ever the better choice for a European founder?

If a founder needs deep, ongoing US-market-specific product strategy — direct exposure to US investors, or an in-person Bay Area presence — a local US team may offer relationship value LaunchStudio doesn't aim to replace. But for the specific task of hardening an existing AI-built prototype's security, payments, and infrastructure for launch, the time-zone and cost mismatch of a US shop rarely pays for itself, especially on a founder's budget.

### What exactly is included in LaunchStudio's fixed price, and can the scope change mid-project?

Each package (Launch Ready, Launch & Grow, Relaunch & Scale, Enterprise Hardening) defines its deliverables and price before work begins — no uncapped hourly clock, no open-ended "additional scope billed separately" clause. If a founder's needs genuinely expand mid-project, that's scoped and quoted as a clear addition, not folded silently into a growing hourly invoice.
