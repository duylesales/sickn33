# Case Study: 5 AI Founders Who Chose LaunchStudio Over a Traditional Agency

Almost every AI-native founder who reaches out to LaunchStudio has already talked to at least one traditional software agency first. It's a natural instinct: you built something real with Lovable, Bolt, or Cursor, you know it needs professional hardening before real customers touch it, and a full-service agency feels like the obvious, safe next step. Then the quote arrives — a discovery phase measured in weeks, a fixed retainer measured in tens of thousands of euros, and a scope document that somehow doesn't mention your existing frontend at all, as if the agency plans to rebuild it from zero regardless of what you already have working. This article walks through five founders who went through exactly that experience, got the traditional agency quote, and chose LaunchStudio instead — and the specific reason each of them made that call.

## Why Founders Shop Agencies First, and What Changes Their Mind

The instinct to call an agency isn't wrong. Agencies are a known quantity: a legal entity, a portfolio, a sales process that feels reassuringly formal after weeks of solo building. What breaks the relationship, almost every time, is the mismatch between what the founder actually needs — production-hardening of an already-built prototype — and what the agency is structured to sell, which is a full custom-build engagement with a discovery phase, a design phase, and a build phase, priced and timelined as if the founder were starting from a blank page. Five founders below hit that mismatch in five different ways, and each one is worth walking through on its own terms.

## Five Founders, Five Agency Quotes, Five Reasons They Walked Away

### Magnus Eriksson — Field Service Scheduling, Sweden

Magnus built a scheduling tool for independent HVAC technicians using **Bolt**, letting solo tradespeople manage job bookings and send automated arrival-time texts to customers. A Stockholm-based agency quoted him 10 weeks and roughly €38,000, with the first three weeks allocated entirely to "discovery and requirements gathering" for a product that already existed and already worked in front of test users. Magnus's decisive factor was timeline: he had already lined up 15 technicians willing to pilot the tool, and a ten-week wait meant losing the seasonal window before winter service calls picked up. LaunchStudio's fixed-scope hardening timeline, measured in business days rather than months, was the deciding factor.

### Amara Chukwu — Diaspora Remittance Tracker, Nigeria/UK

Amara built a tool using **Lovable** that helped Nigerian diaspora communities in the UK track and compare remittance transfer fees across providers in real time. A London agency's proposal was technically competent but priced around £52,000 with payment milestones tied to a rigid Gantt chart, and — critically for Amara — all meetings were scheduled during UK business hours with no flexibility, despite her actually splitting her time between London and Lagos. LaunchStudio's asynchronous, documentation-first communication style, backed by a team already spanning Amsterdam, Singapore, and Ho Chi Minh City time zones, was the specific reason she chose it: no meeting ever had to be the bottleneck.

### Kenji Watanabe — Restaurant Inventory Forecasting, Japan

Kenji, a former restaurant operations manager, built an AI-powered inventory forecasting tool with **Cursor** to help small restaurant groups predict ingredient waste. A Tokyo-based development firm quoted a reasonable-sounding ¥6.5 million but structured the engagement as a full rebuild in their own proprietary framework, discarding Kenji's existing Cursor-built frontend entirely — months of UI decisions and restaurant-specific workflow logic he'd already refined with real chefs, gone. The specific reason Kenji chose LaunchStudio was frontend preservation: the guarantee that his existing interface would stay untouched while only the backend was hardened meant zero risk of losing the product decisions he'd already validated.

### Ingrid Solberg — Nonprofit Grant-Matching Platform, Norway

Ingrid built a platform connecting small nonprofits with matching grant opportunities, using **Bolt**, as a project she largely self-funded. An Oslo agency's quote came in around €41,000 with 40% due upfront before any work began — a cash-flow structure that simply didn't work for a founder without institutional funding behind her. Price and payment structure were Ingrid's decisive factors: LaunchStudio's fixed-price packages, several thousand euros rather than tens of thousands, with payment tied to defined delivery milestones rather than a large upfront deposit, fit a bootstrapped budget in a way the agency's retainer model never could.

### Théo Lambert — B2B Freight Quote Comparison, France/Belgium

Théo built a tool with **Lovable** that let small freight forwarders compare shipping quotes across carriers in real time, aimed initially at the Benelux cross-border market. A Brussels agency quoted €47,000 and 14 weeks, and — the specific sticking point for Théo — the contract included a change-order clause charging an additional hourly rate for any scope adjustment discovered after the discovery phase closed, effectively penalizing him for the agency's own incomplete initial scoping. LaunchStudio's fixed-scope quote, arrived at after a direct codebase review rather than a lengthy discovery phase, meant Théo knew the final cost before any work began, with no change-order clause hanging over the engagement.

## The Pattern Across All Five

None of these five founders chose LaunchStudio because the agencies they spoke with were incompetent. In most cases, the agencies were entirely capable of doing good work — the mismatch wasn't quality, it was fit. Traditional agencies are built to sell full custom builds: long discovery phases, large retainers, and a default assumption that the client is starting from nothing. AI-native founders in 2026 are, by definition, not starting from nothing — they already have a working, demoable product built with Lovable, Bolt, or Cursor, and what they need is narrower, faster, and cheaper than a full build: professional hardening of the backend, security, and payment layer, without disturbing the frontend they already validated with real users.

That mismatch shows up as the same handful of complaints, again and again, across founders who've never spoken to each other: multi-week discovery phases for products that already exist, retainers priced for greenfield builds rather than targeted hardening, rigid meeting-heavy processes that don't account for founders working across time zones, and change-order clauses that turn an already-large quote into an open-ended one. LaunchStudio's fixed-scope, fixed-price, frontend-preserving model exists specifically because this mismatch is common enough to build a company around solving it.

## What "Choosing LaunchStudio Instead" Actually Looks Like in Practice

For all five founders above, the shift wasn't just about the number on the quote — it was about what that number represented. A traditional agency's €40,000+ retainer bakes in months of overhead: project managers, discovery workshops, design sprints for a UI that, in every one of these cases, already existed and already worked. LaunchStudio's packages — from the roughly €800–1,500 Launch Ready tier up to the €5,000–7,500 Enterprise Hardening tier for more complex compliance needs — are priced for the actual work being done: hardening an existing, working prototype, not building one from scratch. That difference in scope is also why the timeline difference is so large: weeks of discovery collapse into a direct codebase review, and months of build phases collapse into the specific, bounded list of security, payment, and infrastructure work an AI builder's output actually needs.

## Key Takeaways

- Founders overwhelmingly go to traditional agencies first — it's the familiar, reassuring option — but the mismatch between what agencies are structured to sell (full custom builds) and what AI-native founders actually need (targeted hardening) is usually what sends them elsewhere.
- Timeline was the deciding factor for founders with a hard external deadline, like Magnus's seasonal HVAC pilot window, where a ten-week agency discovery phase simply cost too much real-world opportunity.
- Frontend preservation mattered most to founders like Kenji, who had already validated significant UI and workflow decisions with real users and couldn't accept an agency's default assumption of a full rebuild.
- Fixed pricing and milestone structure mattered most to bootstrapped founders like Ingrid, where a large upfront deposit simply wasn't compatible with self-funded cash flow.
- Across all five cases, the agencies weren't incompetent — they were simply built to sell a different kind of engagement than an already-built AI prototype actually needs, which is the structural reason LaunchStudio's fixed-scope model kept winning the comparison.

Priya Nair, an Indian EdTech founder, built an adaptive learning platform prototype using **Lovable**, designed to adjust practice question difficulty in real time based on individual student performance. Before contacting LaunchStudio, she got a quote from a traditional Amsterdam-based agency: 12 weeks and €45,000, with a lengthy discovery phase upfront and change-order fees baked directly into the contract for anything discovered after that phase closed. For a solo EdTech founder trying to reach paying school partnerships before the next academic term, both the cost and the timeline made the agency route untenable.

Priya chose LaunchStudio instead, keeping her existing Lovable frontend entirely intact. The engineering team reviewed her codebase directly rather than running a multi-week discovery process, then implemented Row Level Security policies scoped to student and teacher roles, wired a secure Stripe billing flow for school-license payments, and hardened the deployment configuration — all under the **Launch & Grow** package, with a fixed quote delivered before any work began and no change-order clause hanging over the engagement.

**Result:** Priya's platform launched production-ready and secure, with her original Lovable-built interface unchanged — the same UI she had already tested and refined with real students and teachers — at roughly 6% of the traditional agency's quoted cost.
