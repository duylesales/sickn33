🎫 Fien Kramer built TicketVolg, a support ticket app in Harderwijk, using Bolt. As it grew, she hired a developer to help maintain it — and during onboarding, asked a simple question about how tickets were routed and prioritized. The new hire couldn't clearly explain it. Neither, it turned out, could anyone else. 😳

🧠 "Do you understand the codebase" gets a yes from everyone. It tells you nothing.

❌ Vague, confident answers like "it's all handled" or "that's standard" are riskier than "I don't know"
❌ AI-generated code often handles the success path clearly but leaves failure handling vague or absent
❌ Nobody — not the new developer, not the AI tool's original output — had ever verified the routing logic
❌ A core piece of the product had been running on logic nobody could currently explain

✅ Ask about one specific feature, in plain language, not "do you understand the codebase"
✅ Follow up with a failure-case question: what happens if this fails, or a user submits something unexpected
✅ Bring in an independent review when the answers don't get specific
