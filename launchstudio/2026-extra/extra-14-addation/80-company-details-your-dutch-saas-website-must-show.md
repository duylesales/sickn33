Lovable, Bolt and v0 generate beautiful footers with a logo, three links and "© 2028". What they do not add are the details Dutch and EU rules expect from a business offering services online: who you are, where you are, how to reach you and your registration numbers. These details also build trust with customers and help AI assistants identify your company correctly. This checklist is general information, not legal advice; the KVK and a lawyer can confirm your specific obligations.

## Business Identification

Businesses offering services online in the Netherlands are generally expected to make the following easily accessible, for example in the footer or on a contact or "about" page:

| Detail | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Legal company name | Voorbeeld Software B.V. |
| Trade name, if different | Planly |
| Visiting or business address | Street, postcode, city |
| Email address and/or contact form | Direct electronic contact |
| Phone number (recommended) | Faster contact for customers |
| KvK number | Chamber of Commerce registration |
| VAT number (BTW-id) | Where applicable |

The KvK publishes guidance on which details must appear on websites, invoices and correspondence.

## Pages Your Site Should Have

- **Terms and conditions** (algemene voorwaarden), accessible before purchase or sign-up
- **Privacy statement** describing what data you collect, why, where it is processed and users' rights
- **Cookie information and consent** for non-essential cookies
- **Contact page** with the details above
- **Pricing** including whether prices are with or without VAT; consumers must see total prices including VAT

## Consumer vs Business Customers

If you sell to consumers, additional rules apply: clear total prices including VAT, information about the right of withdrawal where applicable, how subscriptions renew and how to cancel. B2B-only services have fewer consumer rules but customers still expect transparency.

## Where AI-Built Sites Fall Short

- Footer without company name, KvK or address
- Privacy statement copied from a template that lists services the app does not use and misses ones it does
- No cookie consent while analytics tags load immediately
- Prices on the pricing page without clarity about VAT
- Contact only via a chat widget

## Structured Data Helps Too

Adding Organization structured data with your legal name, address, contact details and links to your profiles helps search engines and AI assistants connect your website to your real company. That consistency is part of how assistants decide which source to trust.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I have to show my KvK number on my website?

Dutch businesses are generally expected to show their KvK number, along with other identification details, on their website and correspondence. Check the KVK's current guidance for your legal form.

### Do I need to show prices including VAT?

For consumers, yes: total prices must include VAT. For business-only offers, prices excluding VAT are common, but state clearly that VAT is excluded.

### Is a privacy statement template good enough?

Only as a starting point. Your statement must describe what your app actually does: which data, which processors, where it is processed and for how long. Templates rarely match AI-built apps' real data flows.

### Why does Manifera emphasise company details on websites?

Because trust is part of conversion. Manifera's own presence across Amsterdam, Singapore and Ho Chi Minh City is stated clearly on its sites, and 11+ years of client work show that visible, verifiable company details reassure business buyers.

### Do company details help AI assistants recommend my business?

Yes. Consistent company names, addresses and registration details across your site and structured data help AI assistants identify your business as a real, specific entity and cite it with confidence.
