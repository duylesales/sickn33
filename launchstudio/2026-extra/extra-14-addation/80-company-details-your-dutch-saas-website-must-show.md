Lovable, Bolt and v0 generate beautiful footers with a logo, three links and "© 2028". What they do not add are the details Dutch and EU rules expect from a business offering services online: who you are, where you are, how to reach you and your registration numbers. These details also build trust with customers and help AI assistants identify your company correctly. This checklist is general information, not legal advice; the KVK and a lawyer can confirm your specific obligations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Dutch businesses offering services online are generally expected to show key company details:** legal name, address, KvK number, VAT number and contact options. The KVK publishes guidance.
- **AI-generated websites usually omit these details,** and visitors notice: missing company information reduces trust and conversion.
- **Consumers must see total prices including VAT;** business-only offers can show prices excluding VAT if clearly labelled.
- **Privacy statements must match reality,** and non-essential cookies need consent before loading.
- **Organization structured data and consistent details** help search engines and AI assistants recognise your business as a real entity.

## Business Identification

Businesses offering services online in the Netherlands are generally expected to make the following easily accessible, for example in the footer or on a contact or "about" page:

| Detail | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Legal company name | Voorbeeld Software B.V. |
| Trade name, if different | Planly |
| Visiting or business address | Street, postcode, city |
| Email address and/or contact form | Direct electronic contact |
| Phone number (recommended) | Faster contact for customers |
| KvK number | Chamber of Commerce registration |
| VAT number (BTW-id) | Where applicable |

The KvK publishes guidance on which details must appear on websites, invoices and correspondence.

## Pages Your Site Should Have

- **Terms and conditions** (algemene voorwaarden), accessible before purchase or sign-up
- **Privacy statement** describing what data you collect, why, where it is processed and users' rights
- **Cookie information and consent** for non-essential cookies
- **Contact page** with the details above
- **Pricing** including whether prices are with or without VAT; consumers must see total prices including VAT

## Consumer vs Business Customers

If you sell to consumers, additional rules apply: clear total prices including VAT, information about the right of withdrawal where applicable, how subscriptions renew and how to cancel. B2B-only services have fewer consumer rules but customers still expect transparency.

## Where AI-Built Sites Fall Short

- Footer without company name, KvK or address
- Privacy statement copied from a template that lists services the app does not use and misses ones it does
- No cookie consent while analytics tags load immediately
- Prices on the pricing page without clarity about VAT
- Contact only via a chat widget

## Structured Data Helps Too

Adding Organization structured data with your legal name, address, contact details and links to your profiles helps search engines and AI assistants connect your website to your real company. That consistency is part of how assistants decide which source to trust.

## Why These Details Matter Beyond Compliance

Company details are first a legal expectation and second a trust signal. Visitors deciding whether to sign up or pay look for evidence that a real business stands behind a product: a registered name, an address, a way to contact someone. Business buyers check KvK numbers to verify suppliers. Search engines and AI assistants use consistent company information across your site and external listings to understand who you are. Google's guidance on helpful content and its quality rater guidelines emphasise trustworthiness and clear information about who is responsible for a website.

## The Legal Background in Brief

EU rules on electronic commerce require providers of online services to make certain information easily, directly and permanently accessible, including the provider's name, geographic address, contact details including email, trade register entry and VAT number where applicable. Dutch law implements these requirements, and the KVK explains which details businesses must show on websites, invoices and correspondence. Consumer law adds rules for selling to consumers, such as clear total prices, information about the right of withdrawal where applicable and clear terms for subscriptions. A lawyer can confirm the exact obligations for your business.

## Where to Place the Information

| Information | Common placement |
| --- | --- |
| Legal name, address, KvK, VAT | Footer on every page and a contact or legal page |
| Email and phone | Contact page, footer, checkout |
| Terms and conditions | Footer, sign-up and checkout, with acceptance recorded |
| Privacy statement | Footer, sign-up forms, cookie banner |
| Cookie information | Consent banner and a cookie section in the privacy statement |
| Pricing including or excluding VAT | Pricing page and checkout |

Easily accessible means visitors can find it in one or two clicks from any page.

## Privacy Statements That Match Reality

Template privacy statements often list services you do not use and miss those you do. Write yours from your actual data map: which personal data you collect, for which purposes and legal bases, which processors receive it and where, how long you keep it, and how people can exercise their rights. Update it when you add processors or features. The Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens provides guidance on what a privacy statement should contain. A statement that matches reality is also your best preparation for customer questionnaires.

## Cookie Consent Done Right

Under e-privacy rules, non-essential cookies and similar tracking technologies require prior consent. In practice, analytics, advertising pixels and session recording tools must not load until a visitor agrees, unless they qualify for exemptions such as strictly privacy-friendly analytics configurations recognised by the regulator. A consent banner that loads trackers immediately and asks afterwards does not meet this standard. Choose a consent tool that blocks scripts until consent, records choices and makes withdrawing consent as easy as giving it.

## Terms and Conditions

General terms should be available before purchase or sign-up and accepted actively, for example with a checkbox or a clear statement next to the sign-up button. Store the version accepted and the date. For B2B SaaS, terms cover liability, payment, term and termination, intellectual property and data handling; for consumer services, consumer protection rules limit what you can include. Have a lawyer draft or review them, especially if you sell to consumers.

## Structured Data for Your Organisation

Add Organization structured data in JSON-LD with your legal name, logo, address, contact details, website and social profiles. Keep it identical to the information in your footer, KvK entry and Google Business Profile. Google's structured data documentation explains the properties. Consistent entity information helps search engines and AI assistants connect your website to your real-world business and cite you accurately.

## Consumer Versus Business Offers

If you sell to consumers, additional rules apply under Dutch and EU consumer law: total prices including VAT and any unavoidable costs, clear information before purchase, rules on subscription renewal and cancellation, and, for many distance contracts, information about the right of withdrawal and its exceptions for digital content and services. Business-to-business offers have fewer statutory protections, but customers still expect clarity about price, term and renewal. If you serve both, make sure each audience sees the information that applies to them.

## A Simple Checklist for Your Website

- Legal company name, address, KvK number and VAT number in the footer
- Contact page with email and preferably a phone number
- Terms and conditions linked and accepted at sign-up or checkout
- Privacy statement matching your actual data processing
- Consent banner that blocks non-essential trackers until consent
- Prices clearly including or excluding VAT, matching the checkout
- Organization structured data consistent with your registered details
- Company details consistent with your KvK entry and Google Business Profile

## Common Mistakes

- A footer with only a logo and copyright line
- Privacy statements copied from another company
- Analytics loading before consent
- Prices without VAT clarity for consumers
- Different company names on the website, invoices and KvK entry
- Contact only through a chat widget

## The Payoff

Visible, consistent company details cost an afternoon to add and improve conversion, reduce prospects' doubts, support compliance and help search engines and AI assistants understand exactly who you are. Few improvements deliver so much for so little effort.

## Where to Start Today

Look at your website footer. If your legal name, KvK number, VAT number and address are not there, add them today, then compare your privacy statement against the services your app actually uses.

## Company Details on Invoices and Emails

The same consistency applies beyond your website. Invoices must contain your company details and VAT number, and transactional emails should identify your company clearly in the footer with a contact address. Customers who receive an email from an unfamiliar sender name without company details may treat it as phishing. Matching names and details across website, emails, invoices and your KvK entry strengthens trust at every touchpoint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I have to show my KvK number on my website?

Dutch businesses are generally expected to show their KvK number, along with other identification details, on their website and correspondence. Check the KVK's current guidance for your legal form.

### Do I need to show prices including VAT?

For consumers, yes: total prices must include VAT. For business-only offers, prices excluding VAT are common, but state clearly that VAT is excluded.

### Is a privacy statement template good enough?

Only as a starting point. Your statement must describe what your app actually does: which data, which processors, where it is processed and for how long. Templates rarely match AI-built apps' real data flows.

### Why does Manifera emphasise company details on websites?

Because trust is part of conversion. Manifera's own presence across Amsterdam, Singapore and Ho Chi Minh City is stated clearly on its sites, and 11+ years of client work show that visible, verifiable company details reassure business buyers.

### Do company details help AI assistants recommend my business?

Yes. Consistent company names, addresses and registration details across your site and structured data help AI assistants identify your business as a real, specific entity and cite it with confidence.
