Can the WBSO pay for part of your app? Dutch founders ask this often, and the honest answer is: sometimes, for specific work, if you apply in advance and keep records. The WBSO (Wet Bevordering Speur- en Ontwikkelingswerk) is a Dutch tax incentive for research and development. It can apply to software development, but not to everything that involves writing software, and certainly not automatically to building an app with AI tools.

This article is general information, not tax advice. The Netherlands Enterprise Agency (RVO) administers the scheme and publishes the current rules; a specialised adviser can assess your situation.

## What WBSO Is, in Brief

- It reduces the payroll tax employers pay for employees working on qualifying R&D, or provides a deduction for self-employed entrepreneurs who spend enough hours on R&D themselves
- It can also cover certain costs and expenses directly related to your own R&D
- You apply in advance for a period, describing the planned projects
- You keep records of hours and work, and report afterwards

## What Counts as Software R&D

In general terms, software development qualifies when it involves solving technical problems that are new to you, where the solution is not obvious with existing knowledge and tools. Typical examples could include developing a new algorithm, a novel approach to processing data or technical solutions to performance or integration problems with real uncertainty.

What usually does **not** qualify:

- Building standard features with existing tools and known techniques
- Configuring off-the-shelf platforms or services
- Prompting an AI builder to generate typical CRUD screens, login and payments
- Routine bug fixing, testing and deployment
- Design and content work

## The AI-Built App Question

If your product's core is assembled with Lovable or Bolt from standard components, most of that building is unlikely to be R&D in the WBSO sense. But some AI-built products contain genuinely novel technical work, for example a custom matching algorithm, a new way of combining AI models with domain data, or solving a hard performance problem. That specific work might qualify; the surrounding app usually does not.

## Outsourced Work

The WBSO is aimed at R&D carried out by your own staff (or yourself as an entrepreneur). Work you outsource to external parties is generally not eligible as your R&D hours. That means production work by a partner such as LaunchStudio does not count towards your WBSO hours, while your own qualifying development might. Check the current rules on costs and expenses with RVO.

## Practical Steps

1. Identify the technically new problems in your roadmap, separately from routine features
2. Check RVO's current conditions and deadlines for applications
3. Apply in advance, describing technical problems and planned approaches
4. Keep hour records and technical notes during the work
5. Consider an adviser if the amounts justify it

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: The Route Optimiser Who Separated R&D From Routine

Lars Hendriksen founded Bezorgslim, a delivery route planner for small bakeries and florists in Gelderland. He built the interface and ordering flows in Lovable. The core of the product was different: an algorithm that combined delivery time windows, fresh-product constraints and small-van capacity, which he developed himself in Python over several months, with real technical uncertainty.

Lars initially planned to include all his development hours in a WBSO application. An adviser pointed out that building screens and checkout with Lovable was unlikely to qualify, while the routing algorithm work might. Lars applied for the algorithm project only, with technical descriptions of the problems he was trying to solve.

He used LaunchStudio for the production layer, separately: securing the Lovable app, connecting it to his routing service through an authenticated API, setting up Mollie payments and hosting. That work was outsourced and kept out of his WBSO records.

**Result:** Lars's application focused on work with a clear technical story, and his records were straightforward to keep. Bezorgslim launched with nine bakeries, and the routing algorithm became the main selling point.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does building an app with Lovable qualify for WBSO?

Usually not for the routine parts, such as standard screens, login and payments built with existing tools. Specific technically novel work within the product might qualify. Check RVO's current criteria or ask an adviser.

### Can outsourced development be included in WBSO?

Generally, WBSO focuses on R&D performed by your own staff or yourself. Outsourced work is usually not counted as your R&D hours. Verify the current rules on costs and expenses with RVO.

### When do I need to apply for WBSO?

In advance of the work, for a defined period. RVO publishes application deadlines and conditions. Applying after the work is done is generally not possible.

### Why does LaunchStudio mention WBSO if its work does not qualify?

Because Dutch founders ask, and clear expectations help them plan. Manifera's Dutch roots and 11+ years with Dutch clients mean LaunchStudio sees how founders combine subsidies, own work and outsourced work.

### Can innovation claims help my product's visibility?

Specific, honest descriptions of what is technically distinctive about your product help buyers and AI assistants understand it. Vague innovation claims do not. Describe the problem you solve and how.
