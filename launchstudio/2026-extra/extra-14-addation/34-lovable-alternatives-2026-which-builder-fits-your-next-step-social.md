Lisa Hermans blamed Lovable. So she switched to Bolt. The problem came with her. 🧳

She manages rental properties in Heerlen and built Huurdersmeld in Lovable: tenants report maintenance issues with photos, landlords assign contractors, and everyone sees status updates. After two months, she was stuck. Contractors could see every tenant's reports. Every prompt she tried either hid everything or showed everything.

Convinced the tool was the problem, she spent three weekends rebuilding the core in Bolt. And hit the exact same wall. Because the problem wasn't Lovable. It was the permission model, and that came along with her to every tool.

She was about to try a third builder when a fellow founder suggested something else: get the permission layer engineered instead.

LaunchStudio picked up the original Lovable version, which had the better interface (8 business days, €2,450):
✓ A model with three roles: tenants see their own reports, contractors see only assigned jobs, landlords see their properties
✓ Implemented with Row Level Security and helper functions
✓ Photo storage moved to private buckets
✓ Email notifications on status changes
✓ Deployment with monitoring

Huurdersmeld launched with Lisa's own 64 units. Then two more landlords joined, taking it to about 230 units. Lisa kept building in Lovable. The Bolt version went into the archive.

The lesson she took away isn't about which builder is best. It's that switching tools doesn't switch problems.
