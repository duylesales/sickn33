Founders who outgrow doing everything themselves often look at nearshore teams: development companies in countries such as Poland, Romania, Portugal or Ukraine, close to the Netherlands in time zone and often lower in cost than Dutch agencies. Nearshore teams typically work on a dedicated-team or time-and-materials basis, with developers assigned to your product for months at a time.

LaunchStudio works differently. We are not a nearshore team: our front office is in Amsterdam and our engineering is done by Manifera's development centre in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam, with a hub in Singapore. And instead of assigning a team for months, we deliver defined production work at a fixed price, with optional ongoing care. This page compares the two models honestly, including when a nearshore team is the better choice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Nearshore teams provide capacity:** developers working on your roadmap over months.
- **LaunchStudio provides outcomes:** a defined production launch or project at a fixed price.

- **Time zones matter less than process:** clear scope and written updates work across both models.
- **Many founders use a project partner first,** then add a dedicated team once the roadmap justifies it.

## What a Nearshore Team Is Good At

A dedicated nearshore team gives you developers who learn your product and work on it every day. That suits companies with a clear roadmap, a steady flow of features and someone who can lead the team, often a CTO or experienced product manager. Proximity in time zone makes daily stand-ups and quick calls easy, and cultural closeness to the Netherlands is often a plus. For a funded startup building a large product, a good nearshore team can be an excellent long-term option.

## Where the Model Fits Less Well

For a non-technical founder with an AI-built app, a dedicated team can be a mismatch in several ways:

- **Management.** Someone must define tasks, prioritise, review code and make architectural decisions. Without a technical lead, the team either waits for instructions or makes decisions the founder cannot judge.
- **Commitment.** Minimum terms and monthly costs continue even in quiet weeks.
- **Rebuild bias.** New teams sometimes prefer to rebuild in their favourite stack rather than work with AI-generated code, which can be expensive.
- **Scope gaps.** A team assigned to features may not prioritise security reviews, payment reconciliation, backups or monitoring unless asked.

None of this is a flaw in nearshore teams; it reflects what the model is designed for.

## Data Protection Considerations

With partners outside the EU, including LaunchStudio's engineering in Vietnam, access to personal data needs appropriate safeguards and documentation. We work in your accounts with least-privilege access, prefer staging environments with test data where possible, sign a processing agreement and document access, so you can answer customers' questions about subprocessors accurately. Hosting and databases stay in the EU regions you choose.

## Managing a Team Versus Managing a Project

The biggest practical difference for non-technical founders is how much they must manage. With a dedicated team, you are effectively running an engineering department: setting priorities, accepting work, resolving disagreements and judging quality. With a project partner, you agree outcomes, review progress and accept results against a written scope. Founders who enjoy product management and have technical support may prefer the first. Founders who want to focus on customers and sales usually prefer the second, at least until they hire a technical lead.

## Questions to Ask a Nearshore Provider

- What is the minimum commitment and notice period?
- Who leads the team technically, and who decides architecture?
- Will you work with our existing AI-built code, or recommend a rebuild?
- How do you handle security, payments, backups and monitoring?
- Where will code and accounts live, and who owns them?
- How do you handle personal data and processing agreements?
- What do weekly updates look like?

## Quality and Continuity

With any team, quality depends on people and process. Nearshore providers range from excellent to mediocre, and the same is true for offshore ones. Signs of quality are consistent: senior engineers involved, code review, automated tests, documentation, clear ownership of accounts by the client and transparent communication. LaunchStudio's projects include documentation, an account inventory, tests and project instructions for AI tools as standard, so knowledge does not depend on individuals. That also makes it easy to bring in a different team later if you choose.

## How Updates and Communication Work

Founders often ask how they will know what is happening during a project. With a dedicated team, communication usually follows a rhythm of daily stand-ups and sprint reviews, which works well when someone on your side joins them. With LaunchStudio, you receive a short written update at agreed moments, typically every one or two days during active work, plus a call when decisions are needed. Updates say what was done, what is next and what we need from you, in plain language. The shared channel stays open for questions, and the Amsterdam contact handles anything that needs a conversation during Dutch hours.

## The Risk of Buying Capacity Without a Plan

One pattern we see regularly: a founder signs a dedicated-team contract to "move faster", but without a technical lead or a clear backlog. The team spends the first weeks learning the AI-generated codebase, proposes rewriting parts of it and starts on features while security and operations gaps remain. After three months, the monthly invoices have added up and the founder is unsure whether the product is better or just different. That outcome is avoidable with either model, but it is more common when capacity is bought before the work is defined. Defining the production work first, and then deciding how much ongoing capacity you need, keeps spending tied to outcomes.

## Real example

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: A Team Waiting for Instructions

Roos van Dam runs a subscription platform for local farm boxes from Wageningen, built with Lovable and Supabase. After an angel investment, she signed a contract with a nearshore team of three developers to "professionalise the product". Two months in, the team had proposed rewriting the app in a different framework, built a new design system and was waiting for Roos to prioritise a backlog she did not know how to write. Meanwhile, customers occasionally received the wrong box because of a webhook bug, and nobody had looked at security or backups.

Roos paused the contract at the end of its minimum term and asked LaunchStudio to review the original app. The review found that the Lovable app was sound in structure but had the typical gaps: loose database policies between farms, Mollie webhooks without idempotency, emails from the default provider, no staging and untested backups. A Launch & Grow project of €4,200 fixed these, added a simple admin panel for farms and set up monitoring. Roos continued building smaller features herself in Lovable.

**Result:** Wrong-box complaints stopped within a week of release. Over the following six months, the platform grew from 14 to 31 farms. Roos later hired a technical lead, who decided which work to keep in-house and which to outsource, on a documented foundation.
