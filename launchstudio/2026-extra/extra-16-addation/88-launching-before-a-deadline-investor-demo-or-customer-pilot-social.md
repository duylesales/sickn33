⏳ T-minus 12 days to an investor pitch

T-12: THE PLAN
Laura Mertens runs a start-up in Amsterdam that built Zorgmaatje with v0, Cursor and Supabase: it helps family caregivers coordinate tasks for elderly relatives. Pitch with three investors in twelve days. A pilot with a home care organisation a week after that. Her plan for the remaining days: build a medication reminder feature.

T-12: THE CHANGE OF PLAN
LaunchStudio quoted €3,300 within Launch Ready, and convinced her to postpone the new feature.

T-12: DAY ONE
The Supabase project turns out to be in a former co-founder's account. The transfer starts.

T-11 TO T-7: WEEK ONE
The review finds that family members of one elderly person can see other families' tasks through a broad policy, care notes are in a public bucket, and the service key is used in a client-side function. All fixed in the first week, with tests.

T-6 TO T-3: WEEK TWO
✓ Staging
✓ Email authentication
✓ Backups, with a restore test
✓ Monitoring
✓ A separate demo environment with fictional families, and a reset script

T-4: FEATURE FREEZE
Laura rehearses twice on the demo environment, and prepares a one-page summary of the security measures.

T-3: PROJECT DONE
9 business days in total.

T-0: THE PITCH
Two investors ask how the app is secured, and who owns it. Laura answers with the summary. One of them leads her pre-seed round.

T+7: THE PILOT
Starts on time with 25 families, without incidents in the first two weeks. The medication reminder follows a month later.

"I wanted to impress investors with one more feature. What impressed them was that I could explain exactly how families' data was protected." - Laura Mertens
