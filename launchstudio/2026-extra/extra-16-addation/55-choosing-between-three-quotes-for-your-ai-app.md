**Short answer:** To choose between three quotes for your AI-built app, first rewrite them into the same format: what each will deliver, what each excludes, what each assumes and what happens after launch. Then compare total cost, not headline price, including likely extra work, support and provider costs. Finally, judge fit: how well each supplier understood your app, how clearly they communicate and who owns the result. The cheapest quote is often the one that covers the least.

It is a familiar situation. You asked three parties to help make your app production ready, or to add a feature, and received three very different documents. One is a single paragraph with a low price. One is a long proposal with phases and an hourly estimate. One is a fixed price with a list of scope items. The numbers vary widely, and you are not sure whether you are comparing like with like. This page gives you a method to compare them fairly, written for founders without technical background.

## Key Takeaways

- **Normalise first:** put all three quotes into the same structure before comparing prices.
- **Exclusions and assumptions** explain most of the price differences.
- **Total cost** includes extras, support, provider costs and your own time.
- **Fit matters:** the supplier who understood your app best usually delivers best.
- **Ask the same questions** to all three and compare the answers.

## Step 1: Rewrite Each Quote in the Same Format

Create a simple table with one column per quote and these rows:

| Item | Quote A | Quote B | Quote C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pricing model (fixed, hourly, retainer) | | | |
| Price or estimate | | | |
| Scope items listed | | | |
| Deliverables | | | |
| Assumptions | | | |
| Exclusions | | | |
| Timeline | | | |
| Post-launch support | | | |
| Ownership of code and accounts | | | |
| Payment terms | | | |

Fill it in from each document. Where a quote is silent, write "not mentioned". Gaps are information: something not mentioned is usually not included.

## Step 2: Map Scope Against What Your App Needs

For an AI-built app going into production, check whether each quote covers these areas:

- Database access rules tested with several user roles
- Secrets removed from the browser and rotated where exposed
- Authentication settings, redirects and admin access
- Payments confirmed by verified webhooks, with failures and refunds handled
- Email sending from your domain with authentication records
- Private file storage and upload limits
- Staging and production environments
- Deployment pipeline and database migrations
- Backups with a tested restore
- Monitoring and alerts
- Ownership of all accounts
- Documentation and instructions for your AI tool

A quote that covers four of these at a low price is not cheaper than one that covers all twelve at a higher price. It simply leaves eight for later.

## Step 3: Compare Pricing Models

| Model | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed price | Is the scope specific? Are assumptions and exclusions listed? How are changes handled? |
| Hourly estimate | What is the range? What happens if it runs over? How will you see hours spent? |
| Retainer | What is the monthly commitment and notice period? What output can you expect? |
| Phased | Is each phase priced? Can you stop after a phase? |

Hourly estimates often look lower because they show the optimistic case. Ask for the realistic and worst case, and for examples of similar projects and their actual hours.

## Step 4: Estimate Total Cost

Headline price is one part of what you will spend. For each quote, estimate:

- **Likely extras:** items not in scope that you will need, priced roughly
- **Overrun risk:** for hourly quotes, the realistic rather than optimistic estimate
- **Support:** what is included after launch and what ongoing help will cost
- **Provider costs:** whether hosting is billed directly or bundled with a markup
- **Your time:** how much managing and testing each option requires
- **Switching cost:** how hard it would be to change supplier later

Often, the totals end up much closer than the headline prices suggested, or the order changes entirely.

## Step 5: Judge Fit

Price and scope are not everything. Consider:

- **Understanding.** Did they look at your app before quoting? Did their questions show they understood your users and data?
- **Clarity.** Could you understand the quote without help? Plain language usually means clear thinking.
- **Honesty.** Did anyone tell you something you did not want to hear, such as a risk or a limitation?
- **Approach to your code.** Do they work with what you built, or push a rebuild?
- **Ownership.** Will code and accounts stay in your name?
- **Continuity.** What happens if the person you spoke to is unavailable?
- **References.** Can you speak to founders with similar apps?

## A Scoring Table

Give each quote a score from 1 to 5 per criterion, weighted by importance to you:

| Criterion | Weight | Quote A | Quote B | Quote C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope coverage | 25% | | | |
| Total cost | 20% | | | |
| Price certainty | 15% | | | |
| Understanding of your app | 15% | | | |
| Ownership and lock-in | 10% | | | |
| Support after launch | 10% | | | |
| Communication | 5% | | | |

Adjust weights to your situation. A founder with a hard deadline might weight timeline highly; one with sensitive data might weight scope and understanding more.

## Questions to Ask All Three

Send the same questions to each supplier and compare answers side by side:

1. How will you test that users cannot see each other's data?
2. How will payments be confirmed, and what happens with failed renewals and refunds?
3. What exactly do I receive at the end?
4. What is excluded, and what would those items cost separately?
5. What happens if you find something unexpected?
6. Who owns the code and accounts during and after the project?
7. What support is included after launch?
8. Can I speak to two founders you have worked with?

Specific answers suggest experience. Generic answers suggest guesswork.

## Common Patterns in Quote Sets

**The low single paragraph.** Often from a freelancer or small agency, promising "full production readiness" for a low price. Usually covers a few known fixes; much of the scope is implicit or missing.

**The large rebuild proposal.** Often from an agency that prefers its own stack. May be thorough but discards what already works, at a much higher cost and longer timeline.

**The specific fixed-price scope.** Lists items, assumptions and exclusions. Easier to compare and to hold the supplier to.

None of these patterns is automatically right or wrong, but knowing them helps you read between the lines.

## Red Flags

- No questions asked before quoting
- No exclusions listed
- Accounts created in the supplier's name
- Pressure to sign quickly
- A rebuild recommended without explaining why the existing app cannot be fixed
- Unlimited revisions or vague promises such as "bank-grade security"
- Hosting bundled with a markup and no direct access

## When Prices Differ Wildly

If one quote is three times another, ask both suppliers to explain the difference. Usually, the gap comes from scope: one includes reconciliation of past payments, a staging environment and tests, the other does not. Sometimes it comes from approach: a rebuild versus fixes. Occasionally it comes from overhead or margins. Understanding which helps you decide whether the expensive quote is overpriced or the cheap one is incomplete.

## Using a Second Opinion

If you are unsure, ask a neutral technical person to read the three quotes, or ask one supplier to comment on the scope of the others, knowing they have an interest. LaunchStudio is happy to review quotes you have received, including ones from competitors, and tell you whether the scope covers what your app needs, even if you end up choosing someone else.

## Negotiating After Comparing

Once you have compared the quotes, you can go back to suppliers with specific questions. Ask the low-priced supplier whether they would include the missing scope items, and at what price. Ask the high-priced supplier which items could be postponed to a later phase. Ask a fixed-price supplier whether part of the work could be split into an optional phase you approve later. Suppliers usually respond well to precise questions based on a fair comparison, and you often end up with a better-fitting quote than any of the originals.

## Making the Decision

After normalising, mapping scope, estimating total cost and scoring fit, one quote usually stands out. If two are close, choose the one whose team understood your app best and communicated most clearly, because those qualities predict how the project will go when something unexpected happens. Tell the suppliers you did not choose; a short, polite message keeps relationships open for the future.

## A Worked Example

Suppose you receive three quotes for a Lovable SaaS with two roles and Stripe subscriptions. Quote A costs €900 and promises to "secure the app and fix bugs" within a week. Quote B is an hourly estimate of 60 to 90 hours to rebuild the backend in a different framework. Quote C costs €2,600 fixed and lists 18 scope items, five assumptions and seven exclusions.

After normalising, Quote A covers database policies and a few known bugs, but not payments, staging, backups, monitoring or ownership. Adding those items separately would likely cost more than the difference to Quote C. Quote B covers a lot, but replaces working code, has an open-ended cost and leaves you with a new codebase your AI tool has never seen. Quote C covers all twelve production areas and states what happens if something unexpected appears. On total cost and fit, Quote C wins, even though its headline price is almost three times Quote A's.

This example is simplified, and in other situations the low quote may be the right one, for instance when your app is small and you only need two specific fixes. The method matters more than the result.

## What to Do Before Asking for Quotes Next Time

You get better quotes when you ask better. Before contacting suppliers, write one page describing your app: what it does, which tools you used, who the users are and what each may see, whether payments are involved, any deadlines and any problems you have noticed. Give every supplier the same page and the same access. That alone makes quotes far easier to compare, because each supplier responds to the same information.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I compare quotes from developers fairly?

Rewrite them into the same structure: pricing model, scope, deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, timeline, support and ownership. Then compare total cost, including likely extras, and judge how well each supplier understood your app.

### Why are quotes for the same app so different?

Mostly because of scope and approach. One quote may cover only known issues, another a full production setup, a third a rebuild. Assumptions, exclusions, support and margins explain the rest.

### Should I pick the cheapest quote?

Only if it covers what your app needs. A low quote that leaves out payments, staging, backups or ownership often costs more in total once the missing work is added or an incident occurs.
