## Answering Honestly Without Losing the Deal

A "no" is not always a deal breaker. Security teams assess risk, not perfection. They often accept that a startup does not have ISO 27001 if the controls behind it are reasonable, documented and improving. What they do not accept is answers that turn out to be untrue.

We help you phrase honest answers with context. Instead of "No" for penetration testing, an answer might explain that an internal security review was completed with fixes applied, that an independent test is planned before a specific date and how vulnerabilities are handled in the meantime. Instead of "No" for certifications, it might describe the controls in place and the plan to formalise them as the company grows. That kind of answer often satisfies reviewers, especially for smaller contracts.

Where a gap genuinely blocks the deal, such as missing MFA for administrators or no tested backups, the best answer is usually to fix it before submitting. Many such fixes take hours, not weeks.

## Quick Fixes That Often Make a Difference

- Enabling and enforcing MFA for all admin and provider accounts
- Testing a backup restore and documenting the result
- Moving hosting and storage to an EU region where possible
- Making private storage buckets private and using signed links
- Removing secrets from code and rotating any that were exposed
- Turning on dependency update alerts and applying current security updates
- Writing a short incident response procedure with roles and notification steps
- Documenting subprocessors and checking their data processing terms
- Restricting staff and collaborator access to what they need

## Why AI-Built Apps Need Fact-Finding First

Founders who built with AI tools often do not know exactly how their app is configured. The tool chose the database settings, generated the storage buckets and wrote the authentication flow. Answers based on assumptions, such as "all data is encrypted and access is restricted per customer", may be partly wrong. Fact-finding checks the actual configuration: the region of every service, whether every table has a database policy, whether storage is private, which processors receive data and how backups work. Only then are the answers reliable.

## Building an Answer Library

The first questionnaire is the hardest. The second is easier if you keep what you learned. The answer library we deliver groups answers by topic, notes the date they were last checked and links to the supporting documents. When the next questionnaire arrives, most questions map to existing answers, and only new or changed topics need work. We recommend reviewing the library every six months or after significant changes to your app or providers, so answers stay true.

Some founders turn parts of the library into a public security page or trust centre. That reduces the number of questions customers ask in the first place and helps prospects evaluate your product earlier in the buying process.

## Handling Follow-Up Calls

After the questionnaire, many security teams schedule a call. It is usually friendly and practical: they want to understand the architecture, check a few answers in more detail and agree on any follow-up actions. Founders often find these calls stressful because the questions become technical quickly. We prepare a one-page architecture summary for the call, brief you on the likely questions and can join to answer the technical parts. The outcome is usually a short list of agreed actions with dates, which we help you track so they are delivered on time and the next review goes smoothly.

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: 180 Questions and Ten Days to Answer

Annelies Dijkstra runs a training company in Groningen and built Leerpad with Bolt and Supabase, a platform for compliance training with videos, quizzes and certificates. A national logistics company with around 2,000 employees wanted to roll it out to its warehouse staff and sent a questionnaire of 180 questions with a ten-day deadline before its quarterly vendor review.

Annelies could answer about a quarter of the questions herself. Fact-finding showed that the database and storage were in an EU region, HTTPS was enforced and provider encryption was in place. It also showed that admin accounts had no MFA, certificates were stored in a public bucket, backups had never been restored, the AI quiz generator sent learner names to the model provider and there was no written incident procedure or subprocessor list.

LaunchStudio drafted answers for all 180 questions, marking 14 that needed Annelies's decision. Before submission, MFA was enforced for admins, certificates moved to private storage with signed links, a backup restore was tested and documented, learner names were removed from AI prompts and a short incident response procedure and subprocessor list were written. For penetration testing and certification, the answers described the internal review, the fixes applied and a dated plan for an independent test. LaunchStudio joined a 45-minute call with the customer's security officer to explain the architecture.

**Result:** The logistics company approved Leerpad in its quarterly review with one follow-up action, the independent test, due within six months. Annelies used the answer library for two further questionnaires that year, each completed in about a day.
