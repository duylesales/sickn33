Meanwhile, the app works, customers are interested and the founder wonders whether they really need to give away a significant share of the company, or pay a senior salary, before the product has proven itself.

## What a CTO Actually Does

In an early-stage startup, a CTO typically sets the technical direction, chooses architecture and tools, builds or leads the building of the product, hires and manages engineers, represents technology to investors and customers, owns security and reliability and translates business goals into a technical roadmap. In a company with a growing engineering team, leadership, hiring and process become the largest part of the job.

Much of this becomes essential once you have a product with traction and a team to build. Before that, many of these tasks are either premature, such as hiring and managing engineers, or can be handled by a combination of AI tools, focused external help and the founder's own product judgement.

## The Cost of Hiring Too Early

A technical co-founder typically receives a significant share of the company. A senior CTO hire in the Netherlands commands a substantial salary, often with equity on top. Both are reasonable when the person fits and the company needs them. The risk lies in hiring before you know what you need. Founders who bring in a CTO for an early prototype sometimes find that the CTO wants to rebuild the product in their preferred stack, disagrees with the AI-first approach or loses interest when growth is slower than hoped. Undoing a co-founder relationship is painful and expensive, especially when equity is involved.

## When You Probably Do Not Need a CTO Yet

- Your app works and was built with AI tools you are comfortable with.
- You are still searching for product-market fit.
- You have no plan to hire engineers in the next six to twelve months.
- Your technical needs are well defined: launch safely, handle payments, answer security questions.
- Investors are not yet requiring a technical leader as a condition for funding.

In this phase, LaunchStudio plus your AI tools can usually carry the product forward, with a fractional CTO or experienced adviser for strategic questions if needed.

## Signs It Is Time to Hire a CTO

- You have clear traction and a roadmap that needs a dedicated team.
- You plan to hire several engineers who need leadership.
- Technical decisions now shape the company's competitive advantage.
- Investors or major customers require a named technical leader.
- You spend most of your time on technical coordination instead of the business.
- The product's complexity has outgrown project-based help.

At that point, hiring a CTO is usually the right move. A clean, documented, production-ready codebase makes that hire easier, because candidates see a product they can build on rather than a rescue project.

## Fractional CTO: The Middle Option

A fractional CTO works part time, often a few days a month, providing strategy, architecture reviews, investor conversations and hiring support. It is a useful option for founders who need technical judgement without full-time cost. The limitation is execution: a fractional CTO advises, but someone still has to do the work. Many founders combine a fractional CTO for direction with LaunchStudio for delivery, which covers both strategy and implementation at a fraction of a full-time hire.

## What Investors Actually Ask

Founders often assume investors want a CTO on the cap table. Many early-stage investors care more about whether the product works, whether the technology is secure and maintainable and whether the founder understands its risks. A clear answer often satisfies them: the product is built with modern AI tools, it has been reviewed and made production ready by an experienced team, it runs on standard infrastructure the company owns, it is monitored and documented, and a technical hiring plan exists for a later stage. A production readiness summary and a monthly health note are concrete evidence for that answer.

## Questions to Ask Yourself

- What technical problem am I trying to solve in the next six months?
- Do I need someone to lead a team, or to get specific work done?
- How much equity or salary can I commit, and for what?
- What would a CTO do in their first three months here?
- Could AI tools plus focused help cover that for now?

If the honest answer to the first question is "launch safely and keep the app running", you likely do not need a CTO yet.

## What a Good CTO Candidate Will Want to See

When the time comes to hire, strong candidates look at the product and its foundations before committing. They ask who owns the code and accounts, how the app is deployed, whether there are tests and monitoring, what the data model looks like and how security has been handled. A founder who can show a production readiness summary, an account inventory, a deployment pipeline and a year of health notes is far more attractive to an experienced CTO than one who shows a preview link and a list of known bugs. Preparing the product properly is therefore not only an alternative to hiring a CTO; it is also the best preparation for hiring one.

## Avoiding the "Help for Equity" Trap

Many founders receive offers from developers willing to "help for equity". Some of these partnerships become excellent co-founder relationships. Others end with a small amount of work done, a meaningful share of the company given away and nobody responsible when the app breaks. If you consider such an offer, agree in writing what the person will do, over what period, with vesting so equity is earned over time, and what happens if the arrangement ends. A fixed-price project for the production work can make the decision easier, because the urgent technical needs are covered while you get to know each other.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a CTO to launch my AI-built app?

Usually not. Many AI-built products can launch and grow to early revenue with the founder's AI tools plus focused production support. A CTO becomes important when you need to lead an engineering team and own technical strategy long term.

### Is LaunchStudio a replacement for a CTO?

No. LaunchStudio delivers defined production work and ongoing care; it does not provide long-term technical leadership or hire your team. For many early-stage founders, though, it covers the technical needs until a CTO hire makes sense.

### What do investors want to see if I have no CTO?

Usually evidence that the product is secure, owned by the company, maintainable and documented, plus a plan for technical leadership as the company grows. A production readiness summary and ongoing monitoring provide that evidence.

### How does Manifera's experience help founders without a CTO?

Manifera has 11+ years of experience, 160+ delivered projects and 120+ engineers, and its CEO has founded and led technology companies. LaunchStudio brings that experience to founders who need production-grade work without hiring a senior technical leader yet.

### Does having a CTO affect SEO and AI visibility?

Not directly. What matters is the technical foundation: fast, crawlable pages, a custom domain, HTTPS, structured data and stable hosting, plus good content. Those can be delivered with or without a CTO on the team.
