LaunchStudio's front office is in Amsterdam, but many founders who contact us are based elsewhere in Europe, particularly in Belgium and Germany. They built their apps with the same AI tools, face the same production challenges and often sell to Dutch customers as well as local ones. The question they ask is practical: does working with a Dutch-based team across the border change anything?

In most respects, it does not. Our projects are remote by design, our engineering team in Ho Chi Minh City works the same way for every client and the production work is identical. A few details differ: language, invoicing and VAT, local payment methods, legal requirements for websites and some expectations of local customers. This page explains them.

## Key Takeaways

- **Projects run remotely** for all clients, with optional meetings in Amsterdam.
- **Communication is in English or Dutch;** Flemish founders can work in Dutch.
- **Business clients in other EU countries** are generally invoiced under the reverse-charge VAT rules.
- **Local payment methods** such as Bancontact and German preferences are supported through common payment providers.
- **Local legal requirements,** such as the German Impressum, are part of the launch checklist.

## Remote by Design

Every LaunchStudio project works remotely: an intro call by video, access through invitations to your accounts, written updates, a findings call, your check on staging and a handover call. Founders in Antwerp, Ghent, Brussels, Cologne, Düsseldorf, Aachen or Berlin work with us exactly as founders in Rotterdam or Groningen do. If you prefer to meet in person, the team is at Herengracht 420 in Amsterdam, a short train ride from Antwerp, Brussels or the German border region.

## Language

Projects are run in English or Dutch. Founders in Flanders often prefer Dutch, which works well. German-speaking founders usually work in English. Documentation, summaries and project instructions for AI tools are written in English by default, because AI tools and most developers work best with English instructions, but customer-facing texts such as email templates and legal page structures can be prepared in the languages your users need.

## Invoicing and VAT

For business clients in other EU member states, services are generally taxed where the customer is established, and invoices are issued under the reverse-charge mechanism, with your VAT number on the invoice and VAT accounted for in your own country. We need your company name, address and a valid VAT number, which we verify. Your accountant can confirm how to record the invoice in Belgium or Germany. The same package ranges apply: Launch Ready (€800 to €3,500) and Launch & Grow (€2,500 to €7,500 plus €49 per month).

## Local Payment Methods

If your app takes payments, local preferences matter:

| Market | Common payment preferences |
| --- | --- |
| Belgium | Bancontact, cards, sometimes payment by bank transfer and Payconiq by Bancontact |
| Germany | Cards, PayPal, SEPA Direct Debit, invoice-based payment through buy-now-pay-later providers, bank transfer |
| Netherlands | iDEAL, cards, SEPA Direct Debit |

Payment providers such as Mollie and Stripe support many of these methods. We configure the methods that match your customers and make sure webhooks, refunds and reconciliation work for each. Availability of specific methods changes over time, so we check current options with your provider.

## Legal Pages and Website Requirements

Websites aimed at German users are generally expected to include an Impressum, a legal notice with company details, under German law. Belgian and Dutch websites have their own requirements for company information, such as company registration and VAT numbers. Privacy policies, cookie consent and terms should reflect the jurisdictions you serve. We do not write legal texts, but we make sure the pages exist, are linked from every page and contain the technical facts your lawyer needs, and we include them in the launch readiness checklist.

## E-Invoicing in Belgium

Belgium has been introducing mandatory structured electronic invoicing between businesses, using the Peppol network. If your app generates invoices for Belgian business customers, or if your customers expect invoices in a structured format, check the current requirements with your accountant. We can integrate a Peppol access provider or an accounting system that supports it, so invoices from your app meet the requirements.

## Data Protection Expectations

The GDPR applies across the EU, so the rules are largely the same. Expectations can differ in practice. German customers, in particular, often ask detailed questions about hosting location, subprocessors, data processing agreements and technical and organisational measures. Belgian public sector and healthcare customers have their own procurement habits. EU hosting, a clear subprocessor list and a processing agreement are standard in our projects and answer most of these questions. Where a German customer requests hosting in Germany specifically, we discuss options with your providers.

## Selling Across Borders

Many Belgian and German founders also sell to Dutch customers, and the other way round. A production-ready app for multiple countries typically needs multilingual interfaces, local payment methods, correct VAT handling for each customer type and country, time zones handled correctly, legal pages per market and support processes in the right languages. We help plan these as part of the project or as follow-up work.

## Accessibility Requirements

The European Accessibility Act applies across the EU, implemented in each country's national law. Public sector bodies in Belgium and Germany also have their own accessibility requirements for digital services. If you sell to consumers in covered categories or to public bodies in these countries, accessibility work may be required. We review key journeys against WCAG and prepare information for accessibility statements.

## Time Zones and Communication

Belgium, Germany and the Netherlands share a time zone, so calls during business hours work naturally. Engineering in Ho Chi Minh City starts earlier in the European day, which means progress is often visible when you start your morning. Updates arrive in writing, so nothing depends on being in the same office.

The production work itself is identical: database policies tested with real roles, secrets moved server-side, verified payment webhooks, email authentication, staging and pipelines, backups with restore tests, monitoring, ownership of accounts and documentation. The same tools, Lovable, Bolt, v0, Cursor and Claude Code, produce the same patterns regardless of where the founder lives.

Some founders like to meet the team once, for example at the start or at the handover. Herengracht 420 in Amsterdam is reachable from Antwerp in about an hour and a quarter by train and from the German border region in a couple of hours. Most founders never need to visit, but the option is there.

## Contracts and Practicalities

The quote and terms are written in English, or in Dutch if you prefer. Agreements are under the terms stated in the quote. Where personal data is involved, we sign a processing agreement, which can follow your own template if your customers or advisers require it. Payment terms and schedule are stated in the quote.

## Customers in Other EU Countries

Founders in Belgium and Germany often sell beyond their own borders too, to France, Austria, Luxembourg or the Nordic countries. The same principles apply across the EU: GDPR-compliant hosting and processing, VAT rules that depend on whether customers are businesses or consumers, local payment methods and legal pages that match each market. We help plan multi-country launches step by step, starting with the markets that matter most to your revenue.

## Working With Local Partners

Founders sometimes already have a local accountant, lawyer or freelancer. We work well alongside them: the accountant advises on VAT and invoicing, the lawyer on legal texts and contracts, the freelancer on features. We focus on the production foundation and coordinate where our work touches theirs, for example by providing technical facts for a processing agreement or setting up invoicing exports for the accountant.

## Multilingual Apps Done Properly

Serving Belgian and German customers often means supporting Dutch, French and German alongside English. AI-built apps frequently hard-code text in components, mix languages on the same page or translate only the visible interface while emails, error messages and PDFs stay in one language. A production-ready multilingual app stores texts in translation files, detects or lets users choose their language, translates emails, invoices and notifications consistently and formats dates, numbers and currencies according to each locale. For public pages, separate language versions with correct language tags help search engines show the right page to each visitor. We set up the structure, and your translators or a translation service provide the texts.

## Typical Questions From German Customers

German business customers often ask a consistent set of questions before adopting a SaaS product: where exactly is data hosted, which subprocessors are involved and where are they located, is there a processing agreement, which technical and organisational measures are in place, how are backups handled, how quickly are incidents reported and can data be exported or deleted on request? Preparing a short document that answers these in advance, and keeping it current, shortens sales cycles considerably. The production project provides the technical facts for that document.

## Belgian Customers and Public Bodies

Belgian customers, especially in healthcare, education and the public sector, often have their own procurement rules, language requirements and expectations about data protection. Supporting both Dutch and French, documenting hosting and subprocessors clearly and preparing for accessibility questions help when selling to these organisations.

## Domains and Local Presence

Founders selling in several countries often consider local domains such as .be or .de in addition to a .com or .nl domain. Separate domains can increase trust with local customers but add work: certificates, redirects, email authentication for each sending domain and consistent content. Alternatively, one domain with language sections works well for many SaaS products. We set up whichever structure you choose, with correct redirects, language tags and email configuration, so search engines and customers find the right version.

## Real example

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: A Ghent SaaS Selling to Germany

Pieter-Jan De Smet runs a scheduling SaaS for physiotherapy practices from Ghent, built with Lovable and Supabase. It worked well for Belgian practices, and a German practice group with twelve locations in North Rhine-Westphalia wanted to adopt it. The group's data protection officer sent questions about hosting, subprocessors and technical measures, asked for a processing agreement and noted that the website had no Impressum. German patients also expected to pay by card or SEPA rather than Bancontact.

Pieter-Jan contacted LaunchStudio and worked in Dutch, while the German group's questions were answered in English. The Launch & Grow project, quoted at €4,400 and invoiced under reverse charge, separated practices and locations with roles enforced through Row Level Security, moved storage for treatment documents to private buckets, confirmed EU hosting and documented subprocessors. Mollie was configured for Bancontact, cards and SEPA Direct Debit with verified webhooks for each. The interface and email templates were prepared for Dutch, French and German, an Impressum page and legal page structure were added for the German market, with texts provided by Pieter-Jan's lawyer, and a technical annex for the processing agreement was delivered.

**Result:** The German group's data protection officer approved the platform after reviewing the documentation, and all twelve locations started using it. Pieter-Jan later onboarded two more German practices with the same documents.

> *"I thought working with a Dutch team and a German customer would make everything complicated. In practice, the only differences were an Impressum, a payment method and a few answers in German."*
> - **Pieter-Jan De Smet, Founder, Kinerooster (Ghent)**

**Cost & Timeline:** €4,400 (Launch & Grow package: practice separation, private storage, payment methods per country, multilingual templates, legal page structure and processing agreement annex, plus €49 per month, invoiced under reverse charge) - completed in 12 business days.
