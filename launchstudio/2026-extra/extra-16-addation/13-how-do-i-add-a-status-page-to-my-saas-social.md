"Is Roosterlink down?"
"My staff didn't get their shifts"
"Is anyone there??"
...

That was Wouter Kuipers's inbox, about forty minutes into one Friday afternoon. (Subject lines illustrative.)

Wouter built Roosterlink with Lovable and Supabase: shift scheduling for around 120 small hospitality businesses. That Friday, a problem at an upstream email provider delayed shift notification emails for about forty minutes. Wouter found out when his inbox filled up, and several restaurant owners posted complaints on social media, assuming Roosterlink was broken.

He had no status page. So he answered every email individually, which took the rest of the afternoon. Afterwards, a restaurant group evaluating Roosterlink asked how incidents were normally communicated. He didn't have a good answer.

What LaunchStudio set up (4 business days, €1,350):
✓ A hosted status page at status.roosterlink.nl, separate from the app's hosting
✓ Components for login, scheduling, shift notifications, payments and the mobile web app
✓ Uptime checks on login, a database health endpoint and the scheduling API, from several locations, with alerts to Wouter and a backup contact
✓ Email delivery monitored through the provider's webhooks
✓ Links from the app's help menu and error screens, and subscriptions for updates
✓ Update templates for investigating, identified, monitoring and resolved, plus a one-page incident process

📥 Inbox (3)
Two months later, a short hosting incident affected logins for about fifteen minutes. Wouter posted an update within five minutes. Three support emails arrived, each answered with a link to the status page.

And the restaurant group signed, naming the status page and incident process in its decision.
