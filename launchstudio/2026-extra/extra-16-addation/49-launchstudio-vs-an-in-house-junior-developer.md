Once an AI-built app gains customers, many founders think about hiring their first developer. A junior developer seems the natural first step: affordable compared with a senior hire, enthusiastic, comfortable with AI coding tools and available every day. For many companies, that hire works out well. For others, it creates an unexpected problem: the founder now manages a developer without being able to judge the work, while production risks such as security, payments and data remain unsolved because the junior has never handled them before.

This page compares hiring an in-house junior developer with working with LaunchStudio, and explains how the two can work together. It is written by LaunchStudio; we have tried to be fair, including where a junior hire is the right choice.

## Key Takeaways

- **A junior developer adds daily capacity** for features, bug fixes and product work.
- **Production risks need experience** juniors rarely have yet: security, payments, data, operations.
- **The total cost of a hire** includes salary, employer charges, equipment, onboarding and your time.
- **Juniors need supervision;** without a senior to learn from, growth and quality suffer.
- **The strongest setup** is often a production-ready foundation plus a junior with external senior support.

## What a Junior Developer Is Good At

Junior developers bring energy and daily presence. They can build features from your roadmap, fix bugs reported by customers, improve the interface, write content for the app, help with support and learn your product deeply over time. Many are fluent with AI coding tools and can be very productive with Cursor or Claude Code. For a founder who spends hours each week prompting changes, a junior can take that load and free time for sales and customers.

## Where Juniors Usually Struggle

Production engineering is learned through experience, often painful experience. Most junior developers have not yet:

- Designed database access rules that hold up when customers try to access each other's data
- Handled payment webhooks, retries, refunds and reconciliation
- Set up staging, deployment pipelines, migrations and rollbacks
- Configured email authentication, backups and monitoring
- Responded to a security incident or a production outage
- Answered a customer's security questionnaire

That is not a criticism; it is what "junior" means. The risk arises when a junior is the only technical person and is expected to handle these areas alone. Mistakes in production engineering tend to be invisible until they cause an incident.

## Supervision: The Hidden Challenge

Juniors grow fastest with a senior who reviews their work, explains decisions and catches mistakes before they reach production. A non-technical founder cannot play that role. Without it, juniors may learn slowly, adopt habits that create problems later or feel isolated and leave. Many good junior developers specifically look for a team where they can learn.

There are ways to solve this: a fractional CTO, a senior freelancer for code reviews or an external partner who sets standards and reviews larger changes. LaunchStudio can play that role under Launch & Grow, reviewing changes that touch security, payments or data and helping the junior learn the patterns used in your codebase.

## When Hiring a Junior Makes Sense

- Your app is production ready and has a documented foundation
- You have a steady stream of feature work and bug fixes
- You can provide or arrange senior guidance
- You want someone who learns your product and customers deeply
- Your revenue supports a salary for at least a year

## When LaunchStudio Makes More Sense First

- The app has not been reviewed for security, payments and data
- You need the app launched safely within weeks, not months
- You cannot yet afford a full-time salary
- You need documentation for customers or investors
- You want predictable costs while revenue is still uncertain

## Questions to Ask Before Hiring

- What will the developer work on in the first three months?
- Who will review their work and help them grow?
- Is the app production ready, or will they inherit unknown risks?
- Can the business afford the full cost for at least a year?
- How will knowledge be documented if they leave?

## What Your Junior Can Learn From the Setup

A production-ready setup is also a learning environment. The project rules explain why certain patterns are used. Tests show what must never break. Migrations demonstrate how database changes are made safely. Reviews of changes in sensitive areas give the junior concrete feedback from experienced engineers. Founders tell us their junior developers often appreciate this structure, because it shows them professional practices they would otherwise learn only after years or after an incident. That improves retention, which matters because replacing a developer is expensive and disruptive.

## AI Tools Change the Junior Role, Not the Risks

AI coding assistants have made junior developers far more productive. A junior with Cursor or Claude Code can produce in a day what used to take a week. That is good news for feature work, but it also means mistakes can be produced faster. An assistant will happily write a database query without an ownership check, add a key to a client component or disable a failing policy to make a test pass. A senior engineer recognises those patterns immediately; a junior may not. The project rules, tests and reviews in a production-ready setup act as guardrails, so the speed of AI-assisted development does not turn into a faster route to incidents.

## Planning the First Three Months

A clear plan helps a first developer succeed. In the first weeks, the focus is on understanding the product, the customers and the codebase, fixing small bugs and shipping one or two modest features through the full release process. In the second month, the junior takes ownership of a feature area, with reviews for anything touching security, payments or data. By the third month, they should be releasing regularly, writing tests for their own work and contributing to the roadmap. Writing this plan down before the start date also helps you check whether a hire is really what the business needs now.

## A Note on Retention

Replacing a developer who leaves after a few months costs time, money and momentum. Clear structure, good feedback and a codebase that is pleasant to work in are among the strongest reasons developers stay.

## Hiring Practicalities in the Netherlands

Hiring an employee in the Netherlands involves a written employment contract, payroll with wage tax and social contributions, holiday allowance, sick pay obligations, pension arrangements where applicable and occupational health and safety requirements. Many startups use a payroll provider to handle the administration. If you are considering a trainee or apprentice instead, different rules and subsidies may apply. An accountant or HR adviser can help you understand the obligations before you post the vacancy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is hiring a junior developer cheaper than LaunchStudio?

Over a year, usually not. A junior's total cost includes salary, employer charges, holiday allowance, equipment and onboarding. LaunchStudio's projects are fixed price, and ongoing care costs €49 per month. They also do different things: a junior adds daily capacity, LaunchStudio covers production engineering.

### Can a junior developer make my app production ready?

Some can, especially with guidance. Most have limited experience with database security, payments, deployment and operations, which are the areas where mistakes are costly and often invisible. A senior review or external support significantly reduces that risk.

### Can LaunchStudio support my junior developer?

Yes. Under Launch & Grow, we monitor the app, apply updates and can review changes that touch security, payments or data. We also prepare documentation and project rules that help a new developer get productive quickly.
