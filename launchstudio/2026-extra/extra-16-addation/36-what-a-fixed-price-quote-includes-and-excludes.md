**Short answer:** A LaunchStudio fixed-price quote includes a written scope of the work, the deliverables you receive, the assumptions the price is based on, a timeline, how the work is accepted and the post-launch support. It excludes new features not listed, hosting and provider costs, legal advice and certifications, and any extra work that you have not approved in writing. If we find something unexpected, we tell you before doing anything outside the scope, and nothing is added to the price without your agreement.

Founders who built their app with AI tools often have one bad experience with software budgets behind them: a freelancer who said "about a week" and billed three, or an agency estimate that doubled. A fixed price is meant to prevent that. But a fixed price is only as good as what it describes. This page explains exactly what our quotes contain, what they leave out and how to compare fixed-price quotes from anyone.

## Key Takeaways

- **The scope is written down** in plain language, item by item.
- **Assumptions are explicit,** so you know what the price depends on.
- **Exclusions are listed,** not hidden in small print.
- **Unexpected findings are discussed first,** never added silently.
- **Provider costs are separate** and billed directly to you by the providers.

## What the Scope Looks Like

A good scope is concrete. "Improve security" is not a scope; "add Row Level Security policies to all tables, so users see only their own organisation's data, with automated tests" is. Each item in our quotes describes an outcome you can check. For a typical Launch Ready project, the scope might contain ten to twenty items, grouped by area: security, data, payments, email, production setup, monitoring and handover.

The scope also names what we will leave as it is. If your interface stays unchanged, the quote says so. If a known issue is out of scope because it is low risk or needs a separate decision, it is listed with a recommendation.

## Assumptions: What the Price Depends On

Every fixed price rests on assumptions. Ours are written in the quote, for example:

- The app has around 15 tables and three user roles.
- Payments use Stripe Checkout with two subscription plans.
- The current data volume is below 50,000 rows.
- You can provide access to all accounts within two business days.
- The app will stay on its current hosting providers.

If an assumption turns out to be wrong in a way that changes the work, for example the app has 40 tables instead of 15, we raise it immediately. Stating assumptions protects both sides: you know what you are buying, and we can offer a fixed price without padding it for unknowns.

## What Quotes Exclude

| Usually excluded | Why |
| --- | --- |
| New features not listed in the scope | Fixed price requires a fixed scope |
| Redesign of the interface | Our work focuses on production readiness, not design |
| Hosting, database, email, payment and AI provider costs | Billed directly by providers to your accounts |
| Domain registration and developer account fees | Paid to registrars, Apple or Google |
| Legal advice, privacy policy wording, contracts | Requires a lawyer |
| Certifications and accredited penetration tests | Require independent auditors or testers |
| Ongoing monitoring after the support window | Part of Launch & Grow |
| Fixing issues caused by later changes made outside our process | Handled as new work |

## What Happens When We Find Something Unexpected

Reviews of AI-built apps sometimes uncover problems that were not visible during the quote, such as a second database nobody mentioned, an exposed key in an old branch or a payment flow that never worked for one plan. When that happens:

1. **We tell you** what we found, why it matters and how urgent it is.
2. **We propose options,** for example fixing it now for a fixed amount, planning it later or accepting the risk.
3. **You decide.** Nothing outside the scope is done without your written approval.
4. **The original scope continues** at the agreed price regardless of your choice.

Security issues that put customers at immediate risk, such as an exposed service key, are reported the same day so you can decide quickly. In practice, most small surprises are absorbed in the original price; we raise those that would change the work materially.

## How Scope Changes Work

Founders sometimes want to add something during the project: "while you are in there, could you also...". That is fine. Small requests that fit within the scope's intent are often handled without changing the price. Larger requests are quoted as a change: a short description, a fixed amount and the effect on the timeline. You approve or decline. This keeps the project predictable and avoids the slow creep that turns fixed-price projects into disputes.

## Acceptance: When Is the Work Done?

Each quote states how the work is accepted. Typically, we demonstrate the changes on staging, you check the key journeys, and once you confirm, we release to production. The project is delivered when the scope items are completed, released and documented. If you find something that does not match the scope during acceptance, we fix it as part of the price. The 48 hours of post-launch support begin at release.

## Provider Costs: Why They Are Separate

Your app runs on services such as Supabase, Vercel, Netlify, Stripe, Mollie, Postmark or AI model providers. These services bill you directly, in your accounts. We do not resell hosting or add a margin. Keeping provider costs separate makes them transparent and means you own every account. The quote mentions expected provider changes, for example "a Supabase Pro plan is recommended for backups and will cost around the amount listed on Supabase's pricing page", so there are no surprises.

## Payment Terms

Payment terms are stated in the quote. For shorter projects, payment is typically split between the start and delivery; for longer Launch & Grow projects, there may be an intermediate milestone. The €49 per month for Launch & Grow ongoing care is billed monthly. Prices are shown excluding VAT where applicable, and the quote states the VAT treatment for your situation, for example for business customers elsewhere in the EU.

## Fixed Price Versus Hourly

| | Fixed price | Hourly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost certainty | High: price agreed in advance | Low: depends on time spent |
| Risk of overrun | Carried by the supplier | Carried by you |
| Flexibility mid-project | Changes are quoted | Changes are absorbed, at a cost |
| Best for | Known types of work with clear outcomes | Open-ended exploration or unclear goals |
| What you need | A clear scope | Trust and close oversight |

Production readiness for AI-built apps is well suited to fixed prices because the common issues are well known. For open-ended product development, a retainer or phased fixed-price projects usually works better than pure hourly billing.

## Checklist for Comparing Fixed-Price Quotes

Whether you compare LaunchStudio with other suppliers or several freelancers, these questions reveal how fixed a fixed price really is:

- Is the scope written as specific, checkable items?
- Are the assumptions listed?
- Are the exclusions listed?
- What happens if the supplier finds something unexpected?
- How are changes requested, priced and approved?
- What are the deliverables, including documentation and tests?
- How is the work accepted?
- What support is included after release, and for how long?
- Who owns the code and accounts?
- Are provider costs included, separate or marked up?

A quote that answers all ten clearly is a quote you can rely on. One that answers few of them is closer to an estimate.

## Red Flags in Fixed-Price Quotes

- A single line such as "make the app production ready" with a price
- No exclusions, which usually means disagreements later
- Ownership of code or accounts staying with the supplier
- Hosting bundled with a markup and no access to the provider account
- "Unlimited revisions", which is rarely sustainable and often means vague scope
- Pressure to sign before the supplier has seen the app

## Ownership and Intellectual Property

A quote should also say who owns the result. In our projects, the code, configuration, documentation and accounts belong to you. We work in your repository and your provider accounts, so there is nothing to transfer at the end. When the project finishes, we remove or reduce our access at your request. This matters more than founders often expect: suppliers who keep code in their own repository or hosting in their own account can make it hard and costly to switch later.

## What We Need From You to Quote

- A 15-minute intro call
- Read access to your code and configuration, or a screen share
- A list of the tools and services the app uses
- Any deadline, customer requirement or known problem

With that, a written quote usually follows within one or two business days. The [price calculator](https://launchstudio.eu/en/#calculator) gives an indication before the call.

## Real example

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: Two Quotes That Looked the Same

Marloes Peters runs a pet-sitting platform in Leiden built with Bolt and Supabase, connecting pet owners with local sitters and handling payments through Mollie. Before launching in three more cities, she asked two suppliers to make the app production ready. Both quotes came in around €2,500. One was a single paragraph promising "full production readiness, security and optimisation". The other was LaunchStudio's, with 16 scope items, five assumptions, eight exclusions, a timeline and an acceptance process.

Marloes chose the detailed quote, mainly because she could see what she was paying for. During the review, LaunchStudio found that sitters' identity documents, uploaded for verification, were in a public bucket, and that a second, forgotten Supabase project still held old test data including real email addresses. The first issue was within scope and fixed immediately. The second was outside scope; LaunchStudio reported it the same day and offered to export and delete the project for a small fixed amount, which Marloes approved.

The original 16 items were delivered at the agreed price in seven business days. One request Marloes added during the project, a change to the booking confirmation text, was handled without a change in price because it fitted the email work already in scope.

**Result:** Marloes launched in three new cities on schedule and knew the total cost before the project started, apart from the one extra item she chose to approve. She later used the quote's exclusions list to plan her next steps: a privacy policy review with her lawyer and Launch & Grow for ongoing care.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does a LaunchStudio fixed-price quote include?

A written scope of work items, deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, timeline, what we need from you, the acceptance process, post-launch support and the fixed price with payment terms. Everything is in plain language so non-technical founders can check it.

### What if you find more problems than expected?

We tell you what we found, explain the options and their costs, and you decide. Nothing outside the scope is done or charged without your written approval, and the original scope continues at the agreed price.

No. Hosting, database, email, payment and AI providers bill you directly in your own accounts, without any markup from us. The quote mentions expected provider changes so you can plan for them.
