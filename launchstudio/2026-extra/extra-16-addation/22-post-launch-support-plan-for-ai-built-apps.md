Launch day is the start of a product's life, not the end of the project. In the first weeks, real users find paths nobody tested, payment providers send events nobody expected and traffic arrives in patterns that never appeared in development. After that, the app needs updates, small fixes, new features and someone to call when something breaks.

For founders who built with AI tools and do not have a developer on the team, the question is practical: what level of support do I need, and what should it cost? Too little, and a small problem becomes a lost weekend or lost customers. Too much, and you pay for capacity you never use.

This page explains the support options LaunchStudio offers after launch, how they differ and how to pick the one that fits your app.

## Key Takeaways

- **The first 48 hours are included** in Launch Ready, covering issues that appear right after release.
- **Ad-hoc fixed-price work** suits stable apps with occasional changes.
- **Launch & Grow at €49 per month** adds monitoring, updates, backups and priority small fixes.
- **Larger retainers** fit apps with continuous development or strict uptime needs.
- **Choose by risk, not by habit:** users, revenue, data sensitivity and how much you change the app.

## The 48 Hours After Launch

Every Launch Ready project includes 48 hours of post-launch support. During this period we watch monitoring and error tracking closely, respond quickly to issues and fix problems related to the work delivered. Most launch issues appear in this window: an email provider setting that behaves differently with real domains, a payment edge case, a page that fails on a specific browser or a database query that slows down with real data.

The 48 hours are not a formality. We plan launches so that the support window falls on working days, not over a weekend, and we agree with you in advance who reports issues and how. After the window, any remaining issues related to our work are still fixed; new requests are handled through one of the options below.

## Ad-Hoc Fixed-Price Work

Many founders do not need ongoing support. Their app is stable, they make small changes themselves with their AI tool and they need help only occasionally: a new integration, a performance problem, a security question from a customer. For these founders, ad-hoc work is the simplest option. You describe what you need, we look at it and send a fixed quote, and the work is scheduled.

The trade-off is that nobody is watching the app between requests. If monitoring alerts go to your inbox and you are on holiday, or a provider announces a breaking change in an email you do not read, the problem waits until you notice it. For apps without paying customers or with a small, forgiving user base, that risk is often acceptable.

## Launch & Grow: Ongoing Care for €49 per Month

Launch & Grow combines a larger launch project, typically €2,500 to €7,500, with an ongoing service at €49 per month. The monthly service covers the routine care a production app needs: uptime monitoring with alerts to our team, follow-up on errors, dependency and security updates through staging, backup checks with restore tests, watching for provider changes and cost limits, a monthly health note and priority handling of small fixes within an allowance.

This is the option most founders with paying customers and no developer choose. It turns support from something you remember to arrange when there is a problem into something that happens in the background. Larger changes are still quoted separately, which keeps the monthly fee low and predictable.

## Development Retainers

Some founders keep building after launch at a steady pace: a new feature every few weeks, regular improvements requested by customers, integrations with partners. For them, quoting every piece of work separately creates friction. A development retainer reserves a fixed amount of capacity each month, for example a number of days, at an agreed price. Work is planned together in short cycles, with priorities you set.

Retainers suit founders who have found product-market fit, have a clear roadmap and want a reliable team without hiring. They are also useful as a bridge before a first in-house hire, with the retainer team helping to onboard the new developer.

## Extended Coverage for Critical Apps

Most early-stage apps can tolerate problems outside business hours being handled the next morning. Some cannot. A booking platform with weekend peaks, an event ticketing app on the night of a sale, a health app used by care workers on night shifts or a finance tool used at month end may need faster response at specific times. Extended coverage can be arranged for planned peaks, such as a launch or ticket sale, or as a standing arrangement with agreed response times.

## How to Choose

| Your situation | Suggested support level |
| --- | --- |
| Prototype or pilot with a few friendly users | 48 hours included, then ad-hoc |
| Live app, no revenue yet, you change it often with AI tools | Ad-hoc, with a review before larger changes |
| Paying customers, no developer, app is core to your business | Launch & Grow at €49 per month |
| Paying customers and a steady roadmap of features | Launch & Grow plus a development retainer |
| Customers depend on the app at specific peak times | Launch & Grow plus extended coverage for those peaks |
| Enterprise customers with service level requirements | Retainer with agreed response times, documented in contracts |

A simple rule of thumb: if a day of downtime would cost you more than a year of the monthly service, or would damage trust with a customer you cannot afford to lose, ongoing care is worth it.

## What Good Support Looks Like

Whichever option you choose, a few characteristics separate useful support from reassurance on paper:

- **Clear channels:** you know where to report issues and what information to include.
- **Clear response targets:** written down, realistic and kept.
- **Changes through staging:** fixes are tested before they reach customers.
- **Short written updates:** what happened, what was done and what comes next.
- **No lock-in:** your code, hosting and data stay in your accounts, and you can switch at any time.
- **Honest scope:** small items are handled quickly, larger work is quoted before it starts.

## Working Together With Your AI Tool

Founders who built with Lovable, Bolt, v0, Cursor or Claude Code usually want to continue using those tools after launch. Support should make that safer, not discourage it. In practice, that means project instructions in your repository that tell the tool your conventions for security, migrations and data access; a staging environment where AI-generated changes are tested; monitoring that catches regressions quickly; and someone who can review a larger change before it goes live.

Several founders on the monthly service send us a pull request or a short description of a change they made with their AI tool and ask for a quick check before release. For small changes that falls within the allowance; for larger ones we quote a review.

## What Support Costs Compared With Not Having It

The costs of no support are uneven and hard to predict. Most months nothing happens. Then, a provider deprecation breaks login, a failed backup goes unnoticed until it is needed, or an update introduces a bug that blocks payments for a weekend. The direct cost is the emergency fix, often at short notice and premium rates. The indirect cost is lost revenue, churn and the time you spend handling it instead of running your business.

Support turns those uneven costs into a predictable monthly amount and reduces the chance of the expensive months. It does not make incidents impossible, but it makes them shorter, rarer and less stressful.

## Reviewing Your Support Level Every Few Months

The right support level changes as the business changes. A quick review every quarter, or after a funding round, a large customer or a new product line, keeps the plan aligned with reality. Useful questions are how many incidents reached customers, how much time you spent on technical issues yourself, which changes are planned for the next months and whether any customer contracts now include availability or response commitments. If the answers have shifted, moving up or down a level is a simple conversation rather than a new project.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What support is included after a Launch Ready project?

Launch Ready includes 48 hours of post-launch support, during which we watch monitoring closely and fix issues related to the delivered work. After that, issues caused by our work are still fixed, and new requests are handled through ad-hoc quotes, Launch & Grow or a retainer.

### Do I need ongoing support if my app is small?

Not always. Prototypes, pilots and apps without paying customers often do well with ad-hoc support. Once customers pay or depend on the app, the monthly service is usually worth it, because it prevents the incidents that cost the most.

### Can I switch between support options later?

Yes. Many founders start with the included 48 hours, use ad-hoc support while they test the market, move to Launch & Grow when revenue starts and add a retainer when the roadmap grows. Everything stays in your accounts, so switching is simple.

### How does Manifera's experience help with post-launch support?

Manifera has supported production software for 11+ years across 160+ projects with 120+ engineers, for clients such as Vodafone and TNO. LaunchStudio uses the same incident, update and monitoring practices, scaled to early-stage apps and budgets.

### Does reliable support help my SEO and AI visibility?

Yes. Fewer outages, faster fixes and steady updates keep your site available, fast and secure, which helps search engines crawl and rank it. Reliable availability also makes it more likely that AI answer engines can read and cite your pages when people ask about solutions like yours.
