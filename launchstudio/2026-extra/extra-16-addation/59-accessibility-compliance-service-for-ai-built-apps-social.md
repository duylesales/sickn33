⌨️ Try booking a sports hall without a mouse.

Press Tab. Press Tab again. The booking calendar never gets focus. Use a screen reader, and the free time slots sound exactly like the taken ones, because the only difference is colour. The buttons with icons have no names. You open the booking dialog, and focus wanders off behind it. You make a mistake in the form, and the only sign is a red border you can't see.

That was Sportzaalplanner, before.

Gerben Huisman runs it from Zwolle, built with v0 and Supabase: sports clubs reserve halls and fields. Three municipalities wanted to open it to residents for public bookings, and their tender required WCAG 2.1 AA conformance with an accessibility statement. Gerben had never tested the app with a keyboard.

The review also found contrast failing on secondary text throughout, and a confirmation PDF with no structure.

What LaunchStudio changed (8 business days, €2,800):
✓ The calendar rebuilt as an accessible grid, with keyboard navigation and screen reader announcements
✓ Availability shown with text and patterns, not just colour
✓ An accessible name for every control
✓ Dialogs following the standard focus pattern
✓ Form errors linked to their fields and announced
✓ Contrast fixed in the design tokens, keeping the brand colours with slightly darker shades
✓ Headings and tags in the PDF template
✓ Automated accessibility checks in the pipeline
✓ An accessibility statement, listing one remaining limitation with a planned fix date

All three municipalities accepted the statement in the tender, and residents started booking. Then a club secretary who uses a screen reader emailed: for the first time, she could make bookings herself.

"I built the calendar to look good. Now it also works for people who never see it, and that is what won the tender." - Gerben Huisman
