Founders who are considering LaunchStudio often ask a simple question: who will actually work on my app? It is a fair question. Behind a fixed-price package, you are trusting people with your code, your accounts and your customers' data. Knowing how the team is organised, where people are and how work is coordinated helps you judge whether that trust is justified.

This page explains the relationship between LaunchStudio and Manifera, how the team is structured across Amsterdam, Ho Chi Minh City and Singapore, which roles are involved in a typical project and what that structure means for you as a founder.

## Key Takeaways

- **LaunchStudio is a service of Manifera,** a software development company with 11+ years of experience.
- **Founders meet the team in Amsterdam;** engineering happens in Ho Chi Minh City, with a hub in Singapore.
- **Each project has a clear contact** and engineers with relevant experience.
- **Work is reviewed and documented,** so knowledge does not depend on one person.
- **The structure provides depth and continuity** at a price that fits early-stage products.

## LaunchStudio and Manifera

Manifera is a software development company with 11+ years of experience, 160+ delivered projects and 120+ engineers, working for clients that include Vodafone and TNO. Its work ranges from building new software to maintaining and improving existing systems, with dedicated teams and fixed-price projects.

LaunchStudio is Manifera's service for AI-native founders: people who built an app with Lovable, Bolt, v0, Cursor or Claude Code and need it secure, reliable and ready for real customers. LaunchStudio packages that work into two clear offers, Launch Ready (€800 to €3,500, fixed) and Launch & Grow (€2,500 to €7,500 fixed, plus €49 per month), and draws on Manifera's engineers, processes and experience to deliver them.

His background in security shapes how seriously LaunchStudio treats access control, secrets and data protection in every project, not as optional extras but as part of what production readiness means.

## Roles on a Typical Project

| Role | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Project contact | Your main point of contact: scope, updates, decisions and scheduling |
| Lead engineer | Technical review, approach, key fixes and quality of the result |
| Engineers | Implementation of fixes, tests, setup and documentation |
| Reviewer | Reviews changes before they are merged, especially for security, payments and data |
| Support engineer | Monitoring and fixes after release, and ongoing care under Launch & Grow |

On small Launch Ready projects, one engineer may cover several roles, with a second engineer reviewing. On larger Launch & Grow projects, several engineers work in parallel, coordinated by the lead engineer.

## How Work Is Coordinated Across Time Zones

Ho Chi Minh City is five hours ahead of the Netherlands in summer and six in winter. Engineering therefore starts well before the Dutch working day and overlaps with the European morning. In practice:

- Work done overnight from a Dutch perspective is visible when you start your day
- Questions raised in the Dutch afternoon are often answered by the next morning
- Releases can be prepared early and checked during the European morning
- Calls with you take place in Dutch business hours

Written updates, a shared channel and pull requests in your repository keep everyone aligned without relying on everyone being online at once.

## Quality Practices

| Practice | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Pull request reviews | A second engineer checks changes before they reach your app |
| Automated tests | Critical rules such as data access and payments are verified on every change |
| Staging first | Changes are tested away from your live app |
| Written findings and summaries | Decisions and results are traceable |
| Account inventory and documentation | Knowledge is shared, not held by one person |
| Incident procedures | Problems are handled in a consistent order |

These practices are the same ones Manifera uses for larger clients, scaled to the size of an early-stage product.

## Continuity

One of the advantages of a team over an individual freelancer is continuity. If an engineer is ill, on holiday or moves to another project, another engineer can continue, because the work is documented, reviewed and stored in your repository. Under Launch & Grow, the team that responds to alerts knows your app from the project and the documentation, so you do not start from zero each time.

## How We Handle Access and Data

All work happens in your accounts with individual access, and production data is accessed only when necessary, under a processing agreement. Because engineers work from Vietnam, remote access to personal data is treated as an international transfer under the GDPR and covered by appropriate safeguards, such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses, documented so you can list it accurately in your own subprocessor information. Hosting stays in the regions you choose.

## AI in Our Own Work

Our engineers use AI coding tools in their daily work, under business terms that do not allow training on your code, and without putting customer personal data or secrets into prompts. That experience is one reason we understand the patterns your AI tool produces, and why our project instructions help your tool work more predictably after the project.

## How Engineers Learn About Your App

Before writing any code, engineers read what you sent, the findings from the review and the business rules agreed with you, such as who may see what. They work through the main journeys on staging with test accounts for each role. Questions they cannot answer from the code or documentation go to you through the project contact, phrased in plain language. This combination of reading, testing and asking keeps the work grounded in how your business actually works, not in assumptions about what an app like yours usually does.

## Knowledge Sharing Within the Team

Because the team works on many AI-built apps, patterns found in one project improve the next. Common issues in apps from a particular tool, effective approaches to policies, safe migration patterns and good project instructions are shared internally. Founders benefit from that accumulated experience, which is one reason fixed prices are possible, without any of their own code or data being shared with other clients.

## A Day in a Project

A typical project day illustrates how the pieces fit together. Early in the European morning, the engineering team in Ho Chi Minh City picks up where it left off: implementing fixes agreed in the findings call, writing tests and opening pull requests. A reviewer checks the changes before they are merged into staging. By the time you start your day in the Netherlands, a short update is waiting: what was done, what is next and any question that needs your answer. During Dutch business hours, the project contact handles calls and decisions, and engineers join when technical detail helps. If a release is planned, it happens during the morning overlap, with both teams watching monitoring. The rhythm is predictable, and you rarely have to chase anyone for information.

## How Founders Can Check Our Work

You do not have to take quality on trust. Every change appears as a pull request in your repository, with a description of what it does and why. Tests run visibly in the pipeline. Findings and decisions are written down. If you work with a technical adviser, a freelancer or a future CTO, they can review the history at any time. Several founders invite an adviser to look at the pull requests during the project, which we welcome.

## Communication Style

We aim for short, plain updates without jargon, honest answers when something is uncertain and early warnings when a risk appears. If a timeline is at risk, you hear it as soon as we know, along with options.

## Ask Us Anything

If anything about the team, locations or way of working is unclear, ask in the intro call. We would rather answer a direct question early than leave doubts that affect your decision.

## Real example

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: Meeting the Lead Engineer Before Signing

Floris Brandsma runs a SaaS for veterinary practices from Leeuwarden, built with Cursor and Supabase, handling appointments, patient records for animals and invoicing. Before committing to a Launch & Grow project of €5,600, he had one concern: he had never worked with a team abroad and wanted to know who would have access to his practices' data.

LaunchStudio arranged a video call with the project contact in Amsterdam and the lead engineer in Ho Chi Minh City. The engineer walked Floris through the planned approach: staging with generated data, production access only for the planned data migration, two named engineers with access, pull request reviews and the processing agreement with standard contractual clauses. Floris asked about holidays; the engineer explained how documentation and reviews allowed a colleague to take over.

During the project, one engineer was unexpectedly away for a week. A colleague continued from the documented findings and open pull requests, and the timeline did not change. Floris received updates each morning that covered work done while he slept.

**Result:** The project finished in 15 business days. Floris later told another founder that the call with the lead engineer was what convinced him, and that the time difference turned out to be an advantage rather than a problem.

> *"I wanted to see the person who would touch my practices' data before I signed. Meeting them, and understanding how the team covered for each other, made the decision easy."*
> - **Floris Brandsma, Founder, Dierenpraktijkplanner (Leeuwarden)**

**Cost & Timeline:** €5,600 (Launch & Grow package with ongoing care at €49 per month) - completed in 15 business days despite a team change during the project.
