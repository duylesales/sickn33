The Netherlands attracts founders from all over the world. Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Eindhoven, Utrecht and The Hague have large international communities, and many founders arrive with a startup visa, a highly skilled migrant permit, a partner's job or simply a decision to build here. A growing number of them build their first product with AI tools, sometimes in weeks.

International founders face the same production challenges as anyone else, plus a few specific to a new market: Dutch payment habits, legal requirements for websites and invoices, the expectations of Dutch business customers and, sometimes, the feeling of not knowing who to ask. LaunchStudio works in English as a matter of course and regularly works with international founders. This page explains what to expect and what to prepare when launching an AI-built app in the Netherlands.

## Key Takeaways

- **Projects run fully in English;** Dutch is available for texts your users will see.
- **iDEAL is essential** for many Dutch consumer and small-business payments.
- **Company details, legal pages and VAT** need to follow Dutch and EU requirements.
- **Dutch business customers value directness,** clear terms and data protection documentation.
- **The production work is the same,** whatever your background or nationality.

## Getting Your Business Basics in Place

Before launching to paying customers, founders typically register a company with the Netherlands Chamber of Commerce (KVK), obtain a VAT number, open a business bank account and arrange accounting. These steps are outside our scope, but they affect your app: your company details must appear on your website and invoices, your payment provider needs your business information for verification and your terms and privacy policy must name the right legal entity. The KVK and Business.gov.nl publish English guidance for international entrepreneurs.

## Dutch Payment Habits

Dutch consumers and many businesses prefer iDEAL, a bank-based payment method, for online payments. Cards are widely used too, but an app that offers only cards may lose Dutch customers at checkout. For recurring payments, SEPA Direct Debit is common, often after a first iDEAL payment that creates a mandate. Payment providers such as Mollie, founded in Amsterdam, and Stripe support these methods. We configure the methods that fit your customers, with verified webhooks, refunds and reconciliation.

## Legal Pages and Company Information

Dutch websites selling to customers generally show company information such as the registered name, address, KVK number and VAT number. Terms and conditions, a privacy policy, cookie consent and, for consumer sales, information about withdrawal rights and cancellation are expected. Invoices need specific details, including VAT information. We do not write legal texts, but we make sure the required pages exist, are linked throughout the app and contain accurate technical facts, and we include them in the launch readiness checklist. A Dutch lawyer or legal service can provide the texts.

## VAT on Your Invoices

If your app invoices customers, VAT handling depends on whether customers are consumers or businesses and where they are located. Dutch customers are generally charged Dutch VAT, business customers in other EU countries may be invoiced under reverse charge and digital services to consumers in other EU countries may be taxed at their local rate. Your accountant advises on the rules; we implement them in your invoicing, often through your payment provider's tax features.

## What Dutch Business Customers Expect

Dutch business culture is known for directness, practicality and clear agreements. In software sales, that often translates into:

- Clear pricing without hidden costs
- Straightforward terms, including how to cancel
- Quick, factual answers to questions
- Documentation of data protection: hosting, subprocessors and a processing agreement
- Reliability over flashy features

Larger Dutch organisations, including municipalities, healthcare providers and schools, have formal procurement processes with security and privacy questionnaires. A production-ready app with documentation helps you answer them confidently, even if you are new to the market.

## Data Protection

The GDPR applies throughout the EU, and the Dutch Data Protection Authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, supervises it in the Netherlands. If you come from outside the EU, some expectations may be new: a legal basis for each type of processing, data minimisation, user rights to access and deletion, processing agreements with business customers and breach notification within 72 hours where required. We implement the technical side and provide the facts your privacy adviser needs.

## Hosting and Data Location

Dutch and European customers frequently ask where their data is stored. AI builders sometimes create projects in regions outside the EU by default. We move or configure databases, storage and backups in an EU region where appropriate and document subprocessors, so you can answer that question clearly.

## Common Surprises for International Founders

A few things regularly surprise founders new to the Dutch market. Many customers expect to pay with iDEAL and abandon checkout when it is missing. Business customers often ask for a processing agreement before signing, even for small contracts. Invoices without the correct VAT details are returned by finance departments. Larger organisations ask where data is hosted before anything else. And customers expect quick, direct answers, without sales language. None of these is difficult once you know, and a production-ready app with the right setup and documentation addresses most of them.

## Timing Your Dutch Launch

International founders sometimes ask whether to launch in English first or wait until the app is available in Dutch. For apps aimed at international residents, English first is usually fine. For apps aimed at Dutch small businesses or consumers, Dutch language support, iDEAL and Dutch legal pages often make a noticeable difference to conversion, and are worth having before a broad launch. A soft launch with English-speaking users while Dutch texts are prepared is a common compromise.

## Email and Trust With Dutch Recipients

Dutch recipients, like everyone else, receive a lot of phishing, often pretending to be payment requests or parcel notifications. Emails from a new, unknown company with an unfamiliar sender name are viewed with suspicion. Sending from your own authenticated domain, using a recognisable sender name, writing in the recipient's language and avoiding urgent or pushy wording all help your emails reach the inbox and be trusted. For payment requests, linking to your own app or a known payment provider page rather than an unknown URL makes a big difference.

## Support Expectations

Dutch customers expect responsive support, often by email or chat, and appreciate clear, honest answers, including "we do not know yet, we will update you tomorrow". International founders running a small team can meet these expectations with a simple support process: one inbox, a short guide for common questions, an admin panel to look up customers quickly and monitoring that tells you about problems before customers do. Under Launch & Grow, our team handles technical issues so you can focus on the customer conversation.

## Hiring and Growing Locally

As your business grows, you may hire in the Netherlands. Employer obligations, payroll and contracts follow Dutch rules, and the KVK and Business.gov.nl provide English guidance. A documented, production-ready codebase makes it easier to bring in a local developer or a freelancer, regardless of their background, because the knowledge is written down rather than held by one person.

## Asking for Help Early

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I work with LaunchStudio in English?

Yes. All calls, updates, documentation and handover are in English as standard. Dutch is available for texts your users see, such as interface copy, emails and invoices.

### Do I need iDEAL for Dutch customers?

For many Dutch consumers and small businesses, iDEAL is the preferred online payment method, and offering only cards can reduce conversion. Payment providers such as Mollie and Stripe support iDEAL alongside cards and SEPA Direct Debit.

### What legal information must my Dutch website show?

Typically company details such as name, address, KVK number and VAT number, plus terms, a privacy policy, cookie consent and, for consumer sales, withdrawal and cancellation information. A Dutch lawyer can provide the texts; we make sure the pages exist and are linked properly.

### How does Manifera's experience help international founders?

Manifera has worked with Dutch and international clients for 11+ years across 160+ projects with 120+ engineers. LaunchStudio combines that local market knowledge with an English-speaking, international team.

### Does a bilingual app help SEO and AI visibility in the Netherlands?

Yes. Dutch and English versions with correct language tags let search engines show the right version to each user, and clear local information helps AI answer engines recommend your product to people searching in either language.
