**Short answer:** you need to register with the KVK, the Netherlands Chamber of Commerce, once your app is a business: when you offer it independently, aim to make a profit and take part in economic activity, for example by charging users, selling subscriptions or running it as a commercial service. Registration is generally required before or when you start doing business, not after months of sales. A free hobby project used by friends usually does not need registration, but as soon as you plan paying customers, business contracts, a business payment account or an organisation account in app stores, register, choose a legal form and arrange VAT. This is general information; check your situation with the KVK, the Belastingdienst or an adviser.

## Key Takeaways

- **Registration depends on whether you run a business,** not on whether your app is finished or how it was built.
- **KVK criteria include independence, a profit motive and participation in economic activity.**
- **Common legal forms for app founders** are the sole proprietorship (eenmanszaak) and the private limited company (BV), each with different liability, tax and administrative implications.
- **VAT registration follows,** with the small business scheme (KOR) as an option for very low turnover.
- **Payment providers, banks, app stores and business customers** usually require a registered business.

## When Does an App Become a Business?

Many founders build their first version as a side project. The question is when that project becomes a business that must be registered. The KVK describes criteria such as:

- **Independence:** you offer the product or service yourself, not as an employee
- **Profit motive:** you intend to make money, even if you do not yet
- **Participation in economic activity:** you offer the product to the public or to businesses, set prices, advertise or sign agreements

Situations that typically indicate a business:

- Charging users for subscriptions, credits or features
- Offering the app to companies under contract
- Running advertising or affiliate revenue with a commercial intent
- Selling a white-label version to other businesses

Situations that may not, depending on details:

- A free tool shared with friends or a community without commercial intent
- An experiment or portfolio project without customers

The KVK provides a self-check on its website. When in doubt, ask the KVK or an adviser.

## Timing: Register Before You Start Doing Business

Registration generally needs to happen around the time your business starts, and there are deadlines relative to the start date. In practice, founders should register before they:

- Accept their first payment
- Sign a contract with a business customer
- Open a business account with a payment provider
- Apply for an organisation account with Apple or Google
- Invoice anyone

Registering early also lets you use a business name on invoices and contracts, open a business bank account and receive a VAT identification number.

## Choosing a Legal Form

| Aspect | Sole proprietorship (eenmanszaak) | Private limited company (BV) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | Simple registration with the KVK | Notarial deed, registration, minimum formalities |
| Liability | Personal: you are liable with private assets | Limited, in principle, to the company's assets |
| Taxes | Income tax on profit, with entrepreneur deductions if criteria are met | Corporate income tax on profit; dividend or salary for the owner |
| Administration | Lighter | Heavier: annual accounts, filings, separate administration |
| Investors | Not suitable for equity investment | Suitable: shares can be issued |
| Co-founders | Usually a partnership form instead | Shareholders' agreement |

Many solo founders start as a sole proprietorship and convert to a BV when revenue grows, liability risks increase or investors enter. Founders planning to raise money or work with co-founders often start with a BV, sometimes with a holding structure. Discuss the choice with an accountant, because tax consequences depend on your situation. Business.gov.nl explains the forms in English.

## Liability for Software Businesses

Software creates specific liability considerations: data breaches, service outages affecting customers, errors in calculations such as invoices or payroll and claims under contracts. With a sole proprietorship, such claims can reach your personal assets. A BV limits that in principle, although directors can be personally liable in some situations. Professional liability and cyber insurance can add protection. ## VAT

After registration, the Belastingdienst determines whether you are an entrepreneur for VAT purposes and issues a VAT identification number. For an app business:

- **Charge VAT** on your sales according to the applicable rules, typically the standard rate for software services to Dutch customers
- **Reverse charge** may apply for business customers in other EU countries with valid VAT numbers
- **Consumers in other EU countries** may require VAT of their country above the EU-wide threshold, often handled through the One Stop Shop
- **The small business scheme (KOR)** lets businesses with annual turnover up to a threshold opt out of charging VAT, with conditions and consequences, such as not reclaiming VAT on costs

Invoice requirements apply once you invoice. Our guide on [VAT and invoices for SaaS in the Netherlands](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/btw-and-invoices-for-saas-in-the-netherlands-what-your-checkout-needs) explains what invoices must contain.

## Why Platforms Ask for Registration

| Platform | Why registration matters |
| --- | --- |
| Payment providers such as Mollie and Stripe | Business verification is required for receiving payments as a business |
| Banks | Business accounts require registration |
| Apple App Store | Organisation accounts require a legal entity and typically a D-U-N-S number; individual accounts show your personal name |
| Google Play | Organisation accounts require business verification |
| Business customers | Procurement needs a registered supplier with a KVK and VAT number |
| Public sector | Tenders require registered suppliers |

If you publish an app under a personal developer account, your personal name appears as the seller. Most founders prefer an organisation account under the business name.

## Privacy in the Register

The KVK register is public, which raises privacy questions for founders working from home. In recent years, the KVK has limited the visibility of home addresses for many entrepreneurs registered at their home address, while business addresses remain visible. Check the current rules on the KVK website, and consider using a business address if privacy matters to you.

## Co-Founders and Ownership

If you build with a co-founder, decide early how ownership is structured. Two people running a business together without formal arrangements may be treated as a partnership with shared liability, which can cause problems if relationships change. Common approaches are a general partnership with a written agreement, or a BV with a shareholders' agreement covering shares, vesting, roles, decision-making and what happens if someone leaves. Also make sure the code, domain and accounts belong to the business, not to whichever co-founder created them. Investors and acquirers will ask, and fixing ownership later is harder than setting it up correctly from the start.

## Founders Living Outside the Netherlands

Some founders build for the Dutch market while living elsewhere, or move to the Netherlands after starting. Where you register depends on where you actually run the business, among other factors. If you live abroad but sell to Dutch customers, you may register in your country of residence and still meet Dutch customers' requirements, such as a VAT number for EU business customers. If your situation spans countries, get advice from an accountant familiar with cross-border businesses before choosing.

## Costs of Registration and Administration

Registering with the KVK involves a one-time registration fee, published on the KVK website. Additional costs depend on the legal form: a BV involves a notary, more extensive accounting and annual filings. Budget for an accountant, especially in the first year, when choices about legal form, VAT and deductions have the most impact. For most app founders, these costs are modest compared with the cost of correcting mistakes later.

## What to Arrange Before Your First Paying Customer

1. **Register with the KVK** in an appropriate legal form.
2. **Receive and check your VAT identification number.**
3. **Open a business bank account.**
4. **Set up business accounts** with your payment provider, hosting, database, domain registrar and email, so the business owns them.
5. **Prepare terms and conditions and a privacy policy** that name the business correctly.
6. **Set up invoicing** with correct business details and VAT.
7. **Consider insurance** for liability and cyber risks.
8. **Assign intellectual property** from freelancers or co-founders to the business in writing.
9. **Keep administration** from the start, including receipts for AI tools and infrastructure.

## Business Details on Your Website

Once registered, show your business name, KVK number, VAT number where relevant, contact details and address information as required on your website, invoices and terms. Online sellers in the EU have information obligations towards consumers, and business customers expect to find these details easily during procurement.

## Common Mistakes

**Charging users for months without registering.**

**Running payments through a personal account,** which mixes private and business money and causes tax complications.

**Accounts in a personal name,** complicating transfers when you form a BV or bring in investors.

**Publishing in app stores under a personal account,** showing your private name as the seller.

**Forgetting VAT on sales,** especially to other EU countries.

## Keeping Administration From Day One

Keep receipts for AI tool subscriptions, hosting, domains and other costs from the start, and separate business and private spending. Good records make VAT returns, tax filings and any later due diligence much easier.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a KVK registration to launch a free app?

Not necessarily. If the app is a free hobby project without commercial intent, registration may not be required. Once you charge users, sign business agreements, advertise commercially or otherwise run it as a business, registration is generally required. Use the KVK's self-check or ask an adviser.

### Should my app business be a sole proprietorship or a BV?

It depends on liability risk, expected profit, tax position and plans for investors or co-founders. Many solo founders start as a sole proprietorship and convert later, while founders planning to raise investment often start with a BV. Discuss the choice with an accountant.

### Can I use my personal Stripe or Mollie account for my app?

It is not advisable once you run a business. Business payments should go through a business account verified with your registration details, which keeps finances separate, simplifies VAT and administration and avoids issues with the provider's terms.
