For a SaaS product in Europe, GDPR is not optional. Customers ask where their data is stored, larger buyers send processing agreements and security questionnaires, and users exercise their rights to access and delete their data. The Dutch Data Protection Authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, and its counterparts elsewhere in the EU can investigate complaints. For founders who built their app with AI tools, the hardest part is often not the policy text but the product itself: where is personal data actually stored, who can see it and can the app really export or delete it?

LaunchStudio's GDPR implementation service handles the technical side. We map the personal data in your app, configure hosting and processors appropriately, build the features GDPR expects, such as data export and deletion, and leave you with documentation that supports your privacy policy, your records and the processing agreements your customers ask for. Legal interpretation and final policy wording remain with your legal adviser; our role is to make sure the app does what those documents say.

## Why AI-Built Apps Need Specific Attention

AI tools build features quickly but do not think about the lifecycle of personal data. A sign-up form becomes a users table, a profile page adds more fields, an analytics snippet sends page views with email addresses in the URL and an AI feature sends customer text to a model provider. Each step is reasonable on its own. Together they create a data footprint nobody has mapped.

Deletion is the clearest example. Deleting a user in the Supabase dashboard removes the authentication record, but often leaves their profile, uploaded files, comments, records in the payment provider, contacts in the email tool and entries in analytics. A proper deletion feature needs to know every place the data lives, which is exactly what the inventory provides.

## Processors, Regions and AI Features

Most AI-built SaaS products use several processors: hosting such as Vercel or Netlify, a database such as Supabase, email providers, payment providers, analytics, error tracking and, increasingly, AI model providers. Each of them processes personal data on your behalf. Business customers will usually ask for the list, the regions involved and confirmation that agreements are in place.

We check each processor's region options and data processing terms, configure EU regions where they are available and appropriate, and document the rest so you and your legal adviser can assess any transfers. For AI features, we look at what data is sent to the model provider, whether it is retained or used for training under the provider's terms and whether personal data can be minimised or removed before it is sent. Provider terms change, so we note the date of each check in the documentation.

## Consent and Cookies

Cookie banners generated by AI tools often look correct but do not work: tracking scripts load before the visitor makes a choice, rejecting has no effect or choices are not remembered. We configure consent so that non-essential cookies and scripts load only after consent, choices are stored and can be changed and the banner reflects the tools you actually use. Strictly necessary cookies, such as session cookies for logging in, do not need consent in the same way, and we separate them clearly.

The most effective GDPR projects combine legal and technical work. Your legal adviser decides what the policies should say, which legal bases apply, what retention periods are appropriate and how to word processing agreements. We make sure the app actually does what those documents describe and provide precise technical facts for them: which data is collected, where it is stored, who has access, how deletion works and which security measures are in place. ## Keeping Compliance Intact as You Build

GDPR compliance can erode quickly in an app that changes every week. A new feature adds a field, a new integration sends data to another processor, an AI prompt adds an analytics event with an email address in it. To prevent that, we add project instructions to your repository that tell your AI tool how to handle personal data, include the deletion and export functions in automated tests so they fail when a new table is not covered and give you a short checklist for new features and new processors. Launch & Grow customers can ask for a short review whenever a change touches personal data.

## Real example

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: The HR Tool With Data in Seven Places

Eline Mertens runs a small HR consultancy in Den Bosch and built Verlofwijs with Claude Code and Supabase, a tool that lets small employers manage leave, sick days and employee documents. A first business customer with 80 employees asked for a processing agreement, a subprocessor list and confirmation that employee data could be deleted when someone left. Eline realised she could not answer with confidence.

The inventory found personal data in seven places: the Supabase database, a public storage bucket with contracts and sick notes, the email provider, the error tracking tool, which received full request bodies including medical notes, the analytics tool, which tracked URLs containing employee names, the AI provider used to summarise leave requests and spreadsheets exported by admins. The database was hosted outside the EU, deletion removed only the login and every admin could see every employee across all companies.

LaunchStudio moved the database and storage to an EU region, made the storage private with signed links and enforced company and role-based access in Row Level Security. Error tracking was configured to strip request bodies, analytics URLs were changed to use IDs and the AI feature was adjusted to send only the leave dates and type, not medical notes. A deletion function removed or anonymised an employee's data across database, storage and email tool, and retention jobs removed sick notes after the period Eline's lawyer advised. Documentation of data flows and subprocessors was prepared for her processing agreement.

**Result:** Eline sent the customer a processing agreement drafted by her lawyer with the technical annex based on LaunchStudio's documentation. The customer signed within two weeks, and two further employers used the same documents in their own assessments without additional questions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does GDPR implementation for a SaaS app cost?

Most projects fall within the Launch Ready range, typically €1,500 to €3,500, depending on the amount of personal data, the number of processors, roles and whether sensitive data is involved. Apps with enterprise customers or special categories of data are often scoped within Launch & Grow. You receive a fixed quote after a short call.

### Does this make my app fully GDPR compliant?

It covers the technical side: data inventory, hosting, access control, user rights features, retention, consent, logging and documentation. GDPR compliance also includes legal decisions and documents, such as legal bases, your privacy policy and processing agreements, which should be handled with a legal adviser. Together, both sides make a solid basis.

### Can my data stay in the EU?

In most cases, yes. Supabase, Vercel, Netlify and many email and analytics providers offer EU regions or EU data handling options. Where a service processes data outside the EU, we document it so you and your legal adviser can assess the transfer and the safeguards in place.
