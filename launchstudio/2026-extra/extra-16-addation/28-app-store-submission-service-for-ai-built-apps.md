Getting a web app live is one step. Getting a mobile app into the Apple App Store and Google Play is another, with its own rules, accounts, forms and reviewers. Founders who built a mobile app with AI tools, often using Expo, React Native, Capacitor or a wrapper around their web app, regularly hit the same obstacles: builds that fail to sign, rejections for missing account deletion or unclear purchases, privacy forms that do not match what the app actually does, test requirements they did not know about and review cycles that delay launch by weeks.

LaunchStudio's app store submission service prepares your app, completes the store requirements and handles submission and any rejections at a fixed price. You end up with your app in both stores, in your own developer accounts, with a repeatable process for updates.

## Key Takeaways

- **Both stores have their own rules,** and they change; preparation avoids most rejections.
- **Privacy forms must match reality:** what the app and its SDKs actually collect.
- **Account deletion in the app** is required by both stores when users can create accounts.
- **Purchases of digital content** inside the app often must use the store's payment system.
- **Typical price: €1,200-€3,500 within Launch Ready,** depending on readiness and platforms.

## Payments and In-App Purchases

Payments are the most confusing part for many founders. In general, both stores require their own in-app purchase systems for digital goods and services consumed in the app, such as premium features or subscriptions, while physical goods and many real-world services, such as bookings or deliveries, can use other payment providers. In the EU, rules have been changing under the Digital Markets Act, with options for alternative payment methods under specific terms and fees. The details depend on your app, category and region.

We assess which rules apply to your app, explain the options and their costs, and implement the approach you choose. For subscriptions, we often keep Stripe or Mollie for web customers and add in-app purchases for mobile, with a shared entitlement system so customers get the same access wherever they paid.

## Privacy Labels and Data Safety

Apple's privacy details and Google Play's data safety section ask what data your app collects, how it is used and whether it is linked to the user. The answers must cover not only your own code but also the SDKs you include, such as analytics, crash reporting, advertising and authentication libraries. AI-generated apps often include SDKs nobody remembers adding.

We inventory the SDKs and data flows, align the answers with your privacy policy and correct anything that would be inaccurate. Accurate forms reduce rejection risk and protect you from complaints and enforcement later.

## Google Play Testing Requirements

Google Play has introduced testing requirements for some new personal developer accounts, such as running a closed test with a minimum number of testers for a set period before applying for production access. Organisation accounts may have different requirements. We check what applies to your account, help you set up the right account type where appropriate and organise the closed test if needed, so the requirement does not surprise you a week before launch.

## Wrapper Apps and Web Apps

Some AI-built mobile apps are wrappers around a web app, for example using Capacitor or a web view. These can be accepted, but Apple in particular may reject apps that offer little beyond the website. Adding genuine mobile value, such as push notifications, offline support, camera or location features, widgets or deep links, usually resolves that. For some products, a progressive web app is a better first step than a store app. We give you an honest view during the readiness review.

## Owning Your Developer Accounts

Developer accounts should belong to your company, not to a freelancer, an agency or a personal account of a co-founder. Apple and Google tie the app, its reviews, its download history and its signing keys to the account. Transferring an app later is possible but involves extra steps, and losing access to a signing key on Android can make updates very difficult. We help you register the accounts in your company name, add collaborators with limited roles and store signing credentials securely, so the app remains yours whoever works on it.

Iris Kok runs a network of physiotherapy practices in Alkmaar and built Oefenmaatje with Cursor and Expo, a mobile app where patients follow exercise programmes with videos and log their progress. Her first iOS submission was rejected because users could not delete their accounts. The second was rejected because the privacy details said no data was linked to users, while an analytics SDK collected device identifiers. The third was rejected because the reviewer could not log in, since accounts required an invitation from a practice. On Android, her personal account required a closed test she had not planned for.

LaunchStudio added in-app account deletion with a web-based request page, audited the SDKs and removed an unused advertising library, then completed Apple's privacy details and Google's data safety form accurately. A demo practice and patient account were created with clear reviewer notes explaining the invitation model. Camera and notification permission texts were rewritten to explain their purpose. The Google Play account was converted to an organisation account for her company, test tracks were configured and builds were set up with Expo Application Services, with signing credentials stored safely.

**Result:** Oefenmaatje was approved on both stores within a week of resubmission. Iris has since released six updates using the documented process without a rejection.

### How much does app store submission support cost?

Most projects fall within the Launch Ready range, typically €1,200 to €3,500, depending on how ready the app is, whether account deletion, privacy or build issues need fixing and whether both stores are involved. ### Can you guarantee my app will be approved?

No one can guarantee approval, because Apple and Google make the decisions and their guidelines change. Preparing the app against current guidelines, completing forms accurately and providing clear reviewer information prevents most rejections, and we handle any that occur.
