🇳🇱 Things nobody tells you when you launch a Dutch business as an expat founder

Priya Raman moved from Bangalore to Eindhoven for a tech job, and built Kidspace with Lovable and Supabase: parents find and book after-school activities, originally for international families in her neighbourhood. Then Dutch activity providers asked to list their courses and charge through the app. Priya registered a company with the KVK, and turned the side project into a business.

Here's what she learned along the way.

1. Half your customers will expect iDEAL.
Kidspace took card payments only, through a link, without webhooks.
➜ Mollie with iDEAL and cards, verified webhooks and refunds.

2. Your footer matters.
There were no company details and no legal pages.
➜ A footer with company information, terms, privacy policy and cookie consent, with texts from a Dutch legal service Priya engaged.

3. Your invoices need your KVK and VAT details.
➜ Now they have them.

4. Where your data lives is a question people will ask.
The database was hosted outside the EU.
➜ Moved to an EU region.

5. Children's data needs extra care.
Every provider could see every child's name and age.
➜ Providers see only bookings for their own activities, and only the child details they actually need.

6. English-only can feel foreign.
➜ Interface and email texts in English and Dutch, translated by a freelance translator.

The whole Launch Ready project ran in English (8 business days, €3,300).

Within three months, around 40 Dutch activity providers listed their courses, and iDEAL quickly overtook cards as the most-used payment method. Priya said the launch felt local, not foreign.

"I knew how to build the app. I did not know that half my customers would expect iDEAL, or what had to be in my footer. Doing it right from the start made Dutch providers trust a founder they had never heard of." - Priya Raman
