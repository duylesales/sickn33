🪜 Three small steps into a different category

STEP 0: Search and filter.
A recruiter types requirements and sees matching candidates, with matching terms highlighted. Clearly minimal risk.

STEP 1: Add a score.
A model scores each candidate for fit. Seems harmless.

STEP 2: Sort by the score.
Recruiters work from the top of the list.

STEP 3: Hide everyone below a threshold.
Now the product decides who gets seen.

Each step was a reasonable request. Together, they turned a search tool into a high-risk system under the EU AI Act: filtering access to employment based on an automated assessment.

Vincent Doeleman built Kandidaatmatch in Lovable: candidate-to-vacancy matching for recruitment agencies, 23 agencies, around 30,000 candidate records. He never decided to build a high-risk system. Then an agency's client, a large employer with its own compliance function, asked how the system complied with the AI Act. Neither Vincent nor the agencies had any documentation at all. 👥

Stepping back down (LaunchStudio, 9 business days, €6,200):
✓ Every feature described by what it decides, and for whom
✓ Legal advice confirming the automatic exclusion was the deciding factor
✓ The threshold removed: every matching candidate stays visible, the score is one sortable column among several, and the default order is relevance again
✓ The reasoning behind each score shown: which requirements matched and which didn't, instead of an unexplained number
✓ Recruiter overrides recorded, with a log of what was suggested and what was decided, kept and exportable
✓ A statement in the interface that scores are generated and must not be the only basis for a decision
✓ An assessment document recording the categorisation and reasoning
✓ A data processing agreement with the model provider, which had been missing
✓ Only the relevant experience text sent to the model, not the full record

Legal advice placed the revised feature in the limited-risk category, with the reasoning documented, and the employer accepted it. Two agencies said the visible reasoning was a better product than the score had ever been, because now they can explain a shortlist to their own clients.

"I never decided to build a high-risk system. I added a score, then sorted by it, then hid the bottom of the list - and somewhere in those three steps my product started deciding who got seen." - Vincent Doeleman
