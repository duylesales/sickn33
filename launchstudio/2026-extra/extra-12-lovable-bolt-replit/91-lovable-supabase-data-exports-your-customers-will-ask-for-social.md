🥣 A recipe for an accidental data breach (please don't cook this)

INGREDIENTS
1 export button that downloads only the 25 rows on screen, with internal column names and ISO dates
0 ways to export certificate PDFs
1 auditor asking for three years of every officer's training history and certificates
1 Saturday
1 query typed into the database dashboard
1 join through a table not filtered by organisation

METHOD
Run the query. Export the result. Email it.
Serves: 1,900 rows, 640 of them officers from two other companies, with names, dates of birth and certificate numbers.

Wendy Kramer built Opleidingsportaal in Lovable: training records and certificate tracking for 22 private security companies, around 4,100 security officers. The product couldn't do what the auditor asked, so she did it by hand. 🛡️

Taking it off the menu (LaunchStudio, 3 business days, €3,200):
✓ A full account export as a background job: a ZIP with CSV and JSON per entity, every certificate PDF in a folder per officer, a manifest and a README
✓ View exports rebuilt: every matching row, Dutch headers, typed dates, XLSX by default
✓ A person-level export for individual requests
✓ New data.export and data.export_all permissions, for administrators only
✓ Organisation and permission scoping applied explicitly in the export job, with a test asserting no cross-organisation rows
✓ An export audit visible to each company's administrators
✓ Delivery by signed link expiring after 48 hours, with archives deleted after seven days
✓ A rule on the operations page: customer data is never exported by hand from the dashboard

The incident was assessed as a personal data breach and reported to the two affected companies and to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens within 72 hours. The recipient confirmed deletion in writing. The next week, the auditor got the correct export, generated by the customer themselves. Two security companies later cited the self-service export and exit clause when choosing the product.
