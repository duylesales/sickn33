🔥 Unpopular opinion: most "bad" blog posts aren't bad. Nobody can reach them.

Elise Vlasman wrote 31 articles in two years for Planbaas Agenda, the appointment scheduling tool she built in Lovable for beauty salons, physiotherapists and small clinics (96 customers). Four brought traffic. She assumed the other 27 weren't good enough.

The link audit said something else:
• Only 9 of the 31 were linked from anywhere at all
• The blog index showed the ten most recent, so 22 older articles were reachable only through the sitemap
• Nearly every existing link said "lees meer" or "hier"
• No hub structure
• The pages where her product's real value lay, appointment reminders and no-show reduction, had zero internal links pointing at them 💇

No new writing. One business day of plumbing (LaunchStudio, €1,700):
✓ Every page mapped against every internal link, finding 22 orphans
✓ Each orphan given two to four links from relevant articles, written into the text, not tacked on the end
✓ 140 anchors rewritten from generic phrases to descriptive ones
✓ Four hubs: appointment scheduling, no-shows and reminders, salon administration, practice management. Each is a genuinely useful overview, linking to its articles, with every article linking back
✓ Related-article blocks generated from tags to catch future gaps
✓ The blog index listing every article by hub, not just the latest ten
✓ A publishing rule: two links from existing articles before anything new goes live

Four months later:
Organic traffic: 420 ➜ 1,900 visits a month
New articles written: 0
Previously orphaned articles now bringing traffic: 11
Her highest-converting page: the no-shows hub, which didn't exist before, now producing more trial signups than her home page

So before you rewrite the post that "didn't work," check whether anything links to it.
