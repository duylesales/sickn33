📄 THE FINE PRINT, read properly for the first time, two years after signing

"All personal data deleted within 30 days of termination, including backups, with written confirmation."
Could the product do it? No deletion function. The customer's data mixed with everyone else's across fourteen tables. Backups with no retention limit, back to launch.

"An independent audit right."
Could the product support it? No.

"Notification of any breach within 24 hours."
Could the product support it? Not realistically.

Annemieke Vervoort built Leerlingdossier in Lovable: pupil administration and progress tracking for private tutoring organisations, 28 organisations, about 4,000 pupils. She signed each customer's own processing agreement without much scrutiny, because each arrived at the end of a sales process she didn't want to slow down. 28 agreements, at least nine variants, none tracked. Then a tutoring group terminated, and invoked the first clause.

Every one of those agreements was a list of things her software had to be able to do.

Making the fine print true (LaunchStudio, 6 business days, €4,600):
✓ Per-organisation deletion across every table, with a documented split between deleted, anonymised for statistics, and invoices kept for tax
✓ Backup retention set to 90 days, with a procedure so restores never bring deleted data back
✓ A completion certificate, generated automatically
✓ A structured export archive, since two other agreements required one
✓ All 28 agreements read and their obligations tabulated: four different deletion windows, three notification periods, and two audit rights she couldn't have supported
✓ Her own processing agreement, drafted in Dutch and reviewed by a privacy lawyer
✓ A register of signed agreements, with versions and deviations
✓ Breach detection improved, so a 24-hour notification is realistic

The deletion was completed within the window, with a certificate the customer accepted. 19 of the 28 customers have since moved to her standard agreement at renewal. Annemieke's estimate: reading the contracts before signing would have taken four hours across two years, instead of six days of remediation under a deadline.
