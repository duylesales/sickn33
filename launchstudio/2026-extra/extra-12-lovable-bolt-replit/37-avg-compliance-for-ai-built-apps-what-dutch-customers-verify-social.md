📋 A PRIVACY QUESTIONNAIRE: 12 questions from an accountancy group

Answerable before: 4 of 12
Answerable after: 12 of 12

Steven Nijhuis built Urenregistratie in Lovable: time registration for accountancy and bookkeeping offices, 41 offices, around 600 staff. A group of nine offices ran a privacy review before onboarding.

What made the other eight unanswerable:
✗ No processing agreement
✗ A template privacy policy promising 12 months of retention, while the database held everything since launch two years earlier
✗ Subprocessors never listed (there were six)
✗ Deletion meant Steven running a query by hand
✗ Error tracking capturing full request bodies, staff names and client references included, outside the EU, without scrubbing
✗ A sign-up form collecting date of birth, home address and a private phone number, none of which the product used

The part Steven found most alarming: his privacy policy promised strangers things his own software couldn't do.

How the other eight got answered (LaunchStudio, 3 business days, €4,100):
✓ A data inventory by category and purpose, finding three fields collected and never used
✓ Those fields removed from the form and dropped from the schema, with existing values deleted
✓ Retention per category: time entries seven years for tax, account data 24 months after an office leaves, logs 30 days, all enforced by a monthly job
✓ Export and deletion built into the office admin view, so offices can handle staff requests themselves
✓ A processing agreement in Dutch, with legal review
✓ A published subprocessor list with processing locations, after moving error tracking to an EU provider with field scrubbing
✓ The privacy policy rewritten from what the product actually does
✓ Production data access limited to one account, with access recorded
✓ A two-page register of processing activities

The twelve questions were answered from the prepared documents, and the group signed for nine offices. Steven now sends the processing agreement and subprocessor list with every proposal. Three later customers said that was why they chose him over a bigger competitor whose answers took six weeks.
