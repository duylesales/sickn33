❓ What if...

...your email provider pauses your whole account?
It happened. One club imported a member list with several thousand outdated addresses. Welcome emails bounced in bulk, automated abuse protection paused sending, and for two days no club got booking confirmations, password resets or new member invitations. There was no second provider.

...your AI provider triples the price?
Announced, for the model behind automatic match scheduling.

...the project lives in your personal account?
The Lovable project did, not the company's.

...someone who left owns your domain?
It was registered to a co-founder who'd left a year earlier.

Monique van Dam built Baanreservering in Lovable: court booking, memberships and payments for 70 padel and tennis clubs, around 26,000 members. The email pause made her look for everything else that could fail the same way. 🎾

Answering every "what if" (LaunchStudio, 4 business days, €3,800):
✓ An inventory of 14 external services: owner, data, cost, terms, lock-in type
✓ A one-page exit plan for each, fully prepared for the five critical ones
✓ A second email provider verified on the domain, DKIM for both, sending behind a small interface, with automatic failover for confirmations, resets and invitations
✓ Bounce handling, and imported addresses validated before sending
✓ AI calls behind a provider-neutral module, with an alternative tested against the scheduling evaluation set
✓ Nightly encrypted database exports to another provider, with a tested restore
✓ Code synced to a company-owned Git repository, and the Lovable project moved to the company account
✓ The domain transferred to the company, with the former co-founder's cooperation
✓ Critical logins in a shared company password manager, two people per service
✓ An annual review in the calendar

Five months later, the primary email provider had a regional outage. Failover moved critical messages to the second provider within minutes, and clubs noticed nothing. The AI switch, tested in advance, took two days and cut that cost by about 40% against the original price. When a club group asked about business continuity in a procurement, the exit plans answered every question.
