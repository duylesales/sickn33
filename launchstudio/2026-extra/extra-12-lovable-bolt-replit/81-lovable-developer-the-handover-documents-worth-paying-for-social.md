📨 Follow one email

It's a domain renewal notice. It leaves the registrar.
It goes to the account holder: a freelancer, who registered the domain during a project because it was convenient.
It arrives at his old address. He's changed email.
It bounces.
Nobody reads it.
On a Saturday, the domain expires.

Ingrid Ravenstijn built Cateringplanner in Lovable and paid a freelancer €5,400 to secure it, add payments and launch. The work was good, and after five weeks the engagement ended amicably. There was no handover. The scope didn't mention one, and she hadn't thought to ask.

Fourteen months later, that email went nowhere. Cateringplanner was unreachable for three days over a weekend while Ingrid worked out who held the registration and tracked him down. 26 catering companies couldn't see their event schedules on a weekend in June, the busiest period of their year. 🍰

The handover, written afterwards (LaunchStudio, 3 business days, €3,100):
✓ An inventory of every service the product uses, which found four accounts in the freelancer's name: domain, error tracking, email provider and one storage account
✓ All four transferred or recreated under her own accounts, with credentials rotated
✓ A system overview written from the code by a second supplier
✓ A runbook for the eleven failures that had happened or plausibly could, domain and certificate expiry included
✓ A services list with costs, renewal dates, and a calendar reminder 60 days before each
✓ A decisions record rebuilt as far as possible, with the freelancer generously answering questions by email
✓ A statement of known issues
✓ A recorded walkthrough with the second supplier

The reconstruction took three days. As part of the original engagement, it would have taken perhaps half a day. Ingrid's point isn't that the work was bad. It was competent. She just had no way to benefit from it on her own.
