The idea is seductive and an AI tool will build it in an hour. Take a template, combine it with a list of cities, professions or use cases, and publish four hundred pages. "Stock management software for bakeries in Utrecht", four hundred times over, with two words changed.

It used to work. It now mostly does not, and the way it fails is worse than not doing it: a site carrying hundreds of near-identical pages can lose visibility for the pages that were genuinely good, because the whole domain is assessed rather than each page individually.

That does not make the technique worthless. It makes the distinction between the version that works and the version that does not worth understanding precisely.

## What Separates the Two Versions

One question: does each page contain information that is genuinely different and genuinely useful?

A page listing every dentist in Alkmaar with their opening hours is useful, because the content differs completely from the Haarlem page and someone wants exactly that.

A page saying "looking for practice software in Alkmaar? Our practice software is ideal for practices in Alkmaar" is not, because it contains nothing that is true of Alkmaar specifically.

The first is a database rendered as pages. The second is a template with a variable in it. The mechanism is identical and the outcome is opposite, because the first has something to say.

## Where the Data Comes From

Programmatic pages work when you have a real dataset. Small products usually have one of three, and often do not realise it.

**Your own operational data**, aggregated and anonymised. A booking product knows typical prices by region. A recruitment product knows which skills appear together. Published as statistics, this is information nobody else has.

**Public data recombined usefully.** Official registers, published statistics, open data. The value is in the combination and presentation rather than the data.

**Structured content you actually produce.** Detailed comparisons, worked examples, guides per use case — genuinely written, not generated from a pattern.

If you have none of these, you do not have a programmatic SEO opportunity. You have an idea for one, and publishing it anyway is the version that damages the site.

## The Threshold for Publishing

Before generating a set of pages, apply two tests.

**Would this page be worth publishing on its own?** If you would not write it as a standalone article because there is nothing to say, generating four hundred of it does not improve the situation.

**Is there a real audience for this specific page?** Combinations produce absurdity quickly: every service crossed with every village in the Netherlands produces thousands of pages, of which perhaps forty describe something anyone searches for.

The practical approach is to start small. Publish twenty pages covering the combinations that plainly matter, watch for three months, and extend only where they perform. A site of 20 useful pages that grows to 200 on evidence is a different proposition from one that publishes 400 on a hypothesis.

## Signals That Mark a Site as Spam

Search engines have policies about scaled content, and the characteristics they describe are worth avoiding whether or not you consider yourself in scope.

Large numbers of pages published simultaneously with near-identical structure and minimal unique content. Text that is obviously produced from a template, with the same sentences and one substituted value. Content generated by a model and published without review, at scale. Pages that exist only to rank rather than because someone needed them. And thin pages with no distinguishing information, generated by permutation.

The last of these is the most common and the most avoidable. A page with eighty words of boilerplate and a city name is a page that adds nothing to your site's assessment except weight.

## Make the Content Genuinely Different

Where you do have a dataset, the work is in making each page substantially its own.

Lead with the specific data, not with the template's introduction. The numbers, the list, the comparison — the thing that differs — should be the first thing on the page and the majority of it.

Vary the structure where the data varies. A page with three data points should not be padded to the length of one with forty; it should be shorter, and that is fine.

Add human content where you can, even briefly. One or two sentences of genuine local or specific knowledge changes a page's character entirely, and for the twenty pages that matter most this is an afternoon's work.

And link them into your site properly, from real navigation rather than from a footer list of four hundred links, which is itself a recognised pattern.

## Measure Honestly and Prune

Programmatic pages are the easiest content to evaluate because there are many of them and they are comparable.

After three to six months, look at what each page produced: impressions, clicks, and whether anyone converted. Expect a long tail of pages with nothing at all.

Then remove them. Pages that have produced no impressions in six months are not waiting to succeed; they are weight. Deleting them, or consolidating several into one better page, improves how the rest of the site is assessed.

This is the step that distinguishes people who use this technique well. Generating pages is easy and everyone does it; deleting the ones that failed is the part that requires discipline.

## Keeping Generated Pages Current

Data pages have a property that written articles do not: they go stale silently, and a stale data page is worse than no data page because it is confidently wrong.

Median prices from eighteen months ago, a count of active providers that includes twelve who left, a "most requested services" list reflecting last winter. Nothing on the page indicates any of this, and a visitor who acts on it has been misled by you specifically.

Three measures keep it honest. Regenerate on a schedule appropriate to the data — monthly for prices, weekly for counts — rather than at publication time only. Show the date the figures are from, visibly, which both sets expectations and signals that the page is maintained. And set a minimum data threshold below which the page is withdrawn rather than published with a caveat: a median calculated from four quotes is not a median.

There is a benefit beyond accuracy. A page whose figures update regularly gives people a reason to return and gives other sites a reason to link to it as a reference, which is worth considerably more than the page's own ranking. The trade publications citing regional pricing in the example above did so because the numbers were current; they would not have cited a snapshot from a year ago.

Automate the regeneration as part of your scheduled work, with the same monitoring as anything else that runs unattended — because a data page whose refresh job stopped in March is exactly the failure this section is about.

## The Alternative Nobody Considers

For most small products, the honest recommendation is to do something else entirely with the same effort.

Twenty programmatic pages take a day to build and produce, in the good case, a slow trickle across many pages. Six genuinely written articles answering the questions your customers asked before they bought take the same week and typically produce more, because each one is specific enough that the person reading it is the person who needs your product.

The way to find those six: read your last fifty support conversations and your last twenty sales calls. The questions that recur are the articles. They are already phrased the way people search, they are answerable with knowledge you demonstrably have, and nobody else in your niche has written them because nobody else has had those conversations.

This is unglamorous compared with generating four hundred pages in an afternoon, and it is the approach that works for products without a dataset — which is most of them in the first two years.

The sequence that serves most founders: write the six articles first, see what they produce, and only consider generated pages once you have both a real dataset and evidence that the written pages are performing. Programmatic SEO is an amplifier of a working content operation rather than a substitute for one.

## Real example

### Four Hundred Pages, Then Twenty-Six

Ivo Zwiers built Klusprijzen in Lovable: a platform where homeowners get quotes from local tradespeople for renovation work, with 900 registered tradespeople across the Netherlands.

An AI session generated 412 pages: every trade crossed with every town above 20,000 inhabitants. Each had 120 words with the trade and town substituted in, and a call to action. They were published in one week and linked from a footer listing all 412.

For two months, nothing. In the third month, organic traffic to the whole site fell by 60 percent, including to the six genuinely written guides that had been his only source of signups.

Five business days: all 412 template pages removed; an assessment of what data the platform actually held, which was substantial — 14,000 completed quotes with prices, durations and job types by region; 26 pages rebuilt from that data, one per trade in the six largest regions, each leading with real figures: median quote price for that trade in that region, typical range, average time to first response, number of active tradespeople, and the five most requested job types with their own price ranges; each page shorter or longer according to how much data existed, with regions below a data threshold omitted entirely rather than padded; two sentences of genuine written context per page; proper navigation from a regional index rather than a footer list; unique titles and descriptions per page; the 26 published across three weeks rather than at once; and a quarterly review comparing each page's impressions and conversions.

**Result:** traffic recovered to its previous level within ten weeks and exceeded it by month five. The 26 data pages now produce more organic signups than the six guides, and three have been cited by trade publications as a source for regional pricing — which no template page would ever have been. Six of the 26 were pruned at the first quarterly review for producing nothing, leaving 20.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do programmatic SEO pages still work?

Only where each page contains genuinely different, genuinely useful information — usually from a real dataset. Template pages with a substituted variable now harm the whole site rather than merely underperforming.

### How many pages should I publish at once?

Start with twenty covering combinations that plainly matter, publish in batches rather than simultaneously, and extend only where evidence supports it.

### Where does the data come from?

Your own operational data aggregated and anonymised, public data recombined usefully, or structured content you genuinely produce. If you have none of these, you do not have an opportunity yet.

### What marks a site as scaled content abuse?

Many near-identical pages published at once, text obviously generated from a template, thin pages produced by permutation, and footer lists linking hundreds of them.

### Should I delete pages that produce nothing?

Yes. After six months with no impressions a page is weight rather than potential. Pruning improves how the rest of the site is assessed, and it is the step most people skip.
