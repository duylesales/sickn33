For agencies pitching with AI prototypes: one agency's do's and don'ts, learned the expensive way. 🤝

Joost Meijer runs Studio Meridiaan, a three-person digital agency in Utrecht working mostly with Dutch scale-ups. He built a customer portal prototype in Bolt over two days and demoed it to a facilities company in the second meeting. The client loved it and asked for the link to show their board.

Three weeks later, while Joost was still writing the production quote, the client's operations manager emailed that link to 40 customers. It worked, and they were impatient. Customer names, contract details and service requests went into a database with no access control, on a preview URL, with the API key in the frontend.

As far as anyone could establish, nothing was breached. But the client now considered it live, and was unhappy to be quoted €18,000 for what felt like making an existing system slightly better.

❌ DON'T hand over a working link and say nothing about what it is.
✅ DO send every prototype with a one-page written statement of what it is.

❌ DON'T let a demo link live forever.
✅ DO expire every prototype link after 14 days and protect it with a password.

❌ DON'T rely on the conversation to set expectations.
✅ DO show a visible banner on the prototype: *demonstratiemodel - niet voor gebruik met echte gegevens*.

❌ DON'T build the prototype for free and hope it sells the project.
✅ DO quote discovery at €2,500, paid before the prototype is built.

❌ DON'T throw away data a client has already entered.
✅ DO migrate it: LaunchStudio moved the three weeks of real data into a properly built version, which mattered to the client more than anything else.

The rescue took four business days (€4,300): the prototype taken offline with an exact account of what was and wasn't exposed, backed by the access logs; the data migrated; the production work broken into 14 named items, each with its reasoning; and the relationship rebuilt on a two-stage structure.

Across eleven engagements since, the production quote has been accepted nine times, and never questioned because of the prototype's speed. Joost says the banner and the expiry do more work than any conversation.

"They deployed it to forty customers because it worked. That was not their mistake - I had shown them something that worked and said nothing about what it was." - Joost Meijer
