🚨 Fabienne Schuurmans runs a two-person studio in Utrecht building scheduling software for healthcare practices. Pitching a dental group, she demoed the project she'd built for an existing physiotherapy client - and shared the project link so they could "have a proper look". 😳

The prospect forwarded it. A colleague opened the files and spotted the database connection string. Behind it: around 900 real patients - names, dates of birth, appointments and treatment notes. And the full codebase another client had paid for. Here's how to share a Replit demo without giving it all away: 🧠

❌ Demoing with a real client's data because it "looked convincing"
❌ Sending a project link (code, config, fork) instead of a deployment link
❌ A demo sharing a database and credentials with a live client
❌ No password, no expiry, no label saying what it was

✅ Project made private, credentials rotated, access confirmed in writing by all three recipients
✅ The client told within a day, and the breach reported to the Dutch DPA given the health data
✅ A demo dataset of 300 invented patients across two invented practices - awkward cases included
✅ A template cloned per prospect with its own database and no email credentials; password-protected links that expire after 14 days, with noindex and a banner on every screen
