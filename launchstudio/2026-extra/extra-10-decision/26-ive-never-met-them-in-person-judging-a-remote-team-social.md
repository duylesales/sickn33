🚨 Marit almost picked her development team on gut feeling alone — the local option felt safest on the call. Then she actually called the references, and the feeling and the facts pointed in completely different directions. 😳

"I've never met them in person" targets the wrong risk. Physical presence was never what predicted competence — here's what actually is: 🧠

❌ The local team's legal registration checked out, but neither reference she called had worked on anything resembling her project's scope
❌ Their contract was silent on data ownership after the engagement ended — the exact ambiguity that turns into a fight later
❌ One remote team's corporate registration didn't match the name on their own invoice template — that ended the conversation in a day
❌ Testimonials on a website are marketing copy written to be read, not checked

✅ Verify the legal entity name and Chamber of Commerce number directly, not just the brand on the site
✅ Call two references from similarly sized projects — ask what went wrong and how it was handled
✅ Test the team's own process for specificity: "a status update every Monday and Thursday" beats "we communicate regularly"
✅ Read the contract for what it doesn't say — code ownership, data handling, and exit terms after the relationship ends
