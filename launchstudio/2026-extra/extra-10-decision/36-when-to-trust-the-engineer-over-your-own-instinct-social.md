"Always trust your engineer" is advice that's wrong exactly as often as it's right. 🧠

❌ Treating "I feel like this should be faster" as a data point instead of a hope
❌ Overriding a bcrypt-vs-reversible-storage security call with an instinct that has nothing to add
❌ Letting a technically cleaner cancellation flow quietly break a promise already made to broker partners
❌ Confusing "this sounds technical" with "this is technical" — risk tolerance dressed as engineering is still a business call

✅ Ask whose information the disagreement actually depends on — theirs or yours
✅ Defer on library choices, code structure and time estimates you can't independently assess
✅ Push back hard on anything touching a commitment you've already made to a customer or partner
✅ Use the two-question test: if I'm wrong, whose problem does it become — and what specific fact do I know that they don't?
