🚨 Iris pushed her tutoring platform's launch date three times in four months — a better dashboard, then calendar sync, then a referral program, each one feeling essential. Attempt four asked one question and cut the list from eleven items to one. 😳

"Just three more features first" rarely stays at three, because a launch list with no edge can always grow. Here's what actually separates a real gap from stalling: 🧠

❌ Each "final" list felt necessary in the moment and was, in hindsight, the same avoidance wearing a different feature's clothes
❌ Dark mode, a referral program, and calendar sync all made the list — none of them changed whether a tutor could actually pay
❌ The real blocker was hiding in plain sight: a failed card retry silently cancelled the subscription instead of retrying it
❌ Every feature added "before launch" also becomes a feature that needs security review before launch, quietly growing the job

✅ Test every item with one question: would a paying customer refuse to use the product without this, today?
✅ Write a five-condition definition of "ready" before you're tempted to expand it, not a growing feature list
✅ Move everything else to an explicitly dated version-two list instead of a vague "later"
✅ Let a bug found in testing move the date — that's a real gap, not displacement work
