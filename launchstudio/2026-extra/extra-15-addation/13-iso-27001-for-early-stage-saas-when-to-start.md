Sooner or later, a promising B2B deal stalls on a single line in a procurement form: "Is your organisation ISO 27001 certified?" For a founder with a small team and an AI-built product, the question can feel like a locked door. Certification sounds expensive, slow and designed for companies with compliance departments.

It is a serious undertaking, but it is not reserved for large companies. Many small SaaS businesses hold ISO 27001 certification, and some win deals because of it. The more useful question is not whether you can get certified, but when it makes sense to start, and what you should do before that point so the eventual project is shorter, cheaper and built on real security rather than paperwork.

This guide explains what ISO 27001 actually requires, what certification involves, and how to decide on timing.

## Key Takeaways

- **ISO 27001 certifies a management system, not a product.** It proves you systematically identify and manage information security risks, using controls appropriate to your business.
- **Certification takes most small SaaS companies six to twelve months** from serious start to certificate, with ongoing audits every year.
- **Start when customers make it a deal requirement,** typically when selling to enterprises, government, healthcare or finance, not because it seems professional.
- **The technical groundwork comes first:** access control, MFA, logging, backups, secure development and supplier management. These are needed regardless of certification and make up much of what auditors look for.
- **A clear security overview often satisfies buyers** before certification is worthwhile, buying time until the business case is real.

## What ISO 27001 Is

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for information security management systems, often abbreviated ISMS. The current edition, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, has two parts that matter for a SaaS company.

**The management system clauses** describe how you run security as an organisation: understanding your context and stakeholders, leadership commitment, a security policy, risk assessment and treatment, defined roles, documented information, internal audits, management reviews and continual improvement.

**Annex A** lists 93 controls grouped into four themes: organisational, people, physical and technological. Examples include access control, cryptography, secure development, logging and monitoring, supplier relationships, incident management, backup and business continuity. You do not have to implement every control. You assess your risks, decide which controls apply and record that in a Statement of Applicability, explaining why each control is included or excluded.

The companion standard ISO/IEC 27002 gives guidance on implementing each control. Together they form a flexible framework: a five-person SaaS company and a multinational can both be certified, with very different implementations.

## What Certification Involves

Certification is performed by an independent certification body. In the Netherlands, look for one accredited by the Raad voor Accreditatie, or by an equivalent national accreditation body elsewhere, because accredited certificates carry the most weight with buyers.

The typical path:

1. **Scope definition.** Decide what the ISMS covers, usually the SaaS product, its infrastructure and the people who build and operate it.
2. **Gap analysis.** Compare current practice against the standard.
3. **Implementation.** Write policies, perform a risk assessment, implement missing controls, train staff and start operating the system.
4. **Evidence period.** Run the ISMS for a while so there is evidence: access reviews, incident records, supplier reviews, internal audit, management review.
5. **Stage 1 audit.** The auditor reviews documentation and readiness.
6. **Stage 2 audit.** The auditor tests whether the system works in practice, interviewing people and sampling evidence.
7. **Certificate.** Valid for three years, with surveillance audits in years one and two and a recertification audit in year three.

## Realistic Costs and Timelines

Costs vary widely by scope, existing maturity and how much external help you use. As a broad indication for a small SaaS company with fewer than twenty people:

| Cost item | Typical range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Certification body audits | Several thousand euros for the initial audit, plus annual surveillance | Depends on scope and staff count |
| Consultant or compliance platform | From a few thousand to tens of thousands of euros | Platforms automate evidence; consultants guide policy and risk work |
| Internal time | Often several hundred hours in the first year | The largest hidden cost |
| Technical improvements | Varies | MFA, logging, backups, device management, secure development |
| Penetration test | Often requested as supporting evidence | Not strictly required by the standard |

Timelines for small companies typically range from six to twelve months. The fastest projects are those where the technical controls already exist and the work is mostly documentation and evidence. The slowest are those where the product's security foundations are weak and must be rebuilt during the certification project.

## Signals That It Is Time to Start

Start an ISO 27001 project when there is a business case, not before. Strong signals:

- **Repeated deal requirements.** Two or more serious prospects require certification, not just ask about it.
- **Target market expectations.** You sell to enterprises, government, healthcare or financial services in Europe, where ISO 27001 is a common baseline.
- **Tender requirements.** Public tenders list certification as a selection criterion.
- **Partner programmes.** A channel partner or platform requires it for integration.
- **Investor or acquirer interest.** A funding round or acquisition conversation includes security due diligence.

Weak signals, which usually do not justify starting yet:

- A single prospect mentions it in passing.
- Competitors display a certificate logo.
- It "feels professional".

If the signals are weak, invest in the technical groundwork and a good security overview instead. When strong signals arrive, you will be months ahead.

## Alternatives and Intermediate Steps

**A security overview and questionnaire answers.** Many buyers accept a well-documented security description, a sub-processor list, a recent penetration test summary and completed questionnaires from a startup, particularly in pilots.

**SOC 2.** Common with US customers. It is an attestation report rather than a certificate, and many controls overlap with ISO 27001. European buyers usually prefer ISO 27001.

**Sector-specific standards.** Dutch healthcare buyers often expect NEN 7510, which builds on ISO 27001 for health information. Our article on [NEN 7510 and healthcare apps in the Netherlands](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/nen-7510-and-healthcare-apps-in-the-netherlands) explains the relationship.

**Supplier certifications.** Your hosting and database providers are often ISO 27001 certified. That does not certify your company, but it does cover part of your infrastructure, and auditors will take it into account when reviewing supplier controls.

## The Technical Groundwork to Lay First

Whatever you decide about certification, these foundations are worth building now. They are required by GDPR's security article in spirit, demanded by enterprise questionnaires and closely aligned with Annex A controls.

| Area | Groundwork | Related Annex A theme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access control | Row Level Security or equivalent, roles, least privilege, periodic access reviews | Technological, organisational |
| Authentication | MFA for all staff and admin accounts, SSO for internal tools | Technological |
| Secrets | No secrets in code, managed secret storage, rotation procedure | Technological |
| Logging | Authentication, admin actions and data exports logged and retained | Technological |
| Backups | Automated, encrypted, restore tested and recorded | Technological |
| Secure development | Code review, staging, automated tests, dependency scanning | Technological |
| Change management | All production changes through reviewed deploys | Organisational |
| Suppliers | List of processors and sub-processors with agreements and locations | Organisational |
| Incidents | Written response plan, incident log, notification commitments | Organisational |
| Devices and people | Disk encryption, screen lock, onboarding and offboarding checklist | People, physical |

AI-built products deserve special attention on secure development. Auditors increasingly ask how AI-generated code is reviewed, how prompts and agent sessions are controlled and how secrets are kept out of AI tools. Clear rules, code review on protected paths and automated checks give you a good answer.

## A Twelve-Month Roadmap for a Small Team

If the signals are strong and you decide to pursue certification, a realistic plan for a team of five to fifteen people looks like this:

| Months | Focus | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-2 | Technical foundations | MFA, named accounts, logging, backups with restore test, deployment pipeline, secrets management |
| 2-3 | Scope and risk | ISMS scope, asset inventory, risk assessment, risk treatment plan |
| 3-4 | Policies and Statement of Applicability | Policies that describe how you actually work, justified control selection |
| 4-5 | Training and operation | Security awareness training, onboarding and offboarding procedures, supplier reviews |
| 5-8 | Evidence period | Access reviews, incident log, change records, internal audit |
| 8-9 | Management review and Stage 1 | Review of the ISMS by leadership, documentation audit |
| 9-11 | Fix findings and Stage 2 | Corrections from Stage 1, implementation audit |
| 11-12 | Certificate | Certification decision, plan for the first surveillance audit |

Two points make this plan work. First, the technical foundations come first, because every later step depends on them. Second, the evidence period cannot be shortened much: auditors want to see that the system operates over time, not that it was assembled the week before the audit. Founders who start the technical work early effectively give themselves a head start on the evidence period.

It also helps to assign one person as ISMS owner from the start, even part-time. Certification projects without a clear owner tend to drift, and auditors look for clear responsibility.

## Common Mistakes

**Buying a policy pack and calling it done.** Template policies that do not match how you work fail at the Stage 2 audit, when auditors ask staff how things really happen.

**Scoping too broadly.** Including every part of the company in the first certification makes the project larger than necessary. Start with the product and its operations.

**Leaving technical gaps for later.** An ISMS that documents weak controls is still weak. Fix the foundations first.

**Treating it as a one-off.** Certification requires ongoing operation: access reviews, internal audits, management reviews and annual surveillance audits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is ISO 27001 worth it for a small SaaS company?

It is worth it when customers require it to sign, typically in enterprise, government, healthcare and finance. Before that point, the same investment is usually better spent on technical security foundations and a strong security overview, which satisfy many buyers and make later certification faster.

### How long does ISO 27001 certification take for a startup?

Most small SaaS companies need six to twelve months from a serious start to certification. The duration depends mainly on how mature the technical controls already are. Companies that fix foundations before starting the documentation phase usually move fastest.

### Does ISO 27001 require specific technical controls?

It requires a risk-based selection of controls from Annex A, justified in a Statement of Applicability, rather than a fixed checklist. In practice, SaaS companies almost always implement access control, MFA, logging, backups, secure development, supplier management and incident response.
