🕐 Did you know? Five fun facts about time that broke a driving school app

Ricardo Fernandes built Rijlesplanner with Lovable and Supabase: instructors publish availability, students book lessons and get reminders, and driving schools invoice monthly. On the Monday after summer time started, dozens of students turned up an hour late, or found their instructor waiting an hour early.

FUN FACT 1: The Netherlands has two time zones a year.
Weekly availability was stored as a fixed UTC time, so every slot moved an hour when the offset changed.

FUN FACT 2: "Timestamp without time zone" means different things to different code.
The browser read lesson times as local time, the reminder function read them as UTC. Reminders arrived an hour off.

FUN FACT 3: 08:00 is not a fixed UTC time.
A cron job at a fixed UTC hour sent reminders at 06:00 in winter and 07:00 in summer.

FUN FACT 4: 00:30 on the first of the month can be the last day of the previous month.
toISOString() dated those invoices one day early.

FUN FACT 5: A month in UTC isn't your month.
The monthly report counted lessons by UTC day boundaries.

The fixes (LaunchStudio, 8 business days, €2,900):
✓ Lesson times migrated to timestamptz, existing data converted carefully, and each school's time zone stored
✓ Availability as local-time rules in Europe/Amsterdam, with slots generated by time zone aware functions
✓ Reminder instants calculated at booking, sent by a job running every five minutes
✓ Invoice dates as date columns from the school's local date, and reports by local month
✓ Explicit time zones in emails and calendar invitations
✓ Tests for both daylight saving transitions, month ends and server code running in UTC

The October change: zero displaced lessons. The March change the year after: the same. Wrong-time tickets went from a few dozen a month to almost zero.

"I'd never thought about time zones because everyone using my app was in the Netherlands. It turns out the Netherlands has two time zones a year." - Ricardo Fernandes
