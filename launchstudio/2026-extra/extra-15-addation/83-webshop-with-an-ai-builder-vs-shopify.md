With Lovable, Bolt or v0, a founder can generate a beautiful webshop in an afternoon: product grid, product pages, cart, checkout with Stripe or Mollie, order confirmation emails. It looks finished, it is unique, and there are no monthly platform fees. Meanwhile, Shopify powers millions of stores, handles payments, taxes, apps and hosting, and charges subscription and transaction fees in return.

The question many founders ask: should I build my own webshop with AI, or use Shopify? The honest answer is that a webshop is much more than a product grid and a checkout. It is inventory, shipping, returns, taxes, consumer law, fraud, accessibility, SEO, email, reporting and dozens of edge cases that established platforms have solved over years. A custom shop makes sense in specific situations; for many sellers, Shopify, or a hybrid, is the better business decision.

This guide compares the options for Dutch and European sellers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Shopify covers commerce fundamentals out of the box:** checkout, payments including iDEAL, taxes, inventory, shipping integrations, apps and hosting.
- **A custom AI-built shop gives full control** but requires you to build and maintain everything a commerce platform provides.
- **Custom makes sense for unusual models:** complex configurators, B2B pricing, subscriptions with special logic, marketplaces or deep integration with your own product.
- **Headless commerce,** using Shopify as the backend and a custom frontend, combines a unique interface with a proven commerce engine.
- **Legal and operational requirements apply either way:** consumer rights, VAT, accessibility, privacy and payment security.

## What a Webshop Really Involves

| Area | What it includes |
| --- | --- |
| Catalogue | Products, variants, prices, images, collections, search |
| Checkout | Cart, discounts, shipping options, payment, address validation |
| Payments | iDEAL, cards, wallets, refunds, chargebacks, fraud checks |
| Orders | Status, invoices, confirmations, cancellations |
| Inventory | Stock levels, reservations, backorders |
| Shipping | Rates, labels, tracking, carriers |
| Returns | Return requests, labels, refunds, restocking |
| Taxes | VAT rates per product and country, invoices |
| Customers | Accounts, order history, privacy requests |
| Marketing | Email, discounts, reviews, SEO |
| Compliance | Consumer law, accessibility, cookies, privacy |
| Operations | Reporting, accounting exports, staff permissions |

An AI-built prototype usually covers the first two or three rows. The rest is where time and risk accumulate.

## Shopify's Strengths

- **Checkout and payments** that are optimised, secure and support local methods such as iDEAL, depending on the payment setup
- **Hosting, scaling and security** managed by the platform, including payment security handled through the hosted checkout
- **Themes and an editor** for non-technical staff
- **An app ecosystem** for reviews, subscriptions, shipping, accounting, marketing and more
- **Built-in tax settings** for VAT and international selling
- **Inventory, orders and fulfilment** tools
- **Integrations** with common Dutch shipping and accounting tools through apps

The trade-offs: monthly plan fees, possible transaction fees depending on payment setup and plan, app subscription costs that add up, and constraints on customising checkout and data structures.

## Custom AI-Built Shop Strengths

- **Complete design and experience freedom**
- **Unusual business logic,** such as complex product configurators, quote-based ordering or deep integration with a SaaS product
- **No platform fees,** although infrastructure and payment provider fees remain
- **Full data ownership and structure**
- **Unified product,** where the shop is part of a larger application

The trade-offs: everything in the table above must be built, tested, secured and maintained by you, and every new requirement, such as a new payment method, a carrier change or a legal update, is your engineering work.

## When a Custom Shop Makes Sense

- **The shop is part of a larger product,** for example a SaaS where customers buy credits or add-ons
- **Products require complex configuration,** such as made-to-measure furniture, printed products or technical components with compatibility rules
- **B2B ordering** with customer-specific pricing, credit limits and approval flows, closer to an ordering portal than a consumer shop
- **Marketplaces** with many sellers, which require platform payments and seller management
- **Very low volumes of very specific items,** where a simple custom checkout is enough and platform features would go unused

For a typical consumer brand selling physical products, starting on a commerce platform is usually faster, cheaper and less risky.

## Headless: The Hybrid Approach

Headless commerce separates the frontend from the commerce backend. With Shopify's Storefront API, you can build a custom frontend, including with AI tools, while Shopify handles products, inventory, checkout, payments and orders. This gives:

- **A unique, fast frontend** designed exactly as you want
- **Proven commerce operations** behind it
- **Shopify's checkout** for payment security and conversion

The trade-off is complexity: two systems to manage, and some platform features, such as theme-based apps, may not work the same way in a headless setup. It suits brands with strong design ambitions and enough technical capacity.

## Dutch and EU Requirements Either Way

Whichever route you choose, these apply:

- **Consumer rights:** consumers generally have a 14-day right of withdrawal for online purchases, with exceptions such as personalised products. Clear information before purchase, a withdrawal form and refund handling within legal deadlines are required. ConsuWijzer, run by the Dutch consumer authority, explains the rules.
- **Price transparency:** prices including VAT for consumers, with shipping costs shown before payment.
- **VAT for EU sales:** for distance sales to consumers in other EU countries above the EU-wide threshold, VAT of the customer's country applies, often declared through the One Stop Shop scheme.
- **Invoices** meeting Dutch requirements.
- **Accessibility:** the European Accessibility Act applies to e-commerce services from June 2025, with an exemption for microenterprises providing services. Our guide on the [European Accessibility Act for SaaS and webshops](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/european-accessibility-act-for-saas-and-webshops) explains who is covered.
- **Cookies and privacy:** consent for non-essential cookies, a privacy policy and handling of data requests.
- **Payment security:** using hosted payment pages or provider components keeps card data out of your systems.

Shopify helps with many of these through built-in features and apps; a custom shop must implement them deliberately.

## Cost Comparison Over Three Years

| Cost item | Shopify | Custom AI-built shop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial build | Theme setup and customisation | Development of all commerce features |
| Platform fees | Monthly plan | None, but hosting and services |
| Payment fees | Provider fees, possibly platform transaction fees | Provider fees |
| Apps and services | App subscriptions | Integrations you build or services you subscribe to |
| Maintenance | Mostly handled by platform | Ongoing development for changes, security and updates |
| Risk | Platform constraints | Bugs in checkout, tax or inventory logic affect revenue directly |

For most consumer shops, Shopify's fees are smaller than the cost of building and maintaining equivalent functionality. The equation changes when the shop's logic is unusual or when commerce is a small part of a larger custom product.

## Common Problems in AI-Built Shops

When founders build shops from scratch with AI, we often see:

- **Prices and totals calculated in the browser** and trusted at checkout
- **Orders confirmed by the payment redirect,** not by webhooks
- **Stock not reserved,** so the last item sells twice
- **No returns process**
- **VAT applied uniformly** regardless of product type or destination
- **Missing withdrawal information** and terms
- **No order confirmation emails from an authenticated domain**
- **Inaccessible checkout** with unlabelled fields
- **Slow product pages** with unoptimised images

If you do build custom, these are the first things to get right. Our guide on [money, rounding and currency bugs](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/money-rounding-and-currency-bugs-in-saas) covers calculation pitfalls.

## Other Platforms to Consider

Shopify is not the only commerce platform. Depending on your situation, alternatives may fit better:

- **WooCommerce,** an open-source plugin for WordPress, popular with sellers who already run WordPress sites and want control over hosting, at the cost of more maintenance
- **Dutch and European platforms** with strong local integrations for payments, shipping and accounting
- **B2B-focused commerce platforms** for wholesale ordering with customer-specific prices
- **Subscription commerce tools** for recurring product deliveries

The comparison logic stays the same: how much of the commerce table above does the platform handle for you, what does it cost, and does its structure fit your business model?

## Migrating Later

Starting on a platform does not lock you in forever, and starting custom does not either. Products, customers and orders can be exported and imported, although customer passwords, subscriptions with stored payment details and order history need care. If you expect to move later, keep your product data clean, use standard identifiers such as SKUs and EAN codes, and avoid building critical business logic in platform-specific apps that have no equivalent elsewhere.

## Operations Matter More Than the Storefront

Founders often focus on how the shop looks. Customers remember how the shop behaves: whether the order confirmation arrived, whether the parcel came on time, whether the return was easy and the refund quick. Before investing in a custom storefront, make sure fulfilment, shipping, returns and customer service run smoothly. These operational basics, often handled by platform apps and integrations, drive repeat purchases and reviews more than design differences.

## SEO and Visibility

Both routes can rank well. Shopify provides SEO basics, such as editable titles, structured product data through themes and sitemaps. A custom shop must implement server-rendered product pages, structured data, canonical URLs, sitemaps and fast loading. Single-page apps that render products only in the browser are harder for search engines and AI crawlers to index.

## A Decision Guide

| Situation | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer brand with standard products | Shopify |
| Strong design ambition, standard commerce | Shopify, possibly headless later |
| Complex configurator or quote-based sales | Custom or headless with custom configurator |
| Shop inside a SaaS product | Custom, with a payment provider's billing tools |
| B2B ordering with customer pricing | Custom portal or B2B-capable platform |
| Marketplace | Custom with platform payments, or a marketplace platform |

## Real example

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: The Custom Sneaker Shop That Moved to Headless Shopify

Kevin Achterberg runs a small brand of handmade leather sneakers from a workshop in Waalwijk, with a product configurator for colours, leather types and sizes. He built his shop entirely in Lovable with Mollie payments, because Shopify themes could not show his configurator the way he wanted. Sales grew, mostly in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany.

Growth exposed the gaps. The shop calculated prices in the browser, and a customer once paid for a basic model while configuring premium leather. Orders were confirmed on the payment redirect, so some failed payments appeared as orders. VAT was charged at the Dutch rate for all EU customers, although Kevin had passed the EU distance sales threshold, and there was no withdrawal information or returns process, while his accountant warned that personalised products and standard sizes needed different treatment. The configurator pages were slow and invisible to search engines because everything rendered in the browser.

After comparing options with LaunchStudio, Kevin moved to a headless setup. Products, variants, inventory, checkout, taxes and orders moved to Shopify, configured for EU VAT with the One Stop Shop and Mollie-supported payment methods including iDEAL and Bancontact. The configurator remained a custom frontend, rebuilt with server rendering for product pages and connected to Shopify's Storefront API, so the price and options came from Shopify at checkout. Withdrawal information distinguished standard and personalised products, a returns flow was added through a Shopify app, and product pages received structured data and optimised images.

**Result:** Pricing and VAT errors disappeared, and conversion improved after the switch to Shopify's checkout. Organic traffic to configurator pages grew steadily once they were server-rendered, and Kevin now spends his time on product rather than on maintaining checkout code.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I build my webshop with Lovable or use Shopify?

For most consumer shops selling standard products, Shopify is faster, cheaper and less risky, because it provides checkout, payments, taxes, inventory and integrations. A custom AI-built shop makes sense for unusual models such as complex configurators, shops inside SaaS products, B2B portals or marketplaces.

### What is headless Shopify?

Headless Shopify uses Shopify as the commerce backend for products, inventory, checkout and orders, while the storefront is a custom frontend connected through the Storefront API. It combines a unique interface with proven commerce operations.

### Which legal requirements apply to a Dutch webshop?

Key requirements include consumer withdrawal rights and information, prices including VAT, correct invoices, VAT rules for EU distance sales, cookie consent and privacy, and, for businesses beyond microenterprise size, accessibility under the European Accessibility Act. These apply regardless of the platform.

### How does Manifera's experience help with e-commerce decisions?

Manifera has built and integrated e-commerce systems for clients for 11+ years. LaunchStudio applies that experience to help founders choose between platform, custom and headless approaches based on their business model rather than on what is quickest to prototype.

### Does a custom webshop rank better in search engines?

Not automatically. Both Shopify and custom shops can rank well if product pages are server-rendered, fast, well structured and contain unique content. Custom shops built as browser-only apps often rank worse until rendering and structured data are fixed.
