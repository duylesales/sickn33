Imagine ordering your groceries with a screen reader. 🛒

You add vegetables from a local farm to your cart. The quantity buttons have no names, so you hear "button, button, button." You reach the delivery slot picker, and your screen reader can't find it at all. You give up halfway through checkout.

That's what one customer of Buurtboer told its founder by email.

Thijs van der Meer runs Buurtboer, a regional grocery delivery service around Apeldoorn that connects local farms with households. With 14 employees, it isn't a microenterprise, and its Lovable webshop on Supabase sells to consumers. So the European Accessibility Act applies.

An audit showed the problem went far beyond one picker:
• The slot picker was built from clickable divs with no keyboard support
• Quantity buttons had no accessible names
• Form fields relied on placeholder text
• Errors appeared only as red borders
• The brand green on white failed contrast requirements
• The cart drawer trapped keyboard focus
• There was no accessibility statement

What LaunchStudio changed (9 business days, €2,900):
✓ The slot picker rebuilt as an accessible radio group with full keyboard support
✓ Accessible names on every icon button, visible labels, text error messages linked to fields
✓ A brand green with enough contrast, identity intact
✓ Proper focus management in the cart, and status messages announced to screen readers
✓ Automated accessibility checks in the pipeline, an accessibility statement, and accessibility rules in the Lovable project instructions

The customer who wrote in completed her first order the next week and became a regular. Checkout completion across all users rose about 7% over two months.

"I thought accessibility was a legal checkbox. It turned out to be a checkout problem that affected everyone, just most severely the people who couldn't see it." - Thijs van der Meer
