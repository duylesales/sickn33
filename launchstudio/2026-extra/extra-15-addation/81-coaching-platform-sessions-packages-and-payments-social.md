Dear manager in a coaching programme, ✉️

Your employer is paying for your coaching. So, fair question: what can your HR department see?

On Coachruimte today, the answer is clear. HR sees the budget and usage. Not your name next to topics. Not the content. Your coach's notes are visible only to your coach. The action points your coach chooses to share are visible to you.

It wasn't always that clear.

Roos Hendriksen runs a leadership coaching practice in Amsterdam with seven coaches, mostly for companies that buy coaching programmes for their managers. She built Coachruimte with Lovable and Supabase: managers book sessions, coaches write notes, companies get invoices, and HR tracks the budget.

Then a new corporate client's HR director tested it, and found she could open a coach's session notes about one of her managers. The notes table let anyone in the same company organisation read them, HR included. There was more:
• Session packages tracked as one counter, which sometimes went negative when sessions were booked at the same time
• Late cancellations handled by hand
• Coaches double-booked, because the platform couldn't see their Outlook calendars
• Intake answers about stress and burnout emailed to coaches as plain text

What changed (LaunchStudio, 14 business days, €5,800):
✓ The access model described above, with notes encrypted at rest and every access logged
✓ Packages as a credit ledger: reserved at booking, used at completion, the 48-hour rule applied automatically, and expiry dates
✓ Coaches' Google or Outlook calendars connected
✓ Intake answers kept in the platform, with explicit consent and coach-only access
✓ A plain explanation for you of exactly what your employer can see

After reviewing it, that HR director signed a programme for 40 managers. In the following year: around 1,300 sessions, no double bookings, no credit disputes.

"Confidentiality is the whole basis of coaching. The platform has to protect it as strictly as the coaches do." - Roos Hendriksen
