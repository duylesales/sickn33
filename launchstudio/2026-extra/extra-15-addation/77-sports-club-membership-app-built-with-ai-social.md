🏑 WHO SEES WHAT? A hockey club's access matrix, before and after

Jasper Koning, treasurer of a hockey club near Haarlem with around 900 members, built Clubhuis with Lovable and Supabase: online registration, contributions by direct debit, team schedules, and bar shift sign-ups for families. The board loved it, and four nearby clubs asked to use it. Then a parent who works in privacy compliance pointed out what the app really showed.

BEFORE
Any logged-in member ➜ every team's names, birthdates, addresses and parents' phone numbers
All parents in a team ➜ medical notes such as allergies and asthma
Anyone ➜ youth team photos in a public gallery, whatever the photo consent
Everyone ➜ former members' data from three seasons back
Treasurer ➜ a direct debit file uploaded to the bank, with failed collections in a spreadsheet

AFTER (LaunchStudio, 16 business days, €6,600)
Parents ➜ their own children's accounts, linked to their account. Youth members over a certain age can log in for schedules.
Team page ➜ names only, plus the team manager's contact details
Medical notes ➜ only the team's trainers and coaches
Board, treasurer, membership admin, trainers, team managers ➜ roles tied to the season, reassigned in one action
Photo gallery ➜ members only, respecting consent recorded per member
Former members ➜ anonymised two years after leaving, except financial records
Each club ➜ its own Mollie account through Mollie Connect, with online SEPA mandates, automatic family discounts, and iDEAL payment links for failed collections

Row Level Security separates clubs, teams and families underneath it all.

The four clubs joined for the new season, taking the platform to around 4,200 members. Treasurers resolve failed collections far faster, and the parent who raised the alarm now champions the app at the regional federation meeting.

"I built the app as a treasurer. Other clubs adopted it because it finally treated children's data the way parents expect." - Jasper Koning
