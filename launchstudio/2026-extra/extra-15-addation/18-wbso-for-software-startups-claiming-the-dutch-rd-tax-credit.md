For Dutch software startups, one of the most valuable government incentives is also one of the most overlooked. The WBSO, the Dutch scheme that stimulates research and development, can lower the cost of developing new technology significantly. Founders building SaaS products frequently qualify for part of their work, yet many never apply because they assume it is only for laboratories, hardware or large companies, or because the application looks bureaucratic.

The arrival of AI coding tools adds a new question: if Lovable, Cursor or Claude Code wrote much of the code, is there still research and development to claim? The answer depends on what the work actually involves, and understanding that distinction helps you claim correctly, neither too little nor too much.

This guide explains how WBSO works for software startups. It is general information, not tax advice. Rates, ceilings and procedures change each year, so always check the current rules with RVO and consult an adviser for your situation.

## Key Takeaways

- **WBSO reduces the payroll tax** an employer pays for staff doing qualifying R&D, and gives self-employed founders a fixed deduction, with extra benefits for starters.
- **Software development can qualify** when it solves technical problems that are new to you and involves technical uncertainty, not when it applies known solutions.
- **AI-assisted building does not disqualify you,** but prompting a builder to assemble standard features is not R&D. Designing and testing technically new solutions is.
- **You must apply in advance** through RVO for a defined period, describing the planned project, and you cannot claim retroactively.
- **Keep an R&D administration** with hours and progress, because RVO can check it.

## What WBSO Is

WBSO stands for Wet Bevordering Speur- en Ontwikkelingswerk, the act promoting research and development work. It is administered by RVO, the Netherlands Enterprise Agency, and provides a tax benefit for R&D work carried out in the Netherlands.

There are two main forms:

- **For employers:** a reduction of the wage tax and social contributions payable on the wages of employees doing R&D. The benefit is calculated as a percentage of R&D wage costs, with a higher percentage in the first bracket and a higher rate still for starting companies.
- **For self-employed entrepreneurs (IB-ondernemers):** a fixed R&D deduction in income tax when at least a minimum number of R&D hours per year is reached, with an additional starter deduction in the first years.

In addition to wage costs, companies can include other R&D costs and expenses, such as specific equipment or materials, through a fixed rate or actual amounts, depending on the rules for that year. RVO publishes the current percentages, brackets, minimum hours and conditions each year.

## Does Software Development Qualify?

RVO has specific guidance for software development. Broadly, it qualifies when:

1. **It is technically new for you.** You are developing a technical solution that you do not already know how to build, not configuring existing tools or repeating what you have done before.
2. **It involves technical problems.** There is genuine uncertainty about how to achieve a technical goal, for example performance at scale, a new algorithm, a novel way to combine data sources, or reliability under specific constraints.
3. **You do it yourself, in the Netherlands.** The R&D is carried out by your own staff or by you as a self-employed founder, within the EU-relevant rules RVO applies. Work fully outsourced to third parties generally does not count as your own R&D hours.
4. **The software is developed for your own account.** Usually for your own product or process, not as a service delivered to a client who owns the result.

Examples that may qualify, depending on the details:

- Developing a scheduling algorithm that solves constraints existing tools cannot handle
- Building a data pipeline that matches records across sources with unreliable identifiers, where known methods fail
- Designing a system to evaluate and guard AI model outputs for a domain where accuracy requirements are strict
- Solving performance problems that require new architectural approaches, not just standard optimisation

Examples that generally do not qualify:

- Building standard CRUD screens, dashboards or forms
- Configuring authentication, payments or hosting
- Applying known security fixes
- Designing user interfaces
- Testing for routine bugs, maintenance or content work

## How AI Coding Tools Affect Eligibility

AI tools change how code is written, but WBSO assesses the nature of the work, not the keyboard. Two situations illustrate the difference.

**Not R&D:** A founder prompts Lovable to build a booking app with calendars, payments and email reminders. The work is assembling well-known functionality, even if the founder finds it challenging. There is no technical uncertainty about whether booking apps can be built; the AI tool knows how.

**Potentially R&D:** A founder needs the booking app to optimise routes for mobile therapists across overlapping time windows, travel times and qualifications, and no available method gives acceptable results within the response time required. The founder designs, prototypes and tests several approaches, using Claude Code to implement experiments quickly. The AI tool accelerates the work, but the technical problem and the uncertainty belong to the project.

When describing a project to RVO, focus on the technical problem, why existing solutions do not work, what approaches you will try and how you will evaluate them. Using AI tools to implement experiments is a means, not a disqualification. Claiming hours for routine AI-assisted feature building as R&D, however, is likely to be rejected or corrected during an audit.

## How to Apply

The application runs through RVO's online portal, typically using eHerkenning for companies. The key rules:

- **Apply in advance.** An application covers a period, generally between three and twelve months within one calendar year, and must be submitted before that period starts, according to RVO's deadlines. You cannot claim for work already done without an approved application.
- **Describe each project.** For each R&D project, describe the technical problem, the novelty, the planned work, the technical uncertainties and the expected hours.
- **Estimate hours per employee or for yourself.** Be realistic. Overestimating creates correction risk; underestimating limits the benefit.
- **Receive a decision.** RVO issues a decision (S&O-verklaring) stating the approved hours and benefit for employers, or confirming R&D status for self-employed founders.
- **Apply the benefit.** Employers deduct the benefit in their payroll tax returns during the period. Self-employed founders claim the deduction in their income tax return.
- **Report realised hours.** After the year, employers report realised R&D hours to RVO.

Many startups work with an R&D subsidy adviser for the first application. Advisers often charge a fixed fee or a percentage of the benefit. That can be worthwhile, but you remain responsible for the accuracy of the application and the administration.

## The R&D Administration

RVO can check whether the hours and work you claimed actually took place. You need an R&D administration that shows:

- **Hours per person per project,** recorded at least weekly, preferably in a time-tracking tool
- **Technical progress,** such as notes on approaches tried, results and decisions
- **Evidence,** such as design documents, experiment results, code repositories and test reports

Version control helps here. A well-organised Git history, with branches and commit messages tied to R&D projects, combined with short weekly progress notes, provides strong evidence. AI session logs or prompts can also show the experimental nature of the work, if you keep them.

## Common Mistakes

**Applying late.** The most common way to lose WBSO benefit is to start work before the application period begins.

**Describing features instead of technical problems.** "We will build a dashboard for our customers" is not a WBSO project. "We will develop a method to compute real-time aggregates over 50 million events within one second on modest infrastructure, where standard approaches exceed ten seconds" might be.

**Counting all development hours.** Only hours on the qualifying technical work count. Interface design, routine features and project management are typically excluded.

**Counting outsourced work as own hours.** Work done by an external agency is not your R&D hours. If a partner builds your production infrastructure while your own team develops the novel algorithm, only your team's hours on the algorithm count.

**Weak administration.** Missing timesheets or vague notes can lead to corrections and repayment.

## Writing a Strong Project Description

RVO assessors read many applications. Descriptions that are specific and technical are easier to approve than those full of product marketing. A useful structure for each project:

- **Goal:** the technical result you want to achieve, stated in measurable terms where possible.
- **Problem:** why this is technically difficult, and why existing tools, libraries or methods do not solve it for your situation.
- **Novelty:** what is new for your company in the approach.
- **Approach:** the experiments, prototypes or analyses you plan, and how you will evaluate them.
- **Risks:** the technical uncertainties that could make the approach fail.
- **Planning:** the phases and hours per person.

Avoid business language such as "revolutionary platform" or "market-leading experience". Assessors are looking for technical content.

## WBSO and the Innovation Box

Companies with WBSO-approved R&D may also benefit later from the Innovation Box, a corporate income tax regime that taxes qualifying profits from self-developed intangible assets at a reduced effective rate. For most startups this becomes relevant only once profitable, but it is another reason to keep good R&D records from the start. Check the current rules with the Belastingdienst and your adviser.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a software startup qualify for WBSO?

Yes, when it develops technically new solutions to technical problems, carried out by its own staff or the founder in the Netherlands. Routine development, such as standard screens, configuration and maintenance, generally does not qualify. RVO publishes specific guidance for software development.

### Does using AI coding tools mean my work is not R&D?

No. WBSO assesses whether the work addresses technical problems with genuine uncertainty, not which tools you use. Using AI tools to implement experiments can be part of R&D, but assembling standard features with an AI builder is not R&D, regardless of how much effort it takes.

### Can I apply for WBSO after the work is done?

No. Applications must be submitted before the period they cover starts, according to RVO's deadlines. Work done before an approved period cannot be claimed, which is why planning applications early in the year matters.
