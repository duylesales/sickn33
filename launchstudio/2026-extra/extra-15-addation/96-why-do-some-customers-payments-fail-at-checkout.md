**Short answer:** when checkout works for most customers but fails for some, the payment itself is rarely the whole story. In Europe, the most common causes are 3D Secure authentication that the checkout does not handle, payment methods such as iDEAL, Wero or Bancontact that send customers to a banking app and back, cards declined by the bank, amounts or currencies the payment method does not support, and checkouts that decide success in the browser instead of waiting for the payment provider's confirmation. Look at the failed payments in your Stripe or Mollie dashboard, match them to orders, and fix the specific step where customers drop out.

## Key Takeaways

- **The payment dashboard is your best diagnostic tool:** each failed or abandoned payment has a status and often a reason.
- **3D Secure is mandatory for many card payments in Europe;** custom checkouts that do not handle it fail for those cards.
- **Redirect-based methods** such as iDEAL depend on the customer returning to your site, which they often do not.
- **Order status must come from the provider's webhook,** not from the browser's return page.
- **Some declines are genuine** and need a clear message and an alternative, not a bug fix.

## Step 1: Find the Failing Payments

Before changing code, look at the data:

- **In Stripe or Mollie,** filter payments by status: failed, canceled, expired, open or requires action.
- **Note the payment method, card country, amount and device** for each failure.
- **Match them to your orders.** Do you have orders marked "paid" with no successful payment, or successful payments with no order?
- **Ask a customer who reported a problem** what they saw and on which device.

Patterns emerge quickly: all failures on one payment method, all on mobile, all above a certain amount, or all from non-Dutch cards.

## Cause 1: 3D Secure Not Handled

Under the EU's PSD2 rules, many online card payments require Strong Customer Authentication, usually through 3D Secure: the customer confirms the payment in their banking app or with a code. Hosted checkouts such as Stripe Checkout or Mollie's payment page handle this automatically. Custom payment forms generated by AI tools sometimes do not: they create a payment, receive a "requires action" status and treat it as a failure, or never show the authentication step.

**Fix:** use the provider's hosted checkout or its official components, which handle authentication, or implement the "requires action" flow exactly as documented. Test with the provider's 3D Secure test cards.

## Cause 2: Customers Do Not Return After a Redirect

With iDEAL, Wero, Bancontact and similar methods, customers leave your site, confirm in their bank's app and are sent back. On phones, many things interrupt that return: the banking app opens and the customer never switches back, the browser session was lost, the return opens in a different browser, or the in-app browser from Instagram cannot reopen.

If your app only marks the order as paid when the customer reaches the return page, those payments succeed at the bank but fail in your system.

**Fix:** confirm payments with the provider's webhook, which arrives regardless of whether the customer returns. Make the return page check the order status from your server and show "Payment received" or "Waiting for confirmation" accordingly. Our guide on [Bancontact and iDEAL checkout](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/bancontact-and-ideal-checkout-for-benelux-ai-apps) covers the flow.

## Cause 3: Status Decided in the Browser

AI-generated checkouts often mark an order "paid" when the browser reaches a success URL. This fails in two directions: customers who pay but do not return remain unpaid in your system, and anyone who visits the success URL directly gets a paid order without paying.

**Fix:** never trust the browser for payment status. Your server should receive the webhook, fetch the payment from the provider's API to verify it and only then update the order. See [Mollie integration step by step](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/mollie-integration-for-ai-built-apps-step-by-step) for the verified pattern.

## Cause 4: Genuine Declines

Banks decline cards for insufficient funds, expired cards, suspected fraud, limits on online or foreign transactions and business cards with restrictions. Stripe and other providers return a decline code that indicates the reason.

**Fix:** show a clear, polite message ("Your bank declined this payment; please try another card or payment method"), offer alternatives such as iDEAL or another card, and do not retry automatically in ways that look like fraud. Track decline rates: a sudden increase can point to a configuration issue such as missing customer details that issuers use for risk checks.

## Cause 5: Unsupported Amounts, Currencies or Methods

- **Minimum and maximum amounts** differ per method; some methods are not available below or above certain amounts.
- **Currency:** iDEAL and Bancontact work in euros only. An app that sends another currency, or the wrong format, fails.
- **Amount format:** Stripe expects amounts in cents as an integer; Mollie expects a string with two decimals. Rounding errors produce amounts the provider rejects or that differ from the order total. Our guide on [money rounding bugs](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/money-rounding-and-currency-bugs-in-saas) explains the details.
- **Methods not activated** in the dashboard for live mode, although they worked in test mode.

**Fix:** validate amounts and currencies on the server before creating payments, show only methods that apply, and check live-mode activation for every method you offer.

## Cause 6: Double Clicks and Expired Sessions

Customers who click "Pay" twice can create two payments, of which one is abandoned; customers who take too long may hit an expired checkout session. Both appear as failures.

**Fix:** use idempotency keys when creating payments, disable the button while processing, and create a fresh payment if the customer returns to an expired one.

## Cause 7: Mobile and In-App Browser Issues

Checkout in an in-app browser, a tiny pay button behind the keyboard or a blocked pop-up can all stop customers before the payment is even created. See [why apps break on iPhone and Safari](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/why-does-my-app-break-on-iphone-and-safari).

## A Diagnosis Table

| Pattern in the dashboard | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card payments stuck on "requires action" | 3D Secure not handled | Hosted checkout or official components |
| iDEAL paid at bank, order unpaid | Status set on return page | Webhook-confirmed status |
| Orders paid without payments | Success URL trusted | Server-side verification |
| Many declines with specific codes | Genuine declines or missing data | Clear message, alternatives |
| Failures only in live mode | Method not activated | Dashboard configuration |
| Duplicate payments per order | Double clicks | Idempotency and button state |
| Failures mostly from Instagram traffic | In-app browser | Open-in-browser prompt |

## Measure Checkout Success

Track checkout as a funnel: started, payment created, authenticated, paid, confirmed. A healthy checkout converts most created payments into paid ones; a significant gap shows where to look. Monitor webhook delivery and failed payments with alerts, so problems are not discovered at month-end.

Bram de Vries built Ambachtsavond with Bolt and Supabase: a platform where craftspeople such as ceramicists, woodworkers and bakers sell places in evening workshops, paid upfront with iDEAL or card through Mollie. Hosts complained that customers claimed to have paid but did not appear on the attendee list, while Bram's figures showed roughly 20% of started checkouts never completing. In the Mollie dashboard, the picture was clear. Many iDEAL payments had status "paid" while the matching booking remained "pending": the app only confirmed bookings when customers came back to the return page, and many never switched back from their banking app. Card payments from some banks were stuck in authentication because the custom card form, generated in Bolt, did not handle the 3D Secure step. Several bookings were marked paid with no payment at all, because anyone reaching the success URL got a confirmed booking. And workshop prices with a discount code sometimes produced amounts like €42.499, which Mollie rejected.

LaunchStudio replaced the custom card form with Mollie's hosted checkout, which handles 3D Secure and all payment methods. Booking status now changes only after a verified Mollie webhook, and the return page polls the server and shows "Payment received" or "We're confirming your payment". The success URL no longer changes anything. Amounts are calculated in cents on the server, with discounts rounded consistently. Idempotency prevents duplicate payments, and an alert fires when webhooks fail or the paid-to-started ratio drops. Mismatched historical bookings were reconciled against Mollie, and 37 customers who had paid but were missing were added to their workshops.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do some card payments fail while others work?

Often because 3D Secure authentication is required for those cards and the checkout does not handle it, or because the bank declines the payment. The decline code or status in your payment dashboard shows which.

### Why does iDEAL show paid but my app shows unpaid?

Your app probably confirms orders only when the customer returns to your site. Many customers stay in their banking app. Confirm payments with the provider's webhook instead.

### Should I use a hosted checkout or my own payment form?

For most founders, a hosted checkout from Stripe or Mollie is more reliable, handles authentication and all payment methods and reduces your security scope. Custom forms make sense only with careful implementation.
