Most funding advice for startups assumes one path: raise money from angel investors or venture capital in exchange for equity. That path suits some companies, but it is not the only option, and for many Dutch founders building software it is not the best first one. The Netherlands has an unusually rich mix of public and semi-public financing instruments for innovative companies: innovation loans, early-stage financing, regional subsidies, tax credits, microcredit and guarantee schemes.

Each instrument has its own logic, conditions and paperwork, and the landscape changes from year to year. This guide gives an overview of the main options relevant to founders building apps and SaaS products, explains who each one suits, and describes what applicants generally need to show. It is general information; always check current conditions with the organisation concerned and consider an adviser for applications.

## Key Takeaways

- **Funding options are layered.** Tax credits such as WBSO reduce costs, subsidies such as MIT support specific projects, loans such as Innovatiekrediet and Vroegefasefinanciering finance development, and equity comes from angels and funds.
- **Innovatiekrediet** is an RVO loan for technically innovative development projects with commercial prospects, typically for SMEs with a solid plan and co-financing.
- **Vroegefasefinanciering** is a smaller RVO loan for the very early phase, to test whether an idea is technically and commercially feasible.
- **Regional development companies and the MIT scheme** offer funding and subsidies aimed at regional innovation.
- **Every funder looks for evidence.** A working, secure, live product with real users is the strongest evidence a software founder can provide.

## Why Non-Equity Funding Matters for Software Founders

AI tools have reduced the cost of building a first version dramatically. A founder can now validate a product with a budget that would have bought only a design phase a few years ago. That changes the funding question. Instead of raising a large equity round to pay a development team, many founders need a modest amount to take a validated prototype to production, win the first customers and prove the business model.

For that stage, non-dilutive or lightly dilutive funding is attractive. You keep more ownership, and you can raise equity later at a higher valuation, based on traction rather than promises.

## Innovatiekrediet

Innovatiekrediet is a loan from RVO on behalf of the Ministry of Economic Affairs for technically innovative development projects. Its key characteristics, as described by RVO:

- **Purpose:** financing development projects that lead to new products, processes or services with good commercial prospects, where the technical risk makes regular financing difficult.
- **Who:** primarily small and medium-sized enterprises in the Netherlands, with specific conditions for larger companies.
- **Form:** a loan covering a portion of eligible project costs, with interest, and with repayment linked to the project's success; if the project fails technically, part of the loan may not need to be repaid under the scheme's conditions.
- **Co-financing:** you must finance part of the project yourself or through other sources.
- **Assessment:** RVO assesses technical innovation, the project plan, the team, the market and the business case. It is competitive and requires a thorough application.

For software, Innovatiekrediet typically suits companies developing substantial technical innovations, such as a new platform with novel technology, rather than standard applications built with existing tools. The project size and level of documentation expected mean that it is most relevant once you have a team, a validated market need and a clear development plan.

## Vroegefasefinanciering

Vroegefasefinanciering, or early-phase financing, is a smaller RVO loan aimed at the stage before a company is ready for regular financing. It is meant to help entrepreneurs investigate whether their product idea is technically and commercially viable, and to make the company attractive to follow-on investors.

- **Purpose:** feasibility work, such as building a prototype, testing with users and preparing a business case.
- **Who:** innovative starters and SMEs, with separate tracks for certain groups such as academic startups.
- **Form:** a loan for a limited amount and duration, with interest.
- **Assessment:** the innovative character, the team and the plan for the early phase.

Founders who have an AI-built prototype and early user feedback but need a modest budget to reach a production-ready product and first paying customers may find this instrument fits. Check RVO's current conditions, budgets and opening periods, which vary by year.

## WBSO: Lowering R&D Costs

WBSO is not a loan but a tax benefit that reduces wage costs for qualifying R&D work, or provides a deduction for self-employed founders. It suits founders whose product involves genuine technical development, beyond assembling standard features. We cover it in detail in our guide to [WBSO for software startups](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/wbso-for-software-startups-claiming-the-dutch-rd-tax-credit).

WBSO combines well with other instruments: it lowers the cost of R&D, while loans and subsidies finance the remainder.

## MIT: Regional SME Innovation Stimulation

MIT, the MKB-innovatiestimulering Regio en Topsectoren scheme, is a cooperation between national and regional governments. It typically offers:

- **Feasibility projects:** subsidies for studies that explore the technical and economic feasibility of an innovation.
- **R&D collaboration projects:** subsidies for projects in which several SMEs develop an innovation together.

MIT budgets and rules vary per province, and calls open at set times, often with high demand. It suits founders with a specific innovation project and, for collaboration projects, partners to work with.

## Regional Development Companies

Each region in the Netherlands has a regional development company (ROM), such as InnovationQuarter in South Holland, BOM in Brabant, Oost NL in Gelderland and Overijssel, and NOM in the northern provinces, along with others. ROMs typically offer:

- Investment funds for innovative startups and scale-ups, often through convertible loans or equity
- Access to regional innovation programmes and subsidies
- Support with business development and introductions to investors

ROM investments usually expect a scalable business with a strong team and early traction. They often co-invest with private investors.

## Qredits and Microcredit

Qredits is a non-profit lender that provides business loans and microcredit to entrepreneurs who may struggle to get bank financing, including starters. For a founder who needs a modest amount to fund a production launch, marketing or working capital, Qredits can be an accessible option. It typically requires a business plan and offers coaching alongside the loan.

## Bank Financing and Guarantee Schemes

Banks rarely lend to early software startups without revenue, but once you have recurring revenue, financing becomes possible. Government guarantee schemes, such as the BMKB (Borgstelling MKB-kredieten), can make banks more willing to lend to SMEs by guaranteeing part of the loan. These are relevant for later stages, such as financing growth once the product has paying customers.

## Angel Investors and Early-Stage Funds

Equity funding remains important, particularly for products that need to scale quickly or that address large markets. The Dutch ecosystem includes angel networks, early-stage funds, accelerators and programmes connected to universities and regions. Equity investors look for a large market, a strong team, traction and a credible path to growth. Combining equity with non-dilutive instruments, for example an innovation loan alongside an angel round, is common.

## Comparing the Options

| Vroegefasefinanciering | Loan | Idea to early product | Innovative idea, team, plan for feasibility work |
| MIT | Subsidy | Feasibility or R&D project | Innovation project, regional fit, often partners |
| Innovatiekrediet | Loan | Development of substantial innovation | Technical innovation, business case, co-financing |
| ROM funds | Loan or equity | Early traction to scale-up | Scalable model, team, traction |

| Angels and funds | Equity | Validated product onwards | Market size, team, traction, growth plan |

## What Every Funder Looks For

Although the instruments differ, assessors ask similar questions:

1. **Is the problem real?** Evidence from users, letters of intent, pilots or paying customers.
2. **Is the solution credible?** A working product, not only a concept.
3. **Can the team deliver?** Relevant experience and a plan for skills the team lacks.
4. **Is the plan realistic?** Clear milestones, costs and timelines.
5. **What are the risks?** Technical, commercial and operational, with mitigations.

For software founders, the second and fifth questions have changed with AI tools. Assessors increasingly know that a demo can be generated quickly. What distinguishes a credible project is a product that works reliably for real users: secure, live on its own domain, with real data, measurable usage and a sound technical foundation. A prototype that breaks during a demo or exposes user data raises exactly the kind of risk funders want to avoid.

## How a Production-Ready Product Strengthens Applications

Taking an AI-built prototype to production before or during a funding application has several effects:

- **Traction becomes measurable.** Real users, retention and revenue replace estimates.
- **Technical risk drops.** Security, scalability and reliability questions have concrete answers.
- **Budgets become credible.** Production work done at a fixed price gives a clear cost line, and remaining development needs can be estimated more precisely.
- **Due diligence is easier.** Investors and assessors can review a documented architecture, ownership of code and data, and security practices.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between Innovatiekrediet and Vroegefasefinanciering?

Vroegefasefinanciering is a smaller loan for the earliest phase, to test technical and commercial feasibility. Innovatiekrediet is a larger loan for development projects with substantial technical innovation and a solid business case, requiring co-financing. Both are administered by RVO; check current conditions and amounts.

### Can an app built with AI tools qualify for innovation funding?

It depends on the innovation, not the tools. Funding aimed at technical innovation requires genuine technical novelty and uncertainty. Assembling standard features with an AI builder is not innovative in that sense, but a product that solves a new technical problem may qualify, and AI tools can be part of how it is built.

### Do I need a working product before applying for funding?

Not always, but it helps significantly. Early-phase instruments can fund feasibility work, yet even there, evidence from a working prototype and real users strengthens an application. For larger loans and equity, a live product with traction is usually expected.

### Does a live product help my visibility with investors and in AI search?

Yes. A live product on your own domain with real customers, case studies and clear information about what you do gives investors, journalists, search engines and AI answer engines verifiable facts to find and cite, which a preview link cannot.
