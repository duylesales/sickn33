A pattern has become standard among founders building with AI: the marketing site lives in Webflow or Framer, where it is easy to design, edit and publish, and the product lives in an app generated with Lovable, Bolt, v0 or Cursor. The split makes sense. Marketing pages change weekly and benefit from visual editing; the app needs a real backend and should not be touched every time a headline changes.

The trouble is the seam between them. Visitors click "Sign up" on the marketing site and land on a login page with a different font, a different URL and sometimes a security warning. Analytics show the visitor leaving the site, never to return. Google indexes the app's empty login page instead of the pricing page. Password reset emails link to a preview URL.

None of these problems are hard. They are simply easy to overlook because they live between two tools. This guide covers how to connect a Webflow or Framer site to an AI-built app properly.

## Key Takeaways

- **Use a subdomain for the app** (`app.yourdomain.com`) and keep the root domain for marketing. It is the simplest and most reliable setup for most founders.
- **Configure authentication for the real domain:** allowed redirect URLs, email links and OAuth settings must point to the app subdomain, not a preview URL.
- **Keep the app out of search results** where it has no public content, and make sure marketing pages carry the SEO weight.
- **Track the journey across both sites** with one analytics setup and a consent banner that respects GDPR on both.
- **Align the design** so the transition from marketing to product feels like one company, not two.

## Subdomain or Subdirectory?

The first decision is where the app lives. There are two options.

| Option | Example | How it works | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subdomain | `app.yourdomain.com` | DNS record points the subdomain to the app host | Almost every founder |
| Subdirectory | `yourdomain.com/app` | A reverse proxy routes paths to different hosts | Specific SEO strategies, larger teams |

**Subdomains are simpler.** Webflow or Framer hosts `yourdomain.com` and `www`, and your app host, such as Vercel, Netlify or Lovable's hosting, serves `app.yourdomain.com`. Each platform manages its own SSL certificate. No proxy is needed, and the two sites can be deployed independently.

**Subdirectories add complexity.** Serving the app at `/app` requires a reverse proxy, for example through Cloudflare or a custom server, that sends some paths to the marketing host and others to the app host. That proxy must handle caching, headers and certificates correctly. Some founders choose this because they want blog or documentation content generated by the app to share the root domain's search authority, but for a logged-in product there is usually little to gain.

Our recommendation for most founders: marketing on the root domain, app on `app.`, and documentation or a help centre on `help.` or `docs.` if you have one.

## DNS Setup Without Breaking Anything

DNS changes are where launches go wrong, usually because records are edited in a hurry. A typical setup looks like this:

| Record | Name | Points to | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A or CNAME | `@` and `www` | Webflow or Framer, per their instructions | Marketing site |
| CNAME | `app` | Your app host | Product |
| MX | `@` | Your email provider | Company email |
| TXT | `@` | SPF record | Email sender authorisation |
| TXT or CNAME | Provider-specific | DKIM keys | Email signing |
| TXT | `_dmarc` | DMARC policy | Email protection |

Two practical rules help. First, **never delete MX or email TXT records while changing website records**; founders regularly break their own email this way. Second, **add the app record before announcing anything**, wait for the certificate to be issued and test the subdomain over HTTPS on a phone network as well as your office Wi-Fi. Webflow and Framer both document their required records; follow them exactly rather than copying settings from a tutorial for a different host.

## Authentication on the Real Domain

AI-built apps usually start life on a preview URL, and their authentication settings reflect that. Before launch, every place that knows the app's address must be updated.

**Allowed redirect URLs.** Supabase Auth, Firebase Authentication, Clerk and similar services only redirect users to URLs on an allow list. Add `https://app.yourdomain.com` and remove preview URLs you no longer use. A missing entry causes login loops; a leftover wildcard can be abused.

**Site URL for emails.** Confirmation, magic link and password reset emails use a configured site URL. If it still points to the preview, every email sends users to the wrong place.

**OAuth providers.** Google, Microsoft and other sign-in providers have their own lists of authorised origins and redirect URIs. Update them, and update the app name and logo shown on the consent screen so users trust what they see.

**Links from the marketing site.** "Log in" and "Start free trial" buttons in Webflow or Framer should link to the exact app URLs, for example `https://app.yourdomain.com/login` and `https://app.yourdomain.com/signup`. If you pass a plan or campaign parameter, make sure the app reads it.

## Cookies and Sessions Across Subdomains

By default, a cookie set by `app.yourdomain.com` is only sent to that subdomain. That is usually what you want for authentication: the marketing site does not need access to the session, and keeping them separate limits risk if a marketing script is ever compromised.

Some founders want the marketing site to know whether a visitor is logged in, to show "Go to dashboard" instead of "Sign up". The safe way is a separate, non-sensitive cookie scoped to the parent domain, such as `logged_in=true`, set by the app, which the marketing site reads only to change a button. Never share the actual session token with the marketing domain. MDN's documentation on cookies explains the `Domain`, `Secure`, `HttpOnly` and `SameSite` attributes that control this behaviour.

## SEO: Let the Marketing Site Do the Work

Search engines should find your marketing pages, blog and documentation, not your login screen or dashboard routes.

- **Mark app pages as `noindex`** where they contain no public content, using the robots meta tag described in Google's documentation. Login, signup, dashboard and settings pages rarely belong in search results.
- **Do not block the app entirely in `robots.txt` if it serves public pages** such as shared profiles or public listings that you do want indexed. In that case, render those pages on the server so crawlers see real content.
- **Use canonical URLs on the marketing site** so `www` and non-`www` versions do not compete, and make sure only one version resolves.
- **Keep structured data on the marketing site**: organisation, product, FAQ and article markup help both Google and AI answer engines understand who you are.
- **Submit the marketing sitemap** in Search Console, and add the app subdomain as a separate property so you can monitor it.

Many AI-built apps are single-page applications that render content in the browser. That is fine behind a login, but public pages that rely on client-side rendering are harder for crawlers. ## Analytics and Consent Across Both Sites

To understand your funnel, you need to see the journey from first visit to sign-up to activation. That requires consistent tracking across the marketing site and the app.

- **One analytics property** covering both domains, with the app subdomain included in cross-domain or same-site configuration as your tool requires.
- **Campaign parameters preserved** when visitors move from marketing to sign-up, so you know which ads or posts produce customers.
- **A sign-up event in the app** and, ideally, an activation event when the user completes a meaningful first action.
- **A consent banner on both sites** that loads non-essential tracking only after consent, in line with GDPR and the Dutch Telecommunications Act. The consent choice should carry across subdomains so visitors are not asked twice.

If you would rather avoid consent complexity for basic statistics, cookieless analytics tools are an option; our article on [cookieless analytics for EU SaaS](https://launchstudio.eu/en/blog/cookieless-analytics-for-eu-saas-plausible-vs-ga4) compares the approaches.

## Design Consistency

The moment a visitor moves from a polished Webflow page to an app with different fonts, colours and button styles, trust drops. Keeping both aligned is mostly discipline:

- **Shared design tokens.** Define colours, font families, font sizes, border radius and spacing once and apply them in both tools. Many AI builders accept a short style specification in a prompt or a configuration file.
- **Same logo, favicon and page titles pattern.**
- **Consistent navigation language.** If the marketing site says "Workspace", the app should not say "Project".
- **Matching legal links.** Privacy policy and terms should be linked from both, pointing to the same pages.

## The Launch Checklist

| Area | Check |
| --- | --- |
| DNS | Marketing on root and `www`, app on `app.`, email records untouched, HTTPS verified on both |
| Auth | Redirect URLs, site URL, OAuth origins and email templates all use the app subdomain |
| Links | Marketing buttons go to the correct app routes, parameters pass through |
| Cookies | Session cookies scoped to the app, optional non-sensitive flag for the marketing site |
| SEO | App pages `noindex` where appropriate, marketing sitemap submitted, canonical domain chosen |
| Analytics | One property, sign-up and activation events, campaign data preserved |
| Consent | Banner on both sites, consent shared across subdomains |
| Design | Shared tokens, logo, favicon and terminology |
| Emails | Sender domain authenticated with SPF, DKIM and DMARC |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should my app be on a subdomain or a subdirectory?

For most founders, a subdomain such as `app.yourdomain.com` is the better choice. It is simpler to set up, each platform manages its own SSL certificate and the two sites deploy independently. A subdirectory requires a reverse proxy and only pays off in specific SEO situations.

### Will my marketing site's SEO suffer if the app is on a subdomain?

No, provided the marketing site carries your public content. Logged-in app pages rarely have search value anyway. Keep public pages, blog and documentation on the marketing site or a server-rendered docs subdomain, and mark private app routes as `noindex`.

### Can my Webflow or Framer site show whether a visitor is logged in?

Yes, safely, by having the app set a separate non-sensitive cookie on the parent domain that the marketing site reads only to change a button. The real session token should stay scoped to the app subdomain.
