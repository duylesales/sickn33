🛣️ Two routes from pilot to every tenant

THE PILOT
Wendy Hoogland, innovation lead at a housing corporation in Zwolle with around 12,000 homes, built Reparatiemelder in Lovable: tenants report repairs with photos, get an estimated appointment window and follow progress, and the maintenance team sees requests grouped by complex. A three-month pilot with 400 tenants cut calls to the repairs desk by a third.

THE STALL
Rolling it out to everyone stalled for five months. Internal IT asked the right questions:
• Tenants logged in with an email and password unrelated to the tenant portal
• Staff shared accounts
• Data sat in a US-region Supabase project
• Photos of tenants' homes were in public storage
• No processing record, no logging, no support owner

ROUTE A: rebuild on the corporation's platform
IT's estimate: nine months.

ROUTE B: a combined plan
IT and the privacy officer set the requirements. LaunchStudio's engineers met them (Launch & Grow, 18 business days, €5,800, plus €49/month):
✓ Staff SSO via Microsoft Entra ID
✓ Tenant login via the existing tenant portal's identity provider
✓ Tenant and complex-level access enforced in the database
✓ Migration to an EU region, under the corporation's processing agreement
✓ Photos moved to private storage
✓ Audit logging, exported to IT's own monitoring
✓ Documentation and a runbook
IT accepted a managed-service arrangement, with a documented exit plan.

ROUTE B'S ARRIVAL
Live for all tenants eighteen business days after the plan was approved, instead of nine months. Within a year, Reparatiemelder handled around 60% of repair requests. And IT now uses the same arrangement for two other innovation pilots.

"IT wasn't blocking us. They were asking the right questions. We just needed someone who could answer them in weeks instead of quarters." - Wendy Hoogland
