€3,500 sits at an interesting point on the price ladder. It is too much to spend casually and far too little for a traditional agency, which would barely finish discovery for that amount. For taking an AI app to production, though, it is the budget where most small businesses and early SaaS products land: enough to go live properly with payments, not enough to add everything on a wish list. This article breaks down what that money typically covers.

## Where €3,500 Sits on the Ladder

LaunchStudio's fixed prices range from €800 to €7,500. Roughly:

- **€800–€1,500:** a focused review and fixes for a simple app — security basics, data location, backups, domain. Often without payments.
- **€2,000–€3,500:** the mid-range. A working app with accounts and payments made production ready end to end.
- **€4,000–€7,500:** more complex products — multiple roles, marketplaces, integrations, heavy data, peaks, managed operations.

The price calculator reflects this: a Webshop starts around €1,500–€3,500, a Tool around €1,200–€3,000, and a SaaS around €2,833–€7,167, with add-ons such as Payments (+€400), User accounts (+€250), Security (+€500) and Database/backend (+€350).

## AI App to Production for €3,500: What It Typically Covers

For a working prototype built with Lovable, Bolt or Cursor, a mid-range project usually includes:

**Security (roughly a third of the effort).** Server-side access control on every table, secrets moved out of the browser and rotated, admin areas protected by roles, input validation on key forms, rate limiting on login and signup.

**Accounts and data.** Authentication settings checked, password reset and email verification hardened, database in an EU region, backups with a tested restore, account deletion that works.

**Payments.** Mollie or Stripe with iDEAL and cards, confirmation via verified webhooks, refunds and failed payments handled, test and live modes separated.

**Hosting and delivery.** Your own domain with SSL, production hosting, a staging environment, a simple deploy process.

**Email.** Transactional email through a proper provider with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, so confirmations and receipts arrive.

**Visibility.** Uptime monitoring and error tracking with alerts to you.

**Testing and support.** Key flows tested before launch, and 48 hours of post-launch support.

## What Usually Does Not Fit

- Subscriptions with complex plan logic, proration and usage-based billing
- Marketplaces with payouts to third parties
- Multiple integrations with external systems
- Large data migrations
- Native mobile app store submissions
- Significant new features

These are not impossible at €3,500 if the rest of the app is simple, but together they push projects towards the upper range.

## How to Make €3,500 Go Further

**Fix the scope before the project starts.** Scope added mid-project is the most common reason budgets grow.

**Do the non-technical work yourself.** Consolidate account ownership, write your privacy notice and email copy, prepare test accounts and data.

**Postpone deliberately.** Performance tuning, admin dashboards and branded email templates can often wait until there is revenue.

**Start from a working prototype.** A working prototype is priced at 1.0×; design-only starts at 1.3× and idea-only at 1.5×, because each earlier stage adds build work.

## What Moves a Project Up or Down the Range

| Factor | Pushes cost down | Pushes cost up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prototype completeness | Working end to end | Screens without logic |
| User roles | One or two | Many, with teams or organisations |
| Payments | One-off, one provider | Subscriptions, marketplace payouts |
| Data | Non-sensitive, small | Health, children, finance, large migrations |
| Integrations | None | Several external systems |
| Backend | Mature service (Supabase, Firebase) | Custom or platform-locked |
| Scope discipline | Frozen | Growing during the project |

Knowing these factors helps you predict where your project will land before the intro call — and which choices would bring it lower.

## Getting More Value From a Fixed Budget

Several choices stretch a mid-range budget:

- **Arrive with ownership sorted:** accounts on company email with 2FA saves time at the start.
- **Remove unused features** from the prototype before the review; every unused page still needs protection.
- **Choose one payment provider** and a simple pricing model for launch.
- **Postpone integrations** that can be handled manually for the first months.
- **Prepare texts yourself:** emails, privacy notice, help pages.
- **Be available** for quick decisions, which keeps the timeline short.

## Comparing €3,500 With Alternatives

For the same scope, founders commonly receive quotes ranging from a few thousand euros from freelancers billing hourly (with uncertain totals) to tens of thousands from agencies proposing rebuilds. The difference is rarely the hourly rate alone; it is scope — whether the frontend is rebuilt, whether discovery phases are included — and who carries the risk of overruns. When comparing, look at what each quote actually delivers against the work packages above.

## Running Costs After Launch

The build budget is not the only cost. Typical monthly running costs for a small app include hosting, database plan, email sending, payment fees per transaction, domain and monitoring tools, plus optional managed hosting at €49 per month. For many small booking or subscription apps, total running costs are modest compared with revenue, but they should be in your plan from the start.

## When €3,500 Is Too Much

Some apps do not need the mid-range at all: a simple website, a tool without accounts, an app with one user type and no payments. For these, a focused Launch Ready project at the lower end of the range covers the essentials. A good provider will tell you when your needs are smaller than you feared.

## What You Should Receive for €3,500

At the end of a mid-range project, expect concrete deliverables: the findings list with every item marked fixed or deliberately postponed; code changes in your repository with readable commit history; tests for access control and payments; production and staging environments on your accounts; documented environment variables and secrets locations; backup configuration and a restore test result; monitoring and alert routing; and a short handover document describing how to deploy, roll back and get support. If any of these are missing, ask — they are what turn a fixed price into lasting value.

## Paying in Stages

Fixed-price projects are commonly paid in stages tied to milestones: a portion at start, a portion when the structural fixes are on staging and the remainder after launch and acceptance. Stage payments protect both sides and keep attention on outcomes. Acceptance should be based on criteria you can check yourself: the two-account test fails correctly, a closed-tab payment is recorded properly, emails arrive, alerts reach your phone.

## Planning the Next Budget

After launch, many founders budget in smaller steps: managed hosting monthly, a small project in month two or three for postponed items such as performance or admin tools, and a review before the next major feature. Spreading investment this way keeps cash flow manageable and ties each spend to evidence from real usage.

## A Note on Value

€3,500 is a meaningful amount for a small business or early startup. Its value lies less in the hours than in the outcomes: customers who can pay without problems, data that cannot leak between accounts, a product that stays up during busy periods and a founder who no longer spends evenings reconciling payments. For most founders who reach this stage, those outcomes repay the investment within the first months of operation — often through a single avoided incident or a handful of customers who would otherwise have been lost to a broken checkout.

## Frequently Overlooked Costs

Founders budgeting a launch sometimes forget items outside the engineering quote: legal review of terms and privacy notice, a professional email address and domain setup, payment provider onboarding time (sometimes days for verification), app store developer accounts if mobile is planned, and time for their own testing and content preparation. None is large, but together they can surprise a tight budget. Listing them alongside the fixed engineering price gives a realistic total for going live.

## The Mid-Range Sweet Spot

For most AI-built booking apps, webshops and early SaaS products, the mid-range is where safety and affordability meet: enough to protect data and money properly, get live on your own domain and operate calmly, without paying for rebuilds or features you do not yet need. Spend it on the non-negotiables, postpone the rest deliberately, and let real usage decide what comes next. That discipline is what makes a €3,500 launch feel like a sound investment rather than a leap of faith — and it leaves room in the budget for the marketing that brings your first customers in.

## Real example

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: A Music School Booking App at Exactly €3,500

Ilse Dekkers, a piano teacher who runs a small music school in Emmen with six teachers, built Muziekmoment in Lovable: students book lessons with teachers, buy lesson cards, receive reminders and can reschedule within the school's rules. Around 180 students used it informally, paying by bank transfer, which cost Ilse hours of reconciliation every month.

She had €3,500 set aside. LaunchStudio's review found the usual mid-range picture: students could see each other's lesson notes by changing an ID, the Supabase service key was in the frontend, lesson cards had no payment integration at all, the database was in a US region with untested backups, reminder emails went to spam, and the app lived on a Lovable preview link. Ilse also wanted a teacher payroll export and a waiting-list feature.

The fixed quote covered: access control on students, lessons and notes; key rotation; Mollie payments for lesson cards with iDEAL and webhook confirmation, including automatic credit on payment and reversal on refund; EU migration with a tested restore; authenticated email sending; domain, hosting, staging and monitoring; and account deletion. The payroll export fitted as a simple CSV; the waiting list was postponed to a later project.

**Result:** Muziekmoment went live after twelve business days. Bank-transfer reconciliation disappeared, lesson card sales rose because students could pay instantly with iDEAL, and the school grew to 230 students by the end of the season.
