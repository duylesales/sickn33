🚨 Gijs Hendrix, a process operator at the Chemelot industrial site near Sittard, built Carpoolkaart with Cursor in his free evenings: shift workers form carpools matched to rotating shift patterns, with cost-sharing per trip. Then the site's mobility coordinator offered to promote it to several thousand workers - in six weeks. Gijs had about eight hours a week, between rotating shifts. 😳

Week one's inventory showed his Supabase project under a personal email, a Stripe secret key in the frontend and trip data visible across users. So he split the work: LaunchStudio took access control, key rotation, Stripe Connect payouts between drivers and passengers, staging and CI; Gijs kept the privacy notice, matching rules, user texts and twelve test users. Here's a six-week plan for taking AI code to production with a day job: 🧠

❌ Infrastructure learning at 2 a.m. after a night shift
❌ A project owned by a personal account
❌ Secret keys in the browser and shared trip data
❌ Launching without anyone testing night shifts

✅ Week 1: inventory - Week 2: hand off the technical work, keep the decisions
✅ Weeks 3–4: access control, rotated keys, Stripe Connect cost-sharing, staging and CI
✅ Week 5: testing on staging found a time-zone bug in night trips crossing midnight - fixed before launch
✅ Week 6: launch on a Saturday morning after a night shift - after sleeping first
