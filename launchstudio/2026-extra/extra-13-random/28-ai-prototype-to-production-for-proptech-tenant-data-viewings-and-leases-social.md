🚨 Ricardo Santos, a letting agent in Diemen, built Kijkmoment in Lovable: landlords publish rentals, applicants book viewings, agents manage applications to contract. Three agencies and about 40 private landlords used it. To "save time", applicants had to upload a passport, three payslips and an employer statement just to book a viewing. In eight months that meant documents from about 9,000 people — most of whom never got a home. 😳

Every document sat in a public bucket with guessable file names. Agents from one agency could view another agency's applicants through the API. An AI "match score" auto-hid applicants below a threshold using fields that included nationality. And nothing was ever deleted. Here's what PropTech must decide before going from AI prototype to production: 🧠

❌ Passports and payslips collected before a viewing
❌ Sensitive documents in public storage
❌ Agency separation only in the interface
❌ Opaque scoring with discriminatory proxies — and no retention

✅ Viewing bookings with contact details only; documents only from shortlisted applicants
✅ BSN-redacted documents and recorded verification instead of long-term storage
✅ Private, EU-hosted storage with signed links and database-enforced agency separation
✅ Nationality out of scoring, a transparent shortlist agents review, retention rules, access logging — and the backlog deleted after legal confirmation
