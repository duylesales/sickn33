When you are bootstrapping a new venture and cash feels tight, the proposition sounds irresistible: find a freelance developer who agrees to harden and deploy your AI prototype in exchange for 15% to 30% of your future monthly recurring revenue. You risk no upfront capital, the developer gets upside, and everyone is aligned. But beneath this surface elegance lies a fundamental economic misalignment that stalls 9 out of 10 rev-share projects within their first three months.

## The Asymmetry of Unpaid Commitment

A developer working for future revenue is, by definition, working for zero euros today. Inevitably, their paying client work, day job, or personal life will demand their attention. When a client offers them €80/hour cash for an urgent project, your rev-share MVP instantly drops to bottom priority.

Because there is no financial transaction, there is no contractual accountability. You cannot demand delivery deadlines, enforce code review standards, or dictate architecture without risking the developer walking away with all the work half-finished. 

Even worse: if the project does succeed, giving away 20% of top-line revenue forever is the most expensive way to pay for a two-week backend hardening job in the history of your business. If your SaaS reaches €25,000/month, you are paying that developer €5,000 every single month for infrastructure work they completed two years prior.

## The Clean Alternative: Fixed-Price Clarity

Fixed-price engineering removes ambiguity completely:
- **Clean Cap Table and P&L:** You retain 100% of your equity and 100% of your future gross revenue.
- **Strict Timelines and SLAs:** The work is governed by a legally binding delivery scope with agreed-upon delivery dates.
- **Enterprise-Grade Execution:** You get the collective expertise of a battle-tested engineering team rather than the spare-time hobby effort of a single individual.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do developers agree to revenue-share deals if they rarely finish them?
Developers often agree out of initial enthusiasm or during slow periods. But as soon as paid hourly client work arrives, unpaid rev-share commitments are inevitably deprioritized.

### What legal risks exist in informal revenue-share arrangements?
Without formal IP assignment contracts, the developer may retain legal ownership of the code they wrote, preventing you from selling the company or raising venture capital later.

### How does LaunchStudio's pricing compare to a revenue-share agreement over 2 years?
A fixed-price LaunchStudio launch typically costs €800–€3,500 once. A 20% rev-share on a modest €10k/month SaaS costs you €24,000 per year, every year, indefinitely.

### Can I still work with LaunchStudio if I have an existing unfinished codebase from a former collaborator?
Yes. We perform an initial code audit to assess what is usable, secure what is missing, and clean up technical debt without requiring a full rebuild.

### Does LaunchStudio take any equity or royalty in my startup?
Never. LaunchStudio operates on straightforward fixed-price service contracts. You retain 100% of equity, IP, and future revenues.
