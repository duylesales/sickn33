A scale-up founder who brings a working, AI-generated prototype to a traditional software agency for a production-readiness quote tends to get back something surprising: not a scoped fix, but a full discovery-and-rebuild proposal running four months and well into five figures. That's not a traditional agency being dishonest — it's a traditional agency answering with the only tool it's built around, because full custom builds are the model most agencies are staffed and priced for. The honest comparison between LaunchStudio and a traditional agency isn't about which one is "better" in the abstract; it's about which kind of problem you actually have, and the two approaches are built to solve genuinely different ones. Scale-up founders in particular tend to run into this mismatch at a specific, predictable moment — early traction has proven the product works, budget has finally arrived to invest in it properly, and the instinct is to reach for the most established-looking option, which is usually a traditional agency with a polished portfolio and an enterprise client list. The size of that agency's past work says little about whether a full rebuild is what your specific prototype actually needs.

## What a Traditional Agency Quote Usually Assumes

Ask a traditional software agency to review a Bolt-generated prototype and harden it for production, and the proposal that typically comes back treats the existing codebase as disposable groundwork rather than a starting point — a discovery phase to re-document requirements from scratch, a design phase to re-specify the interface even if it already works well, and a build phase staffed by a full team allocated for months. This isn't scope creep or a sales tactic; it's the structurally correct output of an organization built around bespoke, full-cycle engineering, where "audit and harden what exists" isn't a service line most agencies have productized, because their business model is built around selling teams and timelines, not narrow fixed-price diagnostic work.

## Why "Rebuild It Properly" Is the Default Answer, Not a Considered One

A traditional agency's incentive structure quietly favors the larger scope, even when a narrower one would genuinely serve the client better — a four-month engagement staffing several developers is a materially more valuable contract than a two-week fixed-price hardening pass, and the agency's own planning and utilization goals are built around the former, not the latter. This isn't necessarily a conscious decision to oversell; it's what happens when the only proposal template an organization has ever built is the full-build template, and every incoming request gets fit into that shape whether or not it's the shape the actual problem takes.

## The Real Question: Replace It, or Harden It?

Most AI-generated prototypes that reach this decision point don't actually need replacing — the product logic works, the interface reflects real design decisions a founder made and tested, and the core feature that makes the product valuable functions correctly. What's missing is narrower and more specific: authorization enforced at the API layer, properly verified payment webhooks, secrets moved out of the codebase and into managed configuration, structured error handling for external service failures. None of these require re-specifying the product from scratch; they require a focused audit against a known set of risk categories, followed by targeted remediation — a fundamentally different, and fundamentally smaller, kind of engagement than a full rebuild. Distinguishing between the two comes down to a fairly mechanical test: does the product fail because the logic is wrong, or because the layer protecting that logic from misuse is missing? A scale-up founder who's already closed paying customers on the strength of the existing product has already answered the first half of that question — the logic works, or those customers wouldn't be paying — which leaves the infrastructure layer as the far more likely actual gap.

## Timeline and Cost: Weeks and Thousands, Not Months and Tens of Thousands

The practical difference shows up starkly once both proposals sit side by side. A traditional agency's full-rebuild engagement typically runs three to six months and tens of thousands of euros, because that timeline and budget reflect genuinely re-building an application, including the parts that already worked. LaunchStudio's fixed-price packages, by contrast, run one to three weeks and typically a few thousand euros, because the scope is deliberately narrower — the layer beneath the product, not the product itself. For a founder whose actual gap is a handful of specific, identifiable risks rather than a fundamentally broken product, that difference isn't a minor efficiency; it's the difference between launching this quarter and losing the market window entirely while a full rebuild works through its own discovery phase.

## What Gets Lost in a Full Rebuild That Doesn't Need to Be

Beyond time and money, a full rebuild carries a quieter cost: months of accumulated product intuition — the specific interface decisions, edge-case handling, and small design choices a founder refined through real user feedback — gets re-specified from a written brief rather than preserved directly, and something inevitably drifts in translation. A rebuilt product isn't simply a faster version of the same thing; it's a reinterpretation of it, filtered through a new team's understanding of a spec document, and founders who've been through this report finding small but meaningful behaviors from their original prototype quietly missing from the rebuilt version, often not noticed until a returning user points it out. There's a secondary cost too: a full rebuild typically means pausing feature development and customer-facing iteration for the duration of the discovery and build phases, since the team's attention shifts to the rebuild itself, whereas a fixed-scope hardening engagement runs alongside a founder's continued work on the product, because it touches an entirely separate layer of the system.

## Where a Traditional Agency Genuinely Is the Right Choice

None of this means traditional agencies are the wrong choice categorically — for a founder who needs an entirely new, complex enterprise system built from a blank page, who requires an ongoing development team for continuous feature work well beyond an MVP, or whose product genuinely does need re-architecting because the AI-generated foundation is unsalvageable, a traditional agency's full-cycle model is the correct tool. The honest distinction is that this describes a minority of the founders who arrive holding a working AI-generated prototype and a specific, nameable gap — for that far more common situation, hardening what exists is both faster and more faithful to what was actually built.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do traditional agencies usually propose a full rebuild instead of a scoped fix?

Full custom builds are what most agencies are staffed, priced, and productized around, so an incoming request tends to get fit into that template even when a narrower audit-and-harden engagement would genuinely address the actual gap, as in Joris's case.

### How do I know if my prototype actually needs a rebuild versus hardening?

If the product logic, interface, and core feature already work as intended and the concern is specifically about security, payments, or infrastructure, hardening is almost always sufficient; a rebuild is warranted only when the underlying product logic itself is fundamentally broken or absent.

### Is a traditional agency ever the better choice over LaunchStudio?

Yes — for founders needing an entirely new complex system built from scratch, ongoing feature development with a dedicated team, or a genuine architectural overhaul, a traditional agency's full-cycle model fits better than a fixed-scope hardening engagement.

### What specifically gets "lost" when a prototype is rebuilt rather than hardened?

Accumulated product intuition — small interface decisions and edge-case handling refined through real user feedback — often doesn't fully translate through a rebuild specified from a written brief, meaning a rebuilt product can end up subtly different from the one that was actually validated with users.

### How much faster is a LaunchStudio engagement compared to a typical agency rebuild?

Most LaunchStudio engagements complete in one to three weeks versus three to six months for a full agency rebuild, since the scope addresses the specific infrastructure layer rather than re-specifying and reconstructing the entire product.
