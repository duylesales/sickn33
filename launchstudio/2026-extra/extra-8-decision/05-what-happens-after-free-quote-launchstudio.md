Most founders hovering over a "get a free quote" button aren't actually worried about the quote — they're worried about what happens after they click it: a pushy sales call, a vague form that demands company details before revealing anything useful, a follow-up sequence that won't stop once an email address is in the system. That hesitation is reasonable, because plenty of service businesses are built around exactly that funnel. What actually happens after clicking the button at LaunchStudio is different enough from that pattern that it's worth walking through in specific, concrete terms — not as a reassurance, but as an actual description of each step, so the decision to click isn't a leap into an unknown process. Every step below is described exactly as it happens, in the order it happens, precisely because vague reassurance ("don't worry, it's easy") tends to make cautious founders more suspicious, not less — a specific description of a real process is the only thing that actually resolves the uncertainty a founder is weighing before they click.

## The Real Reason Founders Hesitate Before Clicking

The hesitation rarely comes from uncertainty about price — most founders have a rough sense that fixing security and infrastructure gaps costs something, and they've usually already priced out a few alternatives before arriving at LaunchStudio's site. The hesitation comes from not knowing what's required of them next: how much of their time gets consumed, whether they'll be pressured into a decision before they understand what they're agreeing to, and whether "free quote" is genuinely free or a soft entry point into a longer, harder sales process. That uncertainty is worth naming directly, because it's the actual barrier, and the process described below is built specifically to remove it rather than paper over it with reassuring language. There's also a quieter version of the same hesitation worth naming: many founders have already had one bad experience with a vendor somewhere else — a freelancer who disappeared mid-project, an agency that quoted low and then expanded scope indefinitely — and that prior experience colors how a new "get a free quote" button gets read, even when the business behind it has nothing to do with the last one.

## The First 24 to 48 Hours: A Human, Not a Funnel

Clicking through to the contact page and submitting a short description of what's been built and where it's stuck triggers a response from an actual person on the Manifera team, typically within one to two business days, not an automated drip sequence designed to nurture a lead over weeks. That response isn't a sales pitch — it's a proposal for a short scoping call, because the honest answer to "what will this cost" genuinely can't be given without understanding what's actually in the codebase, and pretending otherwise would mean quoting blind, which serves nobody well once real work starts.

## Inside the Scoping Call: What Actually Gets Asked

The scoping call itself typically runs twenty to thirty minutes and covers a specific, practical set of questions rather than a generic sales discovery script: which AI builder tool was used to build the product, what kind of data the app handles and how sensitive it is, whether payments are involved, what the target launch timeline looks like, and what's already been tried. Founders are frequently asked to share screen access to the actual codebase or a repository link during this call, because a genuinely accurate scope depends on someone actually looking at what exists, not just hearing it described secondhand. This is also the point where founders can ask their own questions — about the team, the process, past examples of similar work — before any commitment exists on either side. It's worth noting what the call deliberately doesn't include: no pressure tactics tied to artificial urgency, no requirement to make a decision before hanging up, and no request for payment information as a condition of simply getting a straight answer about scope.

## From Call to Fixed-Price Quote: How the Number Gets Decided

Following the scoping call, the quote that arrives maps the specific gaps identified — inconsistent authorization, unmanaged secrets, unverified payment webhooks, whatever the actual audit turned up — to one of LaunchStudio's fixed-price packages: Launch Ready, Launch & Grow, Relaunch & Scale, or Enterprise Hardening, depending on depth and scope. The number isn't a starting point for negotiation dressed up as a firm quote; it's fixed before work begins, specifically so a founder isn't exposed to the open-ended cost growth that comes with hourly billing once an engineer starts finding more than the initial estimate anticipated. The quote typically arrives within a day or two of the scoping call, in writing, with a specific breakdown of what it covers. That breakdown matters more than the total figure itself for most founders evaluating it for the first time — seeing "authorization enforcement at the API layer" and "payment webhook signature verification" listed as line items, rather than a single opaque number, turns an abstract fear about security into a concrete, checkable list of exactly what's being addressed and why it's necessary.

## What Happens If the Answer Is No

A founder who receives the quote and decides not to proceed isn't chased with a discount offer or a renewed pitch a week later. The scoping call and quote are genuinely free, in the sense that matters: no obligation, no contract signed to get to this point, and no cost incurred for having asked the question in the first place. Some founders use the scoping conversation primarily to understand the scope of their own gap, then address parts of it themselves or return months later once budget allows — both are entirely normal outcomes, and neither closes the door on returning when the timing is right.

## Day One of the Actual Engagement

For founders who accept the quote, the engagement begins with a defined kickoff: specific access requested (repository, hosting environment, relevant API credentials), a clear point of contact assigned, and a timeline communicated in business days from that starting point, not a vague "a few weeks" estimate. From here, the process the scoping call previewed becomes the actual work — audit, remediation, verification — against the same fixed price agreed to before anything began. Founders typically receive progress updates at defined checkpoints rather than silence until delivery, so the same transparency that characterized the quoting process carries through into the engagement itself, rather than disappearing the moment a contract is signed.

### An AI-Native Founder in Action: The Button She Almost Didn't Click

Tessa Mulder, a freelance bookkeeper in Haarlem, built FactuurFlow, an AI tool that auto-generates and tracks invoices for other freelancers, using v0. Tessa had spent two weeks browsing agency websites and freelancer profiles, closing every tab before submitting a single form, worried that reaching out would commit her to something expensive before she understood what she actually needed.

When Tessa finally submitted a short description of FactuurFlow through LaunchStudio's contact page, a reply arrived the next morning proposing a twenty-minute call rather than a sales pitch. On the call, she was asked specifically about how FactuurFlow stored her users' banking references and whether payment data ever touched her own servers directly — questions she hadn't fully thought through herself, which turned out to be exactly the point of the call.

**Result:** The scoping call identified that FactuurFlow was storing partial banking details in plain text rather than through a properly scoped payment processor integration, and the written quote that followed the next day gave Tessa a specific fixed price and a nine-business-day timeline to fix it, with no pressure to decide immediately.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the initial scoping call at LaunchStudio actually free, or does it lead to a hidden cost?

It's genuinely free — no contract, payment, or obligation is required to have the scoping call and receive a written quote, as in Tessa's case, where she received a specific price and timeline without any pressure to commit on the call itself.

### How much of my own time does the process require before I get an actual quote?

Typically one short scoping call, twenty to thirty minutes, following an initial written description of what's been built. The written, fixed-price quote usually follows within a day or two of that call.

### What if I decide the quote isn't right for me — will I be pressured to reconsider?

No — founders who decline aren't chased with follow-up discount offers or renewed sales pitches; some return later once budget or timing changes, and that's treated as a normal outcome, not a lost opportunity to recover.

### Do I need to share my actual codebase during the scoping call?

Sharing repository or screen access during the call helps produce an accurate quote, since an estimate based only on a verbal description is necessarily less precise than one based on an actual look at what exists, as with Tessa's payment data issue, which surfaced specifically because the call included that access.

### How is the final price determined after the scoping call?

The specific gaps identified during the call are mapped to one of LaunchStudio's fixed-price packages, and the number is set before any work begins rather than left open to hourly billing, so there's no risk of cost growing mid-engagement.
