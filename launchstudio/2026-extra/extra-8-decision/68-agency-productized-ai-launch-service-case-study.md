Luuk Hendriks runs a four-person digital agency in Tilburg that specializes in branding and web design for Dutch startups. His team designs logos, builds Webflow websites, creates pitch decks, and runs social media campaigns. What they don't do — have never done, and didn't plan to do — is backend engineering. But in early 2026, three clients in the same quarter arrived with the same request: "We built a prototype in Lovable. Can you help us launch it?" Luuk's team couldn't touch the backend. They also couldn't afford to keep saying no to a recurring revenue opportunity that was walking through their door without being asked for.

## The Opportunity Luuk Saw

Each of those three clients had a similar profile: non-technical founders who used an AI tool to build a prototype, had a working frontend they were proud of, and needed someone to handle the invisible engineering work — authentication, database security, payments, deployment — before they could start charging customers. The clients came to Luuk's agency because they already trusted the agency's design and brand work, and assumed a digital agency could also handle "the technical part." They were wrong about the assumption, but right about the trust — and trust, Luuk realized, was the hard part. The engineering work itself could come from somewhere else, as long as the delivery experience felt like it came from his agency.

## How the White-Label Partnership Worked

Luuk contacted LaunchStudio after reading a case study about another agency's white-label arrangement. The terms were straightforward: Luuk's agency would sell an "AI App Launch" service to its clients at whatever price it wanted, LaunchStudio's Manifera engineering team would handle all backend work under NDA with no client-facing contact, and Luuk's clients would receive the finished product branded as a delivery from Luuk's agency. LaunchStudio's invoice would go to Luuk's agency, not to the end client.

The first engagement was Luuk's simplest client: a Rotterdam founder with a Lovable-built job board for the creative industry that needed authentication, Supabase RLS policies, and deployment. Luuk priced the service at €4,200. LaunchStudio's backend delivery cost was €1,800. The margin was €2,400 — more than Luuk's agency typically earned on a logo design project.

## The Service Luuk Built Around It

After the first successful delivery, Luuk formalized the offering. His agency's website now lists three productized AI launch packages:

**Quick Launch (€3,500):** Authentication, basic security hardening, deployment to production — for prototypes that don't process payments. LaunchStudio cost: ~€1,200. Agency margin: ~€2,300.

**Full Launch (€5,500):** Everything in Quick Launch plus payment integration (Stripe or Mollie), email notifications, and managed hosting setup. LaunchStudio cost: ~€2,500. Agency margin: ~€3,000.

**Launch & Scale (€8,000+):** Full Launch plus ongoing managed hosting (€49/month billed to the agency at cost, marked up to €99/month to the client), priority support, and quarterly security reviews. LaunchStudio cost: ~€3,500 initial + €49/month. Agency margin: ~€4,500 initial + €50/month recurring.

The margins are high because Luuk's agency adds genuine value that LaunchStudio doesn't provide: brand integration (ensuring the launched product matches the client's visual identity), client communication (managing expectations, translating technical status into language founders understand), and project management (coordinating between the client's frontend work and LaunchStudio's backend delivery). The engineering is LaunchStudio's domain; the client relationship is Luuk's.

## The Result After Three Months

By the end of the quarter, Luuk's agency had completed five AI launch engagements, generating €27,200 in revenue with approximately €14,000 in margin — from a service that didn't exist six months earlier and requires zero engineering headcount on his team. The agency's project pipeline now includes three more launch engagements in various stages of scoping.

More importantly, the launch service created a retention loop: clients who launched through Luuk's agency returned for design updates, marketing campaigns, and brand refreshes, generating downstream revenue that the agency wouldn't have captured if those clients had gone elsewhere for their launch.

> *"I was turning away revenue because I thought I'd need to hire engineers to say yes. I didn't need engineers — I needed a backend partner I could trust to deliver under my brand. LaunchStudio is that partner."*
> — **Luuk Hendriks, Director, LH Digital (Tilburg)**

**Agency's LaunchStudio costs over three months:** €11,200 across five engagements. **Agency's revenue over three months:** €27,200. **Net margin:** €16,000 + three new retainer clients.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does LaunchStudio require a minimum number of projects to start a white-label partnership?

No minimum — the partnership works on a per-project basis. Many agencies start with a single client engagement and expand as they see the model work.

### Can my agency access the code and repository during and after the engagement?

Yes — full repository access is standard. The code belongs to the end client (through the agency), and the agency has complete visibility into the work at every stage.

### What happens if a client contacts LaunchStudio directly?

LaunchStudio will not engage with the end client directly under a white-label arrangement. If a client reaches out, they'll be redirected to the agency. The NDA ensures the partnership structure is maintained.

### How does the agency handle client support requests for backend issues?

The agency passes backend support requests through to LaunchStudio's Manifera team and relays the response to the client. On the Launch & Grow plan, this support is covered by the monthly fee — the agency marks up the €49/month to whatever price it wants.

### Can my agency use its own hosting accounts, or does LaunchStudio control the infrastructure?

Either model works — LaunchStudio can deploy to the agency's own Vercel, AWS, or DigitalOcean accounts, or to accounts managed by LaunchStudio. Most agencies prefer LaunchStudio-managed infrastructure for simplicity.
